# Open data supplies context, never causes

**Executive summary.** None of the seven missing inputs can be filled fully from open, commercially usable sources. Two are partly fillable: DHW point availability (as a ceiling, not a census) and the simulator-plus-measured-data pair (Modelica Buildings components plus the SINTEF CC BY 4.0 plant-room dataset). Component failure rates are partly fillable for tank water heaters only. Cause priors, labeled real cases and time per check or repair have proxies only, and the field discrimination of each check, DHW-service failure rates for pumps, check valves, mixing valves, aquastats and balancing valves, and minutes per check have no open source at all. The best free asset is the NYC HPD complaint, violation and emergency-repair family, which gives hot-water complaint volume, apartment-only versus building-wide scope, repair actions and cost, but no cause field, and whose terms are UNCLEAR (likely YES) until a lawyer reads the live text. San Francisco DBI (PDDL, YES) and Seattle (Public Domain, YES) are the cleanest complaint-text sources but are small. Four inputs (cause priors, check likelihoods, DHW-service component hazards, minutes per check) must come from structured expert elicitation, a pilot, or a licensed or negotiated source such as NAVFAC work-order hours. The ranked action list ends this report; the first three items are a lawyer's read of the live terms, a structured elicitation seeded with the open anchors, and a linked analysis of the NYC HPD data. **This is not legal advice.** Every verdict below is the notes' reading of terms the researchers could see, and several terms pages were unreadable to them.

## Complaint logs give volume and repair mix, not causes

No open dataset the researchers found records a clean root cause for a hot-water complaint ([NYC HPD](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/ygpa-z7cr)). The nearest material splits into complaint-volume data with process-only outcomes and repair-action text with some cost. NYC HPD's complaints table holds **16,359,442 rows**, including **851,671 "NO HOT WATER"**, 739,785 "NO HEAT AND NO HOT WATER", 771,288 "NO HEAT& HOT WATER" and 34,937 "SCALDING HOT WATER" problems across **94,175 distinct buildings** with a no-hot-water report ([HPD complaints](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/ygpa-z7cr)). The apartment-only versus entire-building category field maps onto local versus shared-plant causes, which is the one structural signal relevant to a shared-cause model, but the status text holds only outcomes such as "inspected, violations issued" or "unable to gain access." The researchers flagged that the status-description counts came from a summarizing fetch and must be re-run before citation. The companion emergency-repair table has **514,707 rows**, with **20,526 PLUMB orders averaging $2,601** and **15,977 HEAT orders averaging $2,270**, and text matches for "hot water" (39,416), "water heater" (32,454) and "boiler" (34,041) that overlap and are upper bounds, because many are utility-restoration orders awarded at $0 or $1 and generic "heat & hot water related work" text ([HPD OMO](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/mdbu-nrqn)). These orders exist because an owner failed to correct a violation, so they over-represent neglected buildings and emergency-level failures. Violations add 238,024 notices mentioning hot water, 216,521 of them Class C ([HPD violations](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/wvxf-dwi5)), and a smaller handyman-order table has 3,593 hot-water descriptions ([HPD HWO](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/sbnd-xujn)). Linking complaint to violation to emergency repair by BuildingID is the most promising free experiment, because it is the only open path from a complaint to an eventual repair action.

The cleaner-licensed sources are smaller. San Francisco DBI complaints (6,026 hot-water text matches) and notices of violation (5,958 items) carry the Open Data Commons PDDL, verdict YES ([SF complaints](https://data.sfgov.org/resource/gm2e-bten), [SF NOVs](https://data.sfgov.org/resource/nbtm-fbw5)); Seattle's code complaints (1,519 matches, 341 also naming a boiler or heater) are flagged Public Domain, verdict YES ([Seattle](https://data.seattle.gov/resource/ez4a-iug7)). Philadelphia's violation code titles are component-level ("INT-WATERHEATER REPAIR-RES", "INT-WATER HEATER VALVES-RES"), a ready-made taxonomy across 2,541 rows, but 1,714 of those rows have no resolution code and only 382 record "COMPLIED - OWNER REPAIR" ([Philadelphia SQL API](https://phl.carto.com/api/v2/sql)); its license could not be confirmed, verdict UNCLEAR. Chicago's inspector comments name defects ("BROKEN WATER HEATER FLUE IN BASEMENT") across 459 water-heater rows but mostly concern safety-code items, and its terms page returned HTTP 403, verdict UNCLEAR ([Chicago](https://data.cityofchicago.org/resource/22u3-xenr)). Queensland's QBuild work register is CC BY 4.0 by API metadata, but its CSVs sat behind a bot challenge the researchers did not bypass, so field lists and hot-water job counts are unknown; the climate and building mix are also Australian government stock ([QBuild](https://www.data.qld.gov.au/dataset/building-and-asset-services-work-register)). Several of these tables carry owner names, addresses, apartment numbers or tenant contact details, so every pipeline needs a PII scrub. The team's own holdings (the 600 real work orders and FMUCD, where cause text appears in roughly 4-7% of orders per the requester) were not checked for hot-water content in these notes.

For priors, the notes offer anchors, not priors. Crossover was detected in **52% of about 100 California multifamily buildings**, a sample the authors call "not a statistically significant sample size" and a latent condition that is often asymptomatic, so it does not mean half of complaints are crossover ([Ayala & Zobrist](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2016/data/papers/1_59.pdf)). The IBHS split (leak or burst 69%, unknown 13%, supply line 10%, valve malfunction 7%, other 1%; n=700) describes homeowners' water-damage claims, so it over-represents leaks relative to "no hot water" service calls ([PNNL-22921](https://efiling.energy.ca.gov/GetDocument.aspx?tn=74366)). CDC counted equipment failures in 8 of 23 Legionnaires' outbreak investigations in 2000-2014 (35%) but only 3 of 82 deficiencies (4%) in 2015-2019, two different denominators that cannot be combined ([MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/65/wr/mm6522e1.htm), [Microorganisms 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7824450/)). One balancing consultant reports about 95% of his cases are unbalanced loops, a design-stage setting that likely overstates balancing in aging stock ([PHCP Pros](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/19678-dhw-return-design-doing-it-right-and-the-risks-of-doing-it-wrong)). These bound a Dirichlet; none replaces one.

Time data are thinner still. The only open cost-per-task data are scheduled preventive-maintenance dollars from NAVFAC records: a circulating-pump PM averaged **$60.59 (median $38.89, n=184)** and domestic-water-distribution PMs averaged $61.54 (n=186) ([NPS thesis](https://dair.nps.edu/bitstream/123456789/5443/1/NPS-CE-25-452.pdf)). These are scheduled PMs, not diagnostic checks or repairs, in dollars, not hours. The thesis states the underlying MAXIMO data included estimated and actual labor hours but publishes none; its modelled figure of about $484.72 is built on RS Means (Gordian) data and must not be reused. SF and HPD date fields give calendar days to abatement or closure, not labor time. Contractor-marketing and estimator figures (BNi, RS Means) are NO.

## Check discrimination and failure rates rest on proxies and upper bounds

No open source reports sensitivity, specificity or likelihood ratios for a technician check on a hot-water fault. What exists is outcome evidence for temperature surrogates and a few Legionella-framed accuracy numbers. In 28 commercial buildings (269 samples from 45 recirculating systems), a return-line Legionella culture predicted distal positivity with **55% sensitivity and 96% specificity**, while return temperature above 124 F gave 65% sensitivity, 72% specificity and AUC 0.68, and distal temperature and chlorine were not associated; the fetched summary flagged an internal count inconsistency in the temperature figures, so treat them as indicative ([Pathogens 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6963558/)). Those are contamination screens, not discriminators between pump failure and imbalance. Lab tests of crossover detection (pressure-gauge, water-flow, Eatherton) were binary on few runs, with the temperature-differential method graded invalid as a test, and field performance unquantified ([Ayala & Zobrist](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2016/data/papers/1_59.pdf)). Continuous logging beat spot readings in five Quebec systems (heater outlet dipped to 43 C when averages exceeded 60 C; weekly manual readings overstated one system at 62.5 versus 57.8 C), which matters because the likeliest technician input is a spot reading ([Bédard 2015](https://espace.inrs.ca/id/eprint/3147/1/Bedard2015_Manuscript_Temperature%20Diagnostic.pdf)). A Spanish hospital found 54.05% of samples under 60 C Legionella-positive versus 14.13% at or above, a risk marker, not a cause discriminator ([Sci Rep 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7820426)). Thresholds themselves are well covered and mutually consistent: storage at 60 C, circulating floor 49-51 C, scald-protected outlet at or below 43-44 C ([HSE HSG274 Part 2](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg274part2.pdf), [VHA 1061](https://www.va.gov/vhapublications/ViewPublication.asp?pub_ID=9181), [CDC toolkit](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/toolkit/potable-water-systems-module.html)), but the return-loop floor varies by source (120, 122, 124 F, and 55 C in UK healthcare) and should be a model parameter. HSG274 and the NHS HTM 04-01 Part B also supply the best open cause-and-check logic (under-30-second local response implies a working return; "cross flow" and "localised failure of the HWS return" as investigation items), both under OGL ([HTM 04-01 Part B](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/DH_HTM_0401_PART_B_acc.pdf)). The strongest stepwise diagnostic method, Boppe et al. 2016, is CC BY-NC-ND and a NO ([Boppe](https://publications.polymtl.ca/4798/1/2016_Boppe_Investigative_approach_improve_hot_water.pdf)). The team's own HVAC history is the cautionary precedent: an assumed 0.92 detection rate became 0.69 against real data, and lab accuracies from other fields should never be entered as per-check rates.

Component failure data follow the same shape: one usable split, several modelling assumptions, and a long list of absences. The IBHS split above is the only open failure-mode distribution for tank heaters, with mean age at failure 10.7 years (range 1-30) ([PNNL-22921](https://efiling.energy.ca.gov/GetDocument.aspx?tn=74366)). Median age at replacement was 14.3 years for electric (n=164) and 12.6 for gas (n=39) in one Oregon territory, a replacement age not a failure age ([Robison et al. 1996](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/1996/data/papers/SS96_Panel3_Paper16.pdf), UNCLEAR). DOE's commercial-water-heating rule uses lifetimes of 10 years for commercial gas storage and 25 for circulating heaters and hot-water supply boilers, but its repair rates (0.5% per year combustion, 1% controls, 2% high-efficiency controls) are assumptions with no cited data, and its heat-exchanger inputs rest on a 1994 boiler report ([88 FR 69686](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-10-06/html/2023-20392.htm)). ASHRAE's TRP-1237 medians (">21 years", n=304 with 36 replaced) are right-censored and therefore understate true median life ([ASHRAE Handbook A19 ch. 38](https://handbook.ashrae.org/Handbooks/A19/IP/a19_ch38/a19_ch38_ip.aspx)). Pump and valve rates are transplants: nuclear-plant motor-driven pumps fail to run at 2.26E-06 per hour (about 2% a year if run continuously) and check valves fail to close at 1.23E-04 per demand ([INL/EXT-21-65055](https://nrcoe.inl.gov/publicdocs/AvgPerf/AvgPara2020.pdf)), which the notes treat as a floor, not a prior, as are Hong Kong booster-pump installations with a 1.24-year MTTF in harsher duty ([Mui et al. 2012](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/downtime-of-in-use-water-pump-installations-for-high-rise-residen/), UNCLEAR). For mixing valves the open record is three faulty valves replaced in a hospital audit with no denominator ([Hartley et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3236657)) and a 220-valve monitoring study that reports no failure statistics ([IJERPH 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6518245/)); aquastats, balancing valves, PRVs and expansion tanks have nothing quantitative. Dividing 1 by a median life gives a constant hazard only under an exponential assumption that wear-out parts violate. Unopened free leads remain: DOE's TSD chapter 8 and appendix 8F (lifetime distributions and a literature list), the primary IBHS report, NBS Figure 6 (life versus dissolved solids, a scanned figure needing digitization), and a scanned Savannah River component table ([OSTI 752209](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/752209)). Paid sources (Quanterion NPRD-2016 listed at $850; OREDA at NOK 4,000 per volume) are NO for redistribution.

## DHW point availability is a ceiling, and simulation data is real but plant-room only

For the instrumentation tier, the notes produced the most decision-useful numbers in the whole search, all as ceilings. In CBECS 2018 (computed from public-use microdata, public domain, verdict YES), **77.6% of buildings heat water; among them 83.0% have a centralized heater alone or with point-of-use units; 18.5% of all buildings (44.5% of floorspace) have a BAS; and only 15.9% of water-heated buildings (39.4% of floorspace) are both central-or-both and BAS-equipped** ([CBECS 2018](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/commercial/data/2018/index.php?view=microdata)). BAS share of water-heated buildings rises from 11.4% at or under 5,000 sq ft to 20.8% at 5,000-50,000 and 57.3% above 50,000. Because CBECS asks about a BAS controlling heating, cooling or lighting and never about DHW, these are upper bounds on DHW point availability. NYC LL87 audits show how far below the ceiling real DHW sits: among 7,548 audited buildings with an SHW system, **29.1% flag aquastat control against 4.3% flagging EMS/BMS control of service hot water**, and 5.4% flag a building-level BAS, though 30.0% flag BAS on the heating plant, an inconsistency the researchers attribute to noisy auditor entry ([LL87](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/au6c-jqvf)). "Plant" (hot water drawn from the heating-plant boiler) is the SHW type for 57.6% of buildings and 72.8% of multifamily-like ones, so heater outlet, mixing valve and return temperatures may sit on a heating-plant controller or nowhere. A small Minnesota field sample found recirculation pumps on timers or BAS, half to two-thirds with time-of-day control, and hospitality loops running 24/7 ([Schoenbauer, ACEEE HWF 2016](https://aceee.org/sites/default/files/files/pdf/conferences/hwf/2016/Schoenbauer_Session5D_HWF16_2.23.16.pdf), UNCLEAR). The working default for the product is therefore a technician supplying gauge, controller-display and spot readings, with BAS trends as an upgrade tier. Brick v1.4.3 has the vocabulary for the tiers (DHW supply, discharge and leaving temperature sensors, DHW circulator pump, mixing valves, hot water return temperature sensor), BSD-style, YES-WITH-ATTRIBUTION; but the Mortar graphs on Hugging Face contain **zero DHW classes across 15,089 typed entities in 44 files**, which is consistent with rare DHW points but is not a prevalence number, and the dataset has no stated license ([mortargraphs](https://huggingface.co/datasets/gtfierro/mortargraphs)). Gaps for equipment mix: storage versus instantaneous and recirculation presence are absent from CBECS; NEEA's regional capacity-weighted figures (tankless 19% of Northwest capacity) are report-only, with raw data behind a form that bars redistribution ([NEEA CBSA 4](https://neea.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/CBSA-4-2019-Final-Report.pdf)). The Minnesota deck's "75% / 77% / 1.4 million" figures did not reconcile with CBECS 2012 Table B42 and should not be used.

On simulation, the building blocks exist but the assembled thing does not. The Modelica Buildings Library (3-clause BSD plus an enhancements clause) has stratified tanks, finite-volume and plug-flow pipes with heat loss, a leaky `CheckValve`, three-way valves, pumps and junctions, but no ready DHW recirculation example and no thermostatic mixing valve model; faults would be parameter or signal overrides (closed balancing valve as higher resistance, crossover as check-valve leakage), which is the researchers' inference, not a demonstrated result ([Modelica Buildings](https://github.com/lbl-srg/modelica-buildings)). Plug-flow pipes apply heat loss only in the design flow direction, so reverse-flow crossover needs the finite-volume `Pipe`. EnergyPlus is a poor first choice per community reports of weak recirculation physics, and WNTR/EPANET (BSD, MIT) has no thermal model in core ([WNTR](https://github.com/sandialabs/WNTR)). No simulation-based FDD study of a central DHW recirculation loop was found. For measured data, SINTEF's 12 Norwegian buildings (4 hotels, 4 nursing homes, 4 apartment buildings; at least 6 weeks; 2-second sampling; circulation return temperature, return flow and heat-loss power for most) are CC BY 4.0, verdict YES, and document faulty non-return valves causing periods of negative flow and higher cold-water temperatures, a real, undeliberate fault signature ([Data in Brief](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8220317/), [Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/m3xy22pf4j/2)). It is plant-room only, has no riser data, and one apartment building's circulation data are flagged poor quality. The NREL Forbell Street dataset (two multifamily buildings, recirculation controls versus constant recirculation, CC BY 4.0) is a lead only: the two .xlsx files were not opened ([OpenEI 4762](https://data.openei.org/submissions/4762)). Swedish apartment draw data (CC BY-SA 4.0) and Québec draw profiles help with demand, not loop faults. California's ETCC survey reports recirculation loss of 24.1-99.9 W per apartment (mean 62.5) and field losses 1.1-2.3 times idealized calculations, but only as report-level statistics with gated PDFs ([ETCC](https://www.etcc-ca.com/reports/multifamily-domestic-hot-water-recirculation-survey)). No labeled healthy-versus-faulty series exists in any open source found.

## The notes disagree in ten places, and the later, better-read note wins

First, the license for the Sydney TMV-monitoring paper (PMC6518245) is CC BY 4.0, YES-WITH-ATTRIBUTION in the reliability note, which reports it as read, but UNCLEAR in the diagnostic-knowledge note, which saw only a snippet behind a verification wall; the reliability note's reading stands, though counsel should confirm. Second, the ETCC verdict is NO in one note ("All rights reserved") and UNCLEAR in another; use NO for redistribution until counsel reads the terms. Third, Boppe's license was "UNVERIFIED" in the simulation note but verified as CC BY-NC-ND on page 1 in the diagnostic note; NO is firm. Fourth, the FEMP O&M guide was downloaded and searched in the reliability note (no water-heater failure rates) but returned 503 in the diagnostic note; the reliability note stands. Fifth, VHA 1061 appears as a 2014 edition (via a New Jersey copy) in one note and as the 2021 edition amended 2024 in the diagnostic note; the overlapping thresholds (140 F storage, 124 F distribution) agree, but cite the current edition. Sixth, the return-loop floor is 120 F (CDC, ASHRAE G12 Addendum b), 122 F (UFC, OSHA), 124 F (VHA) and 55 C in UK healthcare, and an older G12 text quoted by snippet says 124 F; keep it a parameter. Seventh, UFC 3-420-01 is listed Active on WBDG but the read copy is watermarked CANCELLED. Eighth, the Mortar paper says 90 buildings where the task brief said 91, and the 44 graph files may not be the full set. Ninth, the plug-flow pipe model is described with wall thermal inertia in current Modelica Buildings documentation but without it in an obsolete base class cited in another note; trust the current classes. Tenth, the simulation note reports the Ayala and Zobrist crossover report as "not located," while another note read the open ACEEE paper in full. Smaller inconsistencies also matter for use: LL87's BAS flag (5.4%) versus heating-plant BAS (30.0%); the Hartley audit's tap counts do not add up and an earlier note cites a PHRP page for what looks like the same audit, which was not reconciled; and the 52% crossover figure appears as ">50%" in the abstract and "52%" only through a snippet.

## Elicitation, a pilot or a licence must supply what open data cannot

Four quantities have no open basis and must be elicited: the prior over causes of each complaint type, the likelihood of each reading under each cause, DHW-service hazard curves for pumps, check valves, TMVs, aquastats and balancing valves, and minutes per check and per repair. Elicitation should use the open anchors above as bounds, run with 6-8 service plumbers, facility engineers and junior-to-mid-level technicians (the earlier plan's pilot lesson), and carry sensitivity analysis over wide ranges, since none of the anchors is a complaint-conditional rate. Three inputs need a pilot: cause-confirmed cases (a partner building with work-order access), a real DHW point census at the pilot buildings, and field detection rates from paired checks, which is the only honest way to avoid a repeat of the 0.92-to-0.69 correction. Clip-on loggers cost roughly $70-$200 per point plus labor per the check-catalogue prices, which converts physical-only checks into time series. Licensed or negotiated routes are narrower: NAVFAC MAXIMO estimated and actual labor hours (a federal record), NEEA CBSA data under a permission that allows internal use, ETCC raw monitoring data, service-company CMMS or manufacturer warranty data for failure-mode shares, and a commercial license for Boppe or ASHRAE content if the exact procedures are wanted. A Freedom of Information route to UK NHS trusts' TMV in-service test records was suggested by one search result and is untested.

## Conclusion

The search changes the shape of the problem more than its size. The open record is rich on context: how many buildings have central heaters, how few have BAS-visible DHW points, what temperatures count as healthy, and how many complaints exist and what repairs followed. It is empty on cause, discrimination and time. So the model's default operating tier should be a technician supplying gauge and spot readings, not BAS trends, and the elicitation that cannot be avoided can be narrowed to four quantities anchored by the open numbers rather than started from nothing.

The most consequential gap is also the least visible: because no open dataset carries a confirmed cause, validation of any posterior needs a pilot's labeled cases, and the free HPD linkage can test only the shared-plant grouping assumption, not accuracy. Counsel's read of the live terms is the cheapest step and the first, because the largest free dataset's commercial status still rests on a terms page nobody on this research team could open.
