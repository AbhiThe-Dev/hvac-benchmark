## Q1. Classical theory: how to choose the next probe/test, when to stop, what is guaranteed, and where myopic information gain is known to fail

### Takeaway
The user's design is the direct descendant of de Kleer-Williams GDE/Sherlock (one-step minimum-expected-entropy probe choice, chosen because full lookahead is intractable) plus Heckerman-Breese cost-aware troubleshooting; one-step lookahead is the de facto standard in sequential MBD and is provably within a log factor of optimal only in the noise-free, identify-the-exact-hypothesis case. For noisy tests with a decision/class target (exactly the user's setting) greedy information gain has proven worst-case gaps (Omega(n/log n)) caused by test complementarities, and empirically its performance hinges on the quality of the prior (with wrong priors, random or prior-independent split-in-half selection beat entropy; best-vs-worst heuristic overheads reach 250%+ in Rodler & Schmid and 2000%+ in the RIO experiments).

### Cited Findings

#### GDE / Sherlock: probe selection by minimum expected entropy
- GDE (de Kleer & Williams, Artificial Intelligence 32(1):97-130, 1987; corrected 2008 reprint) frames diagnosis as finding "model-artifact differences"; "If the task is troubleshooting, then the model is presumed to be correct and all model-artifact differences indicate part malfunctions. If the task is theory formation, then the artifact is presumed to be correct and all model-artifact differences indicate required changes in the model." [READ] — [GDE reprint](https://dekleer.org/Publications/gde-2006.pdf)
- GDE explicitly rejects full lookahead: evaluating any sequence of measurements "can be implemented as a classic decision tree analysis, but the computational cost of this analysis is prohibitive. Instead we use a one-step lookahead strategy based on Shannon entropy." The next measurement is the one minimizing expected entropy of the candidate probabilities; entropy H = -sum p_i log p_i is justified as proportional to expected cost of isolating the true candidate (cost of locating a candidate of probability p_i ~ log(1/p_i), a binary-search argument). [READ] — [GDE reprint](https://dekleer.org/Publications/gde-2006.pdf)
- GDE authors chose minimum entropy "because it was the best evaluation function which is efficiently computable (i.e., it is based on one-step lookahead)"; a multi-step alternative used in IN-ATE (gamma miniaverage heuristic search) is "computationally expensive", and "there is no reason, in principle, it could not be incorporated into GDE." [READ] — [GDE reprint](https://dekleer.org/Publications/gde-2006.pdf)
- Sequel literature on the same theme (titles only, papers not opened): "One Step Lookahead is Pretty Good" (de Kleer, Raiman, Shirley, DX-91 / Readings in MBD 1992), "Trading off the costs of inference vs. probing in diagnosis" (IJCAI-95), "Focusing on probable diagnoses" (AAAI-91), "Using crude probability estimates to guide diagnosis" (AIJ 1990), "Diagnosis with Behavioral Modes" (Sherlock, IJCAI-89), Liu, de Kleer et al. "A Unified Information Criterion for Evaluating Probe and Test Selection" (PHM 2008). [SNIP-level: bibliography listing] — [de Kleer publications page](https://dekleer.org/bib.htm)
- de Kleer (IJCAI-2009, from DX-2007) compares probing strategies by expected diagnostic cost DC(D,A) = sum_s p(s) c(D,s,A), "Generally DC must be estimated by sampling"; "In conventional model-based diagnosis, (following the myopic strategy) the best probe to make next is the one which minimizes H = -sum p(D) log p(D)". Worked intermittent-fault example: a strict worst-case bound of 58 probes versus expected cost DC = 22.2 (observed error 0.05) under minimum entropy; "groups of components are simultaneously exonerated so that DC grows roughly logarithmic with circuit size." [READ] — [de Kleer IJCAI-09](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/09/Papers/127.pdf)
- Same paper lists its presuppositions: one observed variable per time instant, no measurement error, measuring does not change the system, components fail independently ("dependent failures may lead to misdiagnosis"), stationary process; it also notes that "two wires may be shorted, making it appear as if a gate is intermittent, when in fact there is an unmodeled and unwanted bridge connection" (an unmodeled structural fault masquerading as a noisy component). [READ] — [de Kleer IJCAI-09](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/09/Papers/127.pdf)
- Rodler et al. characterize the original GDE proposal as offering two query-selection strategies, split-in-half and entropy-based, and state entropy "can make optimal profit from exploiting properly adjusted initial fault probabilities, whereas it can completely fail in the case of weak prior information." [READ] — [RIO, Rodler et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2465)

#### Decision-theoretic troubleshooting (Heckerman, Breese, Rommelse; Scania/Volvo-line work)
- Heckerman, Breese, Rommelse, "Decision-theoretic troubleshooting", Communications of the ACM 38:49-57 (1995): Bayesian-network troubleshooting with repair, observation and configuration-change actions; applied to printing, photocopier feeders, automobiles, gas turbines. [SNIP] — [Springer chapter listing / CORE](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4615-5089-1_15)
- Breese & Heckerman (UAI 1996) "Decision-Theoretic Troubleshooting: A Framework for Repair and Experiment": three action classes (observe; repair then observe; change configuration then observe), needing "persistence" in the belief network for the latter two. The planner "selects the next best action by estimating the expected cost of repair for various plans... Because computation of the optimal plan is intractable" and "We use these cost estimates myopically to determine the next troubleshooting step. After each action, probabilities are updated and a new set of potential plans are generated." [READ] — [Breese & Heckerman 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3563)
- The tractable core (time linear in the number of components, no decision tree) rests on assumptions: one problem-defining variable; device faulty at onset; single fault (sum p_i = 1); components observable or unobservable; action costs independent of earlier actions; no observations other than observation-repair of components. Optimal ordering is by decreasing p_i / C_i (probability over observation cost); expected cost of repair ECR = sum_i (1 - sum_{j<i} p_j)(C^o_i + p_i C^r_i) / normalization. A "service call" (fixed charge that repairs any fault) is a straightforward extension (footnote, citing Heckerman et al. 1995). Information gathering "proceeds until a single cause has been identified or the current diagnosis is sufficiently restricted to support action." [READ] — [Breese & Heckerman 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3563)
- "Mapping nodes": uncertainty about which causal mechanism links cause and effect (e.g. icon "ok / stuck on grey / stuck on normal / backwards") is represented as a persistent variable across actions, i.e. an explicit model-uncertainty device in a troubleshooting BN. [READ] — [Breese & Heckerman 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3563)
- Warnquist (PhD, Linkoping 2015, with Scania CV AB), "Troubleshooting Trucks - Automated Planning and Diagnosis": BN diagnosis (workshop-only and remote/long-monitoring variants) + planners minimizing expected cost of repair (ECR), evaluated on models of an auxiliary braking system, a fuel injection system and an engine temperature monitoring system. States optimal troubleshooting is "computationally intractable [100]", so it is solved "by only computing the plan a limited number of steps ahead and then using a heuristic to estimate the remaining ECR"; "near-optimal" means provable bound on ECR gap. [READ] — [Warnquist thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:856040/FULLTEXT02.pdf)
- Same thesis: Iterative Bounding LAO* (lower and upper cost bounds, stochastic-shortest-path formulation, smaller search graph than comparable algorithms, provable bound on optimal ECR); separating diagnostic actions (tests, repairs) from preparatory actions shortens plan depth; cites the Sun & Weld entropy heuristic h(s) = c_H * H(s) + sum_d Pr(d|s) c_rep(d) used with one-step lookahead, and a Heckerman et al. heuristic "derived from the exact solution of an alternate simpler problem"; describes AO* as returning the optimal AND/OR solution when the heuristic is admissible. [READ] — [Warnquist thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:856040/FULLTEXT02.pdf)
- Lin (2013) "Decision-Theoretic Troubleshooting: Hardness of Approximation": several variants NP-complete; "computing approximate troubleshooting strategies is NP-hard" via set-cover connections. [ABS; a fetched summary said the arXiv entry notes withdrawal after IJAR publication, unverified] — [arXiv 1304.6551](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.6551)
- Related Scania/KTH items seen only in search results: Warnquist/Pernestal/Nyberg "Anytime Near-Optimal Troubleshooting Applied to a Auxiliary Truck Braking System" (SAFEPROCESS 2009), Pernestal/Nyberg/Warnquist "Modeling and inference for troubleshooting with interventions applied to a heavy truck auxiliary braking system" (2012), "A Modeling Framework for Troubleshooting Automotive Systems" (Applied Artificial Intelligence, 2016; page returned 403). [SNIP] — [Applied AI 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08839514.2016.1156955)

#### Test sequencing, AND/OR search, sequential MBD with active testing
- Pattipati & Alexandridis, "Application of Heuristic Search and Information Theory to Sequential Fault Diagnosis", IEEE Trans. SMC-A 20:872-886 (1990): test-sequencing problem (minimum-cost test sequence reaching a diagnostic conclusion), generalized to asymmetric and multi-valued tests; Sequential Diagnosis Tool. [SNIP, paper not opened] — [Sequential diagnosis tool summary](https://www.academia.edu/18906641/Sequential_diagnosis_tool)
- Feldman, Provan, van Gemund, "A Model-Based Active Testing Approach to Sequential Diagnosis" (JAIR 39:301-334, 2010): FRACTAL combines MBD with automated test pattern generation; greedy FRACTAL-G approximates the hard optimal control-assignment computation; evaluated on ISCAS85/74XXX by reduction in remaining minimal-cardinality diagnoses. [ABS/SUMM] — [arXiv 1401.3850](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3850)
- Rodler, Schmid, Schekotihin (2017): query (set-of-measurements) selection optimized on two axes, expected number of queries and cost per query, with polynomially many inference calls. [ABS] — [arXiv 1705.09879](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09879)
- Rodler & Schmid (DX-2018 / RR-2018): "the global optimization of these costs... has been proved to be NP-hard... As a result, SD methods have to confine themselves to a local optimization... a one-step lookahead evaluation of measurements proved to be an already very good trade-off... Hence, most, if not all, state-of-the-art SD approaches leverage such one-step-lookahead heuristics." [READ] — [Rodler & Schmid](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03083)

#### Myopic vs non-myopic: key theorems and known failure cases
- Noise-free identification of the exact hypothesis (optimal decision tree, ODT): approximating within o(log n) of optimal is NP-hard; for ODT, greedy information gain and generalized binary search (GBS) coincide and satisfy c(greedy) <= c(opt) * (ln(1/p_min) + 1), p_min = smallest hypothesis prior; proof via adaptive submodularity and strong adaptive monotonicity. [READ] — [Golovin, Krause, Ray, NIPS 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3091)
- Dasgupta (NIPS 2004): greedy (halving-mass) query rule uses at most 4 Q* ln(1/min_h pi(h)) queries where Q* is the optimal expected number; lower bounds: uniform prior, optimal Theta(log n) but greedy Omega(log n * log n / log log n) on some class; non-uniform prior, optimal < 3 but greedy >= n/2 on some class. Mechanism: "H might consist of several dense clusters, each of which permits rapid binary search. However, the version space must first be whittled down to one of these subregions, and this process, though ultimately optimal, might initially be slower at decreasing entropy than more shortsighted alternatives." [READ] — [Dasgupta](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~dasgupta/papers/greedy.pdf)
- Golovin & Krause (JAIR 2011): adaptive submodularity generalizes submodularity to adaptive policies; adaptive greedy is (1-1/e)-competitive for adaptive stochastic maximization and log-factor competitive for adaptive stochastic minimization (worst and average case); arXiv v5 (Dec 2017) corrected a proof flaw and requires a "stronger version of adaptive submodularity" for the squared-log guarantee. [ABS/SUMM] — [arXiv 1003.3967](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.3967)
- Noisy tests with a class/decision target (Equivalence Class Determination, ECD; this is the user's regime): GBS "can perform very poorly"; greedy (myopic) information gain measured on the class distribution "can pay Omega(n/log(n)) times the optimal cost, even under a uniform prior", and Theorem 8/9 show the same lower bound for the value-of-information policy and for any greedy "posterior-based" policy (one that uses only the posterior over classes); stated cause: "there are complementarities among tests; a set of tests can be far better than the sum of its parts." The EC2 (Equivalence Class Edge Cutting) objective is adaptive submodular with c(pi_EC) <= (2 ln(1/p_min) + 1) c(pi*), holding with non-uniform test costs and correlated noise; in the paper's experiments EffECXtive (EC2 approximation) "outperforms InfoGain significantly, which outperforms Random." The greedy rule analyzed is t* = argmax Delta(t|x_A)/c(t), i.e. benefit-per-cost, the same form as EIG-per-cost. [READ] — [Golovin, Krause, Ray](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3091)
- Javdani, Chen, Karbasi, Krause, Bagnell, Srinivasa (AISTATS 2014): generalizes to overlapping decision regions ("drive uncertainty into a single decision region as quickly as possible"); Hyperedge Cutting (HEC) proved competitive with the intractable optimal policy; notes GBS performs poorly and EC2 has O(log n) guarantee in the disjoint-regions case. Experiments on MovieLens (VoI 8.8, GBS-HEC 7.4, EC2-HEC 6.4 queries) and robotic touch localization. [READ] — [Javdani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.5886)
- Chen et al. (AAAI 2015) "Submodular Surrogates for Value of Information": value of information optimization is intractable and "myopic (greedy) approximations are known to perform poorly"; DIRECT optimizes an adaptive-submodular surrogate aligned with VoI. [SNIP of abstract] — [AAAI 2015](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9694)
- Krause & Guestrin (UAI 2005): first efficient randomized (1-1/e-eps) algorithm for nonmyopic subset selection in graphical models; no polynomial algorithm can beat (1-1/e) unless P=NP. [ABS] — [arXiv 1207.1394](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1394). JAIR 2009 "Optimal Value of Information in Graphical Models": myopic selection "does not provide any performance guarantees in general"; exact nonmyopic algorithms for chain models (subset selection and conditional plans). [SNIP] — [arXiv 1401.3474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3474)
- Value of information is not always concave: Radner & Stiglitz (1984) nonconcavity, with Chade & Schlee and De Lara & Gilotte giving general/tight conditions under which the marginal value of a small amount of information is zero (so myopic VoI can see nothing where larger information has value). [SNIP] — [Chade & Schlee](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=293560). Whitmeyer (2024): "A natural way of quantifying the 'amount of information' in decision problems yields a globally concave value for information. Another (in contrast, adversarial) way almost never does." [ABS] — [arXiv 2404.01190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01190)

#### Empirical evidence inside MBD on heuristics and prior quality
- Rodler & Schmid, real-world ontology-debugging cases, 8 query-selection heuristics, plausible / random / implausible fault-prior scenarios: "The one and only best QSM does not exist"; picking an inappropriate one can cost "overheads of over 250%"; the main drivers of cost are "the bias in and the quality of the fault probability distribution"; "the very popular entropy measure only manifested good (albeit not best) behavior in a single set of scenarios"; with wrongly biased priors "introducing randomness into the query selection procedure saves oracle effort" (random selection beat entropy and even beat prior-independent split-in-half); entropy is "strongly probability-reliant" and diverges as prior bias grows; the MPS heuristic (which "suggests very 'risky' queries" to invalidate as many diagnoses as possible) dominates when the fault prior points neither toward nor against the true diagnosis, yet is "definitely the wrong choice" with a plausible but unbiased (uniform) prior. Results averaged over up to 20 sessions x 3 prior draws per case. [READ] — [Rodler & Schmid](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03083)
- RIO (Rodler et al. 2013): "misleading meta information might result in a dramatic decrease of the performance and its assessment is only possible a-posteriori"; selecting an inappropriate strategy (entropy vs split-in-half) "can result in a substantial increase of more than 2000% w.r.t. number of queries"; RIO adapts by reinforcement (drifts from entropy-like to split-in-half behavior when observed elimination rate is poor), with savings of up to 80% of user interaction on average across good and bad priors. [READ] — [RIO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2465)

#### Stopping rules and sequential testing theory
- Active sequential hypothesis testing (Chernoff 1959 "Procedure A"; Naghshvar & Javidi, Annals of Statistics 41(6):2703-2738, 2013): dynamic-programming lower bounds on optimal total cost; one heuristic policy is asymptotically optimal "in Chernoff's sense" (relative gap to optimal cost vanishes as the penalty of a wrong declaration grows), i.e. a guarantee for a myopic-style rule that holds in the high-confidence regime. [ABS + SNIP for the Chernoff detail] — [arXiv 1203.4626](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.4626)
- Cost-aware sequential hypothesis testing (Vershinin, Cohen, Gurewitz, arXiv Dec 2025, revised Sep 2026): active decision-maker with actions of differing (random) costs, minimizes expected total cost not sample count; optimal expected cost scales as Theta(log(1/delta)); "design principles maximizing information gain relative to cost"; ex-ante cost revelation lets an action be cancelled mid-operation. [ABS/SUMM] — [arXiv 2512.19067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19067)
- In troubleshooting (Breese-Heckerman, Warnquist) the stop rule is goal-based (device repaired, or diagnosis specific enough to act), not a confidence threshold; in noise-free ODT the stop rule is |version space| = 1; in ECD/EC2 the stop rule is "guaranteed to choose the same hypothesis as if it had observed the outcome of all tests." [READ] — [Breese & Heckerman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3563); [Golovin, Krause, Ray](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3091)

### Inferences
- Lineage match. The user's stack is GDE/Sherlock-style one-step entropy probing, HBR/Warnquist-style cost-aware action choice (EIG per cost is the same benefit/cost greedy rule analyzed by Golovin et al. and the same shape as HBR's p_i/C_i ordering), and a sequential-testing stop rule. Nothing in the classical literature argues the myopic core is unusual; it is the accepted engineering trade-off (GDE, Rodler & Schmid, Warnquist all say so).
- What is and is not guaranteed for this system. The (ln(1/p_min)+1) guarantee covers noise-free identification of the exact hypothesis. The user's tests are noisy (Gaussian class-conditionals) and the target is a coarse decision ("documented owner correct?"), which is the ECD/decision-region regime where the proven worst-case for greedy information gain is Omega(n/log n). Caveat: the cited lower-bound construction uses many classes (2^q classes of two hypotheses each), so the proof does not by itself show a bad case for a 2-class target; the mechanism (test complementarity) is what to watch.
- Concrete complementarity risks in HVAC: (a) a physical action (actuate a damper/valve) that makes a subsequent sensor statistic informative while the sensor alone has ~zero EIG; (b) two weak sensors that only discriminate jointly (synergy: mutual information of a pair can exceed the sum of singles); (c) a duct-trace/actuation test whose value is only realized after a second confirming test. Cheap mitigations that stay inside the classical toolbox: two-step lookahead restricted to (physical test, sensor) pairs; EC2/edge-cutting or entropy-over-owner-posterior as a second scorer (owner set is small, so exact computation is cheap); take the max or a vote of scorers (Rodler & Schmid suggest meta-heuristics/ensembles).
- Prior sensitivity is the best-documented empirical failure of entropy-based selection (Rodler & Schmid; RIO). It matters directly for LLM-elicited or blueprint-derived priors over candidate owners: if the prior is confidently wrong, EIG will keep probing to confirm it. RIO-style risk switching (fall back toward split-in-half or randomization when the elimination rate is poor) is a tested remedy.
- Futility/conditional-power stopping has no direct source in this pass; conceptually it is a truncated sequential test on top of Wald/Chernoff-type theory. The high-confidence-regime optimality result (Naghshvar-Javidi) suggests myopic EIG is most defensible when the confidence threshold is strict (rare wrong declarations) and least defensible when thresholds are loose.
- AO*/test-sequencing (Pattipati line) is the offline, non-myopic counterpart: worthwhile only if the candidate set and test set are small enough to enumerate. The user's small owner sets may make exact lookahead over 2-3 steps tractable at run time (unlike circuit-scale GDE), which removes the main reason for myopia.

## Q2. LLM hybrids with model-based diagnosis, BNs, fault trees/FMEA and test sequencing in engineering domains, 2023-2026

### Takeaway
In engineering troubleshooting the LLM is almost always an evidence extractor, knowledge-graph/FMEA constructor, retrieval-QA layer, or explainer; the only work where an LLM actually chooses the next test under cost is medical (MAI-DxO) or generic question-asking (UoT, BED-LLM), and none of the engineering papers I could verify compares an LLM test-chooser against a GDE/Sherlock/AO* baseline. Where non-LLM baselines exist, simple retrieval or deterministic evidence extraction is competitive with or beats the LLM (PHM 2025: retrieval-only 86% vs LLM-only 70% hit rate), and structure (explicit evidence extraction + formal or learned ranking) is what lifts LLM systems.

### Cited Findings
Format per item: mapping (hypothesis space / probe / evidence / stop / cost), LLM role, evaluation, failure modes.

1. **MAI-DxO / Sequential Diagnosis Benchmark (Nori, ..., Horvitz; Microsoft; arXiv Jun-Jul 2025; medical, closest cost-aware analogue)** — [arXiv 2506.22405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22405) [READ]
   - Mapping: hypotheses = differential diagnosis; probes = questions and tests requested from a "Gatekeeper"; evidence = returned findings; stop = the panel "commits to a final diagnosis"; cost = dollar cost of tests (2023 US price table, some LLM-estimated) plus $300 per physician visit.
   - LLM role: a prompted virtual panel of five roles: Dr. Hypothesis (maintains a probability-ranked differential), Dr. Test-Chooser (selects up to three tests per round that "maximally discriminate"), Dr. Challenger (anchoring devil's advocate), Dr. Stewardship (cost-conscious alternatives), Dr. Checklist; plus a budget-tracker variant and an ensemble variant. The Gatekeeper that simulates test results is itself an LLM (o4-mini) holding the full case file, returning synthetic findings when the case lacks the queried test.
   - Evaluation: 304 NEJM Case Challenge cases (benchmark simulation, no deployment); 21 US/UK generalist physicians: 20% accuracy at $2,963/case; GPT-4o 49.3% at $2,745; o3 78.6% at $7,850; MAI-DxO with o3 79.9% at $2,397 or 85.5% at $7,184.
   - Failure/limits stated: curated hard cases, no healthy or benign patients (false-positive rate unmeasurable); costs first-order; physicians barred from search engines/colleagues; risk/invasiveness/wait times not modeled. No Bayesian-network or EIG baseline. Off-the-shelf models were cost-blind (o3 spent about 3.3x MAI-DxO's cost for similar accuracy), and scaffolding, not the base model, fixed it.

2. **Uncertainty of Thoughts, UoT (Hu et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2402.03271)** — [arXiv 2402.03271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03271) [SUMM numbers via arXiv HTML v3; task description also SNIP]
   - Mapping: hypotheses = LLM-generated "possibility set"; probe = yes/no question; evidence = user answer; LLM simulates answer branches, reward = entropy-based information gain over the partition of the possibility set (with a sharpening denominator), propagated through a lookahead tree (non-myopic via tree search); stop = success/turn cap (20 turns).
   - LLM role: question proposer, answer simulator and possibility-set partitioner; the information-gain scoring and tree selection are formal.
   - Evaluation: 5 datasets over 3 tasks (20 Questions, simplified medical diagnosis, a basic troubleshooting task = FloDial, 894 dialogues / 153 faults about computers, devices, machinery). GPT-4 on troubleshooting: UoT 67.3% success (7.8 mean successful turns) vs direct prompting 43.7% (13.4), Reflexion 28.6%, adapted ToT 60.3% (8.2); medical DX: UoT 97.0% vs DP 91.3%. [SUMM]
   - Limits stated: answer sets may overlap ("different answers may lead to exclusion of different possibility sets"); the baselines are LLM prompting variants, not a formal EIG over a real fault model.

3. **BED-LLM (Choudhury et al., ICLR 2026, arXiv 2508.21184)** — [arXiv 2508.21184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21184) [ABS]
   - Mapping: sequential Bayesian experimental design; the LLM's belief distribution is the probabilistic model, questions maximize expected information gain given history; hypotheses filtered for consistency with the history (paper says it is first to use this prior-likelihood pairing without a deterministic-likelihood assumption per a search snippet). LLM role: probability model and hypothesis sampler under an external EIG rule. Evaluated on 20 Questions and user-preference inference vs LLM baselines; "substantial performance improvements" (numbers not extracted). Not an engineering-fault benchmark.

4. **Cloud/data-center RCA agents.**
   - OpenRCA (ICLR 2025): 335 failures from three enterprise software systems, >68 GB of logs/metrics/traces; "even with the specially designed RCA-agent, the best-performing model, Claude 3.5, solved only 11.34% failure cases." [SNIP] — [OpenRCA repo](https://github.com/microsoft/OpenRCA), [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/32093)
   - EviRCA (arXiv Sep 2026): separates deterministic evidence extraction (raw telemetry to structured "evidence cards") from LLM reasoning via read-only tools; correct rate 40.6%-43.9% across two LLMs vs prior baseline up to 15.2%; 15-26x fewer tokens, 3-20x faster. [ABS] — [arXiv 2609.19825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19825)

   - Mapping: hypotheses = faulty component/cause; evidence = telemetry; probes are tool calls (queries) with no explicit information-gain or cost model in the abstracts.

5. **Ground-vehicle fault-code recommendation with technical manuals (PHM Society 2025, LMI)** — [PHM 2025](https://papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/phmconf/article/download/4549/phmc_25_4549) [READ]

### Inferences
Per paper: non-LLM state of the art? and what transfers.
- MAI-DxO. Non-LLM SOTA: for a known probabilistic model, formal EIG-per-cost remains the reference; MAI-DxO does not test against it (no Bayesian baseline). Transfer: shows that an LLM can approximate cost-aware test ordering only when scaffolded (explicit cost tracking, "maximally discriminate" role, challenger to break anchoring); raw o3 over-tests. For HVAC, keep the cost/EIG arithmetic in the formal layer and use an LLM only where a test's outcome model or cost is not in the model (e.g., proposing a physical test not in the catalogue).
- UoT / BED-LLM. Non-LLM SOTA: formal EIG over an explicit hypothesis set remains the gold standard when the hypothesis set and likelihoods exist; these methods exist to import EIG-style scoring into settings where the LLM must supply the hypothesis set and likelihoods. Transfer: the user already has the exact posterior and Gaussian likelihoods, so the analogue is using the LLM only to widen the candidate-owner set or propose test outcome likelihoods, not to score.
- OpenRCA / EviRCA / Roy et al. Non-LLM SOTA: statistical/causal RCA methods were not benchmarked in the pieces I read. Transfer: EviRCA's gain from decoupling extraction from reasoning matches the "LLM as evidence interpreter, formal layer decides" pattern; the 11.34% OpenRCA ceiling shows unscaffolded agents on heterogeneous telemetry are weak.
- PHM 2025. Non-LLM (retrieval) still wins on label matching; LLM added value only when paraphrase/abstraction needed. Transfer: for mapping messy point names or documentation text to structured candidates, expect retrieval/embedding baselines to be hard to beat; use LLM for abstraction cases only.
- HybridRAG / CAMB / HiL KG-RAG. Non-LLM SOTA not compared; baselines are other RAG variants. Transfer: knowledge-graph schema errors (mis-typed relations) and noisy maintenance text are the reported error sources, which is exactly the risk if a KG of building connectivity is LLM-built.
- OGPAL. Non-LLM (RGCN) is the ranker; the LLM is a schema-alignment tool. Transfer: LLM-assisted normalization of heterogeneous documentation into a shared ontology (equipment/point naming) with a learned or formal ranker downstream.
- JFTA-Bench / FTA-GenAI. Non-LLM SOTA: manually authored fault trees/troubleshooting manuals remain the reference; the benchmark measures LLMs following a given tree in dialogue, not choosing tests. Transfer: only that LLMs need a structured textual encoding of the model to track it over long multi-turn troubleshooting, and error-recovery (user mistakes) is a distinct capability.
- Overall placement of the LLM across verified 2023-2026 work: evidence extractor (EviRCA, HiL, OGPAL), KG/FMEA/fault-tree constructor (OGPAL, HybridRAG, NuLLM, JFTA), retrieval-QA/explainer (PHM, HybridRAG, Hy-LIFT), hypothesis generator (MAI-DxO's Dr. Hypothesis, Hy-LIFT zero-shot), test chooser (MAI-DxO, UoT, BED-LLM only, none in verified engineering settings). This confirms and extends the earlier pass: the formal layer should choose tests; the LLM belongs upstream (candidate generation, evidence extraction, model construction) and downstream (explanation).
- Head-to-head numbers versus formal baselines: the only LLM-vs-non-LLM comparisons I could verify are PHM 2025 (retrieval-only 86% > LLM-only 70% > RAG 57-64%) and OGPAL (vs RGCN, [SUMM]); no engineering paper I found compares LLM test choice with GDE/Sherlock entropy probing, AO* test sequencing or HBR cost ordering.

## Q3. Using an LLM to build or repair the structural/causal model (fault tree, BN structure, connectivity KG) that a classical diagnoser then uses, and how its errors propagate

### Takeaway
LLM-built structure is useful mainly when data are scarce, but the evidence shows non-trivial edge error rates (worse on domain-specific networks than on famous textbook ones) and that erroneous prior edges can degrade learned structure; the mitigations are iterating LLM judgments against data and soft constraints. I found no paper that measures how LLM-built BN/fault-tree errors translate into diagnosis quality (number of tests to isolate); the closest proxy is classical evidence that a wrong prior costs entropy-based probing 250%-2000% more queries.

### Cited Findings
- ILS-CSL (Ban et al., arXiv Nov 2023): notes "existing approaches utilizing LLMs for CSL have encountered issues, including unreliable constraints from imperfect LLM inferences"; proposes iterative LLM supervision of the learned DAG using edge-level (not path-level ancestral) constraints, evaluated across eight standard BN benchmark datasets (Cancer 5 nodes up to Barley 48 nodes). Its Table 1 (GPT-4 ancestral constraints from earlier work) lists correct/erroneous constraint counts T/F per dataset in the header order Cancer, Asia, Child, Insurance, Alarm, Mildew, Water, Barley as 5/0, 9/0, 8/2, 10/0, 20/1, 9/6, 5/3, 17/7 (my reading of the extracted table; column-to-dataset mapping inferred) and marks cases where adding LLM knowledge degraded structural accuracy; a comparison method (sepLLM) "shows consistent improvement only in the Cancer and Child datasets, while exhibiting partial performance degradation in others"; ILS-CSL claims to reduce erroneous constraints "theoretically by a factor of O(N)". Data are semi-synthetic benchmark BNs, not engineering systems. [READ] — [arXiv 2311.11689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11689)
- Chen et al. (IEEE TPAMI 2025; arXiv 2306.07032): existing structure learners show "limited resilience to errors in the prior"; theoretical impact of prior errors on Structural Hamming Distance; identifies "quasi-circle" acyclic closed structures as where prior errors are most hazardous. [ABS] — [arXiv 2306.07032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07032)
- PromptBN / ReActBN (Zhang, Zhang, Kordjamshidi, Cui; TMLR 2026; arXiv 2511.00574): PromptBN builds a DAG from one LLM call (O(1) query complexity) in data-free settings; ReActBN refines with data; better than data-only, LLM-only and hybrid baselines "particularly in low- or no-data regimes and on out-of-distribution datasets." [ABS] — [arXiv 2511.00574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00574)
- Downstream, structure errors in LLM-built diagnostic KGs are named as failure sources: HybridRAG's schema ambiguities (relations applied to wrong entity pairs) and noisy source records; OGPAL's LLM extraction "may still require some human oversight". [READ] — [HybridRAG](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-02643-2); [SUMM] — [OGPAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15428)
- Classical proxy for parameter/prior errors propagating into test counts: with misleading fault probabilities entropy-based selection "can completely fail" and picking the wrong strategy costs more than 250% (Rodler & Schmid) and up to more than 2000% (RIO) extra queries. [READ] — [Rodler & Schmid](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03083); [RIO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2465)
- MBD repairing a wrong model (non-LLM): de Kleer, Feldman, Matei (IJCAI 2019) "Correcting Design Errors in Components and Connections" treats an almost-correct design as the model and uses unmodified GDE with "fault modes" that are alternative gate types or alternative wirings; see Q4 for details. [READ] — [dekleer.org ijcai2019r](https://dekleer.org/Publications/ijcai2019r.pdf)

### Inferences
- Error rates in the ILS-CSL table (roughly 0-1 wrong of 10-21 constraints on well-known networks vs 6/15, 3/8, 7/24 on Mildew/Water/Barley, if the reading holds) suggest LLM structure is trustworthy roughly in proportion to how public the domain is. HVAC-specific connectivity (a particular building's VAV-to-zone mapping) is private information the LLM cannot know, so an LLM should supply plausible-alternative candidates (a prior over what could be true), not asserted edges.
- Safest use of an LLM-built or LLM-proposed structure with a classical diagnoser: treat it as a soft prior with an explicit "none of the above / unmodeled owner" hypothesis and a risk-adaptive selector (RIO-style), so that an LLM mistake costs extra tests instead of a wrong confident answer.
- Because no study quantifies structure-error-to-test-count propagation for LLM-built models, this is an open evaluation the user's benchmark (injecting LLM-generated wrong candidates or wrong priors and measuring tests-to-convergence) could contribute.

### Gaps
- No LLM-built fault tree / FMEA evaluated end to end through a probabilistic diagnoser found; the FTA-generation papers only report generation quality (or nothing quantitative).
- LLM "connectivity model" construction (component-connection graphs, P&ID/schematic parsing) not searched (search budget).
- The ILS-CSL T/F table reading is from PDF text extraction and could misalign columns.

## Q4. Diagnosis when the documented/system model may itself be wrong or stale (model repair, faulty models, structural faults), and any LLM-era treatment

### Takeaway
Classical MBD treats "model-artifact difference" as the primitive, so a wrong model is diagnosable within the same probabilistic machinery: connections/wires can be modeled as components with alternative-connection fault modes (de Kleer 2006, 2019), ranked by posterior and isolated with logarithmically many discriminating observations, and wrong priors are handled by risk-adaptive selection (RIO). I found no verified 2023-2026 LLM paper that diagnoses a stale or wrong system model; LLM-KG papers only note schema/corpus errors as limitations.

### Cited Findings
- GDE's own framing: diagnosis identifies "model-artifact differences"; with the model presumed right these are part faults, with the artifact presumed right they are "required changes in the model" (theory formation). [READ] — [GDE reprint](https://dekleer.org/Publications/gde-2006.pdf)
- de Kleer, "Modeling when connections are the problem" (QR-2006): most AI diagnosis models components but "not their interconnections", and when they do, model breaks not that "two connections may join (e.g., through fluid leakage or electrical short circuit)"; two historical blockers are an exponential number of failure possibilities and needing more precise models. Approach: model each circuit node with n+1 variables (one per connected component) so "the number of additional components grows linearly in the worst-case"; structural faults are treated "much like any other fault" inside GDE/Sherlock measurement selection; complexity is tamed by generating candidates best-first by posterior probability, not by fault count; "extremely few possible shorts can explain typical symptoms"; ISCAS-85 circuits up to 400 nodes / 500 components diagnosed in under 1 minute. Related work: Davis (1984) hypothesized bridge faults only "when all single faults were eliminated"; Preist & Welham (1990) insulator components grow quadratically; Boettcher et al. (1996) analog structural shorts via an extra algorithm invoked on multiple faults. [READ] — [de Kleer 2006](https://dekleer.org/Publications/digital.pdf)
- de Kleer, Feldman, Matei (IJCAI 2019) "Correcting Design Errors in Components and Connections": "GDE can treat errors in wiring as well as components": each input wire is modeled as another component whose fault mode says it is connected to some other output (e.g. WIRE(C(in1,X1)) AB -> in1(X1) = a or ci or out(A1)); redesign = a mode diagnosis "except that OBS is what the designer intended"; GDE generates novel inputs "which best differentiate among remaining misdesigns" and picks simplest (min-cardinality) first. Benchmarks (DXC/ISCAS): component-only single-fault mutants, e.g. 74283 (36 gates) 100 mutants; component+wiring single-fault mutants (Table 4): full adder 5 gates 67 mutants, avg 2.6 observations to correct; c17 110 mutants, 2.6; 74182 (19 gates) 1,444 mutants, 9.7; 74283 (36 gates) 3,284 mutants, 7.2; "The cost of isolating a redesign is always much higher with the presence of wiring errors"; observations needed are "roughly logarithmic in the size of the possibility space"; ambiguity groups arise (indistinguishable rewirings); simplest-first can return a functionally correct but unintended design; loops and dangling inputs are excluded as impossible. [READ] — [de Kleer et al. 2019](https://dekleer.org/Publications/ijcai2019r.pdf)
- Related MBD-for-design-debugging work cited there: Friedrich, Stumptner, Wotawa (AIJ 1999) "Model-based diagnosis of hardware designs"; Konighofer & Bloem 2011 (program repair with MBD); spectrum-based fault localization as a cruder model. [READ] — [de Kleer et al. 2019](https://dekleer.org/Publications/ijcai2019r.pdf)
- Uncertain-mechanism modeling inside a troubleshooting BN: persistent "mapping node" with states (ok, stuck, backwards) so that observations before and after an action stay consistent. [READ] — [Breese & Heckerman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3563)
- Prior/meta-information that may be wrong, handled adaptively (interactive KB debugging, where the artifact under diagnosis is itself a model): RIO shifts from entropy-like to split-in-half behavior when performance is poor; Rodler & Schmid show random or prior-independent selection is best under wrong priors. [READ] — [RIO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2465); [Rodler & Schmid](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03083)
- Struss & Dressler (1989) "physical negation" and the strong-fault vs weak-fault distinction (Reiter's theory = weak-fault; de Kleer & Williams extend to fault models), used to prevent impossible diagnoses: the search result shows the literature debate but I did not open the sources. [SNIP] — [Assumptions in MBD](https://ksi.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/KAW/KAW96/fensel/ambd.html)
- Non-AI analogue in power systems: state-estimation topology-error identification tests whether the assumed network model (breaker/switch status, line outages, bus splits) is wrong using normalized residuals and Lagrange multipliers of breaker constraints ("largest normalized residual test"), with two-stage schemes that first isolate suspect buses then model them in detail. [SNIP] — [Kekatos et al. NAPS 2012](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~kekatos/papers/NAPS2012.pdf); [OSTI: detection of topology errors by state estimation](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/6047522)
- LLM-era: in LLM-based diagnosis papers the stale-model issue appears only as a limitation (an outdated 1998 manual used as a case-study source in PHM 2025; KG schema/corpus noise in HybridRAG). No verified LLM paper detects or repairs a wrong documented system model with a probabilistic diagnoser. [READ] — [PHM 2025](https://papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/phmconf/article/download/4549/phmc_25_4549); [HybridRAG](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-02643-2)

### Inferences
- The user's problem is a special case of GDE's model-artifact-difference task with a very small, structured hypothesis space (owner substitution), i.e. de Kleer's wiring-error model ("input wired to some other output") is the closest formal precedent: alternative owner = alternative wiring mode; documentation = design intent. Verified precedent for the key design choices: (i) candidates ranked by posterior with plausible alternatives only (de Kleer's "extremely few shorts explain typical symptoms" supports restricting to physically plausible alternatives), (ii) probes chosen to best differentiate remaining misdesigns, (iii) ambiguity groups (indistinguishable alternatives) are real and require a stopping/reporting convention, (iv) wiring-error isolation costs materially more observations than component-only errors.
- A hidden-structure hypothesis matters: de Kleer notes an unmodeled bridge can masquerade as component intermittency. In HVAC an owner outside the plausible-alternative set (or a shared/multi-owner connection) would look like noisy evidence; an explicit "other/unmodeled" hypothesis (Sherlock-style unknown-mode idea, not verified here) and monitoring of model-evidence fit protect the exact posterior from being confidently wrong.
- Stale-model detection has a mature non-AI analogue (power-system topology error tests based on normalized residuals). It is a residual-based goodness-of-fit check on the whole model, complementary to posterior updating within the candidate set. Using such a fit check as a trigger for widening the candidate set is a transferable idea (inference, not tested).
- LLM's plausible role in model uncertainty (inference): generating the alternative-owner/alternative-wiring candidate set from documentation text and naming conventions; not deciding which is true.

### Gaps
- No LLM-era paper found on diagnosis under a wrong/stale system model; not searched exhaustively (search budget). Also not opened: Struss & Dressler 1989, Friedrich/Stumptner/Wotawa 1999, Preist & Welham 1990, Boettcher et al. 1996, Sherlock unknown-mode details.
- No source found for building-domain "as-built vs as-designed" verification in this pass (assumed covered by another researcher).
- Power-system topology-error results are snippet-level; I did not read the underlying methods.
