## Q1. How well do LLMs ask/probe, what are the frameworks (UoT, BED-LLM, ...), and what does each use as the information-gain estimator?

### Takeaway
Unaided LLMs ask low-information, repetitive questions and plateau early (AR-Bench, QuestBench, MediQ, GuessingGame); the reliable fix across 2024-2026 is to move the selection criterion out of the LLM into an explicit probabilistic model (EIG/MI over a hypothesis set), with the LLM only sampling hypotheses/candidate questions and (approximately) simulating outcomes. Explicit EIG beats "LLM picks the most informative question" by a wide margin (BED-LLM, BALAR ablations), but the gains are demonstrated on simulated 20-Questions/clinical-dialogue games, and information-gain estimators differ sharply in how much they trust the LLM (table below).

### Cited Findings

#### Information-gain estimator comparison (one line each; details in per-paper entries)
| Method (year) | Hypothesis set | How outcome likelihood is obtained | EIG / gain estimator | LLM calls per turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UoT (2024) | Given closed set, or LLM-initialised in "open set" | LLM "answer simulator" partitions the candidate set into yes/no subsets (deterministic, disjoint); uniform prior | Entropy-based reward, scaled by partition balance, propagated through a simulated tree | Many (tree of depth ~3; pruned variant exists) |
| Learning to Ask (2024) | Fixed 8-29 items | LLM annotates yes/no per item; deterministic | Entropy reduction of split (uniform prior); used only to build DPO preference pairs | 0 at inference (trained) |
| BED-LLM (2025/ICLR 2026) | LLM-sampled, then rejection-filtered against history; uniform weights | p_LLM(y given theta, x) from LLM logits | Nested-MC style "prior-likelihood" estimator: H[p_hat(y)] minus mean H[p(y given theta_n)] over ~15 sampled hypotheses | ~15 hypotheses + 15 candidate questions + likelihood evals |
| CA-BED (2026, workshop) | Discrete set | LLM writes numeric P(E given h) as text, smoothed toward uniform (eps=0.7) | Shannon-entropy reduction propagated through a depth-2 conversation tree (branching 3) | High; needs caching |
| BALAR (2026) | LLM-proposed factored latent dimensions, expandable | LLM ordinal label (likely/neutral/unlikely) per (question, user-type, dimension value) mapped to probabilities | Mutual information of each pair; greedy; posterior via Bayes | Front-loaded ("sleep-time") LLM calls + per-round soft-mapping |
| MoBayes (2026) | Closed disease list (KB) | Tabular P(feature given disease), empirical counts (DDxPlus) or LLM-elicited; naive-Bayes | Exact EIG over unasked features | Only parsing + question verbalisation |
| ASIG (2026, workshop) | LLM belief samples | LLM likelihoods | Rao-Blackwellised EIG used as GRPO reward (amortised into weights) | 1 (25-36x cheaper than BED-LLM) |
| MDA (2026) | LLM-proposed mechanistic models | Simulator likelihood (SMC / simulation-based inference) | Mutual information / posterior-predictive variance; CMA-ES for continuous designs | LLM only proposes models |

#### Uncertainty of Thoughts (UoT), NeurIPS 2024 (arXiv Feb 2024) — [FT]
- Mapping: hypothesis space = possibility set Omega (closed set given, or LLM-initialised "open set" then re-initialised from history); probe = yes/no question; evidence = answerer reply (open-ended replies allowed in MedDG); stopping = target identified or max turns (20 for 20Q and troubleshooting, 5 for medical); cost = tokens of tree simulation (pruned UoT and equal-token-cost comparison provided). [UoT paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03271)
- LLM role: (a) generates candidate questions, (b) simulates answer partitions of Omega into affirmative/negative subsets, (c) reward = entropy-based information gain with uniform prior, scaled by partition imbalance, propagated up the tree. [UoT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03271v3)
- Evaluation: 20 Questions (BIG-bench 29 items, Common 111, Things 1,854), medical diagnosis (DX: 104 dialogues/5 diseases; MedDG: 500 curated of 17K, 15 diseases), troubleshooting (FloDial: 894 dialogues, 153 faults); answerer simulated by GPT-4; models Llama-3-70B, Mistral-Large, Gemini-1.5-Pro, Claude-3-Opus, GPT-4. Headline: +38.1% average success rate over direct prompting. GPT-4 closed-set: 20Q-Common 71.2% vs 50.5% direct prompting; DX 97.0 vs 91.3; MedDG 88.0 vs 72.3; FloDial 67.3 vs 43.7 (mean successful conversation length 7.8 turns on FloDial). At equal token cost, adapted Tree-of-Thoughts still underperforms UoT; swapping UoT's uncertainty reward into ToT gives +5.9% on average. [UoT paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03271)
- Failure modes (authors): answer partitions may overlap; answers may not fully eliminate possibilities ("I don't have a fever" does not exclude COVID); benchmark simplified relative to real diagnosis/troubleshooting; uniform prior; the authors say a Bayesian update over probabilities would be the natural extension. [UoT paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03271)
- Third-party evidence that UoT does not travel: AR-Bench reports UoT "performs worse than the zero-shot baseline" [AR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08295); CA-BED shows UoT's hard binary partitions prematurely eliminate hypotheses [CA-BED](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01182); MoBayes' re-implementation scored UoT at 28% top-1 on DDxPlus (vs MoBayes 82%) [MoBayes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20022) (re-implementation by other authors; UoT was designed for disjoint closed-set partitions).

#### BED-LLM (Choudhury et al.), arXiv Aug 2025, ICLR 2026 — [FT]
- Mapping: hypothesis theta = target entity (20 Questions; space NOT enumerated, bounded only by what the LLM can generate) or a text user-profile (movie preferences); probe = yes/no (20Q) or multiple-choice (preferences) question; evidence = simulated answerer LLM; stopping = fixed budget (20 questions; 5 in preference task) plus explicit guess ("Is it X?") when the filtered hypothesis set collapses to one item; cost = LLM calls (target >=15 hypotheses via sample-then-filter, up to 3 cycles; 15 candidate questions). [BED-LLM PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21184)
- Estimator: "prior-likelihood pairing": joint p(theta)p_LLM(y given theta, x); EIG(x) approx = entropy of the hypothesis-averaged predictive p_hat(y) minus average entropy of p_LLM(y given theta_n) (uncertainty measured in outcome space, not hypothesis space); likelihoods from LLM logits. Beliefs: sample hypotheses from the LLM, LLM-check compatibility against every past Q/A pair, reject incompatible ones, assume uniform weights over survivors because p_LLM(theta) is heavily overconfident. [BED-LLM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21184)
- Evaluation: 20Q Animals/Celebrities/Things, 100 targets each, 6 LLMs (GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B, Mistral-Large, Qwen2.5-72B); 200 movie-preference profiles judged by LLM-as-judge. Headline: mean +37.4 percentage points over direct prompting; success more than doubles in over half of setups and never decreases. Selected Table 1 rows (Prompt-Only -> BED-LLM): GPT-4o Animals 45 -> 93, Celebrities 45 -> 86, Things 34 -> 64; Llama-3.1-8B 8 -> 63, 10 -> 58, 10 -> 26; Mistral-Large Celebrities 19 -> 91. Ablations (GPT-4o Animals/Celebrities/Things): "Implicit Maximization" (LLM picks the most informative of the same candidate questions) 70/50/42; Entropy-only 88/64/49; no filtering (raw in-context beliefs) 25/52/19; data-estimation pairing 70/55/26; CoT 62/63/49. Benefit persists under questioner-answerer model mismatch. EIG ranking of candidate questions already matches its converged order at 10 hypothesis samples (vs 85). [BED-LLM PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21184)
- Failure modes (text): raw p_LLM(theta) mode-collapses (200 samples concentrate on a few famous people); in-context updating regularly samples hypotheses incompatible with history (e.g., proposes female candidates after "male") and grows overconfident as history lengthens; data-estimation variant >10x slower; no stopping rule beyond budget; weak models still weak (Llama-3.1-8B Things 26%). [BED-LLM PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21184)

#### CA-BED (Arnould et al.), arXiv 31 May 2026, ICLR 2026 workshop paper — [FT]
- Mapping: discrete hypothesis set; probe = binary question; evidence = answerer reply; stopping = max posterior >= tau=0.8 or turn budget; cost = branching M_q=3, lookahead depth 2, aggressive caching. [CA-BED](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01182)
- LLM role: writes numeric P(E given h_i) as text (not logits), smoothed toward uniform with estimator confidence eps=0.7; questions chosen by EIG propagated through a question/evidence tree. Results (DeepSeek-V3.2, 3 seeds): Detective Cases (100 cases, 5 suspects) 46.0% vs direct prompting 27.6% vs UoT 29.0% at 7.9 turns; 20Q (111 entities) 82.9% vs 57.7% vs 60.4% at 12.8 turns; avg +21.8 points for +1.8 turns. [CA-BED](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01182)
- Key negative/interaction finding: smoothing plus an absolute stopping threshold interact. UoT with eps=0.7 and tau=1.0 never reaches its stopping criterion and exhausts the budget (10.0 and 19.8 turns); lowering tau to 0.8 lifts smoothed UoT on 20Q from 60.4% to 90.1% (matching/exceeding CA-BED's 82.9%), so much of CA-BED's 20Q edge over UoT is likelihood smoothing + a threshold aligned with the likelihood ceiling; on Detective Cases CA-BED still wins (46.0 vs 33.0). "Answer planning" (multiple-choice answers) helps 20Q (82.9 -> 89.2%, 12.8 -> 8.8 turns) but hurts Detective Cases (46.0 -> 38.0%) because the LLM cannot design mutually exclusive answer sets. [CA-BED](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01182)
- Limitations (authors): requires explicit discrete hypothesis set (dynamic candidate generation not evaluated); only two structured game benchmarks, LLM-simulated interlocutors; performance depends on LLM likelihood calibration; substantial latency/cost. [CA-BED](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01182)

#### BALAR (Bayesian Agentic Loop for Active Reasoning), arXiv May 2026 — [FT for mechanism/ablation; ABS for headline table]
- Mapping: hypotheses = factored latent "dimensions" proposed by LLM (e.g., vascular involvement, trigger pattern), each with LLM-elicited prior; probe = clarifying question; evidence = free-form reply soft-mapped by LLM to discrete choices; stopping = answer-probability convergence (max_a p(a) >= 1-alpha) or fraction of dimensions concentrated; cost = front-loaded parallel LLM calls to build likelihood tables (O(|Q|+|Q'|(p+1))|U| calls per expand round). [BALAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05386)
- LLM role: proposes dimensions/priors/questions and fills likelihood tables via a per-cell label in {likely, neutral, unlikely} mapped through a fixed function to probabilities; selection is MI maximisation; posterior by Bayes. [BALAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05386v1)
- Evaluation: AR-Bench-DC (100), AR-Bench-SP (100), iCraft-MD clinical (140); Qwen2.5 7B-32B, QwQ-32B, Llama-3.1-8B; Qwen2.5-32B user simulator; e.g., Qwen2.5-32B DC 46% vs few-shot 40%, SP 52% vs ToT 49%, iCraft-MD 73.6% vs MediQ-Expert 56.5% [ABS]. Ablation [FT] (iCraft-MD, Qwen2.5-32B): MI selection 67.9+/-3.9, random selection 52.1, LLM-prompted selection 61.4, no state expansion 58.6. [BALAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05386)
- Failure modes: independence across dimensions is a modelling choice; label-to-probability maps may be miscalibrated; helps most when the initial context is genuinely ambiguous (overhead unnecessary for well-specified prompts); Llama-3.1-8B falls behind ToT on SP (26.0 vs 31.0) [ABS]. [BALAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05386)

#### ASIG (Amortised Sequential Information Gathering), arXiv 3 Jul 2026, ICML 2026 workshop FoGen (non-archival) — [FT abstract + numbers]
- Mapping: 20 Questions (1,000 entities, 20 categories; eval Animals/Plants 100 each) plus MediQ out-of-distribution; probe = yes/no question; stopping = correct guess or T=20. LLM role = the policy itself, fine-tuned with multi-turn GRPO using EIG (Rao-Blackwellised, tractable because outcomes are binary) as reward. Result: more than doubles 7B success; >25x (25-36x) cheaper inference than BED-LLM but beats BED-LLM in only 2 of 4 settings; MediQ transfer is marginal (+0.9 pt accuracy, +1.7 pt voluntary accuracy at 7B; mixed at 14B). Requires a curriculum (train without EIG first). [ASIG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03426)

#### Learning to Ask Informative Questions (Mazzaccara et al.), arXiv Jun 2024, EMNLP Findings 2024 — [ABS]
- Mapping: fixed candidate set (8 items INLG/Things/Celebrities; 16-29 for larger sets); polar question; EIG = split entropy with uniform prior over candidates. LLM role: Llama-2-chat-7B as questioner/answerer/annotator; DPO on 55K pairs of EIG=1 (optimal) vs EIG<0.8 questions. Results: +12.2% success and -2.1 questions on INLG; +10% / -2.3 on Things; mean EIG 0.45 vs 0.34 baseline; limitations: closed candidate set, polar questions, one model, equal-probability assumption. Baseline open-source LLMs "often perform poorly" at generating high-EIG questions. [arXiv 2406.17453](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17453v2), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.291/)

#### AR-Bench "From Passive to Active Reasoning" (Zhou et al.), ICML 2025 — [FT]
- Mapping: three task families (detective cases DC, situation puzzles SP, guessing numbers GN); probe = free-form question (GN: oracle function); 25 interaction rounds; hidden hypothesis is implicit. LLM role: questioner under test; answerer LLM-simulated (DC/SP). [AR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08295)
- Findings: GPT-4o only 35% exact match on GN; progress is fast early (+7.7% process score rounds 5-10) then plateaus (+2.5% rounds 20-25); ToT with Llama-3.1-70B scores 52% on SP, below zero-shot, because verifiers are unreliable; SFT scores 0 on GN and DPO underperforms zero-shot on SP and GN; human instructions do not help; Proactive CoT marginal, UoT worse than zero-shot; models ask vague/repetitive questions and "converge to local minima" (e.g., repeating GN guesses with a few wrong digits). Dominant errors: timeline misinterpretation (DC), unsupported assumptions (SP), feedback misunderstanding (GN). Dataset sizes reported as 400/100, 400/100, 4940/100 train/test [ABS]. [AR-Bench PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08295), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08295)

#### GuessingGame (EMNLP 2025 main, arXiv Sep 2025) — [ABS]
- 858 games/858 objects; LLaMA-3.3-70B 39.4% success (23.3 queries), GPT-4o 64.1% (20.1), Gemini-2.0-Flash-Lite 74.1% (16.5), expert humans 96.3% (7.0). Two IG metrics: Bayesian belief update over semantic concepts (LLM relevance scores, KL) and ConceptNet candidate-set entropy. One SD more Bayesian IG cuts expected game length ~43%. Cheap prompting interventions: forbidding back-to-back same question types lifts LLaMA 39.4 -> 80.0%; forcing open-ended questions reaches 97.4% at 8.3 queries. Caveat: depends on interpreter-LLM calibration; everyday objects only. [GuessingGame PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19593), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.876.pdf)

#### QuestBench (Li et al., Google DeepMind), arXiv Mar 2025, NeurIPS 2025 — [ABS]
- Underspecified reasoning cast as a constraint-satisfaction problem with one missing variable; subsets Logic-Q, Planning-Q (PDDL), GSM-Q, GSME-Q. Models near ceiling on GSM-Q/GSME-Q but 40-50% on Logic-Q/Planning-Q; models fail to identify the right question even when they can solve the fully specified problem. Instance counts and model list not extracted. [QuestBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22674)

#### MediQ (Li et al.), arXiv Jun 2024 (venue not verified) — [ABS]
- Patient system + adaptive Expert system that abstains when unconfident and asks follow-ups; "directly prompting state-of-the-art LLMs to ask questions degrades performance"; their abstention strategies improve diagnostic accuracy by 22.3% but interactive performance still trails the full-information upper bound. [MediQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00922)

#### Active Task Disambiguation (Kobalczyk et al.), arXiv Feb 2025 — [ABS, abstract only]
- Frames clarifying-question selection as BED "explicit reasoning about the space of viable solutions" and reports it beats reasoning only in the space of questions; estimator details/numbers not extracted. [arXiv 2502.04485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04485)

#### MoBayes (Kesmen et al., EPFL), arXiv Apr 2026 (v3 24 May 2026) — [FT for tables/appendix]
- Mapping: closed disease universe (49 pathologies / 314 features in DDxPlus; 18 diseases in LLM-generated KBs); probe = feature question; evidence = LLM-parsed (feature, value, confidence) triples; stopping = warm-up T_min (12 in their runs), commit when max posterior >= tau, hard cap T_max; cost = tokens/case (MoBayes 16.4K on DDxPlus PatientSim; baseline figures in the same table are garbled in text extraction, so not quoted). LLM "never receives posterior values"; all posterior updates, naive-Bayes EIG and abstention are deterministic. [MoBayes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20022)
- Evaluation: simulated patients only (DDxPlus PatientSim and history-taking simulator, AgentClinic-MedQA), n=50-100 per cell; six frontier LLM baselines vs six cheap "sensor" LLMs with the engine. Table 1 (n=100, DDxPlus PatientSim): MoBayes+gpt-5.4-nano top-1 82, selective accuracy 85, DHS 90; UoT 28/28/44; every MoBayes variant beats every standalone doctor on DHS on at least 2 of 3 benchmarks. [MoBayes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20022)
- Failure/limits: closed-world (cannot diagnose outside the KB), cannot exploit volunteered evidence outside the schema, simulated patients only; LLM parsing errors dominate over KB errors [ABS]. [MoBayes HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20022v3)

### Inferences
- UoT: the closest published analogue to "technician troubleshooting" (FloDial) but its estimator (LLM-guessed disjoint partitions, uniform prior) is strictly weaker than the user's exact posterior + fitted class-conditional Gaussians; transfer = only the benchmark idea (FloDial-style fault troubleshooting) and the finding that reward shaped by uncertainty beats self-evaluation rewards.
- BED-LLM: transfers as a blueprint for how to keep the LLM out of the belief update (sample, filter for consistency with history, uniform-weight survivors) and as evidence that ~10 samples suffice to rank probes; it does NOT cover the user's Gaussian-likelihood case, and its stopping rule is a fixed budget.
- CA-BED: transfers the operational warning that a confidence threshold must be set relative to the likelihood model's effective ceiling (smoothing or overdispersed likelihoods can make a threshold unreachable); for the user, check that posterior confidence thresholds are calibrated against the Gaussian likelihood's actual attainable posterior mass.
- BALAR: the EXPAND trigger (entropy gap > lambda x max-remaining-information x remaining rounds) is effectively a feasibility/futility bound that, instead of abandoning, adds hypotheses; this maps directly onto the user's futility rule and gives it a constructive action (propose new candidate owners).
- ASIG: not useful for a Gaussian-likelihood engine; only supports the conclusion that amortised LLM policies transfer weakly out of domain (+0.9 pt on MediQ).
- Learning to Ask / GuessingGame: show LLM-authored questions sit far below the EIG optimum and that cheap prompt constraints (avoid repeating question types) recover a lot; if the LLM ever proposes probes for the user, screen them through the exact EIG instead of trusting the LLM's choice.
- AR-Bench / QuestBench / MediQ: negative controls that justify not delegating probe selection to a general LLM; note early-gain/late-plateau shape argues for an early-stopping rule.
- MoBayes: the architecture (LLM = parser + verbaliser, deterministic engine = posterior/EIG/stopping) is exactly the user's pattern; its main transferable lessons are (i) a minimum-evidence floor T_min guards against premature commitment when priors are wrong (it made prior choice irrelevant: empirical vs uniform priors gave identical DHS [FT]), (ii) closed-world remains the open weakness the user has flagged, (iii) LLM parsing (evidence extraction), not the KB, dominated errors in that system.

### Gaps
- No paper here evaluates on real physical-equipment troubleshooting with real (non-LLM) answerers; FloDial is a text flowchart dialogue set, not sensor data.
- UoT/BED-LLM/CA-BED estimators all assume categorical answers to questions; none handles continuous sensor-derived statistics with class-conditional Gaussians, so no direct head-to-head with the user's estimator exists.
- Exact instance counts/model lists for QuestBench and MediQ, and Kobalczyk et al.'s numbers, were not extracted.
- MediQ venue/year and Kobalczyk venue unverified (arXiv dates given).

## Q2. LLM as hypothesis/candidate generator feeding a separate posterior: how often does the true cause appear in the LLM's set, and how are missing-candidate ("open-world") errors handled?

### Takeaway
There is no clean "recall of the true cause in LLM top-k" number for equipment faults, but adjacent evidence says coverage is materially incomplete: expert-level differential-diagnosis LLMs include the true diagnosis in a top-10 list in roughly 59% of very hard NEJM cases (arXiv abstract) and frontier LLM root-cause agents recover the exact root-cause set in ~21% of microservice incidents. Working systems handle missing candidates with one of four mechanisms: an explicit "none of the above"/OPEN option (KnowNo), residual/predictive-check-triggered hypothesis expansion (MDA, BALAR), consistency-filtered resampling of LLM hypotheses (BED-LLM), or by declaring closed-world and accepting the limit (MoBayes, CA-BED).

### Cited Findings

#### Coverage / recall evidence
- AMIE differential diagnosis (Google, arXiv Dec 2023; Nature 2025): 302 NEJM case reports; LLM alone top-10 accuracy 59.1% vs 33.6% for unassisted clinicians (p=0.04); LLM-assisted clinicians 51.7% vs 36.1% unassisted vs 44.4% with search. [arXiv 2312.00164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00164) [ABS]. A later medRxiv orchestration system reports 79.1% top-10 (vs AMIE 68.9%) on the same 302 CPCs and "Capture@10" of 97.0% (any top-10 differential leads to the right test of record) — snippet-level; the AMIE 68.9% differs from the 59.1% above (different version/metric not reconciled). [medRxiv 2025.10.28.25338335](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.28.25338335v1.full) [SNIP]
- OpenRCA 2.0 (arXiv Jun 2026) [ABS]: 500 microservice incidents (TrainTicket 320, OTel Demo 38, Hotel Reservation 142; 27 fault types), 11 frontier LLM agents; exact root-cause set recovered in only 20.7% of cases; at least one correct service in 76.0%; a verifiable causal propagation path in only 61.5% ("ungrounded diagnosis" = right service, no verified path, ~14.5 points). Failure modes: premature commitment (stop after one plausible cause), presence bias (absence of activity read as normal), salience capture (blame the loudest signal). [OpenRCA 2.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27154)
- Reasoning failures in cloud RCA (arXiv Jan 2026) [ABS]: GAIA (152) and OpenRCA (148) samples, six open-source LLMs; top-1 hypothesis accuracy 0.01-0.07 on the harder set and 0.25-0.31 on the simpler; top-3 0.29-0.36 (simpler set); 16 failure types incl. "failure to update belief", fabricated evidence, evidential insufficiency; straight-shot prompting often beat complex agent workflows for smaller models. [RCA failures](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22208v1)
- HypoArena / Prospective Hypothesis Discovery (arXiv Jul 2026) [ABS]: 988 cases in six domains (incl. IT operations 146, safety investigation 103), 15 frontier LLMs; evaluates the breadth/discrimination of LLM-constructed hypothesis spaces with pairwise Bradley-Terry-Davidson ratings, and explicitly reports NO recall-of-reference metric; recurrent failures: coverage-breadth deficiency (only familiar directions), candidate compression (rich analysis collapses into a narrow final list), mechanism-granularity errors. [HypoArena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15766)
- "Judging Is Not Enumerating" (arXiv Aug 2026) [ABS]: on complete-truth tasks Qwen2.5 (3B-72B) judging F1 0.60-0.77 vs authoring (enumeration) F1 0.26-0.48; the dominant failure is silent omission, which leaves no visible evidence for reviewers; gate against known-correct probes cut false rejections from 58-92% to <=5%, but self-repair does not work. [Judging vs enumerating](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01000)
- MiRD (arXiv 25 May 2026) [FT abstract only]: splits miscoverage of LLM-sampled candidate sets into (i) sampling failure (finite sampling produces no admissible answer) and (ii) conditional selection failure (conformal filtering removes an admissible answer), giving a marginal upper bound on (i) and a calibrated threshold for (ii); evaluated on TriviaQA, CoQA, NQ with eight models. Numeric results not extracted. [MiRD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27091)
- Candidate-option robustness: alignment-tuned models "often default to selecting invalid options" when the correct option is removed from a multiple-choice list, whereas base models refuse better as they scale (arXiv 2024; abstract only). [Wait, that's not an option](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00113) [ABS]

#### Handling missing hypotheses
- KnowNo (CoRL 2023): LLM generates four semantically distinct options, an extra "an option not listed here" is appended, LLM next-token probabilities feed a conformal-prediction nonconformity score calibrated on N=400 examples; if the conformal set has more than one option the robot asks a human. Guarantee: task completion >= 1-epsilon with probability 1-delta, conditional on the environment being fully grounded in the LLM input and proposed actions being executable. [KnowNo](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.01928v2), [HTML/summary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928)
- BALAR: EXPAND adds a new latent dimension (LLM-proposed, conditioned on history), elicits a prior, recomputes likelihood tables, and adds targeted questions; triggered when the minimum number of rounds needed to close the entropy gap, ceil(gap / max-remaining-information), exceeds lambda x remaining rounds. Ablation: disabling EXPAND lowers accuracy 67.9 -> 58.6 on iCraft-MD (Qwen2.5-32B). The authors note the greedy-MI guarantee covers only the Ask sub-loop, not Expand. [BALAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05386)
- Model Discovery Agent (MDA, arXiv Aug 2026): M-open predictive check — if the best model's held-out interventional forecast error exceeds a threshold, the LLM is prompted (conditioned on previous hypotheses and their residuals) to propose new mechanisms ("local mutations"); all LLM components use Opus-4.7. Ablation: dropping M-open expansion cuts exact rate-law recovery on the hard ChemBench tier to 33% (vs ~42% with it); overall exact recovery ~53% vs 42% for a re-run of LLM-AutoSciLab. Documented "genuine M-open miss" on NeuronBench z-rebound: the LLM initially omitted the correct current and recovery depended on residual-triggered expansion. Authors: recovery "is bottlenecked by whether the LLM proposes a form that covers the truth"; random sampling cannot supply coverage either. [MDA PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09696) [FT]
- BED-LLM: no fixed hypothesis space; LLM proposes candidates, incompatible ones rejected against the full history; hypotheses assumed uniform. Open-space robustness comes from resampling each turn, not from any coverage guarantee. [BED-LLM PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21184)
- UoT open-set variant: prompts the questioner to initialise Omega and re-initialise it from history each turn; open-set UoT still beats direct prompting but is below closed-set UoT (Table 1). [UoT paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03271)
- Closed-world statements: MoBayes can only diagnose diseases in the KB [ABS] [MoBayes HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20022v3); CA-BED requires an explicit discrete hypothesis set and did not evaluate dynamic candidate generation [CA-BED](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01182).

### Inferences
- AMIE/OpenRCA numbers imply that an LLM-only candidate list should be assumed to omit the true answer in a sizable minority of hard cases (about 41% for AMIE top-10 on NEJM cases, about 24% for "no correct service named" in OpenRCA 2.0; far more if the exact multi-cause set is required), so the user's system should keep an explicit unmodelled-cause mass and treat LLM proposals as additions to, not replacements of, topology-derived candidates. (Extrapolation; no HVAC recall study exists.)
- MDA's predictive-check trigger is the most transferable open-world mechanism: for the user's model, "no candidate owner explains the observed consistency statistics" can be detected from low maximum class-conditional likelihood or poor posterior-predictive fit, and only then invoke the LLM to propose new owners (sub-metering paths, shared risers, mislabelled zones); this keeps the LLM off the critical path when the documented hypothesis set is adequate. (Design suggestion, not tested in the papers.)
- BALAR's expand rule and KnowNo's OPEN option are two complementary mechanisms: one is budget-driven (cannot close the entropy gap in the remaining tests), the other is calibration-driven (conformal set size); both are cheap to bolt onto an exact-posterior engine.
- "Judging is not enumerating" supports asking the LLM to verify/rank concrete candidates it is shown, rather than to enumerate all plausible causes; silent omissions are exactly the missing-candidate error the user is worried about.
- MiRD's decomposition suggests reporting two separate metrics for the LLM proposer: probability that the true owner is not in the proposed set (sampling failure) vs probability the posterior ranks it out of contention (selection failure), rather than a single accuracy number.

### Gaps
- No study measures LLM-proposed candidate recall for building-systems/HVAC/equipment faults; closest are medical differentials (NEJM) and microservice RCA.
- MiRD's actual sampling-failure rates, OpenRCA 2.0's per-model breakdowns and the RCA-failures paper's per-failure prevalences were not extracted.
- HypoArena deliberately reports no recall; so "coverage of the true cause" remains unquantified for its 988 cases.

## Q3. LLM as likelihood/outcome simulator: calibration, and comparison with learned likelihood models

### Takeaway
Where a labelled/empirical likelihood exists it is more informative than LLM-elicited likelihoods (MoBayes' empirical KB has higher KL-informativeness than GPT/Gemini KBs), and LLM-elicited priors/probabilities are generally overconfident (OpenEstimate, Xiong et al.). Systems that do use LLM likelihoods add safeguards: logit-based likelihoods with hypothesis filtering (BED-LLM), smoothing toward uniform (CA-BED), coarse ordinal labels (BALAR, MoBayes), and validation that EIG rankings are robust to likelihood mismatch (BED-LLM's questioner/answerer mismatch test).

### Cited Findings
- MoBayes empirical vs LLM-elicited KBs [FT]: LLM-elicited P(feature given disease) tables (GPT-5.4, Gemini 3.1) have lower KL-from-uniform (less informative) than the DDxPlus empirical KB; single-pass verbal-scale elicitation produced a mode-collapse artefact (likelihoods cluster on few discrete values) fixed by a continuous banded prompt; the authors call the gap "likely prompt-addressable" (a hypothesis, not tested). Across two LLM ecosystems, feature names overlap little (45 shared; Jaccard 0.22) yet likelihoods on shared features agree strongly (Pearson r=0.931, MAE=0.071; Gemini +0.055 systematic bias). Cross-KB transfer: MoBayes+GPT-nano top-1 falls from 70 (native) to 52 when patients come from the Gemini KB with only 43% feature coverage. KB "oracle" top-1 was 76 (GPT ecosystem) and 74 (Gemini); MoBayes with a cheap sensor reached 70 and 64; standalone frontier doctors 72 and 70; the paper reports standalone doctors drop sharply on rare diseases even for patients simulated from their own KB. All on LLM-generated synthetic patients, n=50. [MoBayes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20022)
- BED-LLM: likelihood p_LLM(y given theta, x) from logits "whenever possible"; robustness test where answerer and questioner are different LLMs (misspecified likelihood) — BED-LLM's gains persist. [BED-LLM PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21184)
- CA-BED: verbalised numeric likelihoods (text, not logits) smoothed toward uniform with eps=0.7; without matching the stopping threshold to that ceiling, runs exhaust budget. [CA-BED](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01182)
- BALAR: per-cell LLM labels {likely, neutral, unlikely} mapped to probabilities; limitations list miscalibrated likelihood tables and propose online calibration from observed answers as future work; MI selection 67.9% vs LLM-selected 61.4% vs random 52.1% (iCraft-MD, Qwen2.5-32B). [BALAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05386)
- OpenEstimate (arXiv Oct 2025) [ABS]: "LM-elicited priors are often inaccurate and overconfident" against real-world distributions across six frontier LMs; performance "remains largely unaffected by changes in sampling strategy, reasoning effort, or prompt design", improving modestly with how uncertainty is elicited. [OpenEstimate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15096)
- Confidence elicitation (Xiong et al., ICLR 2024) [ABS]: verbalised confidence is overconfident; human-inspired prompts, multi-response consistency and better aggregation help; no method consistently best and all struggle on professional-knowledge tasks; white-box vs black-box AUROC in the 0.522-0.605 range across five LLMs/five datasets. [Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063)
- LLM belief updating itself: "Bayesian Teaching" (Qiu et al., Nature Communications) shows off-the-shelf LLMs "fall far short" of Bayesian updating and that fine-tuning on an optimal Bayesian assistant's predictions (not on oracle answers) transfers to new domains [ABS]. [Bayesian Teaching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17523). BED-LLM independently finds in-context belief updates inconsistent and replaces them by filtering (see Q1).
- ACTMED / Zero-shot AFA / LLM-elicited BN priors were excluded by scope; not repeated.

### Inferences
- The user's fitted class-conditional Gaussians (regression on operating context) are the "empirical KB" side of MoBayes' comparison; the evidence says this is the better likelihood source, and the LLM's role should be limited to cases with no fitted model (new equipment types, physical tests with no data) where an ordinal, smoothed LLM likelihood with explicit "prior-strength" pseudo-counts is the least-bad option (MoBayes/BALAR pattern).
- MoBayes cross-KB result implies LLM-elicited likelihood tables are consistent in magnitude across models but not in feature schema, so any LLM-elicited test-outcome likelihood for physical tests should be elicited against a fixed, human-defined outcome schema rather than free-form.
- CA-BED's threshold-ceiling interaction is a concrete check for the user's stopping rule: verify that the threshold is reachable under the Bernoulli-test likelihoods (physical tests with imperfect sensitivity/specificity cap attainable confidence).

### Gaps
- No paper directly compares an LLM-simulated sensor/test outcome distribution against a learned regression-based likelihood on real equipment data; MoBayes' comparison is on synthetic patients generated from the same LLM (a "fair fight" by construction).
- Calibration of LLM likelihoods (ECE/Brier on held-out outcomes) is not reported by BED-LLM, CA-BED, or BALAR; only indirect ablations.
- Bayesian Teaching effect sizes and Kuhn/Farquhar semantic-entropy results not extracted (see Q6 for snippet-level entries).

## Q4. Sequential testing, calibrated stopping, deferral, and futility for LLM agent loops — measured savings

### Takeaway
Three families exist: (1) posterior-threshold stopping inside a Bayesian engine (MoBayes, CA-BED, BALAR; hyperparameters tuned, guarantees absent); (2) distribution-free calibrated stopping/deferral (KnowNo conformal, MiCP multi-turn conformal, CROS/LTT risk control, E-valuator anytime-valid sequential tests); (3) predictive early abort (probe cascades). Measured savings are real but modest and mostly in turns/tokens, and the newest clinical-stopping paper explicitly cautions its own results are exploratory.

### Cited Findings
- CROS "Safe to Stop?" (Wu and Rus, arXiv 9 Sep 2026) [FT abstract; task details 9 diagnoses / 12 action groups / Qwen2.5-7B LoRA backbone / 367-episode calibration split are from the HTML summary, ABS]: hypothesis space = 9 abdominal-pain diagnoses; probes = 12 action groups (vitals, labs, imaging, microbiology) proposed by the backbone; stopping layer decides stop/continue/defer, controlling selective diagnostic error <= alpha (25%) and autonomous coverage >= gamma (70%) via learn-then-test exact binomial tests; benchmark 1,834 MIMIC-IV-ED episodes (367 calibration, 367 evaluation). Results on the (previously viewed) evaluation split: 16.9% selective error at 78.8% coverage, cost 5.57 and 0.68 tests, vs 30.8% error at 100% coverage, cost 8.14 and 1.53 tests under native stopping; state-error AUROC 0.853 (trained ranker) vs 0.715 (max class probability) vs 0.552 (backbone's native stop score). Caveats reported by the authors: labels reused during development so results are exploratory; forced continuation is non-monotone (error 28.3% with history alone vs 34.3% after full workup); uniform-mixture ablation is cheaper; joint criterion met in only 6 of 20 development resplits. [CROS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09678)
- MiCP (arXiv Apr 2026) [ABS]: conformal multi-turn stopping; error budget alpha split across turns, turn-specific normalised-entropy thresholds, optional "Can't Answer"; datasets NQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA (300 examples each for optimisation/calibration/test), models Gemma-2-9B-IT, Qwen3.5-9B, GPT-4o-mini; M=15 answer samples per turn, max T=3 turns. At 95% coverage on Qwen3.5-9B: 1.78 vs 3.00 average turns on 2WikiMHQA (-41%), 2.56 (-15%) on HotpotQA, 2.71 (-10%) on MuSiQue. Limits: marginal (not conditional) coverage, exchangeability assumption, over-coverage, weaker gains on Gemma. [MiCP](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01413v2)
- E-valuator (arXiv Dec 2025) [ABS]: converts verifier scores to an anytime-valid sequential test with false-alarm-rate control via log-optimal density-ratio e-process; six datasets (GSM8k, MATH, AIME, HotpotQA, MedQA, MMLU-Pro), agents Aviary/Octotools/Claude Sonnet 4; detects failing trajectories earlier (MATH at alpha=0.3: 5.5 steps vs >=6.8 for baselines) and recovers "up to 90% of accuracy with 80% of tokens"; a few hundred calibration trajectories suffice; needs p0/p1 density estimates. [E-valuator](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03109v2)
- Doomed from the Start (arXiv Jul 2026) [ABS]: linear probes on residual-stream activations at each of the first 6 rounds, Clopper-Pearson thresholds for per-round recall budgets and a global recall target; TextCraft 100 tasks, WebShop 200, ALFWorld 120; small open models (Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen3-1.7B); at 90% recall up to 60.2% (TextCraft) and 54.9% (WebShop) token savings; degrades to a no-op when signal is weak; requires white-box hidden states and labelled failure data. [Doomed](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06503)
- KnowNo (CoRL 2023): help-request rate is controlled by a conformal set-size rule with a user-specified target completion rate; baselines: simple-set, ensemble set (needs 20x inference time and can assign zero probability to the correct option), prompt-set; reported 10-24% lower help rates at matched success [ABS phrasing, unverified]. [KnowNo](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.01928v2)
- ConSol (arXiv Mar 2025) [ABS]: SPRT on the two most frequent answers (Bernoulli) to stop self-consistency sampling early; fixed self-consistency uses 40-64 samples per task; claims comparable accuracy at substantially lower cost (savings percentages not extracted). [ConSol](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17587)
- MediQ: abstention (defer to asking rather than answering) improves accuracy by 22.3% in the paper's setting [ABS]. [MediQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00922)
- Posterior-threshold stopping in engines: MoBayes hierarchical rule (warm-up T_min, commit at max posterior >= tau, hard T_max; tau chosen by leave-one-out on 50 cases; single global tau because per-disease calibration has too few positives) [MoBayes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20022); CA-BED tau=0.8 [CA-BED](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01182); BALAR max_a p(a) >= 1-alpha or fraction of dimensions concentrated [BALAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05386); BED-LLM and UoT use fixed turn budgets only [BED-LLM PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21184), [UoT paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03271).
- Futility-style rule: BALAR's expand condition (minimum rounds to close entropy gap = gap / best-remaining-information exceeds a fraction of remaining budget) is the only explicit information-theoretic futility bound found; its action is hypothesis-space expansion rather than abandonment. [BALAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05386)
- Other candidates identified via search but not opened: Semantic Early-Stopping for iterative agent loops (2606.27009), Budgeted Act-or-Defer multi-agent deliberation with local reliability bounds (2606.29654), Statistical Early Stopping for Reasoning Models (OpenReview cmi4FBheRq). [SNIP] [Semantic early-stop](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27009v1), [Act-or-Defer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29654)

### Inferences
- CROS' finding that max-class-probability is a materially weaker error predictor (AUROC 0.715) than a trained risk ranker (0.853) is a warning for any confidence-threshold stop: verify that the user's posterior max-probability is calibrated on held-out cases (reliability curve, ECE) before trusting a fixed threshold; consider LTT/conformal calibration of the threshold to give a finite-sample bound on wrong-verdict rate at a stated coverage. (CROS's own guarantee requires frozen policies before calibration labels; its demonstration did not satisfy that.)
- MiCP/E-valuator show the shape of savings a calibrated rule can give (10-41% fewer turns, ~20% fewer tokens at ~90% of accuracy) — order-of-magnitude expectations for "fewer tests", not directly transferable numbers; the user's cost unit (physical tests) is different and no paper measures dollars/technician-minutes.
- KnowNo's set-size = 1 rule maps onto "verify documented owner vs defer to technician": act when the conformal set over owners has one element, otherwise escalate; this yields a defensible "when to ask a human" rule with a guarantee that the exact posterior alone lacks.
- The "min evidence floor + max budget + calibrated threshold" trio in MoBayes is a low-cost reference design; BALAR's feasibility bound is a principled form of the user's futility rule.

### Gaps
- No LLM-agent stopping paper reports savings in physical-test count or cost; all report turns, tokens, or clinical "test" counts on retrospective data (MIMIC), which have informative missingness (66.5% NO_RECORDED_RESULT at stage 1 per CROS).
- MiCP, E-valuator, Doomed, KnowNo numbers were extracted through summaries only and need a spot-check; ConSol savings percentages missing.
- No source found on SPRT/EIG-based futility for LLM agents specifically beyond BALAR.

## Q5. Automated scientific-discovery / experiment-design loops (LLM hypothesis proposal + BED-selected experiments) and whether LLM-proposed experiments approximate EIG-optimal ones

### Takeaway
The strongest 2026 result pairs LLM proposal of candidate models with SMC posterior and value-of-information design (MDA), showing large data-efficiency gains over LLM-only agents; head-to-head evidence that LLM-proposed experiments approximate EIG-optimal ones is negative or partial (LLM selection recovers roughly half to two-thirds of the gap between random and EIG in BALAR; BoxingGym and Bayesian-optimisation studies find LLM designers weak and insensitive to feedback). Active design also does not always beat random.

### Cited Findings
- Model Discovery Agent (arXiv Aug 2026) [FT]: hypothesis space = LLM-proposed mechanistic models (ODE/SDE structures) with SMC over structures+parameters and simulation-based inference for intractable likelihoods; probe = experiment design from a finite pool (ForceBench 13 designs, NeuronBench 36 designs, per summariser [ABS]) or continuous 7-D box (ChemBench, CMA-ES) [FT]; evidence = simulator output with Gaussian noise; stopping = fixed experiment budget B (no adaptive stop); cost = nested SMC scales O(B^2 N_m) and caps the experiment budget on the stochastic benchmark. VoI = mutual information; for deterministic dynamics with Gaussian noise it reduces to posterior-predictive variance. Results: ChemBench overall exact recovery ~53% vs 42% (re-run of LLM-AutoSciLab; published 35% on a different subset); in-context-learning forecasters "unable to predict reliably" even with the same data; NeuronBench: MDA with EIG (deterministic) or task-driven VoI (stochastic, slightly better) beats random design and ICL. Active design caveats: in ChemBench "random sampling also works well"; in a source-localisation domain "the EIG policy and random sampling perform equally well" (greedy EIG piled probes near a singularity, unstable). The paper does NOT compare LLM-proposed experiments against EIG. [MDA PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09696)
- LLMs for Bayesian Optimization in Scientific Domains (EMNLP 2025 Findings, arXiv Sep 2025) [ABS]: on genetic perturbation and molecular property tasks, "LLM-based agents show no sensitivity to experimental feedback: replacing true outcomes with randomly permuted labels has no impact on performance"; classical linear bandits/GP optimisation consistently beat LLM agents; hybrid LLM-guided nearest-neighbour sampling is competitive. [LLMs for BO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21403)
- BoxingGym (Gandhi et al., arXiv Jan 2025) [ABS]: 10 environments (psychology, ecology, etc.), experimental design scored by EIG; GPT-4o "struggles" at experimental design and model discovery; augmenting the agent with an explicit statistical model "does not reliably improve" results. Venue unverified. [BoxingGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01540)
- LLM-selected vs EIG-selected probes, direct comparisons: BED-LLM "Implicit Maximization" (same candidate questions, LLM judges which is most informative) reaches 70/50/42% for GPT-4o vs explicit EIG 93/86/64% and prompt-only 45/45/34% (Animals/Celebrities/Things) [BED-LLM PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21184) [FT]; BALAR: LLM-prompted selection 61.4% vs MI 67.9% vs random 52.1% [BALAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05386) [FT]; Learning to Ask: baseline Llama-2-7B questions average EIG 0.34 (optimum 1.0 for equal splits of 8 candidates) [arXiv 2406.17453](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17453v2) [ABS]; GuessingGame: higher Bayesian IG per question predicts shorter games (Spearman rho=-0.63) [GuessingGame](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19593) [ABS].
- Also identified, not opened: AutoDiscovery (open-ended discovery via Bayesian surprise, arXiv Jul 2025). [SNIP] [AutoDiscovery](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00310)

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern (LLM proposes, probabilistic machinery scores/selects) validates the user's split of labour; the evidence suggests LLM-proposed probes, if ever added as candidates, should be re-scored by the exact EIG (and EIG-per-cost) rather than accepted.
- MDA's "greedy EIG no better than random in near-singular domains" cautions that the user's one-step EIG can be myopic when evidence is highly non-linear; check that EIG gains in the real data exceed a random-probe baseline (cheap ablation).
- Sequential experiments in these papers use fixed budgets; the user's confidence/futility stopping is ahead of the literature here.

### Gaps
- No direct experiment of LLM-proposed physical tests vs EIG-optimal ones with real outcomes; evidence is indirect (simulated question games).
- BoxingGym's numbers/venue and AutoDiscovery's mechanism not extracted.

## Q6. Test-time compute / multi-sample self-consistency as a cheap uncertainty estimate feeding a formal loop

### Takeaway
Sampling-based consistency is the standard black-box uncertainty signal (semantic entropy, self-consistency frequencies, MiCP's answer-frequency nonconformity, BED-LLM's hypothesis samples) and formal loops use it in three ways: as the hypothesis/outcome sampler (BED-LLM), as a nonconformity score (MiCP, KnowNo's ensemble baseline), or as a sequentially-stopped sample count (ConSol). It is not a free lunch: verbalised confidence is overconfident, sampling changes barely improve elicited priors, ensembles are expensive, and structured extra reasoning does not close the gap to explicit EIG.

### Cited Findings
- BED-LLM uses samples as the belief: ~15 hypotheses per turn suffice, EIG ranking of five candidate questions is already correct at 10 samples (vs 85), and adding chain-of-thought alone (CoT 62/63/49% GPT-4o) does not approach explicit EIG (93/86/64%). [BED-LLM PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21184) [FT]
- MiCP uses M=15 sampled answers per turn to build frequency-based nonconformity scores with normalised-entropy tie-breaks. [MiCP](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01413v2) [ABS]
- ConSol stops self-consistency sampling via SPRT (fixed self-consistency: 40-64 samples). [ConSol](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17587) [ABS]
- KnowNo's ensemble-set baseline needs 20x inference time and can give the correct option zero probability. [KnowNo](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.01928v2) [ABS]
- Semantic entropy clusters sampled outputs by bidirectional entailment and computes entropy over meanings; reported to beat token-level predictive entropy for hallucination detection (Kuhn et al., 2023, ICLR) — snippet-level. [Semantic Uncertainty](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.09664) [SNIP]
- Confidence elicitation: verbalised confidence is overconfident; consistency across multiple responses helps; no strategy consistently dominant (Xiong et al., ICLR 2024). [Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063) [ABS]
- LM-elicited priors remain inaccurate and overconfident regardless of sampling strategy/reasoning effort (OpenEstimate, 2025). [OpenEstimate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15096) [ABS]
- AR-Bench: scaling interaction rounds gives diminishing returns (+7.7% then +2.5% process score per five rounds) and larger models still fail; tree-search extra compute fails when verifiers are unreliable. [AR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08295) [FT]
- Other snippet-level items: Complementing self-consistency with cross-model disagreement (2604.17112), TECP token-entropy conformal prediction (2509.00461), SENECA small-sample entropy estimation (2605.00668). [Cross-model disagreement](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17112), [TECP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.00461) [SNIP]

### Inferences
- For the user's loop the cheap uncertainty signals worth using are: (a) ~10-15 LLM samples to seed/extend the candidate-owner set (BED-LLM pattern), (b) answer-frequency (MiCP-style) only for LLM-side tasks such as evidence extraction from documentation text, (c) never as the posterior itself; the exact posterior already supersedes these for sensor evidence.
- Because verbalised LLM probabilities are overconfident, any LLM-derived prior over owners (e.g., from documentation text) should be tempered (pseudo-count / smoothing as in CA-BED) and its weight tuned on a held-out set of known-correct/known-wrong documentation cases.

### Gaps
- No paper quantifies test-time-compute scaling of LLM hypothesis coverage (does more sampling raise recall of the true cause?) — MiRD frames it theoretically, but empirical curves were not extracted.
- Semantic-entropy Nature 2024 (Farquhar et al.) and other primary calibration results were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).

## Cross-cutting notes for the report writer
- Architectures that keep the LLM out of the belief update (MoBayes, BED-LLM's filtering, MDA's SMC) outperform LLM-in-the-loop update variants in every ablation found (BED-LLM ICL-beliefs 25% vs 93%; MoBayes vs standalone doctors; MDA vs in-context forecaster). This supports the user's design.
- The three open questions map to the literature as: hypothesis-space coverage = MDA M-open check / BALAR EXPAND / KnowNo OPEN / MiRD decomposition; evidence sources = MoBayes (LLM only parses text into structured evidence triples; parsing errors dominate); technician-facing explanation = MoBayes' auditable evidence chain, and OpenRCA 2.0's "ungrounded diagnosis" (76.0% correct service vs 61.5% with verifiable causal path) as a cautionary metric for LLM-written explanations. No paper evaluates explanation quality with real technicians.
- Preprint/venue status matters: CA-BED (ICLR 2026 workshop), ASIG (ICML 2026 workshop, non-archival), CROS, MDA, MoBayes, BALAR, MiCP, OpenRCA 2.0, HypoArena, MiRD, Doomed are 2026 arXiv preprints; UoT (NeurIPS 2024), AR-Bench (ICML 2025), BED-LLM (ICLR 2026), Learning to Ask (EMNLP Findings 2024), GuessingGame (EMNLP 2025), KnowNo (CoRL 2023), LLMs for BO (EMNLP 2025 Findings) are peer-reviewed venues per the sources.
