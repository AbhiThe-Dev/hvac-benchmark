## 1. Knowledge-graph validation with LLMs (erroneous triples, link plausibility, KG-vs-source reconciliation, SHACL, entity alignment) and how verifier errors are controlled

### Takeaway
LLM triple validators are used either as retrieval-grounded checkers against external text (high precision, low recall, human review still required) or as constraint/semantic-consistency checkers; error control is mostly "human-in-the-loop for uncertain items" plus statistical sampling for audit, not calibrated abstention. The most principled cost-aware KG audit machinery (sequential sampling with Bayesian credible intervals and informative priors) is non-LLM and is the closest formal analogue to a documented-claim-with-uncertain-prior setup.

### Cited Findings
- Traceable LLM-based validation of RDF statements (Wikidata-style triples): RAG against chunks retrieved from web search/Wikipedia instead of the LLM's internal knowledge. Eval: BioRED, 1,719 positive statements + 1,719 generated negatives (SNLI used for comparison). Result: precision 88%, recall 44%; authors conclude human oversight is required. LLM role: judge of statement vs retrieved evidence chunk. Not shown: high recall; calibrated confidence. abs-level — [arXiv 2409.07507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07507)
- "Can LLMs be Knowledge Graph Curators for Validating Triple Insertions?" (GenAIK @ ACL 2025): prompts LLMs through four validation sub-tasks (class/property alignment, URI standardization, semantic consistency, syntactic correctness); Llama-3-70B-Instruct reported more accurate/consistent than smaller models; authors list domain generalization, semantic drift and need for human-in-the-loop as open challenges. Datasets/numbers not stated on the abstract page (abs-level) — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.genaik-1.10/)
- KGValidator (ESWC 2024 Text2KG workshop): agent framework that validates KG-completion outputs using three evidence sources: model-intrinsic knowledge, user-supplied context, and agents with external retrieval; motivated as replacing costly human annotation. Numeric agreement with human labels not visible on the abstract page (abs-level) — [arXiv 2404.15923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15923)
- "Knowledge graph validation by integrating LLMs and human-in-the-loop" (Tsaneva, Dessì, Osborne, Sabou; Information Processing & Management, Sept 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104145): existence and metadata confirmed via Crossref; abstract/results NOT retrieved (publisher returned 403) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S030645732500086X); [Crossref lookup](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.title=Knowledge+graph+validation+by+integrating+LLMs+and+human-in-the-loop&rows=1&select=title,abstract,author,DOI,issued)
- Multi-view validation of LLM-generated CKD knowledge graphs: scores triples on semantic plausibility, ontology-grounded type compatibility and structural importance; a "two-LLM" scheme has each triple judged by two open-weight LLM families (snippet-level description) — [PMC12689688](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12689688/)
- Active human verification of uncertain KG edges, pre-LLM lineage: "Active Ensemble Learning for KG Error Detection" combines machine models with humans to validate efficiently (snippet only; venue/numbers UNVERIFIED) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/368839233_Active_Ensemble_Learning_for_Knowledge_Graph_Error_Detection)
- Kontrast (2026): LLM + Text-to-SPARQL compares table/text answers against Wikidata evidence and classifies four inconsistency types (granularity, direct conflict, temporal change, KG incompleteness). Reports cross-modal inconsistencies are common; performance limited by Text-to-SPARQL errors; no precision/recall on abstract (abs-level) — [arXiv 2607.25959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25959)
- SHACL + LLM: the KGValidator-related search surfaced a SHACL-constraint validation agent repo (TheWorldAvatar/kgvalidator) and PolicyKG (LLM pipeline that generates SHACL from policies). I did not read these; no evaluation numbers gathered. Pattern is: SHACL/OWL does deterministic structural checks, LLM does semantic or repair/explanation work (snippet-level) — [GitHub kgvalidator](https://github.com/TheWorldAvatar/kgvalidator); [arXiv 2608.09028](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09028)
- Entity alignment under a query budget (LLM4EA, NeurIPS 2024): LLM used as a noisy annotator of equivalent-entity pairs; an active-learning policy selects which source entities to send to the LLM (prioritizing those whose labels most reduce uncertainty given inter-/intra-KG structure) and a probabilistic-reasoning label refiner filters noisy LLM labels; feedback loop with a base EA model; four benchmarks; claims gains in effectiveness/robustness/efficiency (no numbers on abstract page) — [arXiv 2405.16806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16806)
- Entity matching with LLMs (Peeters & Bizer, EDBT 2025): best LLMs with zero/few examples are comparable to PLMs fine-tuned on thousands of examples, more robust to unseen entities, GPT-4 can give structured explanations and diagnose error causes; prompts must be tuned per model/dataset (abs-level) — [arXiv 2310.11244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11244). GPT-4 zero-shot F1 ranged 69.57-97.67 across datasets (secondary snippet) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11244). A small model (AnyMatch) reaches competitive zero-shot quality at >3 orders of magnitude lower cost than GPT-4 (snippet) — [arXiv 2409.04073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04073)
- Calibration in entity matching: the 2025 UncertaiNLP paper titled "Confidence Calibration in LLM-Based Entity Matching" actually reports on a RoBERTa matcher (ECE 0.0043-0.0552; temperature scaling cut ECE by up to 23.83%); it does not measure LLM calibration on the abstract page (abs-level) — [arXiv 2509.19557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19557)
- Non-LLM, human-budget audit of KG accuracy (Gao et al., VLDB 2019): two-stage weighted cluster sampling with stratification gives unbiased accuracy estimates with user-specified CI; up to 80% annotation-cost reduction and up to 10,000x machine-time speedup vs baselines (snippet) — [arXiv 1907.09657](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.09657)
- Bayesian sequential KG audit (Marchesin & Silvello, SIGMOD 2025): aHPD is a sequential procedure: sample batch of facts, get human labels, update Beta posterior, stop when credible-interval margin of error <= epsilon (typically 0.05). Runs Kerman Beta(1/3,1/3), Jeffreys, Uniform priors concurrently and takes the narrowest interval. Real KGs: vs Wilson CI, annotation time YAGO (accuracy 0.99) 0.60 vs 0.76 h (21% less), NELL (0.91) 1.76 vs 2.08 h (15%), DBpedia (0.85) 3.45 vs 3.60 h (4%); "up to 47%" with TWCS in high-precision regimes. Informative priors from similar KGs (accuracy 0.80 and 0.90) cut DBpedia estimation from 222 +/- 83 to 63 +/- 36 triples (2.55 to 0.72 h). Prior choice matters: annotation costs differed significantly across priors in 8 of 12 cases — [arXiv 2502.18961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18961) (algorithm details from the [HTML version](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18961), fetched summary)
- Interactive ontology debugging (pre-LLM): entropy/split-based query strategies for choosing which axiom-question to ask an oracle to localize faulty axioms (snippet only) — [arXiv 1107.4303](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.4303)

### Inferences
Per-system mapping / non-LLM state of the art / transfer (my analysis, not sourced claims):
- Traceable RDF validation: hypothesis = statement true/false; probe = retrieval of an external chunk; evidence = LLM entailment judgment; stop = single-shot; cost = retrieval. Non-LLM SOTA: NLI/embedding classifiers remain comparable (not tested in the paper). Transfer: an LLM-as-entailment channel is high-precision/low-recall, i.e. it should be modeled as an asymmetric noisy channel (rarely false-positive, often "no evidence"), not a symmetric one.
- KGValidator / GenAIK curators: mapping is validation of an inserted triple against ontology + text; stop = none (one shot). Non-LLM SOTA: SHACL/OWL for structure, KG-embedding classifiers for plausibility. Transfer: split checks into deterministic constraint checks (e.g., HVAC topology/type-compatibility rules) and LLM semantic checks; do not ask the LLM to do what a rule can do.
- LLM4EA: hypothesis = candidate equivalent entity; probe = an LLM query on a chosen entity; evidence = noisy label; cost = query budget; active selection by uncertainty reduction over graph neighbours; label refinement = probabilistic reasoning over noisy LLM answers. This is the closest published "LLM as costed noisy oracle + active selection + noise model" pattern and is structurally the same as using an LLM as one noisy evidence channel in an EIG loop. Non-LLM baselines are the EA models it wraps. Transfer: learn/estimate the LLM channel's confusion matrix and let the posterior, not the LLM, aggregate.
- aHPD / TWCS audits: hypothesis = accuracy of documented facts (a per-claim Bernoulli prior); probe = human annotation (cost measured in hours); stop = credible-interval width; priors are explicit and informative priors from "similar KGs" cut cost about 3x. Non-LLM and still the state of the art for cost-aware KG accuracy estimation. Transfer: (a) documented-claim reliability can be estimated per source/system type and used as the prior; (b) run multiple priors or check prior sensitivity because prior choice changed cost significantly; (c) stopping on credible-interval width is an accepted, published rule.
- Human-review budgets in LLM+KG papers are mostly qualitative ("needs human oversight") — I found no LLM KG-validation paper with calibrated abstention and a quantified review-budget curve (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Tsaneva et al. (IPM 2025) results/abstract not retrieved (403). This is the most on-point "LLM + human-in-the-loop routing of uncertain triples" paper and needs a manual read.
- No LLM KG-validation paper found that reports calibrated abstention or precision-at-review-budget curves; numbers found (88% P / 44% R) come from a single benchmark with synthetic negatives.
- SHACL+LLM, KGValidator and CKD multi-view papers: numeric results not gathered.
- LLM4EA numbers not read (PDF unreadable); "Active Ensemble Learning" details unverified.
- No KG paper found that treats the existing KG edge as an explicit prior for an LLM judge and measures anchoring (see question 6).

## 2. Fact-checking / claim-verification agents with adaptive evidence retrieval, budgets, stopping (and LLM-chosen vs information-gain criteria)

### Takeaway
Deployed LLM fact-checkers mostly stop via the LLM's own self-reported confidence (FIRE) or a heuristic/deterministic controller; principled EIG/VOI/entropy stopping exists in a small 2026 wave of papers (BEACON, ECR, VOI controller, Bayesian stopping) but I found no clean head-to-head of "LLM-chosen retrieval sequence vs EIG" on claim verification. One agentic-cost paper reports agent controllers' effectiveness "varies significantly by model and task" and that larger budgets do not reliably improve answers.

### Cited Findings
- FIRE (Xie et al., Findings of NAACL 2025): single-prompt agent decides at each step whether to emit a final True/False label or issue another search query "based on its confidence in the current judgment"; uses LLM internal knowledge first and retrieves only when uncertain. Benchmarks per README: FacTool-QA, FELM-WK, BingCheck. Result: slightly better/comparable accuracy with 7.6x lower LLM cost and 16.5x lower search cost on average vs fixed-retrieval frameworks. Role of LLM: verifier, query generator, and stopping decider all in one. Not shown: whether the self-confidence is calibrated; no info-gain comparison — [arXiv 2411.00784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00784); [GitHub README](https://github.com/mbzuai-nlp/fire)
- Inference-Time Budget Control for LLM Search Agents (2026): controller allocates dual budgets (tool calls, output tokens) across retrieval/decomposition/answer commitment using a deterministic, training-free task-level VOI score r_t(k) = [u_t(k)]+ / (d_t(k;b_t)+eps) built from critic-derived progress plus structural signals; evaluated on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue, Bamboogle with Qwen3-32B, Qwen3.5-122B, GPT-5.4-Mini. Qwen3-32B relative F1 gains over BAVT +5.7% to +18.4%; 27.2% lower inference time than BAVT. Limits: gains shrink at high budgets and with stronger backbones; cannot recover from incorrect retrieval paths; mid budgets sometimes beat high budgets. (HTML full text read. Note: an earlier PDF-derived summary claimed VOI was "LLM-estimated"; the HTML text says deterministic and I use that.) — [arXiv HTML 2605.05701](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05701)
- BEACON (2026, computational pathology, not fact-checking but the closest architecture to the user's system): keeps a categorical belief over competing diagnostic hypotheses, scores candidate patches (probes) by expected information gain, acquires top-B, and uses an off-the-shelf LLM "evidence controller" to decide answer / continue / zoom. Five WSI-VQA benchmarks; "strongest overall among training-free agentic frameworks" with substantially better evidence-acquisition efficiency; states semantic relevance != diagnostic informativeness. EIG-vs-LLM-controller ablation is mentioned but numbers not seen (abs-level) — [arXiv 2608.05757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05757)
- Entropic Claim Resolution (2026): reframes RAG as entropy minimization over competing semantic answer hypotheses; selects evidence by expected entropy reduction ("most discriminative, not most relevant"); terminates at epistemic sufficiency H <= epsilon subject to coherence. No benchmark numbers on the abstract page; LLM role unspecified (abs-level) — [arXiv 2603.28444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28444)
- Optimal Bayesian stopping for consistent LLM answers (ICML 2026): treats repeated LLM samples as evidence, uses prior information, stops when consistency is sufficient; simplified "L=3 aggregated" posterior is asymptotically optimal; cuts LLM calls by up to 50% at comparable accuracy (abs-level) — [arXiv 2602.05395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05395)
- Bayesian Linguistic Forecaster (K. Murphy, ICML AI Forecasting Workshop 2026): LLM agent maintains a "linguistic belief state" (probability + natural-language evidence summary) updated iteratively, multi-trial logit-space aggregation and Platt-scaling calibration; top public result on 400 ForecastBench questions; v4 adds an appendix sketching value-of-information computation for search termination (abs-level) — [arXiv 2604.18576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18576)
- When Knowledge Is Not Free (2026): cost-tiered evidence access on MS MARCO v2.1; no static selection strategy wins uniformly; more budget does not consistently improve answer quality; LLM agents that choose when/what tier/when to stop show adaptive potential but effectiveness varies significantly by model and task (abs-level) — [arXiv 2606.02245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02245)
- Calibrated selective fact-checking, Evidence Chain Evaluation (2026): tool-using verification agent (web, scholarly search, executable checks) allowed to abstain; on ECE-Bench (95 claims): accuracy 91.6%, coverage 93.7%, selective accuracy 97.8%, 6 abstentions (5 of 6 at the lowest source-reliability tier); it does NOT beat the strongest retrieval baseline on ECE/Brier/AURC. Tiny N (abs-level) — [arXiv 2607.18240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18240)
- Uncertainty of Thoughts (NeurIPS 2024): LLM simulates possible answers and their probabilities and selects the question maximizing expected information-gain-style reward via reward propagation; on 20 Questions, simplified medical diagnosis and troubleshooting, +38.1% average task-success rate vs direct prompting with fewer questions (snippet-level description) — [arXiv 2402.03271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03271)
- Sequential Diagnosis with LLMs / MAI-DxO (Nori et al., Microsoft, 2025; medical, but the only large cost-aware sequential probe study with an LLM orchestrator): SDBench = 304 NEJM cases turned into interactive encounters where each question/test has a cost. MAI-DxO with o3: 80% accuracy (max config 85.5%) vs 20% for generalist physicians, 20% cheaper than physicians and 70% cheaper than off-the-shelf o3. Roles (from a secondary summary, matches my memory): Dr. Hypothesis (probability-ranked differential), Dr. Test-Chooser, Dr. Challenger (recognizes cognitive biases, tests assumptions), Dr. Stewardship (blocks low-value costly tests), Dr. Checklist. Caveats stated: NEJM cases are rare/hard, US-centric cost data, physicians denied colleagues/books. Whether MAI-DxO uses formal EIG is NOT verified (one PDF-derived summary claimed so and also listed wrong personas, so I discarded it) — [arXiv abs 2506.22405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22405); [Emergent Mind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2506.22405); [Microsoft AI blog](https://microsoft.ai/news/the-path-to-medical-superintelligence/)
- TASR (training-free adaptive stopping for iterative retrieval, 2026), Jev-Mem, Stateful Evidence-Driven RAG: appear in search results as further adaptive-stopping work; I did not obtain reliable details — [TASR arXiv 2606.13814](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13814) (UNVERIFIED content)

### Inferences
- FIRE: hypothesis space = {true,false} per atomic claim; probe = web search; evidence = snippets; stop = LLM's verbalized confidence; cost = LLM+search calls. Non-LLM SOTA: not really applicable; the gain comes from stopping early on claims the LLM already "knows". Transfer: shows most of the achievable cost saving in fact-checking comes from not probing when the prior is already decisive, which mirrors documented-claim-with-high-prior. But the stop rule relies on unvalidated LLM confidence, so the user's posterior-threshold + futility rule is strictly more principled.
- VOI controller (2605.05701): deterministic VOI per unit budget beats LLM-driven budget baselines on multi-hop QA at low budgets; the finding that gains vanish at high budgets fits "adaptive stopping matters when probes are scarce", which is the HVAC setting.
- BEACON: mapping is almost identical to the user's system (categorical posterior -> EIG probe -> LLM only as controller). It is the best published evidence that "formal EIG for selection, LLM for control/interpretation" is a working recipe, but it is in pathology VQA, and its EIG-vs-LLM ablation numbers were not read.
- Bayesian stopping over LLM samples (2602.05395): gives a formal recipe for treating N repeated LLM verdicts as evidence with a prior, and stopping. Caveat (inference): it presumes exchangeable samples; repeated draws of the same prompt on the same evidence are correlated and will over-count evidence in a Bayesian update.
- Agentic cost paper (2606.02245): supports the position that LLM-chosen retrieval/stopping is model- and task-dependent rather than reliably near-optimal.
- No direct head-to-head found of LLM-chosen probe sequence vs EIG in claim verification; the general LLM-vs-EIG question was already covered by prior research and is not repeated here.

### Gaps
- No claim-verification paper found that compares an LLM-chosen retrieval sequence to an information-gain criterion on identical tasks with cost curves; BEACON ablation numbers unread.
- FIRE full text unreadable (PDF); its confidence mechanism (verbalized vs token-probability), max-step setting and failure analysis not verified; only abstract and README read.
- ECR benchmark numbers, BLF VOI appendix details, TASR details not obtained.
- MAI-DxO stopping/ablation details not verified (full paper PDF unreadable through the fetch tool).

## 3. Software: documentation-vs-code inconsistency, architecture drift, IaC/config drift (declared vs actual state)

### Takeaway
LLMs are good at reading natural-language documentation and turning it into checkable artifacts (tests, oracles, repair text) but naive "is this doc consistent with this code" prompting produces huge false-positive rates; the working systems add deterministic filtering, executable probes (generated tests) or history. For declared-vs-actual drift, the strongest results treat the deterministic tool (Terraform diff, live telemetry) as the authority and use the LLM only for interpretation, or add cross-validation because tool outputs themselves can be wrong.

### Cited Findings
- DocPrism (ISSTA 2026): LLM finds code-doc inconsistencies with "Local Categorization, External Filtering" (LCEF). Baseline flag rate 98% dropped to 14% with LCEF; F1 0.22 -> 0.77; precision about 0.62-0.63 on real code across Python/TypeScript/C++/Java (Java precision 0.47-0.67); conservative lower bound: inconsistencies in 11% of code-doc pairs. Reason for false positives: LLM treats natural gaps between high-level doc and detailed code as inconsistencies (abs-level + search snippet) — [arXiv 2511.00215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00215)
- CASCADE (FSE 2026): LLM generates unit tests from documentation; a failure signals mismatch; cross-check by having the LLM also generate reference code from the doc and reporting only when existing code fails tests that generated code passes (precision-first design). Benchmark 71 inconsistent + 814 consistent Java pairs plus Java/C#/Rust repos; found 13 previously unknown inconsistencies, 10 since fixed. No precision/recall on abstract page (abs-level) — [arXiv 2604.19400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19400)
- METAMON: search-based test generation captures actual behavior as regression oracles; LLM code reasoning flags oracles inconsistent with the documented spec; metamorphic queries + self-consistency filter. 9,482 code-doc pairs from five Defects4J v2.0.1 projects: precision 0.72, recall 0.48 (abs-level) — [arXiv 2502.02794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02794)
- ReCite (ASE 2026): finds unresolved function names in Linux kernel comments, traces them through git history, LLM writes repair suggestions grounded in that history. 869 stale references on v6.18-rc1; of 200 sampled repairs, 89.0% useful guidance and 42.5% directly applicable; 75 patches submitted, 50 accepted (66.7%). Scope limited to externally induced staleness of function references (abs-level) — [arXiv 2608.03734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03734)
- Earlier code-comment-inconsistency work (LLM + program analysis, FSE'24 companion; CCIBench/CCISolver 2025): exist and report gains over fine-tuned code LLMs; numbers not read (snippet) — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3663529.3664458); [CCISolver arXiv 2506.20558](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20558)
- Architecture drift/conformance: Thoughtworks Radar describes hybrid practice (deterministic tools such as ArchUnit/Spectral plus LLM evaluation for semantic violations); a 2026 safety-critical firmware drift paper restricts the LLM to writing human-readable reports about computed differences from runtime traces (snippet-level). I found no LLM architecture-conformance paper with a reported precision/recall on a real benchmark — [Thoughtworks Radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/architecture-drift-reduction-with-llms); [arXiv 2607.03135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03135)
- RIVA (2026), declared-vs-actual cloud config drift: two agents, Verifier (cross-validates tool responses) and Tool-Generation (creates alternative tool calls for multi-perspective checks), with tool-call history tracking; motivation: ReAct agents assume tools always return correct output and cannot tell a real anomaly from a broken tool. AIOpsLab: with erroneous tool responses accuracy 27.3% (ReAct) -> 50.0%; without erroneous responses 28% -> 43.8%. Still below 50% (abs-level) — [arXiv 2603.02345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02345v1)
- OpenSentinel (IaC): Terraform stays the authoritative drift detector; LLM only interprets drift semantics from locality-aware patch contexts and proposes config-level fixes (snippet-level description; exact source URL for this system not isolated, appeared in the same result set as the RIT thesis "Leveraging Open-Source LLMs for Infrastructure Drift Detection and Correction") — [RIT thesis](https://repository.rit.edu/theses/12654/); [Zenodo record surfaced in same search](https://zenodo.org/records/19408576)
- Kernel/DocPrism-type base rates: 11% lower-bound inconsistency rate (DocPrism) and 869 stale references in one kernel snapshot (ReCite) are the only base-rate-like figures for "documentation is wrong" I found in software (see question 5).

### Inferences
- DocPrism / CASCADE / METAMON: hypothesis = {doc consistent, doc inconsistent} per doc-code pair; probe = LLM reading, or executing a generated test (CASCADE, METAMON) which is a costly physical-ish probe; evidence = test pass/fail plus LLM judgment; stop = single-shot (no adaptive stopping); cost = LLM+test-generation compute. Non-LLM SOTA: static analysis + heuristics for narrow classes; for behavioural inconsistency no classical method matches; the LLM's unique contribution is converting NL doc to an executable oracle. Transfer: the strongest pattern for HVAC is to turn the documented claim ("VAV-12 serves zone 4") into a testable prediction (a probe with an expected sensor response) and let the measurement, not the LLM, decide; and to require agreement of multiple independent signals before flagging (CASCADE's generated-code cross-check).
- False-positive lesson (DocPrism 98% flag rate): an unconstrained LLM judge over-flags mismatch when given claim + evidence; the failure mode there is over-skepticism, not deference to the claim (contrast question 6).
- RIVA: hypothesis = {drift real, tool output erroneous}; probe = alternate tool call; evidence = agreement across tools; stop = agent decision; cost = tool calls. Non-LLM SOTA: Terraform plan/diff is exact when the tool is trustworthy. Transfer: this is the same "sensor fault vs real mismatch" ambiguity in HVAC; RIVA's cross-validate-with-a-different-probe idea supports modeling sensor reliability as a latent variable in the posterior. Its sub-50% accuracy shows LLM agents alone are weak at this.
- ReCite: deterministic detection (unresolved symbol) + history-grounded LLM repair: LLM is used for generation/explanation after detection, consistent with "LLM as explainer".
- Architecture drift and OpenSentinel: convergent design principle: deterministic detector is authority, LLM interprets.

### Gaps
- No rigorous, numeric LLM architecture-conformance evaluation found; only practitioner/position-level sources.
- No software doc-drift paper found that does adaptive probe selection or an explicit stopping rule (all are one-shot classification or test-then-check).
- FSE'24 comment-inconsistency numbers, CCIBench numbers not read.
- OpenSentinel primary source not isolated.

## 4. LLM fault localization / agentic debugging where the agent chooses the next probe; adaptive test selection; git-bisect-style search; SBFL + LLM; numbers vs non-LLM baselines

### Takeaway
LLM FL agents (AutoFL, AgentFL) choose which code to inspect via function calls and beat classic SBFL on Defects4J-style benchmarks, but the strongest recent systems combine SBFL/learning-based FL for candidate narrowing with an LLM re-ranker, and there is credible evidence of contamination and brittleness. The formal, non-LLM adaptive-probe literature (entropy-based test generation, active diagnosis, Bayesian bisection) is exactly the user's design and remains the reference for selecting and stopping; LLMs have been added mostly as per-step labelers (semantic bisect) or navigators, with modest gains.

### Cited Findings
- AutoFL (FSE 2024): LLM given function-call tools (e.g. get_class_covered, get_method_covered, get_code_snippet per search result) autonomously decides, round by round, what to inspect next, and outputs a fault location plus natural-language explanation. Evaluated on 798 real-world bugs (Java and Python); method-level acc@1 improved up to 233.3% over baselines; developers preferred a few high-quality explanations. Hallucination/leakage rates not on abstract page (abs-level) — [arXiv 2308.05487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05487)
- AgentFL: multi-agent (comprehension, navigation, confirmation) with Test Behavior Tracking, Document-Guided Search, multi-round dialogue; 157/395 bugs at Top-1 on Defects4J v1.2.0, about $0.074 and 97 s per bug, complementary to learning-based FL (abs-level) — [arXiv 2403.16362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16362)
- FlexFL: two-stage: SBFL and learning-based FL first shrink the candidate list, then an LLM (Llama3-8B suffices) double-checks suspected methods; locates 42 and 63 more bugs at Top-1 than AutoFL and AgentFL (both GPT-3.5) (abs-level) — [arXiv 2411.10714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10714)
- LLM4FL (multi-agent, graph retrieval + iterative refinement): +18.55% Top-1 over AutoFL and +4.82% over SoapFL on Defects4J v2.0.0 (snippet-level) — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=z91EvZbSI1); [arXiv 2409.13642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.13642)
- Brittleness: 10 LLMs, 750,000+ FL tasks over 1,300+ Java/Python programs; semantic-preserving mutations make LLMs fail on 78% of previously localized faults; reliance on syntactic cues; earlier benchmarks affected by contamination (abs-level) — [arXiv 2504.04372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04372)
- Contamination/data quality: papers flag Defects4J in pretraining corpora as a threat; Defects4J cleanness study reports 55% of fault-triggering tests were newly added to replicate the bug and 22% modified after report, embedding developer knowledge (snippet-level) — [arXiv 2310.19139](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.19139); [SieveFL search hit](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13491v1)
- ChatDBG (FSE 2025): LLM issues debugger commands (navigate stack, inspect state); Python: actionable fix on one query 67% of the time, 85% with one follow-up (abs-level; no non-LLM baseline given) — [arXiv 2403.16354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16354)
- AutoCodeRover: uses spectrum-based FL when tests are available to "sharpen context"; 19% resolved on SWE-bench-lite (300 issues), about $0.43 per issue (abs-level) — [arXiv 2404.05427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05427)
- Entropy-based test generation for fault localization (Campos et al., ASE 2013; EvoSuite "EntBug"): choose new tests that minimize entropy of the spectrum-based Bayesian diagnostic ranking; average 54% entropy reduction and 92% fewer diagnosis candidates to inspect (snippet-level) — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6693085/); [EvoSuite EntBug](https://www.evosuite.org/entbug/)
- Active diagnosis test selection via entropy approximation (Bayesian-network diagnosis, loopy-BP entropy estimates) (snippet-level; authors not verified) — [arXiv 1207.1418](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.1418)
- Bayesian git bisect (git_bayesect): selects the next commit to test by greedy expected-entropy minimization; Beta-Bernoulli conjugacy handles unknown failure rates; handles flaky/probabilistic outcomes. Author-side write-up reports at a 90/10 pass/fail-rate split plain bisect drops to about 44% accuracy vs about 96% for bayesect, and 9% vs 67% at 70/30 (secondary blog/snippet; unaudited). README as fetched did not state a stopping rule — [GitHub](https://github.com/hauntsaninja/git_bayesect); [DEV write-up](https://dev.to/shuicici/git-bayesect-bayesian-git-bisection-for-when-bugs-are-playing-games-with-you-33hh)
- LLM-assisted git bisect ("Time Travel"): fine-tuned DeepSeekCoderV2 (QLoRA, weak supervision with human corrections and self-consistency filtering) does per-commit chain-of-thought semantic analysis to handle flaky tests and nonmonotonic regressions; success 74.2% -> 80.6% and up to 2x faster bisect time (abs-level) — [arXiv 2511.18854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18854)
- Theoretical analysis of git bisect exists (not read) — [arXiv 2312.13644](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.13644)
- Confirmation bias in hypothesis testing: across 11 LLMs in a rule-discovery task, models tend to propose confirming rather than falsifying probes, slowing discovery; counterexample-encouraging prompts helped (baseline discovery 42%; "56% average improvement" as summarized, absolute-vs-relative UNVERIFIED) — [arXiv 2604.02485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02485)

### Inferences
- AutoFL / AgentFL / ChatDBG: hypothesis = set of code locations; probe = code-inspection or debugger command (cheap); evidence = code/state text; stop = LLM emits answer; cost = tokens. The probe is cheap compared with HVAC physical tests, so LLM-chosen navigation is tolerable there; it would not be for costly probes. Non-LLM SOTA: SBFL and learning-based FL remain strong and complementary; the best recent systems (FlexFL) are hybrids, so "LLM alone" is not SOTA. What the LLM contributes: reading code semantics and producing explanations.
- Entropy-based test generation + active diagnosis + git_bayesect: hypothesis = categorical posterior over fault locations; probe = test/commit; evidence = pass/fail (possibly noisy); selection = expected entropy; stop = ranking entropy / posterior threshold; cost = tests. This is the user's system in software form and is non-LLM and still the reference approach. Its noisy-observation handling (bayesect's Beta-Bernoulli) is the piece most transferable to noisy sensor-derived statistics.
- Time-Travel LLM bisect: LLM used as a per-item noisy labeler inside a search procedure; gain (+6.4 points) is modest and needs fine-tuning, supporting "LLM as a noisy evidence channel" rather than "LLM as planner".
- Confirmation-bias result implies that if an LLM is allowed to choose which probe to run given a documented claim, it tends to choose confirming probes; an EIG criterion picks discriminating probes by construction. That is a concrete argument for keeping probe selection formal (my inference; the study is a toy rule-discovery task).
- Brittleness (78% failure under semantic-preserving edits) implies LLM channel outputs should be treated as unstable evidence and averaged/paraphrase-checked before entering the posterior.

### Gaps
- AutoFL's exact Top-1 counts vs SBFL baselines not retrieved (abstract gives only "up to 233.3%"); the 798-bug count is from the abstract page summary.
- No paper found that puts an LLM in the probe-selection role and compares it directly to entropy-based selection in software FL.
- git_bayesect accuracy figures are from an author write-up; no independent evaluation and no stopping rule verified.
- Agentless / SWE-agent / LDB not searched.

## 5. Cross-cutting: is there a formal treatment of "documentation may be wrong", and how is that prior set (LLM, heuristic, base rate)?

### Takeaway
Yes, but almost all of it is in the non-LLM data-management literature: per-source accuracy priors (Knowledge-Based Trust) and Bayesian KG audit with informative priors. In LLM-era software and fact-checking work the "doc may be wrong" prior is implicit (base-rate numbers reported after the fact, or precision-first filtering); I found no paper where an LLM sets the prior probability that a documented claim is wrong.

### Cited Findings
- Knowledge-Based Trust (Dong, Gabrilovich, Murphy et al., VLDB 2015): multi-layer probabilistic model estimates the trustworthiness of web sources from the correctness of their facts, explicitly separating extraction errors from source (factual) errors; applied to 2.8B extracted facts, scoring 119M webpages — [arXiv 1502.03519](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03519)
- aHPD (SIGMOD 2025): explicit Beta priors on KG accuracy; informative priors from similar KGs reduce annotation from 222 to 63 triples; prior choice materially changes cost; no single default prior is best — [arXiv 2502.18961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18961)
- Base rates for documentation error in software: at least 11% of code-doc pairs inconsistent (DocPrism lower bound, four languages); 869 stale function references in one Linux kernel version (ReCite) — [DocPrism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00215); [ReCite](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03734)
- IaC: the declared state is treated as the spec and drift is any divergence; drift causes listed as specification bugs, manual changes and system updates (RIVA introduction, snippet-level) — [arXiv 2603.02345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02345v1)
- LLM-elicited priors in this space: Bayesian Linguistic Forecaster uses data-dependent priors in Platt-scaling calibration but for forecasting, not doc reliability (abs-level) — [arXiv 2604.18576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18576). Optimal Bayesian stopping uses prior information about answer-frequency distribution, not doc reliability — [arXiv 2602.05395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05395)
- Entity alignment: LLM4EA models LLM labels as noisy and uses probabilistic refinement rather than a prior about the KG edges — [arXiv 2405.16806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16806)

### Inferences
- The defensible ways to set the "documented claim is wrong" prior are: (a) empirical base rate by category (DocPrism-style 11% lower bound, KG audit accuracy), (b) source-level reliability learned jointly (KBT), (c) informative Beta priors from comparable systems with explicit prior-sensitivity analysis (aHPD). LLM-elicited priors in this exact role have no published validation that I found; prior research on LLM prior elicitation (already covered elsewhere) should be the only basis.
- KBT's separation of "extraction/measurement error" from "source error" is the formal template for "sensor-derived statistic is unreliable" vs "documentation is wrong" and matches RIVA's tool-error-vs-real-drift framing.

### Gaps
- No source found giving base rates for "documented equipment/topology relationship wrong" in buildings (out of scope of this note).
- No paper found where an LLM estimates per-claim documentation-error probability with validation against ground truth.
- Truth-discovery literature beyond KBT (source-reliability EM models) not searched.

## 6. Anchoring / sycophancy toward the provided claim: do LLM verifiers defer to the documented answer?

### Takeaway
Evidence is mixed and direction depends on set-up: LLMs adopt wrong provided context often (ClashEval >60%), are shifted by prior analysis "anchors" (about 23.5% cross-model susceptibility) and prefer answers matching user beliefs (Sharma et al.), yet in doc-vs-code checking a naive LLM over-flags inconsistency (98% flag rate) and in fact-checking framing shifts retrieved evidence far more than final verdicts. No study directly measures anchoring to a documented claim in KG or documentation verification, so this must be tested for the HVAC pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- ClashEval (NeurIPS 2024 D&B): 1,200+ questions across six domains, six top LLMs including GPT-4o; models adopt incorrect retrieved content over their own correct prior more than 60% of the time; adoption falls as the content becomes more unrealistic and rises as the model's initial-answer confidence (token probability) falls — [arXiv 2404.10198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10198)
- Cognitive heuristics in LLM vulnerability detection (2026): 8 LLMs, 3 languages; cross-model susceptibility framing 33.2%, anchoring by prior analysis results 23.5%, halo 18.4%; models often flip verdicts on context without finding the real flaw; proof-of-concept suppresses up to 97% of detected vulnerabilities — [arXiv 2606.30587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30587)
- Adversarial comments: ALIBI automatically inserts adversarial natural-language comments and reports over 90% attack success across four detectors including frontier multi-agent systems (snippet) — [arXiv 2607.24964](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24964); contradicted in effect size by a large empirical study (100-sample benchmark, 8 frontier models, 9,366 trials) reporting small, statistically non-significant effects of adversarial comments on detection accuracy (snippet) — [awesomepapers listing of arXiv 2602.16741](https://awesomepapers.io/cybersecurity/papers/2602.16741). The two are not reconciled here (different attack strength/adaptivity).
- Sycophancy (Sharma et al., ICLR 2024): assistants systematically match user beliefs; both humans and preference models sometimes prefer convincing sycophantic responses over correct ones; matching user beliefs raised preference probability by about 6% (secondary summary) — [arXiv 2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)
- Fact-checking bias study, HerO / Llama 3.1 (2025): prompting for supporting vs refuting vs neutral perspectives changes about half of retrieved evidence, but final verdicts stay stable; model shows inherent negative bias (refuses to generate supporting evidence for claims it deems false); about half of claims labeled Not Enough Evidence when asked directly (abs-level) — [arXiv 2506.21745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21745)
- Doc-vs-code over-flagging: baseline LLM flags 98% of pairs as inconsistent (DocPrism), i.e. no deference to the documentation in that framing — [arXiv 2511.00215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00215)
- Confirmation bias in probe choice (11 LLMs): tendency to propose confirming tests — [arXiv 2604.02485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02485)
- MAI-DxO includes an explicit "Dr. Challenger" persona whose role is to recognize cognitive biases and challenge assumptions, showing anti-anchoring was built into the orchestrator (role description from secondary summary; no ablation of that persona seen) — [Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2506.22405)
- Verifier bias when verifying its own or provided answers: search surfaced work on self-confirmation and agreement bias in verifiers (cross-model verification mitigates); not read (snippet) — [arXiv 2507.11662](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11662); [arXiv 2512.23518](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23518)

### Inferences
- Anchoring is plausible for an HVAC verifier that is shown "documented: VAV-12 feeds zone 4" plus ambiguous sensor text (ClashEval and heuristics results), especially when the evidence is weak (ClashEval: lower confidence -> higher adoption of provided content). The over-flagging result shows the opposite failure is also real. Net: the sign of the bias is prompt-dependent; the safe design is to measure it, not assume it.
- Mitigations with support in this literature: cross-model verification, counterexample-encouraging prompts (confirmation-bias paper), explicit challenger/alternative-generation role (MAI-DxO), and treating any LLM verdict as an evidence channel with an estimated confusion matrix. A blind variant (LLM sees evidence and the set of candidate owners without being told which is documented) is my proposed control, not something tested in these papers.
- (Fact) Check Your Bias implies verdict stability under framing might be partly an artifact of a strong verdict prior; it does not show robustness to a wrong provided claim.

### Gaps
- No paper found that measures anchoring specifically to a documented claim in doc-drift, KG triple validation or IaC verification.
- Adversarial-comment studies conflict (ALIBI vs the 9,366-trial study) and I only have snippet-level access to both.
- Sharma et al. 6% figure is from a secondary summary.

## 7. LLM claim-verifier calibration (overconfidence, order effects, self-consistency) relevant to using an LLM as one noisy evidence channel

### Takeaway
Verbalized LLM confidence is typically overconfident; consistency across multiple samples and better aggregation improve calibration but remain imperfect; LLM judges show substantial order (position) sensitivity; selective-prediction fact-checkers exist but with small-N evidence and no calibration win over retrieval baselines. Nothing found validates LLM likelihood ratios for direct Bayesian updating.

### Cited Findings
- Xiong et al. (ICLR 2024): verbalized confidence from GPT-3, GPT-3.5, Vicuna shows high ECE (>0.377), overconfident; human-inspired prompts, multi-sample consistency and better aggregation help; calibration and failure prediction improve with model scale but stay far from ideal; diminishing returns of prompting for GPT-4 (snippet-level) — [arXiv 2306.13063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063)
- Position bias in LLM-as-judge (Judging the Judges): position flip rate (verdict changes when response order is swapped) ranges 25% to 50% across judge models; bias varies by judge/task and is driven by the quality gap between candidates (snippet-level) — [arXiv 2406.07791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791)
- Response biases: a survey-style study found LLMs biased toward answering "No" in English, the opposite of human acquiescence (snippet, secondary; source paper not identified) — see search result set at [LLM-judge bias listing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13685)
- Selective fact-checking (ECE-Bench, 95 claims): 97.8% selective accuracy at 93.7% coverage, but no calibration advantage over the strongest retrieval baseline — [arXiv 2607.18240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18240)
- Repeated-sample verification: Bayesian stopping over consistency reduces LLM calls up to 50% — [arXiv 2602.05395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05395). FIRE's stopping relies on model-reported confidence — [arXiv 2411.00784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00784)
- Retrieval-grounded LLM validation shows asymmetric errors (88% precision, 44% recall on BioRED-based test) — [arXiv 2409.07507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07507)
- Robustness: 78% of previously correct FL results fail after semantic-preserving edits — [arXiv 2504.04372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04372)
- Calibration listing for claim-level self-verification (CLAIM-CAL, R2VC etc.) surfaced but not read — [Springer CLAIM-CAL](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-026-02240-w); [arXiv 2609.11955](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11955)

### Inferences
- Treat the LLM verdict as a categorical observation with a learned confusion matrix (per verifier, per evidence type) rather than using its stated probability as a likelihood; calibrate on a labeled subset (Platt/temperature as in BLF and the entity-matching calibration paper).
- Order/position effects imply candidate-owner ordering in prompts should be randomized and averaged, since candidates are a listed set; positions flips of 25-50% in pairwise judging suggest large sensitivity (magnitude for categorical multi-way owner choice not measured).
- Consistency-based confidence (multiple paraphrased prompts, cross-model) is the best supported calibration lever; correlated draws should not be counted as independent evidence in the posterior update (inference).

### Gaps
- No paper found validating LLM outputs as likelihood ratios in a sequential Bayesian update for claim verification; BLF's linguistic belief state is closest but on forecasting.
- Multi-way (not binary) categorical calibration of LLM verifiers not found.
- Position-bias magnitude for multi-candidate categorical selection not found.
- The "No"-bias claim is secondary; underlying paper not identified.

## 8. Synthesis: what transfers to the HVAC documentation-mismatch problem (per-area, one line each)

### Takeaway
Across all four areas the deterministic/Bayesian components (posterior over alternatives, entropy or EIG probe selection, credible-interval or threshold stopping, explicit priors, noisy-channel modeling) are where the rigor and the non-LLM state of the art lie; LLMs contribute hypothesis generation, converting NL claims into testable oracles, per-item noisy labeling, and explanation, and their stopping/selection behavior is unvalidated, model-dependent and bias-prone.

### Cited Findings
(All facts are cited in sections 1-7 above; this section only lists pointers.)
- Closest architectural twin to the user's system: BEACON (categorical belief + EIG probe selection + LLM evidence controller) — [arXiv 2608.05757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05757)
- Closest non-LLM software analogue: entropy-based test generation and Bayesian bisection — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6693085/); [git_bayesect](https://github.com/hauntsaninja/git_bayesect)
- Closest formal "cost of human verification with priors and stopping" analogue: aHPD — [arXiv 2502.18961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18961)
- Closest "measurement error vs claim error" analogue: KBT and RIVA — [arXiv 1502.03519](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03519); [arXiv 2603.02345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02345v1)
- LLM as executable-oracle generator from docs: CASCADE — [arXiv 2604.19400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19400)
- LLM as noisy annotator under budget with noise-aware refinement: LLM4EA — [arXiv 2405.16806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16806)

### Inferences
One sentence each on what transfers (my synthesis):
- KG validation: use the LLM as a high-precision, low-recall channel over retrieved text, split structural checks to rules, and estimate documented-claim reliability with an explicit informative prior plus prior-sensitivity check (aHPD, KBT).
- Fact-checking agents: FIRE-style "skip probes when the prior is already decisive" gives most of the cost saving, but replace LLM self-confidence with a posterior threshold; VOI/EIG stopping (ITBC, BEACON, ECR) is the formal version of the user's futility rule, and the only agentic evidence (2606.02245) says LLM-chosen stopping is model/task-dependent.
- Software doc drift: convert the documented claim into a predicted observable and let the observation, cross-checked by an independent path (CASCADE, RIVA), decide; expect naive LLM judging to over-flag (98% flag rate).
- Fault localization: formal entropy/Bayesian probe selection (Campos, bayesect) is the non-LLM reference for exactly the user's loop and handles noisy outcomes; LLM add-ons (Time-Travel bisect, FlexFL re-ranking) give modest gains and LLM FL is brittle (78% flips) and contamination-prone.
- Anchoring: measure it, since evidence points both directions; use alternative-generation/challenger roles, blind candidate lists and randomized order.
- Calibration: model LLM output as a noisy categorical channel with learned confusion matrix, do not use verbalized probability as a likelihood, and do not treat repeated draws as independent.
- Where LLMs are strongest across these fields: reading unstructured documentation, proposing alternative hypotheses, writing testable predictions/tests, and explaining results; where they are not shown to be reliable: selecting the next costly probe, deciding when to stop, and producing calibrated confidence.

### Gaps
Global caveats:
- I exceeded the suggested tool-call budget to cover eight areas; many entries are abstract-level or snippet-level. Full-text checks still worth doing: FIRE (confidence mechanism), BEACON (EIG-vs-LLM ablation numbers), Tsaneva et al. (KG LLM+HITL), MAI-DxO (stopping/ablations), AutoFL tables, ITBC (critic definition).
- Several fetches of PDFs returned binary; one summarizer produced content that conflicted with the primary page (MAI-DxO personas, ITBC VOI definition). I resolved conflicts in favor of HTML/abstract pages and flagged them.
- No head-to-head "LLM-selected probe sequence vs EIG" found in KG, fact-checking, software drift or FL; that gap is itself a finding.
- No paper found that studies a physical-probe (cost measured in real-world actions) verification of a documented claim outside medicine (MAI-DxO) and pathology (BEACON).
