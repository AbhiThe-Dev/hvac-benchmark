## Q1. Which LLM technician copilots are deployed or rigorously studied (2023-2026), and how grounded / gated are they?

### Takeaway
No industry outside healthcare/IT has a rigorous, independently published, real-technician trial of an LLM troubleshooting copilot; deployed field-service copilots (KONE, Microsoft Dynamics, telecom) report only satisfaction/adoption or unsourced vendor numbers. The best-evidenced designs are the ones where the LLM never decides: it retrieves/ranks certified content (aviation MRO task search), fuses structured evidence against a knowledge graph (automotive HiL), or acts as a gated second-look on a human decision (Penda Health clinical copilot; Microsoft incident-management LLMs). Every deployed system keeps the human as final decision-maker.

### Cited Findings

**A. KONE Technician Assistant (elevators/escalators) [VENDOR]**
- (1) LLM role: generates recommended troubleshooting steps before arrival and answers on-site questions about repairs/adjustments/component access; "technicians retain final judgment", must approve recommendations. Example given: AI suggested checking a floor magnet rather than the drive system, which proved correct (single anecdote). — [KONE newsroom](https://www.kone.com/global/en/newsroom/stories/technician-assistant-ai-elevator-maintenance.html)
- (2) Grounding: connected-equipment (IoT) data, maintenance history, technical documentation, previously solved support cases; restricted to "approved documentation and verified past support cases". — [KONE newsroom](https://www.kone.com/global/en/newsroom/stories/technician-assistant-ai-elevator-maintenance.html). AWS describes the pipeline as preprocessing, retrieval, evaluation and text generation over manuals, historical maintenance reports and IoT data — [AWS case study](https://aws.amazon.com/solutions/case-studies/aws-ai-security-kone-case-study/)
- (3) Evaluation/N: AWS case study: pilot of 100 users over 3 months; "technicians could opt out of the app, but none did"; later ~1,500 active users in 11 countries, target all 40,000 technicians — [AWS case study](https://aws.amazon.com/solutions/case-studies/aws-ai-security-kone-case-study/). KONE page (as of Aug 2026): ~15,000 technicians in 60 countries, 80% satisfaction among surveyed technicians; no time-saved or first-time-fix figures disclosed — [KONE newsroom](https://www.kone.com/global/en/newsroom/stories/technician-assistant-ai-elevator-maintenance.html). (The two pages give different user counts because they were written at different times.) A search-result summary says it is built on Anthropic Claude models on Amazon Bedrock [SNIPPET-ONLY]; the AWS case study page itself does not name the model.
- (4) Limits: no independent evaluation, no FTF/time metrics, no denominators for "satisfaction"; opt-out non-use is a weak adoption proxy.
- (5) Transfer [INFERENCE]: KONE is the closest deployed analogue to "connected-asset data + history + docs -> next diagnostic step", and its publicly reported metrics (satisfaction, adoption) are exactly what the HVAC shadow study should go beyond by logging step-level agreement with human choices and tests-to-diagnosis.

**B. Penda Health "AI Consult" clinical copilot (primary care, Nairobi; OpenAI + Penda) [PREPRINT]** — professional-in-the-loop analogue, not a technician.
- (1) Role: LLM-written recommendations at key points in a visit, acting as a "safety net" that activates only when there might be an error; three-tier signal green (no concerns) / yellow (moderate) / red (safety-critical, requires acknowledgment); clinician stays in control. Triggered asynchronously when the clinician navigates away from key EMR fields (vitals, complaint, notes, investigations, diagnosis, medications). — [Korom et al., arXiv 2507.16947](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16947); [OpenAI write-up](https://openai.com/index/ai-clinical-copilot-penda-health/) (search snippet; page fetch blocked)
- (2) Grounding: prompts tailored with Kenyan epidemiological context, local clinical guidelines and Penda standard procedures (free-form prompt context, not a structured fault tree) — [OpenAI write-up](https://openai.com/index/ai-clinical-copilot-penda-health/) (snippet-level).
- (3) Design/N: clinicians randomly assigned to AI vs non-AI (1:1, block-randomized, stratified by clinic): 106 clinicians (57 AI / 49 non-AI); 39,579 eligible visits (20,589 AI / 18,990 non-AI). Outcome labels: 108 independent physicians, blinded to arm, rated 5,666 sampled visits on 5-point Likert (1-2 = clinically meaningful error), ~25% double-rated. Result: 16% fewer diagnostic errors and 13% fewer treatment errors (relative). 8-day patient follow-up "not feeling better": 3.8% AI vs 4.3% non-AI (non-significant). All AI-group clinicians said it improved quality of care, 75% "substantially". — [arXiv 2507.16947 (html)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16947); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16947)
- Adherence: "left in red" (red alert unresolved at visit end) started at 35-40% and fell to 20% in the AI group after an "active deployment" program (peer champions with real examples, tracking left-in-red with personalized coaching, recognition), vs 40% in the non-AI group. — [arXiv 2507.16947 (html)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16947)
- (4) Limits (authors): ~60% missing patient-outcome data, consent selection effects, single Nairobi context, possible Hawthorne effects; results "required a clinical workflow-aligned AI Consult implementation and active deployment to encourage clinician uptake". — [arXiv 2507.16947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16947)
- (5) Transfer [INFERENCE]: This is the template for the stage after shadow mode: clinician/technician-level randomization, blinded expert rating of decision quality, and logging of whether flagged advice was acted on; note that even safety-critical flags were ignored 35-40% of the time without adoption work.

**C. Aircraft MRO task-search copilot (Korean airline; compliance-preserving retrieval) [PREPRINT]**
- (1) Role: LLM only re-ranks the top-50 retrieved AMM task candidates and returns JSON indices; it receives no procedural text and cannot generate/modify content; falls back to baseline dense retrieval if JSON invalid; technicians open certified PDFs in existing viewers (keeps audit trail and FAA-style certification boundary). — [arXiv 2511.15383](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15383)
- (2) Grounding: embeddings built from ATA chapter hierarchy + task titles (procedural text excluded); certified manuals remain the only source of steps. — [arXiv 2511.15383](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15383)
- (3) Evaluation: Phase 1 synthetic benchmark; Phase 2 with 10 licensed aircraft maintenance technicians (1-10 yrs experience) at a Korean commercial airline: 10 tasks across ATA chapters, each participant reformulated official AMM titles into workplace language in English and Korean (200 searches, 197 analyzed). Top-10 success 90.9% (179/197; 95% CI 86.0-94.1), top-5 86.3% (170/197), mean task-completion time 18.0 s; English 95.9% top-10 / 14.2 s vs Korean 86.0% / 22.2 s. — [arXiv 2511.15383](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15383)
- (4) Limits: failures from lexical mismatch, ambiguity between similar tasks, Korean terminology; N=10; queries were reformulated titles, not real fault episodes; no outcome (fix) measure.
- (5) Transfer [INFERENCE]: Shows the cheapest regulator-compatible pattern (LLM ranks, human reads certified source) and a real-technician evaluation that is feasible with N~10; for HVAC, the analogue is letting the LLM (if used at all) only rank or verbalize candidates while the Bayesian engine and logged evidence stay authoritative.

**D. Aviation MRO documentation LLM framework [SNIPPET-ONLY]**
- Technicians enter detected issues in natural language; system generates recommended corrective actions citing AMM/TSM — search-result summary of [ResearchGate: "Transformation of aircraft MRO documentation using LLMs"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399714517_TRANSFORMATION_OF_AIRCRAFT_MRO_DOCUMENTATION_USING_LARGE_LANGUAGE_MODELS) (page returned 403; evaluation, N, and limits unverified).

**E. Automotive diagnostics**
- Knowledge-graph-guided RAG-LLM for HiL fault diagnosis [PREPRINT]: (1) LLM is "an evidence-fusion, reasoning, and reranking layer" over structured evidence, not a direct classifier; (2) grounding = KG of signal-to-fault mappings/propagation/diagnostic rules + engineered features (mean shift z-scores, first abnormal reaction time) + retrieved similar historical cases; (3) 13 CSV recordings (10 gasoline-engine ASM fault runs, 3 EV), leave-one-recording-out, Top-1/Top-3/MRR/F1/MCC, no human evaluators; (4) single-fault only, small data, controlled HiL not production; (5) transfer [INFERENCE]: closest architectural cousin to a structured posterior + LLM explanation layer, but has no technician evidence at all. — [arXiv 2608.11277](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11277)
- Dealer/OEM diagnostic assistants (Sonatus, MechanicIQ, Tekion, MyKaarma pre-diagnosis) are [VENDOR]: e.g., MyKaarma's pre-diag tool is claimed to cut diagnostic time by "17 hours" and add $180,000/month (trade-press relay of vendor claim, no method/denominator) — [Automotive News](https://www.autonews.com/retail/service-and-parts/an-ai-helps-with-diagnostics-0618/); Sonatus markets combining vehicle telemetry, OEM knowledge and repair procedures — [Sonatus](https://www.sonatus.com/solutions/after-sales-service/). I found no peer-reviewed OEM/dealer technician study.

**F. Industrial / energy**
- SafeLLM (offshore wind maintenance alarms) [PREPRINT]: LLM interprets alarm sequences and recommends maintenance actions; a sentence-distance filter is meant to catch hallucinations/unsafe outputs before reaching technicians; tested only on ChatGPT-4-generated test sentences ("preliminary"). — [arXiv 2410.10852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10852)
- Oak Ridge equipment-assistant (manufacturing) user study reportedly had only 3 participants and 6 prompts [SNIPPET-ONLY] — [arXiv 2509.06734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.06734).
- CAMB benchmark exists because civil-aviation-maintenance LLM evaluation was missing; emphasizes RAG as the dominant real-world pattern (results not retrieved) — [arXiv 2508.20420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20420).

**G. Field-service platforms, telecom, aviation vendors [VENDOR / unverified]**
- Microsoft Dynamics 365 Field Service Copilot: drafts work orders from customer emails, work-order recaps, Teams access; dispatch decisions are "supported by optimization recommendations (not fully automated)"; no quantified technician outcome on the page; one customer quote (Joulz). — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/guides/how-ai-can-give-field-service-technicians-a-boost)
- A consultancy blog claims 20-35% first-time-fix improvement for "TechRev-built" copilots and another vendor claims 92% accuracy for an agent deployed to 1,000 field engineers — no methods, no independent source [VENDOR/unverified]: [TechRev](https://www.techrev.us/blog/ai-in-field-service-management/), [iopex FieldPilot](https://www.iopex.com/innovation/field-pilot).
- Telstra says its AskTelstra knowledge assistant answered >1 million questions from frontline teams (press, usage count only; not confirmed as field technicians) — [Telecompaper](https://www.telecompaper.com/news/telstras-gen-ai-answers-one-million-questions-from-frontline-teams--1537139).
- Lufthansa Technik's AVIATAR "Technical Repetitives Examination" applies AI to logbook write-ups at 20+ airlines; troubleshooting recommendations are described as future — [Lufthansa Technik](https://www.lufthansa-technik.com/en/aviatar-adds-technical-repetitives-examination-to-its-reliability-suite-f58a894db33f64fd). This is analytics, not a generative technician copilot [SNIPPET-ONLY].

**H. Software-operations analogues (structured evidence -> LLM -> owner decides) [PEER-REVIEWED]**
- Ahmed et al., ICSE 2023: (1) LLM (GPT-3.x, fine-tuned/zero-shot) recommends root cause and mitigation for incidents; (2) grounded in incident title/summary text only; (3) >40,000 Microsoft incidents offline, plus human evaluation: 25 actual incident owners (on-call engineers) evaluated outputs for 50 recent incidents in 20-30 min interviews; for each, they picked the best of 6 candidates and scored correctness/readability 1-5. Mean correctness by model pool ranged 2.28-3.16; best-of-candidates mean correctness 3.52-4.64; >70% gave overall usefulness >=3 — [arXiv 2301.03797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03797) and full text; (4) owners' concern: "if the suggestion is incorrect, on-call engineers may take longer to investigate"; missing/classified data limits usefulness; (5) transfer [INFERENCE]: shows the owner-as-labeler design (the person who actually resolved the case rates each suggestion post hoc) which is cheap but hindsight-biased.
- RCACopilot, EuroSys 2024: (1) LLM summarizes collected diagnostics and predicts root-cause category with explanatory narrative; (2) handler-based structured diagnostic collection (alert-type matched) then LLM; (3) one year of Microsoft incidents, micro-F1 0.766; a search-result summary states deployment in 30+ teams [SNIPPET-ONLY]; the paper says the diagnostic-collection component has been in use at Microsoft for over four years; (4) the abstract does not report a formal human-usability study; (5) transfer [INFERENCE]: structured collection first, LLM second — same ordering as the HVAC engine. — [arXiv 2305.15778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15778); [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3627703.3629553)

**I. Sequential-diagnosis benchmark with test cost (closest analogue to "which test next") [PREPRINT]**
- MAI-DxO / Sequential Diagnosis Benchmark (Microsoft): (1) an orchestrator simulating a panel of physicians that proposes differentials and selects "high-value, cost-effective tests"; (2) grounding = LLM (o3) knowledge with a gatekeeper model that reveals findings only when asked; (3) 304 NEJM clinicopathological cases; comparison with 21 US/UK physicians (median 12 yrs; 17 primary care, 4 hospitalists; avg 36 cases each; 764 diagnoses) forbidden from search engines/LLMs/colleagues. Cost = $300 per visit-type query + CPT-priced tests (2023 US health-system price table, >98% match). Abstract-level results: MAI-DxO+o3 ~80% accuracy vs 20% average for generalist physicians, 20% lower cost than physicians, 70% lower than off-the-shelf o3; 85.5% in max-accuracy configuration. Dollar figures (physicians ~$2,963/case; o3 78.6% at $7,850; MAI-DxO 79.9% at $2,397 or 85.5% at $7,184) come from a search-result summary of the paper [SNIPPET-ONLY]. (4) Authors state results "do not yet establish the clinical efficacy... in real-world decision support"; NEJM cases are unrepresentative of everyday conditions; physicians were barred from external resources (search engines, LLMs, online sources), so the human baseline is deliberately handicapped. — [arXiv 2506.22405 (abstract)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22405), [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22405); full paper also at [erichorvitz.com](https://www.erichorvitz.com/sequential_diagnosis_LM.pdf)
- (5) Transfer [INFERENCE]: the accuracy-vs-cumulative-test-cost frontier, with human and model both requesting tests from a gatekeeper, is the right offline report format for "fewer tests" claims; a retrospective HVAC analogue needs a gatekeeper built from cases where full evidence was eventually collected. Caveat: the human baseline was artificially restricted (no external resources), so do not read 20% vs 80% as real-world performance.

**J. Rigorous LLM-assistant field experiments outside technician work (peer-reviewed unless noted)**
- Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond, QJE 2025: (1) generative-AI conversational assistant suggesting responses/knowledge to customer-support agents; (2) trained on high-performer conversations; (3) staggered rollout, 5,179 agents; +14% issues resolved per hour on average, +34% for novice/low-skilled, minimal for experienced/high-skilled; AI-using agents reached in 2 months what took ~8 months. — [arXiv 2304.11771](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.11771); [NBER w31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161); [QJE](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658). (5) Transfer [INFERENCE]: stratify shadow-mode agreement/steps by technician experience; benefits and disagreement patterns are likely concentrated by skill.
- Security Copilot RCT for IT administrators (Microsoft-hosted; vendor-adjacent) [VENDOR-ADJACENT]: 181 Upwork freelancers randomized (91 treatment/90 control) on lab tasks; +34.53% accuracy, -30.69% time (an earlier abstract summary gave 29.79%; version discrepancy); LLM-based grader for free response; authors caution lab results may not transfer to live operations and note Copilot latency slowed users. — [arXiv 2411.01067](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.01067v1)
- Goh et al., JAMA Netw Open 2024: single-blind RCT, 50 physicians (26 attendings/24 residents), 6 vignettes: GPT-4-assisted median 76% vs 74% conventional (+2 pp, 95% CI -4 to 8, P=.60); LLM alone 92%; time 519 s vs 565 s (n.s.). — [JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2825395). A follow-up on management reasoning (92 physicians, +6.5% mean score difference with LLM) is reported in [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03456-y) [SNIPPET-ONLY].
- Alibaba/Taobao field experiment [PREPRINT, May 2026]: (1) agentic AI autonomously resolves "AI-eligible" chats while workers supervise and escalate; (3) randomized, 647 workers, 680,676 chats, Aug 2024, worker-level difference-in-differences; deployment cuts chat duration but lowers ratings for AI-eligible chats; human intervention preserves quality in technical escalations but is weaker in emotional ones because workers engage less; early intervention sustains effort; authors flag possible skill erosion in specialized supervisory roles. — [arXiv 2605.14830](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.14830) / [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14830). (5) Transfer [INFERENCE]: a supervise-the-AI role lowers engagement; shadow mode where the human decides first avoids that failure mode.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Across industries the defensible "LLM role" hierarchy is: (i) retrieve/rank certified content (MRO), (ii) narrate/rerank over structured evidence (automotive KG, RCACopilot), (iii) second-look flagger on a human decision (Penda). No deployed system lets the LLM choose the diagnostic action; the HVAC engine (Bayesian EIG selection) is already more structured than every technician-facing LLM copilot found.
- [INFERENCE] The user's tool should be presented (if at all) as an LLM-free or LLM-verbalized recommender; any LLM component in the shadow phase should be limited to explanation text so that agreement metrics measure the Bayesian policy, not LLM variance.

### Gaps
- No independent randomized or controlled real-technician study of an LLM troubleshooting copilot in elevators, telecom, utilities, automotive dealers, aviation MRO, or medical-device field service was found. Utilities and medical-device (Philips/Siemens Healthineers/GE) field-service LLMs: a search returned only clinical/customer-support AI and nothing usable on field engineers. Otis/Schindler: nothing found on generative technician assistants.
- Not retrieved due to budget/paywalls: Springer study "Evaluating the Usability of a Chatbot-Driven Operational Manual for Maintenance Professionals in Manufacturing" ([link](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-03725-1_32), title only; N and results unknown); AeroCopilotBench ([arXiv 2608.16349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16349), flight-deck not maintenance, "safety-gated" scoring seen only in a snippet); Penda mixed-methods clinician-experience study ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.14.25333740.full.pdf), 403).
- Siemens Industrial Copilot and HVAC agents were excluded per instructions.

## Q2. Evaluation with REAL technicians: what has actually been measured (time, FTF, errors, adoption), and how good is the evidence?

### Takeaway
Technician-level evidence is thin: N=10 licensed airline technicians (retrieval accuracy/time only, preprint), a 100-user 3-month KONE pilot (adoption/opt-out only, vendor), and a 3-participant manufacturing user study. First-time-fix and time-saved figures come only from vendor marketing without methods. The strongest quantitative designs are from adjacent professions (customer-support agents, physicians, IT admins, consultants) and show large benefits for novices and small/no benefit or harm for experts or out-of-distribution tasks.

### Cited Findings
- Rigorous or near-rigorous designs with N: staggered rollout N=5,179 agents (QJE) — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161); randomized clinician-level trial N=106 clinicians/39,579 visits with blinded expert rating (preprint) — [arXiv 2507.16947](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16947); RCT N=50 physicians — [JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2825395); RCT N=181 freelancers — [arXiv 2411.01067](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.01067v1); preregistered N=758 consultants — [HBS AI Institute](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier/).
- Only technician-level numbers: 10 AMTs, 90.9% top-10 retrieval success, 18.0 s mean time — [arXiv 2511.15383](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15383); KONE pilot 100 users/3 months, none opted out, 80% satisfaction later — [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/solutions/case-studies/aws-ai-security-kone-case-study/), [KONE](https://www.kone.com/global/en/newsroom/stories/technician-assistant-ai-elevator-maintenance.html).
- Vendor-only FTF claims (unverified, no methods): 20-35% FTF improvement — [TechRev](https://www.techrev.us/blog/ai-in-field-service-management/); MyKaarma "17 hours" diagnostic time reduction — [Automotive News](https://www.autonews.com/retail/service-and-parts/an-ai-helps-with-diagnostics-0618/).
- Heterogeneity by skill: +34% for novices, minimal for experts — [arXiv 2304.11771](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.11771). Jagged frontier: +>25% speed, +>40% human-rated quality, +>12% task completion inside the frontier — [HBS AI Institute](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier/); -19 percentage points correctness on a task outside the frontier — [SSRN abstract 4573321](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321).
- Access to the LLM did not improve physician diagnostic reasoning in an RCT even though the LLM alone scored 92% vs 74% for conventional-resource physicians — [JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2825395).
- Outcome measures used in the better studies: blinded expert-rated error rates and left-in-red adherence (Penda), owner-rated correctness 1-5 (Ahmed), cost-per-case vs accuracy (SDBench), issues/hour (Brynjolfsson) — sources as in Q1.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The only outcome that transfers across these studies to "minimize tests" is a step- or case-level efficiency measure (tests to confident diagnosis, cost-weighted), and none of the technician studies measured it; the HVAC shadow study would be novel on this axis.
- [INFERENCE] Plan for a skill-stratified analysis and an "outside the frontier" slice (unusual topologies, undocumented retrofits), since averages can hide harm on the tail (Dell'Acqua).

### Gaps
- No technician-level randomized study reporting first-time-fix, callbacks, or truck-roll reduction for an LLM copilot; no error-rate study with technicians. No published N for technician satisfaction surveys (KONE 80%).

## Q3. Human factors: over-reliance, trust calibration, contradicting the documentation, confidence/uncertainty and explanation formats

### Takeaway
Across experiments, showing more explanation does not reliably improve joint accuracy and can raise acceptance of wrong advice; confidence displays calibrate trust but do not by themselves improve team accuracy, and high displayed confidence can induce over-reliance; interventions that force independent thought reduce over-reliance but are disliked. There is no direct evidence on technicians reacting to AI advice that contradicts drawings/documentation, and no maintenance-specific deskilling study; regulators (EASA) explicitly flag complacency and over-reliance for maintenance-assistance AI.

### Cited Findings
- Explanations raised acceptance of AI advice regardless of correctness; net team gain minimal (three datasets, mixed-methods user studies) — Bansal et al., CHI 2021, [arXiv 2006.14779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.14779), [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3411764.3445717).
- Confidence scores calibrate trust but alone were insufficient to improve joint human-AI performance; local explanations problematic — Zhang, Liao, Bellamy, [arXiv 2001.02114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02114).
- Cognitive forcing functions significantly reduced over-reliance vs simple XAI, but participants rated the designs that reduced over-reliance most as least favorable; benefit larger for high need-for-cognition — Buçinca et al., PACMHCI 2021, [arXiv 2102.09692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692).
- Explanations can reduce over-reliance when they lower the cost of verifying the AI; task difficulty, explanation complexity and incentives matter (five studies, N=731, maze task) — Vasconcelos et al., [arXiv 2212.06823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06823).
- First-person natural-language uncertainty ("I'm not sure, but...") in an LLM search engine lowered agreement, lowered confidence, and increased correct answers (preregistered, N=404, medical questions) — Kim et al., FAccT 2024, [arXiv 2405.00623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.00623), [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3630106.3658941).
- N=28 healthcare professionals: high AI confidence raised trust but caused over-reliance and lower accuracy; low confidence lowered agreement and lengthened diagnosis time; some transparency features raised stress [PREPRINT] — [arXiv 2501.16693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16693).
- 220 clinicians, within-subject factorial: ML recommendations did not improve treatment selection on average; incorrect recommendations paired with limited-but-easily-interpretable explanations significantly reduced accuracy vs baseline; high recommender accuracy is not enough — Jacobs et al., Translational Psychiatry 2021, [PubMed 33542191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33542191/).
- LLM advice outside its competence hurts: -19 pp correctness for consultants on an out-of-frontier task even as persuasiveness improved — [SSRN 4573321](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321).
- Professionals ignore flagged advice without adoption support: red safety alerts left unresolved in 35-40% of visits, 20% after champions/coaching/recognition — [arXiv 2507.16947](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16947).
- Supervising an autonomous AI reduced worker engagement in hard escalations; authors raise skill erosion risk for specialized supervisory roles [PREPRINT] — [arXiv 2605.14830](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.14830).
- EASA's maintenance use case (image-based inspection support classified Level 1B) states: "A risk of complacency and over-reliance on the applications exists. Inspectors may be biased in their final decision... and they may not check as thoroughly as today" — [EASA Concept Paper Issue 01](https://www.easa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/dfu/easa_concept_paper_first_usable_guidance_for_level_1_machine_learning_applications_issue_01_1.pdf).
- EU AI Act Art. 14 (high-risk systems) requires enabling overseers to stay aware of "automation bias" and to disregard, override or reverse output — [Art. 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/).
- Do NOT use: a vendor blog asserts "over-trust develops within 60 to 90 days" and "38% of AI-related MRO incidents reviewed by the FAA 2021-2024" cite no primary source (publisher is a CMMS vendor; I checked the page for citations and found none) — [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/aviation-management/human-factors-ai-aviation-maintenance-risks) [UNVERIFIED/likely unreliable].
- KONE reports technician "satisfaction" and says technicians approve every recommendation, but gives no override/acceptance rates — [KONE](https://www.kone.com/global/en/newsroom/stories/technician-assistant-ai-elevator-maintenance.html).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] For a visible-recommendation shadow mode, the most defensible display is: ranked next tests with the posterior over owners and the recommendation's expected information/cost, plus calibrated uncertainty; avoid persuasive narrative text, since fluent explanation increases acceptance regardless of correctness (Bansal) and calibrated confidence alone does not raise accuracy (Zhang).
- [INFERENCE] To measure the technician's independent test choice, log it before revealing the tool's suggestion (commit-then-reveal, a cognitive forcing function) or randomize reveal on/off across cases; otherwise anchoring (Jacobs) contaminates the very "human choice" you want to compare. Expect participants to dislike forcing (Buçinca).
- [INFERENCE] When the tool's posterior contradicts the drawings, that is precisely a high-stakes disagreement slice; log it separately with the technician's reason, and treat drawings-vs-tool conflicts as a stratum, since no published evidence tells us how technicians resolve these.
- [INFERENCE] Novices are likely to defer more (Brynjolfsson heterogeneity; Rezaeian trust results), so record experience level and independently measure prior-belief/agreement with drawings.

### Gaps
- No study found on technicians' reactions to AI advice contradicting drawings/manuals; no deskilling study for maintenance technicians; no technician-specific explanation-format findings. No HVAC-technician trust study.
- Medical-domain over-reliance RCTs on LLMs (e.g., a medRxiv "Automation bias in LLM-assisted diagnostic reasoning among AI-trained physicians") were seen only as titles; page was 403 ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.23.25334280.full.pdf)).

## Q4. SHADOW MODE / offline evaluation: protocols, agreement metrics, logging schemas, and the "human action changes what is observed" problem

### Takeaway
"Silent trial" methodology is well established in clinical AI (frozen model on live data, predictions hidden, staged, then prospective trial), with concrete published examples showing large development-to-silent-trial degradation from data drift and recommended logging/reporting practices (Kwong, STEP, DECIDE-AI, Epic sepsis). Published protocols for comparing a tool's recommended next step with a human's actual next step, especially with test-cost metrics, are scarce; the closest are SDBench cost-accuracy comparison, owner-as-labeler review (Ahmed), and, for the confounding problem, selective-labels and offline-RL OPE literature, which mostly warns about what cannot be identified from logged human decisions.

### Cited Findings

**Silent-trial design and protocols (healthcare CDS)**
- Definition and stages: a frozen AI system runs prospectively on live data in the deployment environment while predictions stay invisible to clinicians and do not influence care; a 3-stage roadmap = exploratory development, silent trial, prospective clinical evaluation — search-result summaries of [npj Digital Medicine 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01506-4) and [PMC10436137](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10436137/) [SNIPPET-ONLY; pages not fetched]. A 2025 scoping review of silent trials exists ([Nature Health](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44360-025-00048-z), title only, not read).
- Kwong et al., Frontiers in Digital Health 2022 [PEER-REVIEWED]: model for infant hydronephrosis surgery risk; clinicians blinded and managed per standard care; Silent Trial 1 (Aug-Dec 2020): 523 kidneys/150 patients; Silent Trial 2: 711 kidneys/202 patients; training 1,643 kidneys/294 patients. AUROC 0.90 in development -> 0.50 (sens 100%, spec 0%) in silent trial 1 -> 0.85 after refinement -> 0.91 in silent trial 2. Causes: age shift (35+-39 vs 61+-92 weeks), laterality shift (42% vs 36% right-sided obstructed), and image format (processed JPEG in training vs unprocessed/PNG). Clinician predictions were not compared with model outputs (blinded). Also logged: time-to-prediction, workflow interruption, user questionnaires; recommendations include running 2+ sequential silent trials, version-controlling preprocessing, conservative sensitivity targets. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2022.929508/full)
- STEP silent-trial engine (pathology AI; three institutions: Mount Sinai, TUM, MSK; EGFR-mutation model) [PREPRINT]: persistent collections for cases/slides, jobs, results (scores, timing, errors), audit events, and trial configuration, so one can reconstruct what was discovered, submitted, completed, failed and later attached; metrics = case-capture rate, inference success/failure, turnaround, data availability, temporal actionability; idempotency to avoid resubmission. — [arXiv 2608.28708](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28708)
- DECIDE-AI reporting guideline (Nature Medicine/BMJ 2022): for early-stage live evaluation of AI decision support at small scale, covering safety and human factors; 17 AI-specific items (28 subitems) + 10 generic; two-round Delphi (123 then 138 experts) + consensus meeting of 16. — [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-022-01772-9) (via search summary)
- Retrospective/vendor-reported performance may not survive local validation: Epic Sepsis Model externally validated on 27,697 patients/38,455 hospitalizations: AUC 0.63, sensitivity 33%, PPV 12%, versus vendor-reported AUC 0.73-0.83 — [JAMA Intern Med](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2781307) (numbers via search summary).

**Aviation / ATM and MLOps shadow deployments**
- SESAR/E-OCVM validation ladder runs from expert workshops through human-in-the-loop simulation to shadow-mode and operational trials, with active shadow-mode trials "usually not until V3" (search summary of EUROCONTROL document; details of agreement metrics not retrieved) — [E-OCVM v3.0 Vol I](https://www.eurocontrol.int/sites/default/files/2019-08/e-ocvm-version-3.0-volume-1.pdf) [SNIPPET-ONLY].
- EASA data-recording objective (EXP-04): record operational data needed to explain the AI system's behaviour post-operation, for (a) safety monitoring within the operator's safety management system (detect deviations, nature/time/severity, trends) and (b) incident investigation (reconstruct the sequence of events); recording starts before/when the system operates and stops after; does not have to be a capability of the AI system itself — [EASA Concept Paper Issue 01](https://www.easa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/dfu/easa_concept_paper_first_usable_guidance_for_level_1_machine_learning_applications_issue_01_1.pdf).
- MLOps shadow-deployment blogs [VENDOR, not validated protocols]: define agreement rate = percentage of cases where the agent reaches the same decision as the human; example success criteria "agreement above 85%" and rollback if "<80% for two consecutive days" — [Brightlume](https://brightlume.ai/blog/shadow-mode-rollouts-ai-agents-pilot-production); fleet predictive maintenance run in shadow mode for 4 weeks logging predictions without triggering work orders — [iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/predictive-maintenance/predictive-maintenance-fleet-management-transportation). Treat thresholds as illustrative only.

**Offline / counterfactual evaluation when humans decide**
- Selective labels problem: observed outcomes exist only for cases where the human decided one way, and unobservables available to the human but not recorded confound comparison; the "contraction" technique estimates a failure-rate-vs-acceptance-rate curve by exploiting heterogeneity among decision-makers (e.g., judges of different leniency) — Lakkaraju et al., KDD 2017, [PMC5958915](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5958915/), [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3097983.3098066).
- Offline RL for sequential clinical decisions: Gottesman et al. (Nature Medicine, Guidelines for RL in healthcare) identify evaluation pitfalls (choice of off-policy evaluation method, insufficient confounding analysis) — [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-018-0310-5) [SNIPPET-ONLY, seven pitfalls per a search summary]; the Komorowski "AI Clinician" advantage was later shown to be partly an artifact of the OPE procedure/discretization (Jeter et al.), and extreme action imbalance (ratios up to 6,400:1 quantified in one 2026 study) makes importance sampling unreliable — search summary of [arXiv 2608.30442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30442) [SNIPPET-ONLY].
- Counterfactual estimators for logged bandit feedback (OPE) and their use in recommenders as "unbiased offline A/B tests" — [RecSys 2021 tutorial](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3460231.3473320). A 2026 paper bounds the causal effect of an updated ML model using prior RCT data plus monotonicity ("counterfactual correctness") assumptions, but does not address human-in-the-loop advice (as I read the abstract) — [arXiv 2607.21806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21806).
- Sequential test-selection comparison against humans with cost: SDBench design (gatekeeper reveals finding only when queried; cost per visit + priced tests; humans and models on identical cases) — [arXiv 2506.22405](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22405).
- Outcome labeling by expert review: Penda (108 blinded physicians, 5-point scale, double rating ~25%) and Ahmed (incident owners, hindsight) — [arXiv 2507.16947](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16947); [arXiv 2301.03797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03797).
- Regulatory classification relevant to a tool that recommends a next test while the human decides: EASA Level 1B ("Human assistance", automation support to decision-making, where "the decision is solely taken by the human end user"); Level 1A = information acquisition/analysis support; Level 2 = overseen/overridable automatic decisions. For maintenance, Level 1 is compatible with the "human signature and explicit release to service" philosophy of Reg. (EU) 1321/2014. — [EASA Concept Paper Issue 01](https://www.easa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/dfu/easa_concept_paper_first_usable_guidance_for_level_1_machine_learning_applications_issue_01_1.pdf). Issue 02 (Level 1 & 2 guidance) published 6 March 2024 — [EASA news](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/newsroom-and-events/news/easa-publishes-artificial-intelligence-concept-paper-issue-2-guidance).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Concrete shadow protocol a small team could copy (assembled from the sources above):
  1. Freeze the model, likelihood tables and data preprocessing; version-stamp every case (Kwong: 0.90 -> 0.50 from a file-format change). Plan two sequential shadow phases and expect the first to expose drift/data-plumbing faults; report per DECIDE-AI items.
  2. Choose the arm structure explicitly: (a) silent (tool hidden; cleanest human baseline; Kwong), (b) visible with commit-then-reveal (log technician's intended next test first), or (c) randomized reveal by technician/crew/case (Penda-style randomization stratified by site) so anchoring can be measured rather than assumed away.
  3. Log schema per step (STEP audit-event style + EASA EXP-04 reconstructability): case/connection ID, candidate-owner set and prior, posterior before/after each observation, tool's full ranked recommendation with EIG, cost, expected posterior shift and stop/futility flags, whether it was displayed, technician's committed choice, final performed test and outcome, timestamps, technician experience level, free-text/structured override reason (esp. when tool contradicts drawings), tool/model version, and eventual verified ground truth.
  4. Metrics: (i) per-step top-1/top-k agreement with tie handling by EIG-equivalence (not string equality); (ii) model-based replay: run the tool's posterior along the technician's realized test sequence and compute the number of tests/cost at which posterior crosses the stop threshold vs. the tool's own recommended sequence (estimated "tests saved") — this is model-dependent because un-run tests have no observed outcome; (iii) cost-weighted accuracy frontier à la SDBench; (iv) time-to-diagnosis; (v) posterior calibration and futility-stop precision on cases with verified ground truth (Epic-style local validation); (vi) stratify by experience and by drawings-contradiction cases.
  5. Confounding: the technician's chosen test decides which evidence exists (selective-labels/OPE problem). Options: restrict inference to steps where the human's realized test equals or is informationally equivalent to the tool's suggestion; use technician heterogeneity in test-selection style (contraction-like) if there are enough technicians; use retrospective full-information cases with an SDBench-style gatekeeper; and finally a randomized "follow-the-tool" arm. Do not draw regret/"tests saved" conclusions from importance-weighted estimates without checking support overlap (action-imbalance warnings from offline RL).
  6. Governance framing: Level 1B under EASA, human signs off; keep the data recording independent of the tool so incidents can be reconstructed.
- [INFERENCE] The published record contains no protocol that reports "expected tests saved" or "regret" for a human-decides workflow; the HVAC study would need to define these itself and justify the model-based replay assumption.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved (search budget exhausted / paywall): detailed silent-mode results for TREWS and Duke Sepsis Watch; published ATC/SESAR shadow-mode agreement metrics; the Nature Health scoping review of silent trials and the npj Digital Medicine framework paper (only snippets); Meta and Microsoft AIOps shadow-deployment papers; any peer-reviewed predictive-maintenance shadow-mode protocol; Google AMIE real-world/feasibility study.
- No source found that specifies agreement-rate thresholds validated against outcomes; the 80/85% figures come from a vendor blog.
- Whether the Nature Medicine follow-up (92 physicians) and DECIDE-AI/Epic numbers match the primary pages is unverified (search summaries).

## Q5. Failure incidents, documented harms/near-misses, and regulatory/safety frameworks

### Takeaway
I found no publicly documented harm or near-miss caused by an LLM maintenance assistant (absence of evidence within a limited search, not evidence of absence). The documented failure modes are adjacent: retrieval assistants that fail to surface manual warnings, LLM advice degrading expert accuracy outside its competence, professionals leaving safety flags unresolved, and vendor-reported external-validation gaps. Regulation for aviation maintenance is the most developed (EASA Level 1/1B guidance); EU AI Act human-oversight rules apply to high-risk systems, which a technician diagnostic aid likely is not by default (legal reading unverified).

### Cited Findings
- No incident found: searches for LLM maintenance assistant hallucination incidents returned benchmarks and frameworks rather than incident reports (see gaps). The one "FAA incidents" statistic encountered is unsourced vendor text — [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/aviation-management/human-factors-ai-aviation-maintenance-risks) [UNVERIFIED].
- DeepTest 2026 tool competition (ICSE 2026 workshop): four tools tried to expose an LLM car-manual retrieval assistant failing "to appropriately mention warnings contained in the manual" — [arXiv 2604.12615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12615). - Cloud on-call engineers' stated risk: an incorrect LLM suggestion could lengthen investigations — [Ahmed et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03797) (in the full text).

EASA reported 900 comments from 34 stakeholders on the consultation — [EASA roadmap slides](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/139582/en).
- EU AI Act Art. 14: human-oversight requirements (automation-bias awareness, ability to override) apply to high-risk systems listed in Annex III — [Art. 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Shadow-mode logging with the human deciding maps to EASA Level 1B ("support to decision-making"), the lowest class that triggers explainability guidance, and its data-recording objective is a ready-made requirement for the log schema in Q4.
- [INFERENCE] Because incident evidence is absent, the shadow phase should itself collect near-miss data: log every case where the tool's recommended test would have been unsafe, skipped a mandatory check, or contradicted drawings, and how the technician handled it; report these separately from efficiency metrics (a "safety-gated" score, cf. AeroCopilotBench's safety-gated evaluation idea, [snippet-only](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16349)).
- [INFERENCE] HVAC/building-services-specific regulation was not researched here; the EASA framework is the most concrete transferable structure, but its legal force is limited to aviation.
