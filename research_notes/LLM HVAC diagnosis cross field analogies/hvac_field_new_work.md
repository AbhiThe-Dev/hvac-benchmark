## Q1. What does the critical review arXiv 2609.05314 say (taxonomy, "industry-ready" roles, missing evidence, documentation/commissioning/topology)?

### Takeaway
The review (Neubauer, Hong, Li et al., 4 Sep 2026) codes **66** studies (not 45; I found no 45-paper version) and finds no "ready-now" role; the most industry-ready roles are bounded, human-in-the-loop *semantic/workflow* roles (point-name cleanup, document-grounded operator/technician support, BEM assistance, MPC/RL advisory), while FDD reasoners are all research-only. Documentation/metadata fragmentation is the review's motivating problem, but it reviews no LLM work on commissioning, as-built reconciliation or topology verification.

### Cited Findings
- **Identity and method** — arXiv:2609.05314v1, 4 Sep 2026, "Preprint submitted to Energy and Buildings, August 31, 2026"; authors Alexander Neubauer (TU Berlin / LBNL), Tianzhen Hong, Han Li (LBNL), Mengbo Yu, Amin Darbandi, Yannick Fürst, Martin Kriegel. Corpus: 66 peer-reviewed studies, Jan 2023–Mar 2026, Scopus-indexed English only, search run 1 Apr 2026; arXiv preprints and grey literature excluded by design; coding by a single rater with no inter-rater statistics [FT]. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05314), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **Taxonomy** — 5 application families: A1 FDD (n=9), A2 load forecasting (3), A3 building energy modelling (32), A4 control/optimisation (15), A5 comfort/occupant (7). 3 method families: M1 prompting and supervised adaptation (ICL, fine-tuning, LoRA), M2 context-grounded orchestration (RAG, tools, memory, agentic), M3 multimodal input. A1 cells: M1=3, M2=6, M3=0 (graphical abstract). Dominant cell A3×M2=15 [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314). (A first fetch-tool summary of the HTML page gave A1 M1=4/M2=5; the PDF text contradicts it, so the PDF values are used.)
- **Evidence-realism scale** — conceptual / simulation-only (30 papers) / retrospective field data (23) / lab study (9) / pilot deployment (4) / operational deployment (0). Lab-coded studies are mostly participant/expert evaluations, not hardware or live-building tests [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **Readiness** — ready-now 0, near-term 3, research-only 63. Ready-now would require sustained operational deployment of a reusable bounded artefact under human oversight [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **The three near-term papers** — (1) Li, Comesana, Weyandt, Hong, RAG data pipeline for heterogeneous building documents, 13 real LBNL buildings, ~30% gain in extraction coverage and structural preservation vs generic baselines, coded "lab study" but validated on operational documents, bounded/human-supervised; (2)+(3) two Sawada et al. Office-in-the-Loop papers (real office, 30 workers, two periods ≈7.5 weeks, 47.9% energy saving in the LLM-only condition, 26.4% comfort gain); the 2025 paper is an offline agentic-reasoning re-analysis of the same deployment, not a second deployment [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **Pilots** — the four pilot-coded papers correspond to three distinct deployments; Jia et al. (LLM-integrated sensor-data protocol, 4K-token limit) is pilot-level but research-only; the fourth pilot paper is not identified in the text I extracted [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **Roles called closest to deployable (Sec. 6.4, Table A6/A7)** — five bounded roles: point-name cleanup and metadata alignment (few-shot mapping to Brick/Haystack; "human validation remains necessary" for spatial/equipment relations); grounded document/knowledge-base support (RAG; "retrieval quality determines output quality"); MPC/RL advisory interfaces ($0.013–0.032 per call; LLM must not override the physics solver); BEM workflow automation (>90% authoring-time reduction; needs qualified review); technician training/procedural O&M ("explanatory, not executive: retrieve and explain trusted procedures; do not generate new maintenance actions") [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **Fault diagnosis positioning (Table 6)** — better default tools are FDD algorithms, rule libraries, expert systems, knowledge graphs; LLM value is "linking alarms, manuals, logs, and work orders into understandable diagnostic narratives"; boundary "recommendation only; technician or engineer acts". "Validate first (research-only)": FDD reasoning without causal-reasoning verification, autonomous multi-step agentic operation, direct closed-loop control without physics guardrails [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **Minimum deployment package (Table 7)** — source grounding over trusted documents; human review before safety-adjacent execution; hard-coded safety constraints outside the LLM; fallback controller/operator override; logging of prompts/sources/outputs; task-specific evaluation on real building data [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **A1 (FDD) corpus, Table A1 (9 papers; 6 retrospective field, 2 lab, 1 simulation; zero pilot; all research-only)** [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
  - Zhang C. et al. 2024, *Energy and Buildings* 305:113877: GPT-4 itemset interpretation on Shenzhen chiller plant, recall 89.17%, $17.68 per run, offline audit only.
  - Zhang J. et al. 2025, *Applied Energy* 377:124378: LoRA-fine-tuned GPT-3.5 + SMOTE, 29.5% → ~100% on ASHRAE RP-1312; >98% on unseen LBNL AHU data; VAV and chiller each needed separate fine-tuning; "correct labels, flawed causal reasoning".
  - Langer et al.: fine-tuned DistilBERT on serialised Modelica series, F1 82–99%, simulation data only.
  - Babaei Ravandi & El-Diraby, EC3 2025: GPT-3.5 multi-agent facility-management workflow with knowledge-graph data integration; depends on work-order completeness.
  - Qiu et al. 2025: LLM-guided coding-free virtual flowmeter, metro chilled water, RMSE 8.84–9.05 m3/h, single building.
  - Chen Y-T et al. 2026, *Results in Engineering* 29:109461: Re2G chiller expert on manuals, +23.5% retrieval relevancy, −31.2% hallucinations vs standard RAG, lab study.
  - Deng et al. 2026, *Building and Environment* 289:114056: graph-constrained association-rule mining + knowledge-enhanced LLM, 89.1% accuracy (best of five LLMs, GPT-4o+RAG) on real large-office data, candidate rule space cut >95%, single building, 2–5 s per inference.
  - Chen S. et al. 2025, *Applied Energy* 393:126169: customized model for human-AI O&M collaboration, 96.3% fault-diagnosis accuracy, lab study, tool-development burden high.
  - Xiao & Xu 2024, *Energy and Buildings* 322:114691: multi-agent optimisation from unstructured data, RAG retrieval F1 0.8894, ~$5 per audit, run-to-run variation in recommendations.
- **Failure modes FM1–FM6 (Sec. 6.5)** — FM1 reasoning brittleness (right label for wrong reason; run-to-run variation); FM2 semantic hallucination in generated logic (silently wrong pipeline code); FM3 "executable but wrong" (BEM); FM4 plausible-but-unfaithful explanations; FM5 latency incompatible with real-time control; FM6 proprietary-model dependence / silent API updates / data residency. Standard metrics (accuracy, F1, R2) do not expose FM1–FM4 [FT]. Note the review's Sec. 4.1 uses FM numbering inconsistently with Sec. 6.5 (e.g., knowledge-base dependency labelled FM2, heat-pump "plausible but physically wrong" labelled FM4). — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **Missing evidence the review names** — no sustained operational deployment anywhere; zero heat-pump FDD papers; only five papers report API cost; not one paper evaluates on-premise/federated deployment under real data-governance constraints; no formal LLM–MPC/RL integration with verifiable safety; no portfolio-scale field-validated benchmarks; A1 evaluations rely on curated manuals/ontologies/annotated datasets, giving "limited evidence" they persist where documentation is "incomplete, inconsistent, or outdated"; only 13/66 papers meet "experimental or field realism" (and mostly lab); prompt-injection/input-provenance safeguards for ingested manuals and work orders are rarely designed [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **Documentation and metadata quality (verified passages)** — abstract and intro: BAS data are "insight-poor because heterogeneous point naming, missing metadata, and fragmented documentation obstruct their operational use"; buildings are "decades old, repeatedly retrofitted, and insufficiently documented, so integration effort frequently exceeds the projected savings"; the LLM's proposed role is "semantic interface between documents, operators, tools, and HVAC workflows". A3 "operation and maintenance model updating": energy models decay after retrofits, sensor replacements, setpoint changes and "undocumented control-sequence edits"; evidence needed to update sits in "work orders, commissioning notes, BAS trend logs, manuals"; early LLM-agent prototypes recalibrate virtual models and sensors from BAS data, metadata and design documents, but "current evidence remains thin" and is a "future-facing extension". A3 risk: workflows may "encode incorrect geometry, schedules, HVAC relationships, calibration assumptions, or semantic mappings" while still executing; evidence that outputs preserve "HVAC topology, metadata relationships" across buildings is limited; many workflows "assume unusually clean documentation". Brick tagging (Ababsa et al., F1 92–98%, three real buildings + one synthetic) is research-only; relation accuracy for complex spatial relationships "remains only partial". Table A7 guardrail for asset managers: "ontology alignment check; spot-check audit trail" [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **Explicit gaps in what the review covers** — the word "commissioning" appears only as "commissioning notes" (a data source); "as-built" does not appear; no application family for commissioning, as-built reconciliation, sequence-of-operations audit, or topology verification; none of Clauß et al., Liu–Zhou–Li, PILLM, JARVIS, Buildrix, BrickLLM, ThermoLLM, Puktalae et al. is cited (text search of the extracted PDF found no hits) [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **Other** — mentions an ASHRAE MTG on generative AI, and cites five SKKU/Yoon-group papers (GPT-based / agent-based digital twin, Brick virtual-sensor metadata schema) as A3 metadata/digital-twin work [FT]. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)

### Inferences
- **Review → your problem.** The review's "closest to industry-ready" roles sit upstream of verification (making metadata legible), so LLM-based *verification* of documented connections is off its map. The correct prior is "no field evidence", and your system occupies an unmapped cell.
- **Review → your architecture.** Its Table 6 puts rule libraries/FDD algorithms/knowledge graphs as the better default for diagnosis with the LLM as narrative linker, and FM1 (right label, wrong reason) is the argument against letting an LLM own the posterior. Both match a design where the EIG/Bayesian layer decides and the LLM only proposes/explains.
- **Absence of Clauß/Liu/PILLM etc.** follows from Scopus-only scope and the 1 Apr 2026 search date (Liu et al. went online 21 Mar 2026), so the review understates 2025–26 agentic-FDD work; the reverse also holds, the newest agentic systems below have not been through its readiness filter.

### Gaps
- Supplementary file S1 (per-paper coding of all 66) not accessed; identity of the fourth pilot paper unresolved.
- The "45 papers" figure from the brief could not be matched; v1 says 66.

## Q2. Is there ANY LLM work on commissioning, as-built vs as-designed reconciliation, SOO/point-list auditing, metadata-quality scoring, Brick/Haystack validation, topology inference/verification, digital-twin consistency?

### Takeaway
Topic by topic: commissioning — none found; as-built reconciliation — none found (only statements that it is a manual practice and a good target for automated inference); SOO/point-list auditing against drawings — none found; metadata-quality scoring — none found for LLMs, only metadata *creation/tagging/query* (thin, mostly 2025–26); Brick validation — SHACL is non-LLM, LLM work targets querying and generation; topology inference/verification — no HVAC LLM work found, and VLM work on drawings→graph elsewhere shows weak edge accuracy; digital-twin consistency — closest is LLM-agent recalibration of a virtual model (Yoon group), not structural consistency checking.

### Inferences
- **All LLM→KG work assumes the KG is true.** BuildingQA/BrickQA/Build2SPARQL measure whether the LLM retrieves what the graph says, not whether the graph matches the plant. The documentation-mismatch problem is therefore a hidden precondition of this literature (and of the Puktalae agent's backward graph traversal).
- **Drawing/document extraction adds its own error.** If documented topology is itself LLM/VLM-extracted from drawings, edge-level accuracy in the general-diagram benchmark is only ~33–58 F1, so extraction confidence should be carried into the prior over "documented owner correct" rather than treated as certain (consistent with the earlier finding that LLM-elicited priors are overconfident).
- **Closest published framings of your task** are non-LLM or aspirational: ITcon's "validate delivered metadata against as-built conditions" and Puktalae's proposal that agents "flag any information they cannot reconcile with the observed data before acting".

## Q3. Commercial / industry LLM copilots for building FDD and technicians: what is claimed, and is any of it independently evidenced?

### Takeaway
Every vendor LLM feature found is an explainer/advisor/chat layer over the vendor's existing analytics or documents (RAG over manuals, KG-grounded chat, "tell me more" explanations); none publishes an independent evaluation of the LLM component, and analyst coverage (Memoori) says deployment data for the gen-AI layers are absent. No vendor claims that an LLM selects diagnostic tests or verifies documentation.

## Q4. New (2025–26) academic LLM-agent work that calls FDD tools, queries a building KG, or proposes next diagnostic steps: role, evaluation, failure modes

### Takeaway
The strongest and most relevant new result is Puktalae et al. (UCL/Tacit Analytics, Sep 2026): a deterministic rule engine supplies evidence and an LLM agent investigates through SPARQL and Python; on a real building the ungrounded agent invents faults (false positives), the evidence-triggered agent does not. Elsewhere evaluations are lab/retrospective, and no paper has an LLM propose physical tests via information gain.

### Cited Findings
- **Puktalae, Giudice, Kallioras, Mavrokapnidis, Rovas — "Knowledge graph-based AI Agents for HVAC FDD: impact of physical grounding" (UCL / Tacit Analytics; ECPPM 2026; Zenodo 2026-09-09)** [FT; the PDF is the reviewer-filtered conference version, final may differ]. — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/22679910), [PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22679910/files/ECPPM2026_201.pdf)
  - *LLM role*: investigator/orchestrator (ReAct loop), not detector: a "semantic rule engine" first produces daily evidence; the agent then runs `sparql_query` on a Brick knowledge graph and `run_python` (whitelisted pandas/numpy, stateless) to confirm or refine it into diagnoses drawn from a predefined fault catalog, once per unit per day.
  - *Evidence layer (non-LLM)*: six Guideline-36-inspired rules on SAT/RAT and valve/fan commands with a 1 °C threshold; each rule gated by SHACL shapes (a manifest) so it runs only where required sensors exist; evaluated every 15 min, aggregated daily (triggered if active ≥3 consecutive steps in an operating mode).
  - *Evaluation*: real London university building; 48 active of 102 fan-coil units; April 2025; labels agreed by authors, facility managers and an existing FDD tool; authors state the label set is partial (unnoticed faults may exist). Two LLMs (Claude Opus 4.7, open-source GLM-4), each with vs without rule evidence. Known events: four-day chiller fault (11–14 Apr, ~20 °C supply water), units running around the clock, a cooling-valve stuck-open around 17 Apr.
  - *Results*: both models generate more faults without evidence than with it "even across 497 days with at least one rule triggered"; ungrounded agents "very often invent additional" faults, up to ~30% more for GLM-4; e.g., the ungrounded Opus run flagged "inefficient control" seven times on a fan that is not designed to modulate, plus heating-valve leakage and SAT bias on two other days; efficiency gains (tokens, tool calls) are model-dependent (Opus up to 4× fewer tool calls than GLM-4; GLM-4 ~10× cheaper). Accuracy figures are in images (Figs 2–4), not text; case studies are qualitative.
  - *Not shown / failure modes*: no controlled accuracy table in the text; only two models; diagnosis flips between equally plausible faults on consecutive days; agents tend to report only the most impactful of concurrent faults; rules still produce false positives during transients; the knowledge graph "can never encode all knowledge" (ontologies emphasise topology over attributes); the KG topology is assumed correct (the chiller case requires traversing the graph backwards); alert fatigue is named as the main deployment risk (citing Diallo et al. 2026); no physical test selection (auto-commissioning only suggested).
  - *Future-work claims relevant to documentation*: link manuals/datasheets to equipment as external references; keep only human-confirmed fault memory with timestamps/versions; for retrieved documentation "track where each retrieved fact comes from ... and flag any information it cannot reconcile with the observed data before acting".
- **Clauß et al. (SINTEF, 2025) and Liu–Zhou–Li (2026)** — see Q5 and Q6.
- **Yoon / SKKU group**: Li et al. (JII 2025) GPT-4o agent + digital twin + predefined toolkits, twin RMSE 1.04→0.30 °C after correcting an introduced sensor bias on a real lab HVAC system [FT via review]; Yoon, Song, Li (JBE 2025) Brick + callable FDD/energy/virtual-sensing apps for an LLM agent, bounded to preprocessed data, one building, one HVAC scope [FT via ITcon]. — [review PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314), [ITcon PDF](http://www.itcon.org/papers/2026_43-ITcon-Elias.pdf). Puktalae et al. describe another Li et al. "agentic FDD" framework (ontology + generalized diagnosis rules covering actuator/sensor/load/plant/setpoint faults, validated on a real HVAC system, agent mainly interprets rule results and writes reports/maintenance suggestions); I could not confirm the exact source paper (their in-text citation [19] points to Liu et al.) [SUM/SNIP]. — [Zenodo PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22679910/files/ECPPM2026_201.pdf)
- **Wei Zhang et al., "A Multi-Stage Post-Training Framework for Domain-Specific Language Models in Fault Diagnosis" (*AI* 7(9):383, 20 Sep 2026)**: RAG + supervised fine-tuning + RL with rewards on root-cause identification and action recommendation; real HVAC equipment data from Ningbo Rail Transit; Qwen3-0.6B base gives +97% diagnostic accuracy and +48% expert-evaluation score, outperforming SVM/RF/MLP; N and split not visible in the abstract [ABS]. Role: classifier plus action recommender. — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.3390/ai7090383)
- **Chujie Lu et al. (KU Leuven / TU Delft) — LLM as elicitor of Bayesian diagnostic structure**: "Retrieval-Augmented LLM Framework for Robust Automated Generation of HVAC Diagnostic Models" (ACM Sustainability Week, 20 Jun 2026): retrieval-augmented LLM automates construction of diagnostic Bayesian networks; "preliminary results" show it mitigates hallucinations and improves structural quality of generated DBNs [ABS]. Companion papers seen by title only: "Towards Automated Generation of Diagnostic Bayesian Networks for HVAC Systems Using LLMs: A Feasibility Study" (SSRN 2026) and "A Hybrid Fuzzy Bayesian Network with LLM-Assisted Knowledge Elicitation for FDD in AHUs" (SSRN 2026) [TITLE]. — [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3765611.3815346), [SSRN feasibility](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7211771), [SSRN fuzzy BN](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7309201)
- **Zhengyang Wang et al., "Sample-efficient LLM fault diagnosis for HVAC systems via active sampling and SHAP-guided evidence condensation" (*Applied Energy*, online Jul 2026, Southeast University)** [TITLE]; abstract inaccessible, so what is actively sampled and how is unverified. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2026.128381)
- **Zhang J. et al., LightLLM4FDD (*Advanced Engineering Informatics*, 11 May 2026)**: distilled lightweight LLMs for HVAC FDD, said (by Puktalae et al.) to run locally on consumer hardware [TITLE/secondary]. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aei.2026.104794)
- **Human–AI O&M assistants**: Chen S. et al. (2025) 96.3% fault-diagnosis accuracy in a lab study and Chen Y-T et al. (2026) Re2G chiller expert on manuals (details under Q1); both keep a human technician deciding [FT via review]. — [review PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)
- **DiagnosticIQ (De Silva et al., arXiv 2605.08614, May 2026)** — industrial (whether HVAC assets are included is unverified): benchmark of LLMs translating symbolic monitoring rules into technician maintenance actions; 6,690 expert-validated multiple-choice questions from 118 rule–action pairs across 16 asset types; 29 LLMs; 9 practitioners averaged 45.0%; every model loses 13–60% relative accuracy under distractor expansion; under condition inversion models still pick the original answer 49–63% of the time; authors conclude "the deployment bottleneck is not capability but calibration" [ABS]. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08614)
- **LLM as data-access layer for agents**: BrickQA / BuildingQA / Build2SPARQL (Q2) [ABS].

### Inferences
- **Puktalae et al. → your system.** A cheap deterministic evidence layer gating the LLM (their SHACL-gated rules ≈ your regression-conditioned consistency statistics) is what cut false positives; the ungrounded agent generated extra hypotheses. This supports using the LLM as hypothesis generator/explainer only behind a physically constrained candidate set (your "documented owner plus physically plausible alternatives") and keeping stopping/selection in the EIG layer.
- **Puktalae et al.'s KG assumption** (topology trusted while tracing a chiller fault to fan-coil units) is exactly what your verifier tests; a wrong documented edge would silently mis-direct such an agent, so verified-topology posteriors are a natural input to LLM agents rather than a competitor to them.
- **Chujie Lu's DBN elicitation** is the closest published use of an LLM for priors/structure in Bayesian HVAC diagnosis; results are "preliminary", consistent with the earlier finding that LLM-elicited priors beat uniform but are overconfident, so any use should temper or cap LLM-elicited priors.
- **DiagnosticIQ** (brittle under distractors, pattern-matching under inversion) reinforces not letting an LLM choose actions or tests directly.
- **Wei Zhang, Chen, Deng, Zhang J.** show LLMs as classifiers/action recommenders reaching high accuracy on curated or single-building data; none tests documentation being wrong, so none transfers beyond "LLM can narrate/suggest given a trustworthy diagnosis".
- **Nobody found** has an LLM choose between physical tests by information gain per cost; Puktalae's "exercise the valve" remark is the only nod toward test actions.

### Gaps
- Numeric results for Puktalae et al. (Figs 2–4) and the abstracts of the Wang (active sampling), Lu (SSRN), LightLLM4FDD and Yoon-group papers were not accessible; DiagnosticIQ asset list not checked for HVAC coverage.
- The WebSearch budget ran out, so discovery of other 2026 agentic-FDD preprints (e.g., beyond OpenAlex-indexed items) may be incomplete.

## Q5. What is now confirmable about Clauß et al. 2025 (SINTEF, EIA Nordic, DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-03098-6_9)?

### Takeaway
Confirmed: it is a single-room GPT-4o prototype that reasons over a pre-assembled snapshot (SAREF/TFM-based Neo4j graph + InfluxDB time series) and was "evaluated" on four injected scenarios with dashboard screenshots and no quantitative metrics. ### Cited Findings

An LLM (GPT-4o) processes this data to detect anomalies and generate contextual explanations and recommended actions in natural language. One exemplary room serves as the primary testbed, showcasing how knowledge-driven reasoning enables accurate, actionable alerts with reduced false positives. - **Architecture:** as far as the accessible text shows, the LLM itself does the anomaly judgement from an assembled snapshot; no separate rule engine or discrete FDD tool is described, unlike Puktalae et al. Whether Cypher is LLM-generated or a fixed template is not stated (unverified).

## Q6. What is now confirmable about Liu–Zhou–Li 2026 (DOI 10.1080/17452007.2026.2647805)?

### Takeaway
Confirmable: a three-agent (Planner/Executor/Reporter) LLM framework that turns textual expert rules into executable diagnostic tasks, runs quantitative analyses with iterative self-correction, and reports VAV health; evaluated on 29 VAV boxes with "complete consistency" against manual labels and better than a monolithic-LLM baseline. Not confirmable: the LLM(s) used, data source, fault types, exact rule set, and whether any fixed FDD tool library or rule engine exists (the abstract implies the Executor produces analyses by itself with self-correction).

### Cited Findings
- **Bibliographic** — Deli Liu, Xiaoping Zhou (corresponding), Yu Li, Beijing University of Civil Engineering and Architecture; "Large language model-driven multi-agent framework for fault detection and diagnostics of variable air volume boxes"; *Architectural Engineering and Design Management* 22(3):791–808; received 13 Dec 2025, accepted 13 Mar 2026, online 21 Mar 2026, print 4 May 2026; closed access; Crossref lists 53 references (OpenAlex 47). — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/17452007.2026.2647805), [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1080/17452007.2026.2647805), [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17452007.2026.2647805)
- **Abstract (reconstructed from OpenAlex inverted index; matches the T&F search snippet)** — the study "proposes a large language model (LLM)-driven multi-agent framework that performs health inspection of VAV terminal units through structured expert knowledge reasoning rather than supervised learning… developed under the GBF (Gaia–BDI–FIPA) architecture, which defines three cooperating agents – the Planner, Executor, and Reporter… The Planner interprets expert rules into executable diagnostic tasks, the Executor performs quantitative analyses with iterative self-correction, and the Reporter integrates verified results into interpretable health assessments. This design transforms static textual knowledge into adaptive, data-driven diagnostics capable of dynamic reasoning validation. A case study with 29 VAV boxes demonstrates the proposed framework achieves complete consistency with manually labeled ground truths and significantly outperforms a monolithic LLM baseline in task completeness, execution reliability, and diagnostic accuracy." [ABS]. — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1080/17452007.2026.2647805)
- **Independent paraphrase** — Puktalae et al. describe it as an "explicitly agentic design" in which a planner translates expert rules into executable diagnostic tasks, an executor does quantitative analyses with iterative self-correction, a reporter assembles verified results into a VAV health assessment, and "turning static textual knowledge into adaptive diagnostics that outperform a single-LLM baseline" [FT]. — [Zenodo PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22679910/files/ECPPM2026_201.pdf)
- **Not in the Neubauer review** — no citation found in the extracted review text (Scopus-only scope; search date 1 Apr 2026, about ten days after online publication) [FT]. — [review PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05314)

### Inferences
- **Architecture (inference, unverified):** the Executor "quantitative analyses with iterative self-correction" most plausibly means LLM-written analysis code executed and repaired on error, with rules coming from textual expert knowledge; that would make it a rule-to-code compiler and reporter rather than a discrete-FDD-tool orchestrator. I cannot confirm whether any pre-built tool library or rule engine exists.
- **Evidence strength:** "complete consistency" on 29 boxes against manual labels, with a monolithic-LLM baseline, is a small-N retrospective/case-study result; it says the multi-agent decomposition beats a single LLM prompt, not that it resists documentation errors (the VAV-to-zone mapping is presumably taken as given).
- **Transfer to documentation mismatch:** the useful pattern is verification of LLM-generated analyses (Executor self-correction, Reporter over "verified results") as a check on the LLM layer itself, complementing a statistical layer that decides.

### Gaps
- Full text not accessible (T&F 403; no OA copy; ScienceDirect/ResearchGate not applicable); LLM model, dataset provenance, fault taxonomy, baseline definition, metrics, and any limitations section unverified.
