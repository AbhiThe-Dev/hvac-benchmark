# Quantitative field evidence on hot-water / recirculation faults and performance (priors, check reliability, durations, physics)

Grading key used on every bullet: **PEER-REVIEWED** (journal or refereed proceedings), **GOVT-REPORT** (DOE lab, state agency, utility-program or public-agency report), **INDUSTRY-REPORT** (consultant, trade or utility-contractor report, slide decks), **VENDOR** (manufacturer/seller material), **SNIPPET-ONLY** (seen only in a search-result summary or an abstract page; full text not read by me). Where I read the full text I say "full text read". Where a number is a secondary quotation (paper A quoting study B), I say so and the grade is that of the paper I actually read, with the caveat that the primary was not retrieved.

Geography is labeled on every bullet: US-CA (California), US-NW (Pacific Northwest), US-NYC, US-NY/NJ/CA mix, CA-QC (Quebec, Canada), ES (Spain), DK (Denmark), PL (Poland), UK, AU.

Headline caution for the report writer: **I found no public labeled-fault dataset, no failure-rate (MTBF / field failure %) data for recirculation pumps, thermostatic mixing valves, check valves, aquastats or heat exchangers in DHW service, and no time-on-task data for plumbers or engineers.** The strongest quantitative anchors for cause priors are (1) crossover in 52% of 100 California multifamily buildings, (2) the CDC outbreak-deficiency tallies, (3) the Quebec five-system temperature diagnostic, and (4) the Barcelona 8-year hospital temperature-vs-positivity study. These are anchors for expert elicitation, not a replacement for it.

## 1. Field studies and monitoring campaigns of central DHW with recirculation: which faults, how often?

### Takeaway
The best-quantified prevalence number is cold/hot **crossover (failed shower/faucet mixing cartridges) in 52% of 100 California multifamily buildings** (industry/CEC-funded, tested with a pressure-gauge method). Field recirculation heat loss runs 1.1 to 2.3 times the idealized calculation (California) and UA multipliers of 1.35 and 1.95 in two Seattle buildings, attributed to insulation gaps, crossover, imbalance and mixing-valve/pump mis-commissioning, but **no study found tallied "cause of complaint" frequencies or return-temperature distributions across many loops**.

### Cited Findings

**Crossover prevalence (strongest single prior)**
- Crossover (cold water entering the hot system through a worn shower/faucet mixing cartridge, driven by a hot/cold pressure imbalance) was detected in **52% of 100 multifamily buildings** in California. The sample was chosen to mirror California building size, age and region (census-based), but the authors say 100 was "relatively arbitrary" and "not a statistically significant sample size." No building characteristic (pipe material, water quality, valve type, age decade) reliably predicted crossover; a slight correlation with building size was seen (more valves = more chances for one to fail). The abstract describes it as "over 50%." Symptoms listed: sudden rapid temperature fluctuations (most common), lukewarm water, no hot water, long waits; but "many times these issues are asymptomatic." [Ayala & Zobrist, ACEEE Summer Study 2016, "Testing For and Determining the Prevalence of Crossover in a Multifamily Central DHW System" — full text read; PEER-REVIEWED (conference proceedings), US-CA, n=100 multifamily buildings, CEC-funded](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2016/data/papers/1_59.pdf)
- Same paper, secondary citations (not verified against primaries): crossover may be 5-10% of total multifamily hot water energy (Sachs et al. 2011); a 104-unit community that replaced all shower cartridges reported savings of $23,328 or 62% (Pfaff 2009, an anecdotal single case); two of three PIER-monitored buildings showed signs of crossover (Howlett & Stone 2007); recirculation pump controls alone can cut loop heat losses by over 50% and hot water gas use by 15% (Bonneville 2010). [same paper](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2016/data/papers/1_59.pdf)
- Same paper reports the crossover table by building vintage (e.g., 1900-10: 1 of 1; 1910-20: 1 of 3 with 1 unknown; 1920-30: 0 of 3; 1940-50: 0 of 5), n too small per cell for use. [same paper](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2016/data/papers/1_59.pdf)

**Field loss vs idealized calculation**
- California survey of multifamily recirculation (project ET24SWE0061, 2024-2026): recirculation loss per apartment **24.1 to 99.9 W, mean 62.5 W**; field-measured losses **1.1 to 2.3 times** idealized calculations; the team attributes this mainly to insulation imperfections and cold-water crossover; "no clear correlation" with building characteristics, so each building needs quantitative and qualitative assessment. Number of sites is not stated on the project page ("multifamily sites across California"); the full report is behind a download form. [ETCC project page — GOVT-REPORT (utility emerging-tech program), page read; US-CA](https://etcc-ca.com/reports/multifamily-domestic-hot-water-recirculation-survey)
- Two monitored Seattle multifamily buildings with manually balanced risers and constant-speed pumps: Building A (384 apts, digital mixing valve, supply 120-127 F, usage 117-122 F) needed a **UA multiplier of 1.35** to match observed temperature-maintenance loss to the as-designed calculation; Building B (71 apts, mixing valve "much too large" and not installed per manufacturer, "extremely variable supply temperatures," a pipe run through an unconditioned garage, uninsulated hangers) needed **1.95** (1.53 in a worst-case assumption). Delivery heat loss was under 10% of total distribution heat loss. A simplified calculator matches the authors' nodal simulation within 5%. Interviews/photos list unquantified loss sources: uninsulated hangers and riser supports, penetrations through joists, insulated circulated branches feeding uninsulated uncirculated branches (natural convection), re-routed pipe, ventilated spaces, "improperly commissioned or ill-maintained pumps and mixing valves," "imperfect branch balancing." [Ecotope for BPA/WSU, "Domestic Hot Water Distribution Heat Loss," March 2022 — full text read; GOVT-REPORT, US-NW, n=2 buildings, several weeks monitoring](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/energy-efficiency/emerging-technologies/20220506-DHW-Distribution-Heat-Loss---Task-7.pdf)
- Distribution heat loss is about **30% of total domestic water heating** in typical multifamily buildings (same report). Observed instantaneous heat loss showed large transient spikes after draws (supply temperature overshoot before the mixing valve readjusts), so only multi-hour averages are usable. [same report](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/energy-efficiency/emerging-technologies/20220506-DHW-Distribution-Heat-Loss---Task-7.pdf)
- Zhang (2013, CEC-500-2013-011), 28 multifamily buildings (11-250 units, 2-5 stories, California): average DHW system efficiency **35%**, average recirculation heat-loss fraction **33%**, remaining ~32% mostly water-heater losses. I read this only as a quotation in the 2024 ACEEE paper; the CEC primary was not retrieved (searches did not surface it). [ACEEE 2024 (Zeyghami & Zhang), full text read; PEER-REVIEWED (conference) quoting a GOVT-REPORT; US-CA](https://aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Best%20Practices%20for%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20Systems%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings-A%20Comparative%20Evaluation%20of%20Balancing%20Methods.pdf)
- Same paper quotes Bøhm (2013, Energy Conversion & Management, 13 Danish apartment buildings + 2 offices/institutions): **23-70% of the heat supplied to each apartment is lost in the recirculation/distribution system**. Attribution is inconsistent across sources (a Zaragoza thesis credits the same range to Bøhm & Danig 2004); DTU's abstract says "two institutions" not "two offices." [ACEEE 2024 as above](https://aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Best%20Practices%20for%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20Systems%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings-A%20Comparative%20Evaluation%20of%20Balancing%20Methods.pdf); [DTU Orbit record SNIPPET-ONLY, DK](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/production-and-distribution-of-domestic-hot-water-in-selected-dan/)
- Ayala & Zobrist (2012), quoting Heschong Mahone Group estimates: of CDHW input energy ~35% reaches the tap, **33% is lost in the recirculation loop**, 1% in other distribution pipes. [ACEEE Summer Study 2012, full text read via PDF; PEER-REVIEWED (conference), US](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2012/data/papers/0193-000030.pdf)
- Same paper quotes a KEMA (2008) inspection of "hundreds of water heating systems": **70% had build-up in tank-type heaters, 50% dirty burner tips, 10% of pumps had failed, 80% had uninsulated piping**. Secondary quotation only; building types, geography and sample size not given. [same paper](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2012/data/papers/0193-000030.pdf)

**Faults in lab / instrumented systems (useful as fault signatures)**
- Quebec hospital/health-care hot water systems, 5 systems: a **broken faucet return valve in a shower faucet let cold water into the hot feed and riser; fixing it raised minimum temperature by an average of 5 C across all 10 subordinate risers** in that wing. In another wing, insufficient recirculation caused significant night-time heat loss, fixed by adding a local pump on the subordinate return loop. [Bédard et al. 2015, Water Research 71:244-256, "Temperature diagnostic to identify high risk areas…" — accepted manuscript full text read; PEER-REVIEWED, CA-QC, n=5 health-care HWDS](https://espace.inrs.ca/id/eprint/3147/1/Bedard2015_Manuscript_Temperature%20Diagnostic.pdf)
- A faulty temperature-sensor installation (riser 9) appeared even in PG&E's lab (a reminder that sensor faults are part of the observed data). [ACEEE 2024 lab study, full text read; PEER-REVIEWED (conference), US-CA](https://aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Best%20Practices%20for%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20Systems%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings-A%20Comparative%20Evaluation%20of%20Balancing%20Methods.pdf)
- Monitoring of central heat pump water heaters (Davis Energy Group, Oct 2011-Jan 2013): problems uncovered included a compressor not running while circulating pump and fan made the unit look operational, mis-placed sensors, non-optimal setpoints (140 F heat-pump upper limit with 5 F deadband, 120 F resistance, 120 F tempering valve feeding the recirculation loop), and early evaporator-fan failure. Counts not given. [DOE Building America webinar page, SNIPPET-ONLY, US-CA](https://www.energy.gov/eere/buildings/articles/building-america-webinar-central-multifamily-water-heating-systems)

**Return-temperature / balancing behavior (lab, PG&E, full text read)**
- Full-scale lab loop for a 36-unit, 3-story, 12-riser building; 125 F mixing-valve outlet, 70 F ambient. With manual balancing at the highest recirculation flow (about 0.5 gpm/riser) risers were within **2-5 F** of each other; reducing total recirculation flow ~50% (level 6 to 3) kept all riser tops above 120 F. For all valve types, flow **above 0.25 gpm/riser** gave satisfactory riser temperature distribution; **below 0.25** some valves showed large discrepancies; **below 0.1 gpm/riser none** could balance. Only 1 of 4 thermostatic balancing valves actually regulated flow to a setpoint; automatic-flow valves lost control at low pump flow because the far risers lose differential pressure. Best valve cut distribution loss up to **12%** vs manual balancing at 0.5 gpm/riser; 7% was achieved just by lowering flow with the same valve type. Pump speed control had little effect. Manual valves required a time-consuming initial setup and gave the most uniform temperatures when set up with cold flow. [ACEEE 2024, PEER-REVIEWED (conference), US-CA lab, 7 valves](https://aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Best%20Practices%20for%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20Systems%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings-A%20Comparative%20Evaluation%20of%20Balancing%20Methods.pdf)
- NYC Brooklyn, two ~50-apartment buildings, controls retrofit (NREL Building America case study, ARIES): DHW fuel use fell **7%** with demand control, **2%** with temperature modulation, **15%** combined; recirculation pump runtime dropped to ≤14 min/day. A search summary says demand control runs the pump only on draw and when the loop has cooled below 100 F (not confirmed on the page I read). [NREL/OSTI 1335801, GOVT-REPORT, US-NYC, n=2](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1335801)
- Time-clock/aquastat recirculation controls cause delayed hot water during off periods, and "occupant complaints often lead building managers to bypass or remove these controls." [MN CEE report page, GOVT-REPORT/utility program (summary page only; no numbers), US-MN](https://www.mncee.org/final-report-evaluation-new-dhw-system-controls-hospitality-and-commercial-buildings)
- Ayala & Zobrist 2012: turning up the setpoint "can relieve the symptoms" while hiding causes such as tank build-up, a failing circulation pump or a faulty shower mixing cartridge. [ACEEE 2012](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2012/data/papers/0193-000030.pdf)

### Inferences
- Crossover (worn cartridge/check) is a high-base-rate latent fault in multifamily (about half of buildings), often asymptomatic, so as a cause of "lukewarm/fluctuating/low return temperature" its prior should be high and its prior of being *the* complaint cause should be lower than its prevalence (many buildings carry it without complaints). The 52% is "present in at least one valve," not "causing this complaint."
- The two Seattle UA multipliers (1.35, 1.95) and California 1.1-2.3 give a plausible lognormal-ish range for "unmodeled loss" that a simulator can sample, with insulation gaps/crossover/transient mixing-valve behavior as drivers.
- Lab thresholds (0.25 gpm/riser adequate, 0.1 gpm/riser failure, 2-5 F riser spread when balanced) are directly usable for simulator calibration of "balanced vs imbalanced riser."

### Gaps
- No study found reporting the **proportion of loops with return temperature below target (e.g., below 120 or 124 F)**, the proportion of **risers unbalanced**, or the proportion of **mixing valves out of calibration** in multifamily or commercial field populations. The California statewide survey (ET24SWE0061) probably has such data (including crossover and imbalance) but its full report is gated; the 2026 ACEEE Summer Study papers were not retrievable (the fetched URLs returned proceedings listing pages).
- Zhang 2013 (CEC-500-2013-011) and Bøhm 2013 primaries were not retrieved.
- No cause-of-complaint tally for hot-water callbacks (e.g., "of N no-hot-water calls, x% were pump, y% were crossover…"). Not found.
- No Ecotope/WSU/UC Davis WCEC/NYSERDA/Bonneville field fault-frequency tables located beyond the BPA two-building study.

## 2. Component failure statistics (mixing valves, recirculation pumps, check valves, aquastats/sensors, elements, heat exchangers)

### Takeaway
**No usable failure-rate data (MTBF, annual failure %, warranty-claim rates) were found for any of these components in DHW service.** The only quantitative scraps are median service-life tables, a vendor valve-life statement, one secondary "10% of pumps had failed" survey figure, and qualitative audit findings. These should be elicited from experts.

### Cited Findings
- Pumps: ASHRAE service-life table reproductions list median service life of **pipe-mounted pumps 10 years, base-mounted 20 years** (one other source says 25), sump/well 10, condensate 15. The reproductions do not say whether a DHW circulator is "pipe-mounted"; that mapping is my inference. These are opinion-survey medians reproduced on secondary sites, not field failure data. [ASHRAE equipment life chart (secondary reproduction), SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.shipleyenergy.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/ASHRAE_Chart_HVAC_Life_Expectancy.pdf); [Maine West MEP document (25-year base-mounted claim), SNIPPET-ONLY](https://maine207.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Maine_West_Combined_MEP.pdf)
- A KEMA (2008) inspection quoted via Ayala & Zobrist found **10% of pumps had failed** among hundreds of water heating systems (population unspecified). [ACEEE 2012, secondary quotation](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2012/data/papers/0193-000030.pdf)
- Pump warranties: Bell & Gossett NBF 3 years, Taco 0034ePlus 1 year; installer-forum anecdotes only (brand failure-rate disputes). No MTBF data published. [retail listings and forums, VENDOR/anecdote, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://firstsupply.com/Product/BEL103401LF)
- Mixing valves: Rheem states normal life of these devices is **5-6 years** and failure is typically gradual, letting more hot water through; a plumber and a Caleffi commenter report under 2 years in some water conditions (degraded O-rings). [Rheem, VENDOR, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.rheem.com/?p=18310); [Caleffi blog, anecdote, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.caleffi.com/en-us/node/2533)
- Mixing valve manufacturer statement: TMVs should not be called "failsafe"; malfunction is "almost always progressive" and detectable with temperature checks. [RADA Controls manual, VENDOR, UK, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.radacontrols.com/media/58940/v1-1300905-w2-c-rada-320-web-version.pdf)
- Royal Adelaide Hospital audit of hot water outlets: several taps/showers delivered scald-capable temperatures; "several thermostatic mixing valves were found to be faulty (and replaced) or poorly set (and re-set)"; maintenance relied on complaints rather than scheduled outlet temperature checks. No failure rate reported. [PMC3236657, PEER-REVIEWED, AU, SNIPPET-ONLY (PMC page blocked for my fetch)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3236657/)
- NHS alerts on a specific TMV3 model: a test recorded two instances of mixed temperature **3.4 C above** the permitted maximum for shower outlets (model-specific alert, not a population rate). [NHSEI 2020-005 alert, GOVT-REPORT, UK, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.iheem.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/NHSEI-2020-005-ALERT-RWC-TMV3-audit-failure-FINAL.pdf)
- Mixing valve oversizing/mis-installation produced "extremely variable supply temperatures" in a monitored 71-apartment building (one-building observation). [BPA/Ecotope 2022](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/energy-efficiency/emerging-technologies/20220506-DHW-Distribution-Heat-Loss---Task-7.pdf)
- Cartridge/check-valve failure as source of crossover: "repair can be very simple, often only requiring the replacement of faulty mixing cartridges and check valves. However, locating the sources of crossover flow can be difficult." (search summary of the ETCC/ACEEE material) and crossover detection methods are in Section 4. [ACEEE 2016](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2016/data/papers/1_59.pdf)
- Boilers have long retention (100% of boilers installed in 1995 retained after 10 years, SoCalGas survey 2005); water heaters "often 20+ years." [ACEEE 2012 secondary citation](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2012/data/papers/0193-000030.pdf)
- A plumbing-company statement (non-authoritative) that water heaters often last 7-12 years. [SNIPPET-ONLY](https://johntheplumber.ca/?p=2220)
- Dorm case: domestic recirc pump leaked and stopped when a bearing seized due to water contamination (single anecdote). [Oxmaint blog, anecdote, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://oxmaint.com/industries/property-management/rca-repeated-pump-failures-buildings)
- Sample-level fault data on faucet return (check) valves in hospital HWDS: see Bédard 2015 (Section 1): one broken shower faucet valve depressed minimum riser temperature by ~5 C across 10 risers. [Bédard 2015](https://espace.inrs.ca/id/eprint/3147/1/Bedard2015_Manuscript_Temperature%20Diagnostic.pdf)

### Inferences
- Wear-type faults (cartridges, TMVs) are progressive; this supports modeling them as drifting-degradation states, not sudden binary failures, with a hazard that rises with age (5-6 y vendor life for TMVs; 10 y median pump life).
- Because pump failure is invisible when a setpoint is raised to mask it (Ayala 2012), a "pump-off/pump-failed" cause may be under-represented in complaint-based records.

### Gaps
- **Not found:** MTBF or annual failure fraction for recirculation pumps, TMVs, check valves (ASSE 1016/1017/1070 devices in service), aquastats/immersion sensors, electric elements, heat exchangers/plate HX fouling, expansion tanks, balancing valves. No insurance or utility claim datasets located. No ASSE 1017 field-issue statistics, no Leonard/Powers/Watts field notes retrievable. ASHRAE Owning & Operating cost database primary table not accessed (water heater and DHW pump entries not verified).
- NHS HTM 04-01 audit surveys of TMV failure rates were suggested by the search tool but not found.

## 3. Typical measured values (delta-T, heat loss, flow per riser, time-to-hot, temperature profiles healthy vs faulty)

### Takeaway
There is enough to parameterize a simulator: riser flow of about 0.25-0.5 gpm (balanced), loop heat-loss targets of ≤5 C (≈9 F) heater-to-return, per-apartment recirculation loss ~25-100 W (mean 62.5 W), and dead-leg wait-time tables. Per-foot loss values from Hiller/Klein and NREL HWSIM were not retrieved in numeric form.

### Cited Findings

**Recirculation delta-T / healthy vs faulty (Quebec health-care, 5 systems, CA-QC)**
- Principal return loop mean temperatures ranged **50.4-58.9 C**; heater-outlet means were above 60 C in 4 of 5 systems, but **online logging showed output repeatedly below 60 C and down to 43 C**; system 3 averaged 62.5 C from weekly Saturday-morning manual readings over 24 months but only 57.8 C on a typical weekday when logged. Heater-to-principal-return temperature loss exceeded the ≤5 C guideline target in **3 of 5** systems; for system 5, loss was **3.5-16.3 C** depending on which subordinate return (single or dual) was checked, while the combined principal return looked acceptable (53.9 C). Individual subordinate returns monitored for 2 months ranged 45.7, 46.6, 48.0 and 58.1 C. [Bédard 2015, full text read; PEER-REVIEWED](https://espace.inrs.ca/id/eprint/3147/1/Bedard2015_Manuscript_Temperature%20Diagnostic.pdf)
- Point-of-use sequential-volume profiling (1-minute flush protocol): systems 1-3 (meeting heater and return recommendations): **86% of points reached 55 C and all exceeded 50 C within 2 minutes**; system 4 (low heater output) : **57% of points never reached 55 C** (all >50 C); system 5 (heater and principal return fine): **47% of points below 55 C and 19% below 50 C** (i.e., principal return OK while subordinate loops/risers failed). [same](https://espace.inrs.ca/id/eprint/3147/1/Bedard2015_Manuscript_Temperature%20Diagnostic.pdf)
- Guidelines cited in the same paper: recirculation sized for <5 C system heat loss or minimum 50-55 C at end of return loop (ASPE 2008); minimum velocity 0.2 m/s which gives about 30 min residence time and ~5 C loss in large insulated HCF systems. [same](https://espace.inrs.ca/id/eprint/3147/1/Bedard2015_Manuscript_Temperature%20Diagnostic.pdf)
- Stagnation cooling (lab, 81 cm of 1.25 cm copper, 20 C room): insulated pipe fell from 60 C to below 50 C within **30 minutes**; uninsulated within **10 minutes**; both reached room temperature after 3.5 hours. [same](https://espace.inrs.ca/id/eprint/3147/1/Bedard2015_Manuscript_Temperature%20Diagnostic.pdf)
- 28 commercial buildings (NYC 25, SF 2, NJ 1; 45 recirculating systems): mean hot-water **return** temperature after 1-minute flush **122.8 ± 17.9 F**; distal 119.95 ± 73.2 F (huge variance); incoming cold-water free chlorine 0.34, tank 0.23, return 0.07, distal 0.05 mg/L (mean). [Pathogens 2019, PMC6963558, full-text summary read; PEER-REVIEWED, US-NYC/SF/NJ](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6963558)
- Surveys of building water managers (US): both median water-heater setpoint and median recirculation-loop temperature are **at least 10 F (6 C) below recommended levels**; 27% do not meet equilibrium hot water time guidelines; over 33% do not monitor recirculation loop temperature. [Virginia Tech repository abstract, PEER-REVIEWED (abstract only), US, sample size not stated on the page](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/handle/10919/108863)
- Poland (30 hospitals + 32 social-welfare homes, 581 samples, 2014-2018): only **8.95% of samples were ≥55 C**; mean installation temperature 46 C (2014) rising to 47 C (2018). [AAEM article page, PEER-REVIEWED, PL, page read](https://www.aaem.pl/Temperature-as-a-determining-factor-in-the-occurrence-of-Legionella-sp-bacteria-in,195633,0,2.html)
- Hospital (Spain) guidance now: 60 C in accumulators, 50 C at endpoints and return (Royal Decree 487/2022). [Barcelona hospital study summary, SNIPPET-ONLY, ES](https://portalrecerca.uab.cat/es/publications/role-of-hot-water-temperature-and-water-system-use-on-legionella-/)

**Flow, losses, insulation (multifamily)**
- PG&E lab loop: balanced riser flow 0.25-0.5 gpm/riser; total recirculation 1-6 gpm for 12 risers; piping: 3 in 40 ft (2 in insul.), 2 in 102 ft, 1.5 in 107 ft, 1 in 130 ft (1.5 in insul.), 3/4 in 724 ft (1 in insul.). Test-chamber R-19 walls. Heat loss reported as W/apartment vs flow (figure only; numbers not in text I could extract). [ACEEE 2024](https://aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Best%20Practices%20for%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20Systems%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings-A%20Comparative%20Evaluation%20of%20Balancing%20Methods.pdf)
- Recirculation loss per apartment in California field sites: 24.1-99.9 W, mean 62.5 W. [ETCC](https://etcc-ca.com/reports/multifamily-domestic-hot-water-recirculation-survey)
- R-4 insulation on 3/4-inch pipe "triples cool-down time" (slide text claim only; the underlying cool-down graph values were not readable). DOE ZERH advisories: stored volume between loop and furthest fixture ≤1.0 gal; pump setpoint at least 5 F below heater setpoint; temperature sensor on the return line that takes longest to get hot. [DOE ZERH webinar slides (Ayala/Klein), INDUSTRY-REPORT/GOVT, US](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2017/05/f34/Efficient%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings%2004252017.pdf)
- Bøhm (DK) secondary figures: DTU circulation-loop field tests lowered district-heating return temperature from 46.4 C to 34.1 C (parallel) and 27.9 C (serial); one Danish office/education study reports >85% of DHW-related energy lost from the recirculation circuit (snippet). [DTU Orbit, SNIPPET-ONLY, DK](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/336754809/1-s2.0-S037877882300720X-main_1_.pdf)

**Time-to-hot at fixtures vs pipe length/diameter**
- ASPE benchmarks (via PNNL): 0-10 s reasonable, 11-30 s possibly acceptable, >30 s unacceptable. PNNL Table 1 (wait time in seconds, dead-leg 10 ft / 25 ft): at 0.5 gpm: 1/2-in 25 / 63; 3/4-in 48 / 119. At 1.5 gpm: 1/2-in 8 / 21; 3/4-in 16 / 40. At 2.5 gpm: 1/2-in 5 / 13; 3/4-in 10 / 24. At 4.0 gpm: 1/2-in 3 / 8; 3/4-in 6 / 15. [PNNL-SA-156938, "DHW Temperature Maintenance Technology Review," Aug 2021, full text read; GOVT-REPORT, US](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/PNNL-SA-156938.pdf)
- Klein/Hiller/Zhang: at 0.5-2 gpm in 3/4-inch pipe, **1.5-2.5 times the pipe volume (about 2:1)** flows before water reaches >105 F; time-to-tap (s) = 0.46875 x feet x (oz/ft) x ... / gpm formula given; feet of pipe holding 8 oz: 3/4-in type L copper 2.49 ft, 1/2-in L copper 5.16 ft, 1-in L copper 1.46 ft, 3/4-in PEX 3.34 ft. [PHRC webinar handout, Gary Klein, Nov 2025, full text read; INDUSTRY-REPORT, US](https://www.phrc.psu.edu/assets/docs/Webinars/PHRC-Webinar_Best-Practices-for-High-Performance-Hot-Water-Systems_Handouts.pdf)
- Measured delays of up to 46 s in three UK homes; for 15 m of pipe a recirculation loop would draw about 30 W less than a local thermal store (abstract only). [Moss & Critoph, Energy & Buildings 260 (2022) 111850, PEER-REVIEWED, UK, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/162351/)
- Commercial lavatories on metered valves deliver ~0.25 gal per actuation, so in some buildings a 60-second wait means hot water never arrives (trade article). [Plumbing & Mechanical, INDUSTRY-REPORT, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/87768-hot-water-how-fast-br-julius-ballanco)
- Unquantified here: UA/heat-loss per foot. A forum cites ~27-34 Btu/h-ft for 1/2-in pipe at 52-68 F delta-T (not authoritative; do not use). NREL HWSIM was validated against lab data with typical deviations under 8% for uninsulated/insulated copper, PEX, CPVC. [NREL 55203 / EERE model pages, GOVT-REPORT, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy13osti/55203.pdf)

### Inferences
- A "healthy" hospital loop in the Quebec data looks like: heater outlet ≥60 C continuously, principal return 53-59 C, heater-to-return loss ≤5 C, ≥86% of distal points ≥55 C within 2 min. A "faulty" signature class set from the same data: (a) low heater output (57% of points <55 C), (b) principal return fine but subordinate loops starved (47% of points <55 C), (c) cold ingress via faucet valve (min riser temp -5 C on 10 risers), (d) night-time recirculation inadequacy.
- Return temperature at the principal return can mask subordinate-loop failure (system 5): this directly limits the discriminating power of a single return-temperature reading and argues for modeling check reliability as dependent on measurement location.
- Spot manual readings can miss intermittent heater undershoot (62.5 C weekly vs 57.8 C weekday; excursions to 43 C): spot-check sensitivity for "heater undershoot" must be modeled as time-of-day dependent.

### Gaps
- No numeric heat loss per foot (Btu/h-ft) for insulated and bare 1/2-2 inch copper from a source I could read (Hiller 2009 Phase III, NREL HWSIM tables, Title 24 ACM reference not retrieved).
- No distribution of loop delta-T (supply minus return) across many buildings; no commercial hot-water-return measurement in office buildings; no riser flow field measurements (only lab).
- Typical heater supply/return setpoints for hospitals in the US (survey) not found beyond the Virginia Tech abstract (no sample size).

## 4. Studies using loggers/BAS data to detect DHW faults; accuracy of surrogate measures

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed DHW-specific automated fault-detection study with reported accuracy was found. The closest quantitative evidence on **check reliability** is: (a) the 28-building study showing return-line Legionella culture has sensitivity 55% / specificity 96% for distal positivity and that chlorine, temperature and HPC were not predictive; (b) the Quebec study showing logging beats spot readings and subordinate-return monitoring beats principal-return monitoring; (c) lab validation of crossover detection tests (qualitative detected/not-detected).

### Cited Findings
- 28 commercial buildings (25 NYC, 2 SF, 1 NJ; 5-57 floors; 269 samples from 45 recirculating hot water systems, March-Sept 2015): 65/269 samples (24.2%) positive; 15/28 buildings (53.6%) had ≥1 positive. Hot-water **return** positive in 12/45 (26.7%); distal sites positive in 20/45 (44.4%); return-vs-distal concordance 35/45 (77.8%); distal positive when return positive 11/12 (91.7%), when return negative 9/33 (27.3%). Return line as a screening test for distal positivity: **sensitivity 55% (11/20), specificity 96% (24/25), PPV 91.7%, NPV 72.7%**. Return temperature >124 F as a predictor: sensitivity 65%, specificity 72%, ROC AUC 0.68, association p=0.013 (the fetched summary flagged an internal inconsistency in the paper's counts of systems above 124 F; verify before citing). Distal temperature, free chlorine (incoming, return, distal), HPC (3 to 2.1M CFU/mL), pH, metals and TOC were **not** significantly associated with distal positivity; incoming-chlorine ROC AUC 0.47. [Pathogens 2019 8(4):295, PMC6963558, full-text summary read; PEER-REVIEWED, US](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6963558)
- Logging vs spot checks and location: online monitoring revealed heater outlet below 60 C for periods and down to 43 C although averages exceeded 60 C; weekly manual readings overstated system 3 temperatures (62.5 vs 57.8 C); principal return "merely represents the mean temperature of the blended recirculated hot water," masking subordinate returns of 45.7-48.0 C; spot sampling of 20% of taps per year is "time consuming and yet insufficient for rapidly detecting faulty equipment such as defective valves." Recommended monitoring sequence (from the paper's flowchart): continuous logging at heater outlet, 1-minute flush temperature at each point of use, one week of return-loop logging at subordinate loops. 60/55/50 C thresholds classify risk; "systems with heater >60 C consistently and network >55 C were negative for L. pneumophila by culture and qPCR" (system 1,2,3); culture positivity was 22% (system 4) and 27% (system 5) with qPCR positivity >80% in both. [Bédard 2015, PEER-REVIEWED](https://espace.inrs.ca/id/eprint/3147/1/Bedard2015_Manuscript_Temperature%20Diagnostic.pdf)
- Crossover detection methods were tested on a lab replica (new vs worn cartridges): **pressure-gauge method** (valve off cold feed/isolate hot, drain, watch hot-side pressure rebuild) detected crossover and gave no false positive in the controlled scenarios; "quickest, building-level test" but cannot pinpoint the valve; **water-flow method** (cold feed to heater shut, drain hot side; flow continues and turns cold) validated, building-level and pinpointing possible; **Eatherton method** (heat a shower valve's hot side, open a nearby lavatory hot tap and see if it cools) validated, pinpointing, non-invasive; **water-meter method** validated but needs meters on both lines; **temperature-differential method** (log supply/return temperature minima vs mean) was graded "invalid" as a test but "can be indicative"; **pump-control method** "invalid/discarded." Lab results were binary on a small number of runs, not sensitivity/specificity; authors note field building conditions (occupants drawing water) could degrade results. [Ayala & Zobrist, ACEEE 2016, full text read; PEER-REVIEWED, US-CA lab](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2016/data/papers/1_59.pdf)
- A rule-based commercial FDD rule flags DHW flow temperature outside 50-70 C (suggested diagnoses: sensor calibration, control logic). No accuracy reported. [CopperTree Analytics, VENDOR, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://support.coppertreeanalytics.com/?p=314)
- A patent describes heater-failure detection by comparing sensors (one reading Legionella-friendly while others read ~71 C) and pump-failure detection from loop temperature decaying toward cold-inlet temperature (~15.6 C) or a steady downward trend. Patent disclosure, no validation. [US 11815272, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11815272)
- Adjacent anomaly-detection work (not recirculation): isolation forest F1 0.87 with 5.2% false alarm rate for residential heat-pump hot water demand patterns (arXiv 2506.15719); a labelled district-heating substation dataset (arXiv 2511.14791) includes a DHW circuit and notes step changes in DHW setpoint trip reconstruction models. [arXiv 2506.15719, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15719); [arXiv 2511.14791, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14791)
- Port of Dover case: ~1,000 taps/showers and ~150 storage tanks logged with alarms and 6-year storage (trade press, no accuracy). [Modbs, VENDOR/trade, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://modbs.co.uk/news/fullstory.php/aid/251/Legionella-safe_hot_water_beats_risk_of_scalding.html)
- Hot-water-distribution temperature sensors for demand-control recirculation: place the sensor on the return line that takes longest to get hot (multi-return loops). [DOE ZERH slides](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2017/05/f34/Efficient%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings%2004252017.pdf)

### Inferences
- Reliability model hints: (1) a single principal-return temperature has weak discrimination for subordinate-loop faults; (2) a time-averaged/logged measurement is far more reliable than a single spot reading for intermittent heater undershoot; (3) chlorine/HPC/temperature at distal sites are poor surrogates for microbial status (AUC near chance for chlorine), but return-line culture is a high-specificity (96%) low-sensitivity test; (4) among crossover tests, pressure-gauge and water-flow tests are near-perfect in the lab but field reliability is unquantified, and temperature-differential logging is only suggestive.
- Because the Pathogens 2019 paper's counts conflict slightly, treat the 124 F figures as indicative.

### Gaps
- No published sensitivity/specificity of **recirculation-loop FDD rules in the field**; no study of BAS-trend-based detection of DHW faults, no labeled data. No evidence on detection of pump failure from BAS data other than the patent.
- No field sensitivity/specificity of crossover tests; the 52% prevalence used one method (pressure gauge) with unknown false-positive rate.
- 28-building study did not report positivity by temperature band.

## 5. Legionella-related studies: proportion positive vs temperature; contributing-factor tallies (translate into cause priors)

### Takeaway
Outbreak-investigation tallies (CDC, US 2000-2014) give the best contributing-factor frequencies: water temperature in the growth range in 52% and inadequate disinfectant in 70% of 23 investigated outbreaks; healthcare facilities are about half of drinking-water outbreaks. Temperature-vs-positivity relationships are strong but **only in properly dimensioned, recirculated, daily-used systems** (Barcelona hospital); poorly designed areas remain heavily colonized even at 55 C.

### Cited Findings

**CDC outbreak tallies (US)**
- Deficiencies in environmental control, 27 land-based Legionnaires' outbreaks 2000-2014 (23 with enough data): all 23 had ≥1 deficiency; 11 (48%) had deficiencies in ≥2 categories. Categories: process failures 15 (65%), human errors 12 (52%), equipment failures 8 (35%), unmanaged external changes 8 (35%). Specific deficiencies (my count from the flattened Table 2; individual cells may be miscounted): **inadequate disinfectant 16 (70%)**, **water temperature in Legionella growth range 12 (52%)**, water heater issues 4 (temperature below thermostat setting, heater temperature, failure to drain), thermostatic mixing valve placement 1, stagnation 2, flushing not performed after main break 1, construction-related 3, water main problems 3, inadequate water management program 3, insufficient clinical testing 2, hot tub 2, decorative fountain 3, cooling tower 4, staff knowledge gaps 2. Settings in the 23: hotel/resort 11, hospital 4, long-term care 3, senior living 2, community 2, workplace 1; temperature deficiencies occurred in 8 hotel, 1 hospital, 1 LTC, 2 senior living outbreaks. All 27 outbreaks: 415 cases, 65 deaths, median 10 cases; potable water 56% of sources; health-care-associated outbreaks 57% of cases and 85% of deaths. [CDC MMWR 65(22), June 2016, Garrison et al., full page read; GOVT-REPORT (peer-reviewed), US](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/65/wr/mm6522e1.htm)
- 2011-2012 US drinking-water outbreaks: Legionella in 21 of 32 outbreaks (66%); of 21 community-water-system Legionella outbreaks, **14 (67%) occurred in hospitals/health-care facilities**; healthcare facilities were the setting for 16 of 32 (50%) of all drinking-water outbreaks; a hospital with copper-silver ionization at manufacturer levels still had an outbreak. [CDC MMWR 64(31), Beer et al. 2015, page read; GOVT-REPORT, US](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm6431a2.htm)
- 2015-2020: 214 drinking-water outbreaks in 28 states, 187 biofilm-related, more than half in healthcare facilities (all healthcare outbreaks biofilm-related); Legionella 37% of illnesses but 97% of hospitalizations and 98% of deaths; outbreaks per year 14 (2015), 31 (2016), 34 (2018), 18 (2020). [CIDRAP/AIHA news summaries of CDC report, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/legionella/cdc-most-healthcare-acquired-legionnaires-cases-could-be-prevented); [AIHA, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.aiha.org/news/240314-cdc-report-characterizes-waterborne-disease-outbreaks)
- CDC Vital Signs 2015 data: 2,809 confirmed cases; 85 (3%) definite and 468 (17%) possible healthcare-associated; an estimated 75% of healthcare-acquired cases preventable with better water management. [CIDRAP summary, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/legionella/cdc-most-healthcare-acquired-legionnaires-cases-could-be-prevented)

**Proportion positive vs temperature**
- Barcelona tertiary hospital, 8-year monthly hot-water sampling (Gavaldà et al. 2019): in **well-designed areas** the share of Legionella-**negative** samples was **83.2% at ≥55 C, 64.9% at 50.1-54.0 C, 51.6% at ≤50 C** (so about 17%, 35%, 48% positive; my arithmetic), none of the ≥55 C group reaching 10^3 cfu/L. In **poorly designed areas** (dead legs, low-use taps) only **44.7% of samples were negative** and **28.9% reached ≥10^3 cfu/L even at 55 C**; supplies not used daily raised odds of colonization **OR 2.84** and of ≥10^3 cfu/L **OR 3.18** regardless of temperature. [UAB research portal abstract + related summaries, PEER-REVIEWED (journal not confirmed in the pages I saw), ES, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://portalrecerca.uab.cat/es/publications/role-of-hot-water-temperature-and-water-system-use-on-legionella-/)
- Same hospital follow-up (Quero 2021, Scientific Reports): colonisation dropped dramatically after pipeline repairs and temperature stabilization; distal points were weak points. No numbers retrieved. [Sci Rep via DOAJ/PMC7820426, PEER-REVIEWED, ES, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7820426)
- Poland, 30 hospitals + 32 welfare homes, 581 samples: bacterial-limit exceedance **13.04% in samples <55 C vs 3.85% at ≥55 C** (the paper reports exceedance of the allowable limit, not raw positivity); overall about 12.2% (71/581). [AAEM, PEER-REVIEWED, PL](https://www.aaem.pl/Temperature-as-a-determining-factor-in-the-occurrence-of-Legionella-sp-bacteria-in,195633,0,2.html)
- Denmark, 98 care homes (268 samples): Legionella in 77% and 81% of care homes in the two municipalities; 13% and 16% exceeded 10,000 CFU/L (27% in municipality 1 when first-flush samples are included); ≥50 C (municipality 1) and ≥55 C (municipality 2) associated with low levels. Sample-level positivity and return temperatures not retrieved; a 2023 corrigendum exists. [Nielsen & Uldum, J Water Health 20(9):1393-1404 (2022), PEER-REVIEWED, DK, SNIPPET-ONLY (PDF fetch returned 403)](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/legionella-colonisation-in-hot-water-systems-in-care-homes-from-t)
- Bédard 2015 literature compilation (secondary): HCF hot water positivity reported 10-50%; one hospital's positive taps fell from 100% to a mean 12% over 10 years after raising the heater from 45 to 65 C (Darelid 2002); another fell from 60-90% to 30-40% after raising heater from 50 to 65 C, with remaining positives in areas with inadequate recirculation (Blanc 2005); field studies show **4-11% positive at ≥55 C vs 14-82% at <55 C** (Arvand 2011, Bargellini 2011, Hruba 2009). [Bédard 2015](https://espace.inrs.ca/id/eprint/3147/1/Bedard2015_Manuscript_Temperature%20Diagnostic.pdf)
- 28-building US study: distal temperature (1-min flush) not associated with positivity (p=0.170); return >124 F associated (AUC 0.68). [Pathogens 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6963558)
- Case-level contributing factors in hospital investigations (trade/vendor summaries): temperature control capping water at 45 C and dead legs (Wesley Hospital, Brisbane 2013); calorifiers operating at 38-42 C because of manufacturer temperature restrictions; poorly performing subordinate loops, sub-optimal pumps, dead legs, and TMVs without service history. [OpenAccessGovernment, VENDOR/trade, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.openaccessgovernment.org/ebook/the-cost-of-not-doing-it-right-continuous-legionella-challenges-in-a-healthcare-setting/192417/)

### Inferences
- A usable cause prior for a **hospital/LTC Legionella-risk finding** (as opposed to a hot-water *complaint*): roughly half of investigated outbreaks involved temperature in the growth range, 70% inadequate disinfectant; equipment failures involve ~35%, with water-heater temperature issues the largest hardware category (4/23, 17%) and TMV placement 1/23. These are small-n counts from outbreak investigations with severe selection bias (outbreaks, not routine complaints).
- Temperature-positivity relationship: a simulator/check model could use P(positive | ≥55 C, good design) ≈ 0.17, ≈0.35 at 50-54 C, ≈0.48 at ≤50 C, and ≈0.55 even at 55 C when dead legs/non-use exist (Barcelona); in other studies 4-11% positive at ≥55 C.

### Gaps
- No CDC-level tally of "return loop temperature below target" or "recirculation pump failure" as outbreak contributors; the MMWR categories are coarse. Full MMWR Table 2 cell-level mapping of deficiency to category was not verifiable from the flattened table.
- The 2015-2020 CDC report was read only through news summaries.
- No Journal of Hospital Infection / ICHE tallies of hot-water-fault causes in healthcare Legionella clusters retrieved beyond the above.

## 6. Simulation resources for recirculating DHW loops (licenses, fault simulation)

### Takeaway
No ready-made open-source, validated recirculating-DHW-loop library was found. Practical routes: Modelica Buildings / AixLib plug-flow pipes plus pumps and valves; EnergyPlus plant loop with forced circulation; NREL HWSIM concepts; the Ecotope nodal-analysis approach (described but program not published as open source in what I saw); and a custom heat-transfer layer on EPANET/WNTR since EPANET lacks thermal effects.

### Cited Findings
- **Modelica Buildings library** (LBNL): free, open source, described as 3-clause BSD license; scope covers HVAC, controls, heat transfer, multizone airflow, DC/AC electrical; **no DHW recirculation component was confirmed** in the pages I saw. [LBNL Modelica pages / escholarship, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/14d271qz)
- Buildings library plug-flow pipe model computes transport delay by the 1D wave equation without wall thermal inertia (in the obsolete PlugFlowCore at the time of that doc; check current classes). [LBNL docs, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v9.1.0/help/Buildings_Obsolete_Fluid_FixedResistances_BaseClasses.html)
- **AixLib**: SimplePipe approximates thermal wave propagation with n volumes (advised only where dead time is negligible); PlugFlowPipe for longer pipes but can give unphysical peaks at small flows. License not seen. [OpenModelica doc pages, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://build.openmodelica.org/Documentation/AixLib.Fluid.FixedResistances.SimplePipe.html)
- **BuildSysPro (EDF)**: Systems.DHW and Systems.Distribution packages (hydraulic and capacitive pipe), BSD-3-clause; DHW section lists examples, not recirculation-loop models. [OpenModelica docs, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://build.openmodelica.org/Documentation/BuildSysPro.Systems.DHW.html)
- **IDEAS.Fluid.Taps** (tap examples) and **WaterHub (Eawag)** (Modelica library for DHW material/energy flow analysis; documentation says "under construction"; license not verified; Python module for draw profiles). [IDEAS docs, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://build.openmodelica.org/Documentation/IDEAS.Fluid.Taps.Examples.html); [WaterHub docs, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://waterhub.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)
- **EnergyPlus** (BSD-3-like license per README/DOE slides; GitHub metadata says "other," so read LICENSE.txt): WaterUse:Connections can be coupled to a plant loop; a user-forum answer says a recirculation loop only matters if pipes lose heat (non-adiabatic) and the plant loop must be forced to circulate at all times. [EnergyPlus I/O Reference + Unmet Hours, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://unmethours.com/question/29692/dhw-recirculation-in-energyplus)
- **California Title 24 ACM** has an hourly recirculation-loop pipe-heat-loss method with flowing and no-flow modes (reference documentation). [EnergyCode ACE reference, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://energycodeace.com/site/custom/public/reference-ace-2016/Documents/53b51hourlyrecirculationlooppipeheatlosscalculation.htm)
- **Ecotope nodal-analysis program**: calculates flow, temperature and heat loss per pipe section and iteratively adjusts temperature-maintenance flow per branch to hit a target temperature at each balancing valve (emulating manual or thermostatic balancing); inspired by an earlier CEC program (Zhang 2013); Total flow = delivery + temperature-maintenance; heat loss partitions in proportion to flow; a simplified calculator is published with the BPA report. [BPA/Ecotope 2022, full text read](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/energy-efficiency/emerging-technologies/20220506-DHW-Distribution-Heat-Loss---Task-7.pdf)
- **EPANET/WNTR**: NIST states EPANET is used for premise plumbing but "lacks thermal effects" and it is working with developers on temperature modeling; I found no published thermal extension of WNTR. EPA lists a Premise Plumbing Modeling tool (PPMtools) for demand estimation (temperature support not stated). A SINTEF paper describes a calibrated premise-plumbing temperature model (radial and axial heat transfer) validated on a lab pilot with six showers; temperature fluctuations from non-radial transfer are non-negligible at low/no demand. [NIST page, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.nist.gov/node/1676581); [SINTEF, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/2396179); [EPA/WNTR GitHub, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://github.com/USEPA/WNTR)
- **NREL HWSIM / distribution model** validated vs lab data (typical deviation <8%) for uninsulated/insulated copper, PEX and CPVC; **TRNSYS** DHW types were not investigated. [NREL/EERE, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy13osti/55203.pdf)
- Fault parameters that map to physical simulator knobs, with reported magnitudes: crossover flow ~1.4 gpm induced in lab test with open hose bib; UA multiplier 1.1-2.3; riser flow below 0.1 gpm/riser = unbalanced; return flow collapse at low pump flow; supply-temperature overshoot after draws with oversized mixing valve. [ACEEE 2016](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2016/data/papers/1_59.pdf); [BPA 2022](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/energy-efficiency/emerging-technologies/20220506-DHW-Distribution-Heat-Loss---Task-7.pdf); [ACEEE 2024](https://aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Best%20Practices%20for%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20Systems%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings-A%20Comparative%20Evaluation%20of%20Balancing%20Methods.pdf)

### Inferences
- A lean physical simulator can be built from: lumped/plug-flow pipe segments with UA (times a fault-driven multiplier sampled from 1.0-2.3), riser parallel paths with resistance (imbalance = valve position), a mixing valve with a first-order lag and overshoot, a circulator curve, and fixture draw events. Calibration targets: riser temperature spread 2-5 F when balanced; cooldown 60 C to <50 C in 30 min (insulated 1.25 cm copper) and about 10 min uninsulated; time-to-hot tables above.

### Gaps
- Did not verify license text for AixLib, IDEAS, WaterHub; did not check TRNSYS Type for DHW pipes or EnergyPlus WaterHeater:Mixed pipe-loss modeling; no confirmation of any publicly released thermal EPANET fork. No open validated recirculation fault simulator found.

## 7. Time-on-task for plumbers/engineers on hot-water callbacks and checks

### Takeaway

[Bédard 2015](https://espace.inrs.ca/id/eprint/3147/1/Bedard2015_Manuscript_Temperature%20Diagnostic.pdf)
- Building operators often leave the system unattended "until such time that a tenant complains," so issues "persist over a long period." [Ayala & Zobrist 2012](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2012/data/papers/0193-000030.pdf)

### Inferences
- Durations will have to be elicited; the literature supports ordering the checks by cost: building-level pressure-gauge test (quick) < water-flow test < per-fixture Eatherton pinpointing (scales with number of units) < long logging (days to weeks) < manual rebalancing (hours, repeatable).

## 8. Quantities the diagnostic model needs that I could NOT find (elicit from experts)

### Takeaway
Everything needed for the conditional-probability tables of a diagnostic model is missing except a few anchors: crossover prevalence (52% MF, CA), outbreak-deficiency shares (CDC), temperature-vs-positivity tables (Barcelona), and distal/return positivity relationships (28-building study).

### Cited Findings
- (see Sections 1-7 for what was found; this section lists absences. No additional claims are made here.)

### Inferences
- Because no labeled fault dataset exists, the prior over causes of a hot-water complaint will need to be an expert-elicited Dirichlet, with the 52% crossover prevalence, CDC deficiency shares and the Quebec failure archetypes as anchors and sensitivity analysis over wide ranges.

### Gaps (to elicit)

4. **Check reliability**: sensitivity/specificity of (a) return temperature, (b) riser surface temperature, (c) pressure-gauge or water-flow crossover test in the field, (d) pump amp draw or "feel", (e) flow checks at balancing valves, (f) TMV outlet checks, (g) BAS trend review.
