## 1. Archetypes: what plant configurations exist, components, flow directions, where hot and cold interact

### Takeaway
The sourced evidence supports roughly 8 plant archetypes (gas/electric storage, storage heater plus separate tank, boiler plus indirect tank, steam/HHW heat exchanger semi-instantaneous or with storage, central heat-pump water heater with swing / parallel / return-to-primary layouts, solar preheat, point-of-use, plus heat-trace as a distribution alternative) crossed with 5 distribution variants (single loop, riser returns with balancing valves, dual loops, demand-controlled recirculation, no-return heat trace). The recirculation return can re-enter the plant at three different places (cold makeup/primary tank, a swing tank, a parallel tank, or the master mixing valve), and that return location is the main way hot and cold streams interact inside the plant.

### Cited Findings

**Storage heaters, multiple units (parallel / series / reverse return)**
- Parallel piping manifolds inlets and outlets across all heaters; series piping tends to wear out the first (lead) unit early because it carries most of the load. — [State Water Heaters bulletin 64](https://www.StateWaterHeaters.com/lit/bulletin/bulletin64.pdf) (MANUFACTURER, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Parallel heaters usually lack alternating controls, so friction path through each heater should be equalized (reverse-return or parallel manifold); a lower-resistance heater will carry more of the load. — [PHCP Pros, "Hot water heaters and reverse return piping"](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/1913-hot-water-heaters-and-reverse-return-piping) (TRADE, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Conventional gas storage plants return the recirculation line to the cold-water makeup connection of the storage tank, where it mixes with incoming cold water to be reheated. — [Brooks, Neal, Young (AEA), ACEEE 2024](https://www.aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Eliminating%20the%20Swing%20Tank%20and%20Other%20Design%20Considerations%20in%20Large-Capacity%20CO2%20Heat%20Pump%20Water%20Heating.pdf) (GOVT/peer-reviewed conference paper, text read)
- Heat traps (valve or 18 in. minimum loops) are specified on cold makeup inlet and outlet connections of storage tanks. — [CED Engineering M03-039, adapted from the Unified Facilities Criteria](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/M03-039%20-%20An%20Introduction%20to%20Hospital%20Domestic%20Water%20Systems%20-%20US.pdf) (TRADE adaptation of a GOVT criteria document; text read)
- ASHRAE Guideline 12-2020 reportedly extends the 140 F outlet expectation to heaters "with any storage capacity", including heaters with separate storage tanks and semi-instantaneous units, citing stratification (outlet 130 F+ while bottom of heater 85-110 F). — [Plumbing Engineer Oct 2021](https://digital.plumbingengineer.com/Plumbing-Engineer-October-2021/55) (TRADE, SNIPPET-ONLY; page fetch returned 503/504)

**Boiler plus indirect storage tank**
- A DOE ZERH webinar slide shows the "proper recirculation loop configuration" as boiler plus storage tank with: hot water supply to units, hot water return back to the plant, cold water inlet, ball valves isolating the pump, a demand-control recirculation pump, hose bibb to purge air, and a check valve. — [DOE ZERH, "Efficient Hot Water Distribution in Multifamily Buildings", 2017 slides](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2017/05/f34/Efficient%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings%2004252017.pdf) (GOVT; slide text read)
- Indirect tanks report tank temperature to the boiler control through an aquastat or a tank sensor; "reverse indirect" tanks put boiler water in the tank shell and heat DHW through an internal coil (effectively on-demand). Source is installer forum threads only, so very weak. — [HeatingHelp forum threads, e.g. dhw priority question](https://forum.heatinghelp.com/discussion/151793/dhw-priority-question) (TRADE/forum, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- AERCO states its standard gateway handles gas-fired boilers/water heaters and "indirect water heaters" as separate equipment categories with standard control points. — [AERCO CG-1/XPC Communications Gateway data sheet](https://www.aerco.com/dfsmedia/0533dbba17714b1ab581ab07a4cbb521/58013-source/cg-1-communication-gateway-data-sheet) (MANUFACTURER; PDF text read)

**Steam or hot-water-to-DHW heat exchangers (instantaneous vs semi-instantaneous, with/without storage)**
- NIH practice: semi-instantaneous shell-and-tube steam heaters (or indirect HW heaters) with modulating, fail-safe pneumatic or electric fast-positioning control valves (position feedback); heat to 140-145 F, then temper to 125-130 F for distribution through ASSE 1017 master thermostatic mixing valves arranged in parallel for N+1 redundancy; double-wall heat exchangers required for potable/lab water; semi-instantaneous heaters need no storage tank or circulating pump, though some units add a tank and pump for high peak demand; when a storage tank is used, minimum 140-145 F. — [NIH ORF Technical Bulletin, Steam Water Heaters, Nov 2018](https://orf.od.nih.gov/TechnicalResources/Documents/Technical%20Bulletins/18TB/Steam%20Water%20Heater%20Nov%202018%20-%20Technical%20Bulletin_508.pdf) (GOVT; text read)
- Hospital criteria: provide redundant shell-and-tube instantaneous or semi-instantaneous central heaters such that demand is met with the largest unit offline; supply at least 130 F at peak flow; high turndown ratio; provision for 170-180 F water at reduced flow for thermal eradication; plate-and-frame HX may be considered; instantaneous systems "require thermostatic mixing valves" because heating capacity to hot-water volume ratio is small; semi-instantaneous units add limited storage for surges. — [CED Engineering M03-039](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/M03-039%20-%20An%20Introduction%20to%20Hospital%20Domestic%20Water%20Systems%20-%20US.pdf) (TRADE adaptation of GOVT criteria; text read)
- Steam-side accessories typically: strainer, steam control valve, steam trap, isolation valves, pressure gauge, thermometer; relief valve capacity at least equal to heat input. — [Univ. of Maryland Baltimore spec 22 35 00P (via search)](https://www.umaryland.edu:443/media/umb/af/dc/documents/division-22/223500P---Domestic-and-Laboratory-Water-Heat-Exchangers-09-06-2022.pdf) (TRADE/owner spec, SNIPPET-ONLY; fetch 404)
- Watts QuickDraw manual covers semi-instantaneous and storage variants with steam/condensate connections, steam valve actuators and an optional electronic low-water cutoff. — [Watts QuickDraw installation manual](https://www.watts.com/dfsmedia/0533dbba17714b1ab581ab07a4cbb521/53942-source/quickdraw-semi-instantaneous-and-storage-installation-manual-500-25) (MANUFACTURER, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Hospital criteria also call for booster heaters (simplex shell-and-tube, 140-160 F at point of use) for cage washers/sterilizers and duplex steam-coil boosters (180-195 F) for dietetic equipment. — [CED Engineering M03-039](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/M03-039%20-%20An%20Introduction%20to%20Hospital%20Domestic%20Water%20Systems%20-%20US.pdf) (TRADE adaptation of GOVT; text read)

**Central heat pump water heaters (CO2 and others), swing tank**
- Three recirculation-return options: return to primary storage tank(s), a "swing" tank in series with the primary tank, or a parallel temperature-maintenance tank. Return-to-primary is common for multi-pass units and uncommon for single-pass; swing tank (first introduced by Ecotope) lets very hot primary water charge the swing tank during draws and relies on its (usually electric-resistance) element during low demand; parallel tank receives no water from primary storage so all loop losses are met by its own heater (typically a heat pump). — [AEA/ACEEE 2024](https://www.aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Eliminating%20the%20Swing%20Tank%20and%20Other%20Design%20Considerations%20in%20Large-Capacity%20CO2%20Heat%20Pump%20Water%20Heating.pdf) (GOVT-funded paper; text read)
- Multi-pass units add ~10 F per pass to a mixed tank; single-pass units heat a stratified tank top-to-bottom; some products (Lync, Mitsubishi) require a secondary heat exchanger between the HP loop and the DHW tank; others heat potable water directly. — [AEA/ACEEE 2024](https://www.aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Eliminating%20the%20Swing%20Tank%20and%20Other%20Design%20Considerations%20in%20Large-Capacity%20CO2%20Heat%20Pump%20Water%20Heating.pdf) (text read); [PNNL BASC](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/central-heat-pump-water-heaters-multifamily-buildings) says 5-10 F per pass (slight discrepancy between the two sources)
- Mitsubishi Heat2O: HP delivers 176 F water to the HX, ~165 F reaches storage; closed "primary" loop with pump built into the unit; "secondary" loop pump between storage tank and HX runs when a flow switch on the primary loop sees flow; HP starts/stops from "On sensor" and "Off sensor" temperature sensors in the storage tank(s); METUS requires a separate controller in the US (remote monitoring possible); recommended with a swing tank in the US. — [AEA/ACEEE 2024](https://www.aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Eliminating%20the%20Swing%20Tank%20and%20Other%20Design%20Considerations%20in%20Large-Capacity%20CO2%20Heat%20Pump%20Water%20Heating.pdf) (text read)
- Site-built HX package at the demonstration sites: brazed plate HX, variable-speed secondary pump, buffer tank, temperature and flow sensors, gauges; in-pipe sensors caused pump hunting between two HP units, replaced with surface-mount thermistors; air-purge problems on the closed loop addressed with a feed tank or auto-fill valve. — [AEA/ACEEE 2024](https://www.aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Eliminating%20the%20Swing%20Tank%20and%20Other%20Design%20Considerations%20in%20Large-Capacity%20CO2%20Heat%20Pump%20Water%20Heating.pdf) (text read)
- WaterDrop skid: 2-9 residential-size SANCO2 HPs in parallel plus tanks, swing tank, mixing valve and recirculation pump in an outdoor insulated box. — same AEA paper (text read)
- PNNL guide configurations: parallel-loop tank (dedicated multi-pass HPWH on the return), in-series swing tank (primary storage must run 150 F or higher), return-to-primary (loop return to bottom of primary storage), and a retrofit variant (primary HPWH as preheater, existing plant becomes the swing tank). Components: primary HPWH banks, primary storage, temperature-maintenance system, backup heater, thermostatic mixing valve, controls. — [PNNL BASC, Central HPWHs in multifamily buildings](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/central-heat-pump-water-heaters-multifamily-buildings) (GOVT; page read)
- Ecotope's Energy Trust design guide (separate sections on swing tank, parallel loop tank, return-to-primary single/multi-pass, no-recirculation designs); full guide not read. — [Energy Trust fact sheet](https://energytrust.org/fact-sheets/new-buildings-design-guide-for-central-heat-pump-water-heaters) (GOVT/utility program, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- A 2026 ACEEE paper reportedly describes moving away from swing tanks to return-to-primary switching valves, non-CO2 heat pumps with full return-to-primary, and parallel maintenance with integrated units. — [ACEEE SSB26 paper 070](https://www.aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb26/pdfs/070_0959_1434_000735.pdf) (SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Field problem (hot/cold interaction): HPWH inlet and outlet were piped to the mixing-valve inlet and cold makeup, causing erratic HPWH and mixing valve behavior; dedicated tank connections fixed it. — [PNNL BASC](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/central-heat-pump-water-heaters-multifamily-buildings) (page read)

**Solar preheat**
- Commercial solar systems are normally configured to deliver preheated water to existing boiler/water-heater equipment, which remains as backup/booster; collector loop with pump, storage tank, differential controller, and either drainback reservoir or glycol loop with heat exchanger. — [Solahart](https://solahart.com/commercial-solar-hot-water-systems) (MANUFACTURER, SNIPPET-ONLY); [PNNL BASC drainback guide](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/drain-back-solar-hot-water) (GOVT, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Differential controller typically uses a collector-outlet sensor and a tank-bottom sensor, pump on at ~8 F differential (sources cite roughly 3-10 F); check valve in collector supply prevents night thermosiphoning. — [opentextbc.ca Plumbing, active solar thermal](https://opentextbc.ca/plumbing3g/chapter/active-solar-thermal-system-operation/) (TRADE/textbook, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Unusual variant: solar array heats the loop return going back to the storage tank (evacuated-tube heat-pipe collectors, federal building). — [NREL preprint](https://research-hub.nrel.gov/en/publications/evacuated-tube-heat-pipe-solar-collectors-applied-to-the-recircul/) (GOVT, SNIPPET-ONLY)

**Point-of-use heaters**
- Hospital criteria: instantaneous tankless point-of-use (electric or gas) heaters are not permitted as the primary hot water source; permitted for emergency fixtures and similar special cases. — [CED Engineering M03-039](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/M03-039%20-%20An%20Introduction%20to%20Hospital%20Domestic%20Water%20Systems%20-%20US.pdf) (TRADE adaptation of GOVT; text read)
- Fixture-level point-of-use tempering (ASSE 1070 sinks/lavatories, ASSE 1016 thermostatic/pressure-balancing for showers, ASSE 1071 for eyewash/emergency showers) is separate from plant-level master mixing. — same source (text read)

**Master mixing valves and tempering**
- Master thermostatic mixing valves to ASSE 1017 temper plant output; "hilow" arrangement for wide flow range; lockable bypass (normally closed) for thermal eradication; check stops and unions on inlets. A high-temperature alarm is required to detect mixing valve failure (alarm when temperature exceeds setpoint by 5 F). — [CED Engineering M03-039](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/M03-039%20-%20An%20Introduction%20to%20Hospital%20Domestic%20Water%20Systems%20-%20US.pdf) (text read)
- Worked example: 140 F supply, 120 F mixed, loop designed for 10 F delta-T gives 110 F return; paper warns mixing valves can misbehave at low recirculation flow and recommends a balance valve on the return to the heater. — [Deppmann, mixing valve paper](https://www.deppmann.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/08/printer_friendly_2011_08_29.pdf) (MANUFACTURER, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Typical tempered-loop layout: return from the farthest fixture goes to the mixing valve recirculation port and cold supply; circulator controlled by an aquastat set 5-10 F below the mixed outlet setpoint. — search summary of manufacturer/trade pages (e.g., [highperformancehvac.com mixing valve article](https://highperformancehvac.com/hot-water-mixing-valve/)) (MANUFACTURER/TRADE, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Chicago Public Schools standard (reported): DHW plants produce 140 F and a main mixing valve reduces to 120 F for building distribution. — [CPS standards link from search](https://www.cps.edu/link/d659eabffc294bac8242c7658f99a9d4.aspx) (owner standard, SNIPPET-ONLY)

**Recirculation loop layouts and distribution variants**
- Riser-based returns: in many mid/high-rise multifamily systems, supply splits into vertical risers as parallel paths; flow follows least resistance, so the farthest riser starves without balancing; three balancing valve types: manual (needs differential pressure across orifice and valve Cv; rule-of-thumb minimum 0.5-1.0 gpm per path), automatic flow-limiting cartridge, thermostatic (opens when cold, throttles when hot). — [Zeyghami and Zhang (PG&E), ACEEE 2024](https://aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Best%20Practices%20for%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20Systems%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings-A%20Comparative%20Evaluation%20of%20Balancing%20Methods.pdf) (GOVT/utility lab paper; text read)
- Same lab test (36 units, 12 risers): manual balancing at ~0.5 gpm/riser gave most uniform riser-top temperatures; all valve types acceptable above 0.25 gpm/riser; large nonuniformity below that; automatic valves performed worse and were affected by pump speed control; farthest riser held above 120 F in tests; manual-valve system could be balanced once at highest flow and then flow reduced. — same paper (text read)
- Dual loops instead of one long loop serving the same units are recommended; keep mechanical room central; minimize 90-degree turns. — DOE ZERH slides (text read), link above
- Recirculation controls: DOE ZERH requires on-demand recirculation for central systems based on a demand indicator and loop temperature; temperature sensor on split loops goes on the return line that takes longest to heat; one pilot used a flow sensor on the cold makeup as the demand indicator and a temp sensor strapped to the return upstream of the pump. — DOE ZERH slides (text read), link above
- Energy-code form (Washington adoption of 2021 IECC Commercial, C404.7): circulation pump required, with electronically commutated motor with speed adjustment for balancing; dedicated return pipe; gravity/thermosiphon prohibited; controls start the pump on identified hot-water demand; automatic controls, temperature sensors and pumps must be accessible; heat trace must switch off automatically when there is no demand and comply with IEEE 515.1. — [WAC 51-11C-40407](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/washington/WAC-51-11C-40407) (STANDARD, page read). Single-riser pump-off at design supply temperature with restart at least 10 F lower, and demand-recirc limit of 104 F on water entering the cold line, came from the search summary of the same WAC family, not the fetched text (SNIPPET-ONLY).
- Heat trace as a no-return alternative: self-regulating cable replaces pipe heat loss and removes return piping, pumps, check valves and balancing valves; nominal maintenance temperatures 105/115/125/140 F quoted by one vendor; claims of ASHRAE 188/Guideline 12 acceptance are vendor claims. — [Thermon HLX](https://thermon.com/hlx-self-regulating-heat-trace-for-sustainable-electrification-of-hot-water-systems/) (MANUFACTURER, SNIPPET-ONLY). Counterpoint: hospital criteria say heat tracing "shall not be used in lieu of hot water recirculation systems" ([CED M03-039](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/M03-039%20-%20An%20Introduction%20to%20Hospital%20Domestic%20Water%20Systems%20-%20US.pdf), text read).
- Hospital loop rules: recirculation required in all patient-care buildings; hot water at each outlet within 15 s; velocity 4-5 fps max; loop temperature loss not to exceed 6 F; redundant recirculation pumps for patient-care buildings with a sequence that prevents both running together; separate check, isolation and balancing valve on each return at its connection to the loop; contract-document notes tell the balance agency which gpm to set; high-rise pressure zones need separate water heaters because return loops of very different pressure cannot join one heater. — [CED M03-039](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/M03-039%20-%20An%20Introduction%20to%20Hospital%20Domestic%20Water%20Systems%20-%20US.pdf) (text read)
- Wisconsin health-care rule: hot water distribution under constant recirculation; uncirculated hot-water piping limited to 3 ft developed length; patient showers/baths need pressure-balanced thermostatic valves holding 110-115 F. — [Wisconsin SPS 382.50](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/administrativecode/SPS%20382.50(3)(b)7m.) (STANDARD for WI; only 6.a and (3)(ag)1 text confirmed by fetch; the 3 ft and 110-115 F text is SNIPPET-ONLY from search)

**Where hot and cold interact (summary of cited items)**
- Recirc return enters cold makeup of tank (conventional gas) — AEA paper.
- Recirc return enters swing tank / parallel tank / primary bottom in HPWH plants — AEA, PNNL.
- Master mixing valve blends hot + cold + loop return — CED/UFC, Deppmann (snippet).
- Fixture-level crossover: pressure imbalance pushes cold water into the hot loop through single-handle or faulty valves; crossover prevention = ASSE 1016 valves with integral checks, check valves on incoming hot and cold per unit, check valve on cold main and hot return. — DOE ZERH slides (text read). Field measured recirc losses were 1.1-2.3x idealized calculations, attributed mainly to insulation imperfections and cold-water crossover. — [ETCC multifamily DHW recirculation survey](https://etcc-ca.com/reports/multifamily-domestic-hot-water-recirculation-survey) (GOVT/utility program, summary page read; losses 24.1-99.9 W per apartment, mean 62.5 W; project listed as active, full report behind a form)
- Recirculation heat losses are roughly one-third of hot-water system energy in existing single-family and multifamily buildings per the PG&E paper's literature review; another cited study found 23-70% of supplied heat lost in recirc and distribution. — PG&E/ACEEE 2024 (text read)

### Inferences
- (Mine) Canonical archetype set for conditioning cause/check lists: A1 storage heater(s) (gas/electric, 1 vs N, parallel vs series); A2 condensing/modulating heater plus separate storage tank; A3 boiler plus indirect tank; A4 steam/HHW semi-instantaneous HX (± storage) with master TMV; A5 central HPWH with return-to-primary / swing / parallel tank variants; A6 solar preheat feeding any of the above; A7 point-of-use (mostly not a central archetype); crossed with distribution D1 single loop, D2 riser returns with balancing valves, D3 dual loops/zones, D4 demand-controlled pump, D5 heat trace, and a mixing flag M0/M1 (no master TMV / master TMV, plus per-fixture TMVs).
- (Mine) Cause lists should differ by where the return re-enters: return into cold makeup (A1/A3 and multi-pass HPWH) can raise tank-bottom temperature and cut heater capacity; return into a swing/parallel tank puts loop heat on an electric element; return to a master mixing valve makes mixed-outlet temperature sensitive to low loop flow.
- (Mine) Riser-return systems (D2) add imbalance-specific causes (starved far riser, failed check valve on a return, balancing valve mis-set) that single-loop (D1) systems do not have; hospital/pressure-zoned buildings add multiple plants.
- (Mine) CO2 heat-pump plants are a different failure space from combustion plants (sensor-driven on/off, defrost, backup resistance running unnoticed per PNNL), so the plant archetype should switch the cause list.

## 2. Measurement and actuation points per archetype; what is on a BAS vs local only

### Takeaway
There is no census of which DHW points are on a BAS. The only quantitative anchors are generic: roughly 76% of US commercial buildings with water heating use a centralized system (CBECS 2012), and BAS penetration is low overall (about 10% of all buildings, about 13% of buildings under 50,000 sq ft, per secondary snippets). Sourced point lists exist from equipment vendors (AERCO gateway, digital mixing valves, HPWH controllers) and from design criteria (gauges/thermometers plus Pete's plugs, sensors for the EMCS), but the likelihood that a given point reaches a BAS has to be treated as unknown and probabilistic in the tool.

### Cited Findings

**Prevalence anchors**
- CBECS 2012: of 5,557 thousand US commercial buildings, 4,423 thousand have water heating; 3,348 thousand (about 76%) centralized, 690 thousand (16%) distributed, 385 thousand (9%) combination. By floorspace: 47,534 of 79,015 million sq ft (60%) centralized, 12,688 (16%) distributed, 18,793 (24%) combination. Lodging: 5,821 of 5,826 million sq ft have water heating; health care 4,153 of 4,155. Multifamily is not in these tables. — [EIA CBECS 2012 Table B31](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/commercial/data/2012/bc/cfm/b31.php) and [Table B32](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/commercial/data/2012/bc/cfm/b32.cfm) (GOVT, pages read). Caveat: the fetch tool mislabeled column headers in B31; I took the rows as total buildings per equipment type, which is internally consistent (3,348 + 690 + 385 = 4,423).
- About 10% of US buildings use BAS or central controls; BAS buildings are typically large (>100,000 sq ft); over 90% of buildings are small (<5,000 sq ft) or medium (5,000-50,000 sq ft) and do not use BAS. — [PNNL-22169 scoping study (LBNL/ORNL/PNNL), 2012](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1063081) (GOVT, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- A 2024 LBNL-hosted conference paper reports only 13% of buildings under 50,000 sq ft have a BAS; a 2025 ACEEE brief estimates about 70% of medium and 85% of small commercial buildings lack building energy management and control systems. — [LBNL ETA publications entry](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/bibcite/reference/36940) and [ACEEE brief](https://www.aceee.org/topic-brief/2025/11/building-energy-management-control-systems-small-and-medium-commercial) (GOVT/NGO, SNIPPET-ONLY, not directly comparable with each other)
- No DHW-specific BAS point-census, CBECS item, or utility-program statistic was found.

**Vendor/equipment-exposed points (what CAN reach a BAS)**
- AERCO gateway: translates BACnet MS/TP, LonWorks, Johnson N2 and Modbus; standard map covers up to 12 gas-fired boilers/water heaters, four indirect water heaters or a BMS II, with standard control points "including set point, fire rate, outlet temperature, unit status, run cycles and run hours"; a custom option supports up to 300 customer-selected points; the gateway is an add-on ("available for all AERCO boilers and water heaters"), so BAS visibility depends on someone buying and commissioning it. — [AERCO CG-1/XPC data sheet, rev 1/13/2011](https://www.aerco.com/dfsmedia/0533dbba17714b1ab581ab07a4cbb521/58013-source/cg-1-communication-gateway-data-sheet) (MANUFACTURER, text read)
- Digital mixing valves: Leonard Nucleus has integral temperature sensors on hot inlet, cold inlet and return, programmable high/low alarm, BACnet/Modbus; Armstrong DMC has programmable hi/lo alarms that can notify the BAS; AERCO ADMS mixing station can include recirc pump and flow/BTU monitoring. Traditional thermostatic (ASSE 1017) valves have no electronic outputs. — [Leonard Nucleus on BIMobject](https://www.bimobject.com/da/leonardvalvecompany/product/megatron-nv-200-lf), [Armstrong DMC data sheet](https://armstronginternational.com/wp-content/uploads/Data_ModelDMC40BSZonePHE_Brain_DMC_EN.pdf), [AERCO ADMS brochure](https://aerco.com/dfsmedia/0533dbba17714b1ab581ab07a4cbb521/57658-source/brochure-aerco-adms-aerco-digital-mixing-station) (MANUFACTURER, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- ASPE: monitoring cold and hot inlet temperatures to a mixing station can reveal a failed recirculation pump, overheated cold lines, and failed heaters. — [ASPE Pipeline](https://aspe.org/pipeline/?p=3974) (TRADE, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Central HPWH: temperature sensors in thermowells at multiple tank heights (often needs custom tank spec), sensors on HP supply/return piping, controls staging HPWHs/backup/maintenance heaters on tank temperatures, alarms for equipment failure or loss of DHW, utility or time-schedule inputs; external reporting to a responsible party recommended; alarm logs available from the manufacturer. — [PNNL BASC](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/central-heat-pump-water-heaters-multifamily-buildings) (GOVT, page read)
- Many field CHPWH systems ran on electric-resistance backup for long periods because installation or equipment errors went unnoticed. — same PNNL page (page read). This is evidence of weak monitoring in this archetype.
- AEA pilot sites were "heavily instrumented" for research and are not representative of normal BAS coverage. — AEA/ACEEE 2024 (text read)

**Design-criteria point/gauge requirements**
- Hospital criteria: provide Pete's plugs at all temperature and pressure indicating devices, "analog (gauges and thermometers) and digital (sensors for the EMCS system)"; temperature shall be measured throughout the hot and cold domestic system (also an indicator of flow); incoming water monitored for temperature, oxidant level, pH and pressure by gauges, sensors and a grab-sample port; high-temperature alarm for mixing valve failure; redundant recirc pumps. — [CED M03-039](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/M03-039%20-%20An%20Introduction%20to%20Hospital%20Domestic%20Water%20Systems%20-%20US.pdf) (text read)
- Univ. of Idaho BAS standard: end devices must interface with BAS (dry contacts for status/metering, 4-20 mA for feedback/reset); DHW point list itself not visible in the result. — [U of Idaho content hub](https://content-hub.uidaho.edu/api/public/content/05d6837750f948c2bb34f22a66056e98) (owner standard, SNIPPET-ONLY). Harvard Division 25 requires point lists (AI/AO/BI/BO, alarming, naming) and sequences on drawings with flow diagrams. — [Harvard Division 25](https://campusplanning.hms.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/mcu791/files/slide-images/Facilities-PDF/Division%2025.pdf) (owner standard, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Recirculation control sensors: temperature sensor on the return upstream of the pump; on split loops, the sensor on the return that takes longest to heat; flow sensor on cold makeup can serve as demand indicator; a flow sensor on the hot line cannot be used because recirculation looks the same as demand; temperature sensor should sit at the end of the loop. — DOE ZERH slides (text read); [Univ. of Illinois Smart Energy Design Assistance Center tip sheet](https://smartenergy.illinois.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Smart-Tip-HotWaterRecirc-Ver6.pdf) (GOVT/university, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Lab instrumentation in PG&E's 36-unit test rig: 54 temperature sensors, 37 pressure sensors, flow meters, one power meter (research-grade; indicates what is not normally present). — PG&E/ACEEE 2024 (text read)
- Most recirculation pipes are behind walls, making riser temperature distribution nearly impossible to assess in the field. — PG&E/ACEEE 2024 and ETCC lab-testing page (text read / snippet)

### Inferences
- (Mine, not measured) Likely point tiers by archetype. Treat BAS likelihood as a prior, not a statistic.
  - Storage gas/electric (A1/A2): heater outlet/tank temp and high-limit alarm usually local (aquastat, built-in display, gauge or thermometer); BAS only if a vendor gateway or hardwired alarm contact was added. Recirc pump run/command often BAS if the pump is on a BAS output; loop return temperature gauge or strap-on aquastat is the common local instrument.
  - Boiler plus indirect (A3): tank aquastat/sensor local to the boiler control; boiler status and supply temp often on BAS (boiler integration is common for heating); DHW-specific tank temp rarely separate.
  - HX semi-instantaneous (A4): HX leaving temperature, steam/HHW valve command and position feedback, and mixing-valve outlet temperature most likely on a BAS in institutional buildings with EMCS (NIH and hospital criteria specify modulating valves with position feedback and EMCS sensors), but absent in small or older buildings.
  - CHPWH (A5): rich controller data (tank thermowell temps, HP status/alarms) exists, but remote visibility depends on the manufacturer controller/portal rather than the building BAS.
  - Solar (A6): collector and tank-bottom temps and pump status on the solar controller (local), rarely on BAS.
  - Distribution: per-riser return temperatures, balancing-valve settings and branch flows almost never measured continuously; only gauges/thermometers or none (evidence: PG&E, ETCC).
- (Mine) Recommended point vocabulary for the tool: actuation (heater enable/firing rate, steam or HHW valve command, mixing valve position/setpoint, recirc pump command, speed), sensing (heater outlet, tank top/mid/bottom, mixing valve hot/cold/mixed, loop return, per-riser return, cold makeup, flow/BTU on recirc, pump amps/kW, differential pressure), status/alarm (pump run/fail, high-temp alarm, low-temp alarm, heater fault, backup-heater-on), setpoints and schedule. Each should carry a "source tier" attribute: BAS-trended, BAS-live, local controller display, gauge/thermometer, portable-instrument-only, absent.

### Gaps
- No census (CBECS, utility program, or vendor) of DHW points on BAS; the 10% and 13% BAS figures are all-building or small-building generics and come from snippets.
- No actual BACnet/Modbus point list for a commercial gas water heater found (search returned HVAC and heat-pump lists). AERCO data sheet gives category names only, not object lists; Modbus register maps for specific models would need to be requested from manufacturers.
- Univ. of Idaho / Georgia / Duke / Johns Hopkins standards contain DHW BAS or documentation requirements that I could not open; contents not confirmed.
- Digital mixing valve claims (Leonard, Armstrong, AERCO ADMS) are from catalog snippets.
- No data on pump amps/speed points on DHW recirc pumps specifically (ECM pump speed is required by IECC C404.7 adoption but BAS connection not stated).

## 3. Typical operating setpoints and design values

### Takeaway
Storage/heater outlet is typically 140 F (CDC, Guideline 12, VA directive, UFC-derived criteria), semi-instantaneous heaters discharge at least 130 F, delivered/mixed temperatures 120-130 F for general distribution (110-115 F or lower at patient fixtures), and recirculating return should not fall below 120 F (CDC) or 124 F (healthcare practice). Design loop temperature drop is 5-10 F in multifamily practice (6 F in hospital criteria). The user's numbers (140 / 120-125 / return at or above 124 / delta-T 5-10) are consistent with the sources, with a split between a CDC-style 120 F floor and a healthcare 124 F floor.

### Cited Findings
- CDC toolkit: store hot water above 140 F (60 C) and keep circulating hot water above 120 F (49 C); recirculate continuously if possible; use point-of-use TMVs to allow circulating temps above 120 F while preventing scalds; thermal shock not recommended. — [CDC, Controlling Legionella in Potable Water Systems](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/toolkit/potable-water-systems-module.html) (GOVT, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Guideline 12-2000 section 4.1.6 (as quoted by trade pages): hot water stored above 140 F with minimum recirculated return of 124 F; the 2020 edition reportedly requires heater outlets at 140 F or higher for any heater with storage. — trade search summaries, e.g., [Plumbing Engineer Oct 2021](https://digital.plumbingengineer.com/Plumbing-Engineer-October-2021/55) and [PHCP Pros](https://phcppros.com/articles/14203-legionella-cost-effective-control-part-3) (TRADE, SNIPPET-ONLY; I did not read the guideline)
- VA Directive 1061 as reported: storage at least 140 F; 130 F discharge for instantaneous/semi-instantaneous exchangers; distribution piping water no lower than 124 F before any temperature-reducing mixing valve. The same article states ASHRAE 188 contains no temperature guidelines. — [PHCP Pros, Legionella prevention a priority in plumbing system design](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/13102-legionella-prevention-a-priority-in-plumbing-system-design) (TRADE, page read)
- Hospital criteria: tank heaters at least 140 F with capability to 170-180 F for eradication; semi-instantaneous discharge at least 130 F so circulating water is no lower than 124 F; patient-accessible outlet no more than 110 F via tempering; loop heat-loss temperature drop no more than 6 F; velocity 4-5 fps max. — [CED M03-039](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/M03-039%20-%20An%20Introduction%20to%20Hospital%20Domestic%20Water%20Systems%20-%20US.pdf) (text read)
- NIH: heat to 140-145 F, temper to 125-130 F. — NIH bulletin (text read)
- Wisconsin (state code, healthcare): "Water stored and circulation initiated at a minimum of 140 F and with a return of a minimum of 124 F" (SPS 382.50(3)(b)6.a); "Hot water shall be initiated and stored at a minimum of 140 F" (SPS 382.50(3)(ag)1). — [Wisconsin SPS 382.50](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/administrativecode/SPS%20382.50(3)(b)7m.) (STANDARD, fetched text)
- Multifamily CHPWH: loop maintained 115-125 F; swing-tank primary storage at least 150 F; Heat2O HP 176 F to HX, about 165 F into storage. — PNNL BASC and AEA/ACEEE 2024 (read)
- DOE ZERH: recirc pump operating temperature at least 5 F below water heater setpoint (advisory); stored volume between loop and furthest fixture 1 gal or less (advisory); R-4 insulation on loop. — DOE ZERH slides (text read)
- Deppmann example: 140 F supply, 120 F mixed, 10 F loop delta-T, 110 F return. — [Deppmann paper](https://www.deppmann.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/08/printer_friendly_2011_08_29.pdf) (SNIPPET-ONLY). Mixing-valve aquastat set 5-10 F below mixed setpoint (search summary of manufacturer pages, SNIPPET-ONLY).
- PG&E lab: farthest riser top maintained above 120 F; balancing at 0.5 gpm per riser baseline; thermal losses cut up to 12% with best balancing valve. — PG&E/ACEEE 2024 (text read)
- Legionella growth range cited as 68-123 F (20-50 C) in the hospital course (text read); CDC snippet cites 77-113 F as favorable and growth down to 68 F.
- Statistic: temperature maintenance losses are about 30% of thermal load in multifamily central DHW, up to 60% — attributed to a Connecticut Green Bank presentation. — [CT Green Bank MF DHW design, 2018](https://www.ctgreenbank.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/MF-Domestic-Hot-Water-Design-2-16-18.pdf) (SNIPPET-ONLY; the document 404'd when fetched)
- Chicago Public Schools: 140 F plant, 120 F after main mixing valve. — CPS link above (SNIPPET-ONLY). Duke DHW standard operating range 100-140 F; Johns Hopkins limits heater supply to 140 F and 120 F in childcare. — owner specs (SNIPPET-ONLY).

### Inferences
- (Mine) Default setpoint tables for the tool: heater/storage outlet 140 F; semi-instantaneous 130-145 F; master-mixed distribution 120-130 F; patient/point-of-use 110-115 F; loop return alarm floor 120 F (CDC) or 124 F (healthcare/VA), made configurable per occupancy; loop delta-T design 5-10 F (6 F hospital); HPWH storage 150 F+ with swing-tank, loop 115-125 F; thermal-eradication capability 170-180 F in hospitals.
- (Mine) A low return temperature alone is ambiguous across archetypes: in a tempered loop (return into the mixing valve) a 110 F return can be by design (Deppmann example), whereas in an untempered 140 F system the same value is a fault.

### Gaps
- Guideline 12 and ASHRAE Handbook values not verified at primary source; ASPE values not seen.
- No source giving residential-style multifamily operating setpoints for recirc aquastat in practice (ZERH advisory and Deppmann only).
- CDC page content is from a search summary, not read.
- CT Green Bank 30%/60% claim unverified (fetch 404).

## 5. Typical documentation and its availability (riser diagram, schedules, nameplates, balancing report)

### Takeaway
Design standards expect DHW balancing to be a documented TAB/commissioning scope item, with design flows (gpm per balancing valve) on the contract documents and measured flows in the final balance report, but I found no evidence on how often a DHW balance report actually exists. Field evidence points the other way: balancing is hard, pipes are hidden, and thermostatic or automatic balancing valves record no flow data.

### Cited Findings
- Hospital criteria: contract documents carry notes for the balance agency to set each return's flow to the gpm calculated by the designer, computed from piping heat loss at a 6 F allowable drop; separate check, isolation and balancing valves on each return circulating line; balancing valve type per specification section 22 05 23. — [CED M03-039](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/M03-039%20-%20An%20Introduction%20to%20Hospital%20Domestic%20Water%20Systems%20-%20US.pdf) (TRADE adaptation of GOVT criteria; text read)
- Dartmouth campus guideline lists domestic hot water recirculation among systems the TAB contractor must test, adjust and balance, coordinating with the commissioning agent. — [Dartmouth 15990 / 23 05 93](https://www.dartmouth.edu/campus-planning/documents/15990_23_05_93.pdf) (owner standard, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- City of Winnipeg spec requires measuring recirculation flow at every automatic flow-balancing valve and recording it in the balance report, with TAB complete only when the final report is approved. — [Winnipeg spec 230593](https://legacy.winnipeg.ca/finance/findata/matmgt/documents/2012/45-2012/45-2012_Specification_230593.pdf) (owner spec, Canadian, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Manual balancing valves require differential pressure measurement across the orifice and the valve's Cv from the manufacturer spec sheet to infer flow (so a manual-valve balance report is expected to contain per-valve settings and measured dP/gpm); thermostatic valves self-adjust and automatic valves have a fixed cartridge. — PG&E/ACEEE 2024 (text read)
- Balancing is "a very challenging task", systems are "never optimally balanced" (partly promotional source); a design requiring 0.5 gpm per hotel balancing valve could not be achieved by contractors at those low flows. — [ASEE conference paper on thermostatic valves](https://strategy.asee.org/installation-and-data-acquisition-study-to-test-circuit-solver-trv-devices.pdf), [PHCP Pros, bottom lines for balancing valves](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/1867-bottom-lines-for-balancing-valves-in-recirc-systems) (TRADE/MANUFACTURER-affiliated, SNIPPET-ONLY)
- Mechanical-room design documents typically include: sequences with flow diagrams and point lists (Harvard Division 25, SNIPPET-ONLY); UGA Division 22 DHW documentation requirements ask for equipment information, temperatures and recirculation strategy ([UGA Division 22](https://www.architects.uga.edu/sites/default/files/pdf/standards/2025/Division%2022.pdf), SNIPPET-ONLY).
- Retrofit guidance for HPWH: measure loop losses with a flow meter and temperature sensors because the pre-existing data are not available; commissioning tests draw down tanks and confirm HPWH start/stop. — [PNNL BASC](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/central-heat-pump-water-heaters-multifamily-buildings) (page read)
- The ETCC field survey combined field monitoring, loss calculations, expert interviews, and "qualitative system surveys" because building characteristics did not predict losses. — [ETCC survey page](https://etcc-ca.com/reports/multifamily-domestic-hot-water-recirculation-survey) (read)

### Inferences
- (Mine) Where a balancing/TAB report exists it is most likely in the O&M manual or commissioning binder (not on a BAS), and for riser/loop systems with manual balancing valves it should list per-valve setting, dP and gpm. The tool should treat "balance report present" as an intake question and tag its absence as a reason to expect low confidence about riser-level flow causes.
- (Mine) Documentation location by item: riser diagram and plumbing schedule (heater capacity, tank volume, pump gpm/head, mixing valve model) are on the contract drawings (Division 22 sheets); nameplates have model/rating/serial/setpoint limits on the equipment; sequences and point lists are in Division 23/25 BAS submittals; balance report in the TAB/commissioning close-out. No source verified these specific locations as universal.

### Gaps
- No evidence on the actual frequency with which DHW recirculation balance/TAB reports exist (new or existing buildings). All sources are specification expectations or difficulty anecdotes.
- Plumbing balancing is often outside the TAB contractor's scope (plumbing vs mechanical trades); I found no source stating this.
- Two ETCC reports (full survey; lab balancing study) were not opened beyond summary pages; the ETCC survey's full report is behind a form.
- Dartmouth, Winnipeg, Harvard, UGA, Idaho documents are from search snippets only.
