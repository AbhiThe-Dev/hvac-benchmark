# Open / public-domain, commercially usable diagnostic knowledge for hot-water troubleshooting

Research date: 2026-10-07. Not legal advice. "Read" means I extracted the full text of the PDF locally (pypdf) and quoted from that. "SNIPPET-ONLY" means I only saw a search-result summary or a fetch summary and could not open the primary text. Facts, thresholds and procedures can be re-expressed in your own words; copyright attaches to expression, not to a 60 C threshold. I still list each source's terms so you can decide what to copy and what to re-derive.

Bottom line: the open corpus is strong on **temperature thresholds, monitoring checklists and Legionella-driven investigation lists**, and very weak on everything else you asked for. I found **no open source** with (a) a "no hot water / lukewarm / too hot / pump failure" symptom-to-cause table, (b) sensitivity/specificity for any check, or (c) minutes per check or per repair. The best diagnostic-method paper I found (Boppe 2016) is CC BY-NC-ND, so it is a NO for your product.

## Key question 1: Symptom-to-cause tables for the seven symptoms

### Takeaway
No open source I found organises causes by the complaints a tenant or technician reports. The open government corpus is organised around Legionella compliance, so cause lists exist only for "temperature not achieved at sentinel points / positive sample", which maps loosely onto "lukewarm or slow", "recirculation not working", "mixing valve" and "hot/cold crossover". "No hot water", "too hot or fluctuating" and "pump problems" have essentially no open cause table. Consumer and trade symptom tables exist but are not licensed for reuse.

### Cited Findings
- HSG274 Part 2 gives a diagnostic rule for slow warm-up: when return-leg or outlet temperature rises slowly, confirm the outlet is on a long leg and "not that the flow and return has failed in that local area" — [HSE HSG274 Part 2, Table 2.1](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg274part2.pdf)
- HSG274 Part 2 Fig. 2.11 states that where the hot-water return is local to the outlet, a much quicker response (under 30 s, typically 10-20 s) is expected, and failing to achieve it "may indicate a HWS return fault"; a failed return creates a dead leg — [HSE HSG274 Part 2](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg274part2.pdf)
- HSG274 says pump design and correct commissioning of balancing valves are the key issues for flow through all parts, especially return legs, and that balancing is "critical" to avoid long stagnant, cooler pipe lengths — [HSE HSG274 Part 2](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg274part2.pdf)
- HTM 04-01 Part B, in its positive-sample response, lists things to investigate: usage frequency, outlet corrosion and scale, local heat gain, local dead ends, "cross flow between hot and cold and vice versa", and "localised failure of the HWS return" — [DH HTM 04-01 Part B](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/DH_HTM_0401_PART_B_acc.pdf)
- HTM 04-01 Part B also says to check that the hot port of a mixing tap is not fed by an upstream TMV (creates a dead leg), and that TMVs should not be installed in series (para 6.40, 7.42, Fig. D3) — [DH HTM 04-01 Part B](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/DH_HTM_0401_PART_B_acc.pdf)
- HTM 04-01 Part B remedial ladder for a positive outlet: review control measures (flow/return temps, usage, flushing), change outlet/hose, change or remove the washbasin TMV, full plumbing investigation (dead legs, flexibles, recent works), then consider disinfection unit, then inform the water safety group — [DH HTM 04-01 Part B](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/DH_HTM_0401_PART_B_acc.pdf)
- HSG274 TMV considerations: fit TMVs close to the point of use; one TMV serving several taps raises risk; scale in TMVs and pipework matters at higher temperatures; scald-risk locations should get fail-safe, preset type 3 TMVs; TMVs need a minimum hot-to-mixed temperature differential to work — [HSE HSG274 Part 2](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg274part2.pdf)
- CDC toolkit design and maintenance items from which causes of lost temperature can be inferred (no dedicated troubleshooting section): no continuous recirculation, dead legs, missing or undersized pumps, uninsulated pipe, unmaintained mixing valves — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/toolkit/potable-water-systems-module.html) (the cause framing is my inference from design advice, as the page states causes only implicitly)
- Boppe 2016 (NOT licensed for you, listed so you know the content exists): root cause of poor temperature in a 10-storey hospital was deficient hydraulic distribution, with defective devices also playing a role; the specific defective-device finding was a shower mixing valve that left hot and cold connected when improperly closed; locally added secondary pumps were a failed first fix — [Boppe et al. 2016](https://publications.polymtl.ca/4798/1/2016_Boppe_Investigative_approach_improve_hot_water.pdf)
- Spanish hospital (CC BY): temperature fell with distance (heat loss), galvanised-iron pipe in one circuit was a problem; replacing it with PVC and raising tank setpoint to reach about 55 C at the return stabilised temperatures — [Sci. Rep. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7820426)
- Heating-element, thermostat and pilot-light causes for "no hot water" are documented only on commercial and consumer pages (e.g., Chadwell Supply, HomeTips, Inspectapedia) that are not open — SNIPPET-ONLY, e.g. [Inspectapedia](https://inspectapedia.com/plumbing/Hot_Water_Lost.php)

### Inferences
- A usable open cause list for your five circulation/temperature symptoms can be assembled from HSG274 Fig 2.11 + HTM 04-01 investigation list + CDC design items, but it will be organised by "where the temperature fails" (heater flow, return, loop branch, outlet, TMV) rather than by tenant complaint.
- For "no hot water", "too hot or fluctuating", and "pump problems", you would need manufacturer manuals (check licences individually), a paid book, or your own domain expert.
- The crossover cause is thinly covered in open sources: HTM names "cross flow" as an item to investigate; the mechanism (failed check valve / improperly closed mixing valve plus favorable pressure) appears in full only in the NC-ND Boppe paper.

### Gaps
- No open symptom-to-cause table for "no hot water" or "pump problems" found.
- Did not open GSA, HUD/NSPIRE, EPA, DOE Energy Saver, NAVFAC P-710/MO-405, Army TM 5-610 or the DTIC CAIS plumbing manual, so I cannot say they lack such tables.

## Key question 2: Quantitative thresholds, and mandatory vs advisory

### Takeaway
The thresholds agree across sources within a narrow band: storage 60 C (140 F); circulating return/distribution floor 49-51 C (120-124 F); outlet 50 C (55 C in UK healthcare) within one minute; cold below 20 C; scald-protected outlet at or below 43-44 C. Only VHA treats numbers as mandatory ("must") and the UK documents are guidance/ACOP, so mandatory status depends on jurisdiction. No source gives delta-T, flow-rate or pressure thresholds for diagnosis, except one velocity figure in the NC-ND paper.

### Cited Findings
- UK HSG274 Part 2: store at minimum 60 C, distribute so outlets reach minimum 50 C within one minute (55 C in healthcare); monthly check of calorifier flow (modulate as close to 60 C as practicable without going below) and return (not below 50 C); return legs of principal loops monthly at minimum 50 C (surface measurement on metal pipe allowed); subordinate loops quarterly; cold water below 20 C within two minutes of running; TMVs annual maintenance by competent person; calorifier inspection annually — [HSE HSG274 Part 2, Table 2.1 and para 2.82](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg274part2.pdf)
- HSG274 is guidance: "Following the guidance is not compulsory, unless specifically stated"; following it is normally enough to comply with the law — [HSE HSG274 Part 2, p.2](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg274part2.pdf)
- HTM 04-01 Part B: calorifier flow minimum 60 C; return connection minimum 50 C; healthcare monitoring table uses 55 C for sentinel and return legs; calorifier drain flushing quarterly; "necessary to maintain the balance of flows to individual pipe branches" to achieve return temperature (paras 7.32-7.34, monitoring table) — [DH HTM 04-01 Part B](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/DH_HTM_0401_PART_B_acc.pdf)
- VHA 1061 (mandatory for VHA): storage tanks at least 140 F; instantaneous/semi-instantaneous heat exchanger discharge at least 130 F; distribution piping no lower than 124 F ahead of any mixing valve (and kept as low as achieves that, to limit scald); cold water at or below 67 F; continuous temperature monitoring at incoming supply, storage tanks, heater discharge, circulation-loop returns and representative remote points, reviewed at least weekly; outlet mixing valves limiting to 110 F (43.3 C); mixing valves tested and serviced at least annually; thermal remediation option 160-170 F; emergency showers/eyewash 60-100 F — [VHA Directive 1061, App. A and B](https://www.va.gov/vhapublications/ViewPublication.asp?pub_ID=9181)
- CDC: store above 140 F (60 C); keep circulating hot water at or above 120 F (49 C), recirculating continuously where possible; TMVs as near the fixture as possible; flush low-flow runs and dead legs at least weekly; thermal shock not recommended — [CDC toolkit](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/toolkit/potable-water-systems-module.html)
- UFC 3-420-01 (2021, copy marked CANCELLED): store at 140 F minimum; master mixing valve to 131 F per ASSE 1017; recirculation loop at least 122 F; return at least 122 F at the heater cold inlet/master mixing valve; strainers upstream of the TMV on both hot and cold — [UFC 3-420-01](https://www.wbdg.org/api/documents/media/4a306cd6-d127-4f00-a4a5-67c63670c6ed/file)
- OSHA healthcare eTool: store at 140 F, deliver no less than 122 F to outlets, periodic 158 F or chlorination where not retrofittable — SNIPPET-ONLY, [OSHA eTool](https://obis.osha.gov/SLTC/etools/hospital/hazards/leg/leg.html)
- Boppe 2016 (NOT licensed): target velocity at least 0.2 m/s in return loops; heater outlet at least 60 C; faucet to reach 55 C within 1-2 min; compare pressure differential between hot feed and return as a diagnostic step; one case showed riser median 31.5 C with the shower valve left open vs 39.0 C closed, with cold side 111 psi vs hot 109 psi — [Boppe et al. 2016](https://publications.polymtl.ca/4798/1/2016_Boppe_Investigative_approach_improve_hot_water.pdf)
- Spread to note: the return-loop floor is 120 F (CDC), 122 F (UFC, HSG 50 C, OSHA), 124 F (VHA), and UK healthcare uses 55 C (131 F) at return legs — sources above.

### Inferences
- Use 49-50 C as the generic circulating floor and 60 C as the generator floor; carry jurisdiction as a parameter (healthcare vs not; VHA vs UK vs ASHRAE).
- "Mandatory" is a property of the adopting authority, not of the number. Of the sources above, only VHA's directive is mandatory in its own terms (for VHA buildings). HSG274 is advisory; L8 ACOP (not read) has special legal status in the UK.

### Gaps
- No open delta-T (supply minus return) threshold, pump head/flow acceptance range, or TMV mixed-water tolerance (e.g., plus/minus degrees at a given flow) found. The UK TMV tolerance sits in BS EN 1111/1287/NHS D08, not opened here and likely not open. Did not obtain VA Plumbing Design Manual or spec 22 11 23.
- HSG274 Table 2.2/2.3 Legionella action levels were located but not extracted.

## Key question 3: Time / labor standards

### Takeaway
I found **no open per-check or per-repair time standard** for hot-water systems. The only open maintenance-cost data is dollars per PM in a 2025 Naval Postgraduate School thesis, which does not report hours. Lead documents that could contain labor-hour norms (AF M85-43, NAVFAC P-710, Army TM 5-610) could not be opened.

### Cited Findings
- NAVFAC actual PM cost per task (Dahlgren buildings, FY20-24): D2020906 circulating pump, n=184 PMs, mean $60.59, median $38.89, max $243.76; D2020 domestic water distribution overall n=186, mean $61.54 — [NPS-CE-25-452, Table 6](https://dair.nps.edu/bitstream/123456789/5443/1/NPS-CE-25-452.pdf)
- The same thesis reports a modelled (RS Means/OEM) circulating pump PM at about $484.72 mean (n=23), and NAVFAC-standard PMs averaging $173.82 per task vs $439.01 OEM-guided overall; NAVFAC schedules circulating pump, storage tank and backflow PMs at 1 per year — [NPS-CE-25-452, Tables 9 and 11, abstract](https://dair.nps.edu/bitstream/123456789/5443/1/NPS-CE-25-452.pdf). The RS Means-derived numbers are Gordian data; do not reuse.
- The thesis states the underlying NAVFAC MAXIMO data included estimated and actual labor hours for each PM, but the thesis publishes dollars, not hours — [NPS-CE-25-452, data section](https://dair.nps.edu/bitstream/123456789/5443/1/NPS-CE-25-452.pdf)
- HSG274 gives frequencies (monthly, quarterly, annual) but no durations — [HSE HSG274 Part 2](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg274part2.pdf)
- Commercial labor figures exist (e.g., 1-2 hours for a residential heater maintenance visit, 1.5-5 hours for shower mixing valve replacement) but come from contractor marketing pages and paid estimators (BNi, RS Means) — SNIPPET-ONLY, not suitable: [BNi estimator](https://www.bnibooks.com/products/2026-national-plumbing-hvac-estimator)
- Leads reported by search but not opened: Air Force labor-hour handbook AF M85-43 ([GlobalSpec](https://standards.globalspec.com/std/272393/af-m85-43), 403), NAVFAC P-710 and Army TM 5-610 (EverySpec index; see https://everyspec.com/FSC-CODE/FACR/?page=3), DTIC CAIS Building Plumbing manual (https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA305520.pdf, 403)

### Inferences
- Wage-rate-free time estimates would need either a technician-time study of your own, or a Freedom of Information style request for NAVFAC MAXIMO estimated/actual hours per PM code. The latter would be a federal record with no copyright.
- Any dollars-to-hours conversion from the NPS data would need an assumed loaded labor rate and a split between labor and material that the thesis does not give.

### Gaps
- Per-check minutes (measure return temperature, test check valve, test TMV, balance branch) and per-repair hours (replace pump, replace TMV cartridge, replace check valve): not found in any open source.

## Key question 4: Evidence on how reliably each check separates causes

### Takeaway
None. No open source reports sensitivity, specificity or likelihood ratios for a diagnostic check on a hot-water fault. What exists is outcome evidence that a temperature surrogate (distal temperature at or above 55-60 C) tracks Legionella presence, plus qualitative statements of what a slow rise implies.

### Cited Findings
- In a Spanish hospital, samples under 60 C were Legionella-positive 54.05% of the time vs 14.13% at or above 60 C (p < 0.0001); after repairs, one sample was under 60 C and none were positive — [Sci. Rep. 2021 (CC BY 4.0)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7820426)
- Boppe 2016 reports L. pneumophila concentration correlated with the maximum tap temperature, with a significant decrease at 55 C or above; after balancing, taps reaching 55 C within 2 min rose from 11% to 74% and prevalence fell from 93.1% to 46.1% at 4 weeks — [Boppe et al. 2016 (CC BY-NC-ND, NOT licensed for you)](https://publications.polymtl.ca/4798/1/2016_Boppe_Investigative_approach_improve_hot_water.pdf)
- HSG274 offers qualitative discrimination only: a response under 30 s at a local outlet suggests return is working, slow rise means long leg or failed return and needs confirmation — [HSE HSG274 Part 2](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg274part2.pdf)
- HTM 04-01 notes that outlets with blended water, TMVs, showers or hoses are poor locations for systemic Legionella testing, since the result reflects local conditions — [DH HTM 04-01 Part B](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/DH_HTM_0401_PART_B_acc.pdf)
- A 220-TMV continuous temperature logging study (MDPI) compared flow events with microbial results — SNIPPET-ONLY; results not read — [PMC6518245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6518245/)

### Inferences
- These show that "distal temperature below about 55-60 C" is a useful risk marker, not that a particular check discriminates between pump failure, balancing, stuck check valve and so on. The discrimination strengths your model needs will have to come from expert elicitation or your own field data.
- The Boppe stepwise order (heater outlet, remove local pumps, main pump velocity, hot-feed vs return pressure differential, balance via flow restriction on privileged loops, dead legs, flow inversion, point-of-use profiles) is a good ordering of checks, but it is NC-ND; you could ask the authors/Elsevier for a licence.

### Gaps
- No lab validation of a cold-feed-isolated crossover test or any temperature-based surrogate test found in open literature during this search (I ran one literature query on TMVs/temperature; I did not search specifically for crossover test papers).
- PMC6518245 results unread; its licence unconfirmed.
