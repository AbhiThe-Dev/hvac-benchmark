## Q0. Headline numbers, sources and commercial-use verdicts (summary table)

### Takeaway
No open dataset gives a DHW-specific BAS point census. The best proxies are (a) CBECS 2018 microdata, which gives building-level central vs point-of-use water heating, fuel, and a building-level BAS flag (public domain, YES), and (b) NYC Local Law 87 audit data, which is the only open dataset I found that records, per audited building, the SHW system type and whether its controls are "EMS/BMS" (terms ambiguous, leaning usable). CBECS says nothing about storage vs instantaneous, recirculation, or which points are trended, so the data-tier fractions for DHW points have to be inferred from BAS presence, which is a ceiling and not a measurement.

### Inferences
- The key product-relevant read of CBECS 2018: of buildings that heat water at all, about 83% have a central heater (alone or with point-of-use), but only about 16% of water-heated buildings are central AND have a BAS (about 39% by floorspace). So for the typical (by count) building the model should assume "no BAS access to DHW points" and plan around gauges, local controller displays and technician measurements; BAS-based tiers only dominate by floorspace and in buildings above about 50k sf (roughly 50-57% have a BAS).
- BAS presence is an upper bound on DHW point availability. Nothing in the CBECS item says DHW points are on the BAS; the question is about a BAS that controls heating, A/C, or lighting (see Q2).

### Gaps
- No open source gives storage vs instantaneous, or presence of a recirculation loop, per building at the national level (see Q1).

## Q1. How common is each kind of DHW equipment (central vs distributed, fuel, storage vs instantaneous, recirculation, heat pump), by building type and size?

### Takeaway
Central vs point-of-use and fuel are well covered by CBECS 2012/2018 (public domain); storage vs tankless, recirculation and heat pump are not in CBECS microdata and are only available regionally (NEEA, capacity-weighted) or for NYC large buildings (LL87). Multifamily is outside CBECS; RECS 2020 covers apartments by water-heater fuel/size but my tools could not confirm whether a heater is shared by the building.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For a product keyed to hot-water complaints, the dominant scenario in count terms is a small building with a single (central, per CBECS definition) tank heater and no BAS; in floorspace terms, large buildings with central plant and BAS dominate (CBECS: central-only is 67% of water-heated floorspace and "both" is another 16%).
- NYC large multifamily is structurally different from the CBECS national picture: the majority of LL87 multifamily buildings draw DHW from the main heating plant, so the heater outlet temperature, mixing valve and return temperature may sit on a heating-plant controller (if any) and not on a dedicated water heater.
- Applying LL87 shares nationally would be wrong (NYC steam-boiler stock, large buildings only, self-selected filers); use it only to illustrate the large-multifamily tier.

The 2018 codebook has no heat-pump-water-heater variable (`HPHWTR` in 2018 means water loop heat pump).
- Central vs in-unit DHW for multifamily at national level: not found. RECS 2020 `H2OAPT`/`H2OMAIN` codebook not retrieved.
- Table B43 (2018 water-heating equipment) did not render through my fetch tool (it returned the EIA landing page), so I did not quote EIA's own published 2018 table; my own computation from microdata is substituted and matched B21 only for the total.
- NEEA CBSA 4 and RBSA data files, ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager technical reference, DOE BPD, LBNL Commercial Building Energy Data, CEC Title 24 research, NBI, Boston BERDO, Seattle/Washington/Chicago benchmarking, NYSERDA multifamily and HUD audit data: not examined in this pass (time and tool limits). From general knowledge, benchmarking datasets such as LL84/BERDO carry energy and area but not DHW equipment lists; I have not verified this and it should be checked before relying on it.

## Q2. What share of buildings have a BAS/EMCS, or temperature/flow monitoring of DHW?

### Takeaway
BAS prevalence is strongly size-dependent (about 11% of water-heated buildings under 5k sf to about 83% over 1M sf in CBECS 2018) and is a building-level flag that does not say whether DHW points are on it. The only open DHW-specific control data I found is LL87: 4.3% of NYC audited buildings with SHW report EMS/BMS-controlled SHW, versus about 29% with aquastat control; the only field-study source (Minnesota CEE) says recirculation pumps are controlled by BAS or timers, with about half to two thirds of sampled systems on time-of-day controls.

### Cited Findings
- CBECS 2018 BAS question wording: "Does this building have a building automation system, which may also be referred to as BAS, smart building controls, an energy management and control system, or EMCS?" with follow-ups on whether it controls heating (`EMCSHT`), A/C (`EMCSCL`) or lighting (`EMCSLT`). DHW is not an item. `SMRTTHRM` asks about smart/internet-connected thermostats. — [2018 CBECS codebook](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/commercial/data/2018/xls/2018microdata_codebook.xlsx)
- 2012 CBECS footnote: "In earlier CBECS publications, BAS was referred to as Energy Management and Control System (EMCS)." — [EIA CBECS 2012 Table B42 notes](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/commercial/data/2012/bc/cfm/b42.php). The EMCS flag is therefore the same concept across 2012 and 2018, though the 2018 questionnaire added the heating/cooling/lighting follow-ups and wording.
- Results **[computed, CBECS 2018]**: BAS = Yes for 18.5% of all buildings (RSE 6.4%, n=6,193 valid responses; 243 buildings have a missing `EMCS` and are excluded from BAS denominators) and 44.5% of floorspace; 19.6% of water-heated buildings; 19.1% of centralized-or-both buildings and 21.8% of point-of-use-only buildings; 47.2% / 40.1% of floorspace respectively. BAS also controls heating in 15.2% of water-heated buildings that are centralized or both. By building type (BAS share of water-heated buildings, RSE): Education 42.4% (9%), Strip mall 38.1% (15%), Inpatient health 71.8% (12%), Office 20.0% (11%), Lodging 18.2% (26%), Outpatient health 19.8% (19%), Public assembly 19.5% (13%), Religious worship 22.5% (15%), Food service 17.3% (20%), Retail non-mall 15.2% (25%), Nursing 9.7% (48%, weak), Nonrefrigerated warehouse 8.4% (35%), Service 5.4% (28%). Size table: see Q0 row 9. 2012 equivalents for comparison **[computed]**: BAS in 15.9% of buildings, 44.8% of floorspace; central-or-both AND BAS 14.7% of water-heated buildings; by size band of water-heated buildings <=5k sf 8.5%, 5k-50k 18.1%, >50k 61.5% BAS.
- LL87 SHW controls **[computed, latest submission per audit ID, n about 7,484-7,488 with SHW system and a valid response]**: Aquastat-based 29.1%, demand-based 8.5%, EMS/BMS 4.3% (any of up to several SHW systems), other 1.7%, no controls 1.6%, time-based 0.6% (flags can overlap). Building-level BAS flag = Yes 5.4%. Among BAS = Yes buildings (n=404), 23.5% also flag EMS/BMS on SHW; among BAS = No (n=7,082), 3.2% flag EMS/BMS on SHW (an internal inconsistency in auditor-entered data, which shows the field is noisy). By SHW type, EMS/BMS on SHW: direct-fired instantaneous 2.7%, direct-fired storage 2.1%, indirect-fired instantaneous 5.2%, indirect-fired storage 4.5%, "Plant" 5.3%, other 1.3%. Demand-based controls are most common for direct-fired instantaneous (27.8%). The share of audited buildings whose "Building Automation System: Heating Plant 1" flag is yes is 30.0% (n=6,788), much higher than the building-level BAS flag (5.4%), so LL87's building-level BAS flag likely understates BAS on the heating plant; I have not resolved why. — [NYC Open Data LL87](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/au6c-jqvf); the data dictionary is linked from that page as `Data_Dictionary_Local_law_87_2019_2024.xlsx`.
- LL87 scope: LL87 requires energy audits and retro-commissioning for buildings over 50,000 gross sf on a 10-year cycle (from a search summary of the DOB/Audit Template guidance; 3,999 of 7,484 analyzed rows have GFA under 100k sf, consistent with a mix just over the threshold). Dataset page notes the "Column Information" tab and says the data were collected through DOE's Audit Template. — [data.gov catalog record](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/ll87-energy-audit-data)
- Field study (Minnesota CEE, 2016 deck): "Recirculation pumps controllers by building automation systems or timers to turn off in periods of low use"; schools: most systems (about 66%) had time-of-day controls and ran 18.75 hours per day on average; commercial buildings: half of systems had time-of-day controls, 16 hours per day when controlled; several buildings' time-of-day controllers were adjusted or disabled for early/late workers; hospitality: all surveyed and monitored systems ran 24/7, one aquastat was set too high to turn the pump off; in about 1/3 of measured systems return water temperature exceeded 125 F; average loop temperature drop 15 F in idle periods and up to 30 F in some small buildings. — [Schoenbauer, ACEEE HWF 2016](https://aceee.org/sites/default/files/files/pdf/conferences/hwf/2016/Schoenbauer_Session5D_HWF16_2.23.16.pdf) (text extracted from the PDF; small sample, 14 buildings had runtime data and 6 were fully monitored).
- Same project's final report (2018): demand controller trial at six sites (two middle schools, one capitol administration building, three hotels) found 13% average savings in energy to heat water at the four sites fully analyzed; at one commercial site, the controller "continually tripped sensors and alarms" on the existing BAS, which assumed the recirculation pump would run 24/7, and staff could not fully adjust the alarms. — [CEE final report, June 2018](https://www.mncee.org/sites/default/files/report-files/522971.pdf)
- A California survey of multifamily recirculation (field monitoring, still listed as active) reports recirculation losses of 24.1-99.9 W per apartment (mean 62.5) and says measured losses were 1.1-2.3 times idealized calculations. The page gives no prevalence of central DHW or controls and the download requires login and terms acceptance. — [ETCC multifamily DHW recirculation survey](https://www.etcc-ca.com/reports/multifamily-domestic-hot-water-recirculation-survey)

The NYC terms page (nyc.gov/html/data/terms.html) returned 404 when I fetched it directly, so I could not read the current text. Verdict UNCLEAR, leaning YES (public-sector open data with no commercial-use bar found); confirm with the city's open data office. — [search summary of NYC Open Data terms](https://www.nyc.gov/html/data/terms.html), [OSM imports thread](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/imports/2011-November/001172.html)
- Minnesota CEE report: published for the Minnesota Department of Commerce CARD program (ratepayer funded); no license stated in the PDF I extracted. Verdict UNCLEAR (cite, do not copy figures wholesale).
- ETCC reports: copyright 2026 Energy Transition Coordinating Council, ratepayer funded, login and terms required. Verdict UNCLEAR.

### Inferences
- A defensible data-tier prior for a typical engineer: Tier "BAS with DHW points trended" is no more than the BAS share (about 11% of small and about 57% of large water-heated buildings in CBECS) and is probably much lower for DHW specifically, since in LL87 (large buildings) only 4.3% of buildings flag EMS/BMS-controlled SHW even though 30% flag BAS on the heating plant.
- The most common "control" on DHW equipment is local: aquastat (29% in LL87) and time clocks (about half to two thirds of recirculation loops in the small Minnesota sample), which supports a "local gauges / local controller only" default tier.
- A technician in a typical building should therefore be assumed to supply: heater outlet temperature from a local gauge or controller display, mixing valve outlet temperature from a spot reading, pump on/off status from observation, and no trended return temperature. This is an inference, not a measured census.

### Gaps
- No open dataset reports the availability of specific DHW points (heater outlet temperature, recirculation return temperature, mixing valve outlet temperature, pump status, alarm) on BAS vs local controller vs gauge. I looked for a DHW-specific BAS point census in CBECS, LL87, NEEA CBSA 4 (report), the Minnesota CEE work, the ETCC page, Brick/Mortar models, and web searches; none provides one.
- Share of buildings with temperature or flow monitoring of DHW: not found in any open source I could access.
- NEEA CBSA 4 raw data may contain BAS/controls and water heater tables; not accessible without the form and agreeing to terms, and the terms bar sharing.
- ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager, DOE BPD, LBNL Commercial Building Energy Data, CEC Title 24/CASE reports, NBI, Boston BERDO, Seattle/Washington/Chicago, NYSERDA multifamily and HUD datasets were not examined; paywalled market reports (Navigant/Guidehouse BAS penetration) were not accessed and should be marked NO for commercial use unless licensed.

## Q3. Is there a census of BAS points for DHW in open building datasets (Brick, Haystack, Mortar, BDG2, Open Building Models)?

### Takeaway
Brick has a rich DHW vocabulary (so a DHW point schema exists), but the one large open set of real Brick models I could inspect (the Mortar graphs on Hugging Face) contains no DHW equipment or points at all, so there is no open census of real DHW point availability.

### Cited Findings
- I downloaded Brick v1.4.3 `Brick.ttl` and listed matching classes **[computed]**. DHW-related classes present include: Domestic_Hot_Water_System, Domestic_Hot_Water_System_Enable_Command, Domestic_Hot_Water_Supply_Temperature_Sensor / _Setpoint, Domestic_Hot_Water_Discharge_Temperature_Sensor / _Setpoint, Domestic_Hot_Water_Temperature_Sensor / _Setpoint, Entering_ and Leaving_Domestic_Hot_Water_Temperature_Sensor / _Setpoint, Domestic_Hot_Water_Differential_Pressure_Sensor / _Setpoint, Domestic_Hot_Water_Circulator_Pump, Domestic_Hot_Water_Valve, Hot_Water_Return_Temperature_Sensor, Hot_Water_Storage_Tank, Water_Heater, Mixing_Valve, Thermostatic_Mixing_Valve, Electronic_Mixing_Valve, Hot_Water_Usage_Sensor, Hot_Water_Meter, Hot_Water_Booster_Pump. (The release contains a Domestic_Hot_Water class and Hot_Water_Circulator_Pump as well.) A DHW recirculation return temperature has no dedicated "Domestic_Hot_Water_Return_Temperature_Sensor" class in this grep; the generic Hot_Water_Return_Temperature_Sensor is the closest. — [Brick v1.4.3 Brick.ttl](https://github.com/BrickSchema/Brick/releases/download/v1.4.3/Brick.ttl). The file's license pointer is `https://github.com/BrickSchema/brick/blob/master/LICENSE` (BSD-style).
- Mortar: the LBNL paper describes a testbed spanning 90 buildings and over 9.1 billion data points described with Brick. — [Mortar paper page, LBNL](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/publications/mortar-open-testbed-portable-building) (via search summary). The dataset card says "Mortar is a public dataset of anonymized Brick models and timeseries data from real buildings," and cites commercial and institutional buildings with temperature, occupancy, luminance and HVAC readings. — [gtfierro/mortar README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/gtfierro/mortar/resolve/main/README.md)
- Brick models on Hugging Face (`gtfierro/mortargraphs`, 3.6 MB, 44 `bldg*.ttl` files that I downloaded) **[computed]**: 15,089 typed `a brick:` triples, dominated by Zone_Air_Temperature_Sensor (2,085), VAV (2,056), Target_Zone_Air_Temperature_Setpoint (1,631), Command (1,181), Supply_Air_Temperature_Sensor (951). Zero occurrences of any Domestic_Hot_Water, Water_Heater, Hot_Water_System, Mixing_Valve, Hot_Water_Storage or Circulator class. The only hot-water content is in a single file: one `brick:Boiler` with Leaving_Hot_Water_Flow_Sensor, Leaving_Hot_Water_Temperature_Sensor and an Entering_Water_Temperature_Sensor (a boiler loop, not a DHW heater). The dataset page and the Hugging Face API list no license, and the Mortar card says "No dataset card yet" for the graphs repo. — [mortargraphs repo](https://huggingface.co/datasets/gtfierro/mortargraphs)
- The task brief refers to 91 buildings; the paper I found says 90. I did not reconcile the difference, nor test whether the 44 graph files are the full Mortar set.

### Inferences
- Brick models of real buildings are built from HVAC-centric BAS points (VAV, AHU, chillers), so DHW is probably absent because DHW is rarely on the BAS, but that is also consistent with modelers only tagging HVAC. The absence is evidence the points are rare in those buildings but is not a prevalence number.
- Brick's vocabulary can be used as the target schema for the product's input tiers (outlet temperature, return temperature, mixing valve, pump status), with BSD attribution.

### Gaps
- Not examined: BDG2, Haystack sample projects, "Buildings-900K"/Open Building Models, NREL ComStock service-water-heating assumptions, BuildingSync examples, LBNL Smarter Small Buildings data. My ComStock search returned only the reference-doc landing pages (e.g., [NREL ComStock Reference Documentation v1](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy23osti/83819.pdf)), so I have no verified statement of how ComStock assigns central vs distributed water heating or its license. ComStock is derived from CBECS, so it would not be independent evidence.
- Mortar timeseries and the full 90-building model set were not opened beyond the 44 TTL files; some buildings may include DHW and not appear in this subset.
- The cited Mortar dataset license is not stated; treat reuse as NO until the maintainer confirms.
