## 1. Categorized map: what exists, who buys it, how it is sold, and whether it diagnoses or only detects/alerts/reports

### Takeaway
The electrical space is dense with detect/alert/report products (EPMS, circuit-level monitoring, thermal inspection, breakers with trip codes) sold to facility/engineering teams or OEM channels, and the fastest-growing money is in data centers and residential electrification, not commercial-building technician troubleshooting. The only products I found that explicitly rank causes and name the next test are text-based LLM assistants aimed at (mostly UK/residential) electricians, not tools using building meter/BAS data.

### Inferences
- Detect/alert/report dominates. Diagnostic claims cluster in three forms: (1) hardware discriminators (Square D TSD trip-timing, Eaton blink codes, Intelli-Arc RF/leakage readings), (2) OEM/expert service layers (Schneider Power Advisor service plan, Dranetz AnswerModules), (3) chat LLMs. None of the first two is symptom-in, ranked-next-test-out for a commercial building.
- Money and M&A focus is on data centers (Verdigris, Southwire, ETAP/NVIDIA, Fluke Azima for backup power) and residential electrification (Span, Lumin, Sense). Commercial-building electrical troubleshooting did not appear as a funded category in what I searched.
- Verdigris moving from "commercial buildings" to "AI-scale data center infrastructure" suggests the general-commercial-building circuit-monitoring market was harder to monetize. This is an inference from its repositioning, not a stated reason.

## 2. Does any competitor guide a technician through WHICH TEST/CHECK TO DO NEXT for an electrical symptom, ranked by likelihood/cost?

### Takeaway
Yes, in marketing language: Elec-Mate (UK, BS 7671, subscription) and Jenova's Electrician's Assistant explicitly claim ranked likely causes plus the specific test to confirm or eliminate each. Both are text/photo LLM chat tools using the technician's typed inputs, not meter/BAS/panel data, and neither mentions cost-ranking. I found no such guidance in any EPMS, FDD, CMMS or smart-panel product I checked.

### Cited Findings
- **Elec-Mate (UK).** Quoted from page: the tool "ranks the probable causes by likelihood based on the symptom pattern, the circuit type, and historical fault data"; "For each probable cause, the AI 'recommends the specific test to confirm or eliminate it'"; tests sequenced as "the most efficient diagnostic path — starting with the simplest, quickest tests." Users "describe the fault in plain English." Pricing "from £6.99/mo" after 7-day trial; "built to BS 7671:2018+A4:2026"; "2,100+ UK electricians." — [Elec-Mate AI fault finding guide](https://www.elec-mate.com/guides/ai-electrical-fault-finding) [FETCHED]
- **Jenova Electrician's Assistant.** "ranked failure modes from symptoms, wiring era, and actual measurements"; asks for specific voltage readings before opening a box; measurement-first sequence "source verification, protection-device status, circuit tracing, connection integrity, then load assessment." — [Jenova](https://www.jenova.ai/en/resources/ai-electricians-assistant) [FETCHED]
- **ServiceTitan Atlas** "identify likely causes, and follow guided repair steps," but supported trades on the help page are Commercial HVAC, Residential HVAC and Plumbing (no electrical) and it is technician-initiated Q&A. — [ServiceTitan help](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/use-atlas-for-troubleshooting-with-field-pro-in-mobile) [FETCHED]
- **Square D TSD** is a physical trip-timing procedure that narrows fault class (ground fault vs arc fault vs overload/short) on specific breakers; procedural, not software-ranked. — [Schneider FAQ FA125415](https://www.se.com/us/en/faqs/FA125415/) [FETCHED]
- **Siemens Intelli-Arc** supplies measurements and a stored trip event for nuisance AFCI/GFCI trips; Siemens itself says it is "not a replacement for good troubleshooting techniques." Companion app has "step-by-step 3D installation and usage tutorials" (about the tool, not fault logic) and lets users "share data directly with Siemens technical support for deeper analysis." — [Trade Hounds](https://www.tradehounds.com/news/siemens-intelli-arc-troubleshoot-tripping-breakers) [FETCHED]
- **Fluke 1770**: guided instrument setup and wiring correction; blog does not give next-test recommendations beyond reports and analysis. — [Fluke blog](https://www.fluke.com/en-us/learn/blog/power-quality/troubleshooting-the-most-common-power-quality-problems) [FETCHED]
- **Verdigris** homepage: "prioritized alerts" and "validated operating limits" but no technician next-check guidance in the page text returned. — [verdigris.co](https://www.verdigris.co/) [FETCHED]
- Fluke Azima DLI (vibration): "prioritized, actionable repair recommendations" for rotating machinery, not electrical-distribution symptoms. — [Fluke AI blog](https://www.fluke.com/en-us/learn/blog/vibration/keeping-uptime-alive-after-experts-retire-the-role-of-ai-in-data-centers) [FETCHED]

### Inferences
- Elec-Mate/Jenova are the nearest "which test next, ranked" claims, but they differ from the requester's HVAC tool in three ways: no building-system data (typed symptoms only), no stated cost model, no evidence of calibration or evaluation. I verified marketing copy only, not behavior.
- The sequential-testing-with-quantified-uncertainty approach (check cost, likelihood, stopping rule) was not described by any product I read. This is "not seen on the pages I checked," not proof nobody does it internally.

### Gaps
- Did not test Elec-Mate or Jenova with real inputs, so I cannot say how good the ranking is or whether it uses cost.
- Did not check Schneider/Eaton/ABB/Siemens service-technician apps (e.g., field-service mobile apps for their gear) or Fluke's AI features in Fluke Connect beyond snippets.
- Did not search LinkedIn job posts or Crunchbase for stealth startups in "electrical troubleshooting copilots for commercial facilities."
