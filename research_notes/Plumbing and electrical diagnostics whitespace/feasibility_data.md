# Technical feasibility and data availability for extending a symptom-scoped, data-driven diagnostic tool from HVAC to building plumbing and electrical systems (as of Oct 2026)

Provenance conventions used below:
- "DIRECT" = I downloaded or queried the primary artifact myself on 2026-10-06 (ontology .ttl files, NYC Open Data API) and counted/grepped it. Highest confidence.
- "FETCHED" = a page was fetched, but the content passed through a summarizer, so details could be lossy.
- "SNIPPET" = only seen in a search-result summary, not read in full. Treat as unverified until checked.
- "INFERENCE" = my reasoning, not a sourced fact.
- Search budget ran out partway through the session. Several ScienceDirect, MDPI, Nature and ASCE pages returned 403 or a login redirect, so some items stay at SNIPPET level.

## 1. Ontologies and standards: how well do they model electrical distribution and plumbing/water, and is there a "feeds" equivalent?

### Takeaway
Every major standard has a directed "flows from A to B" relation that is medium-agnostic, so the HVAC "what feeds what" map can be expressed for electricity and water. Brick and RealEstateCore 4.x give equipment-level coverage of the electrical chain (transformer, switchgear, breaker panel, circuit breaker, meter) and decent DHW/valve/pump/meter coverage. They have no first-class pipe, wire/circuit, fixture or branch objects. ASHRAE 223P is the most expressive for physical connections (Pipe, Conductor, Faucet, Sink, Shower, FlushToilet, ElectricityBreaker, Domain-Plumbing), but it lacks panel and water-heater classes and is not point-centric.

### Cited Findings

**Brick (DIRECT read of the nightly Brick.ttl, which carries owl:versionInfo "1.5.0", 1,466 owl:Class entries)**
- Source file: [Brick nightly Brick.ttl](https://github.com/BrickSchema/Brick/releases/download/nightly/Brick.ttl) (downloaded 2026-10-06, grepped and walked as a class tree).
- `brick:feeds` is defined as "The subject is upstream of the object in the context of some sequential process; some media is passed between them". It is an Asymmetric and Irreflexive property, inverse of `isFedBy`, and `owl:equivalentProperty rec:feeds`. The definition is medium-agnostic, so it covers electricity and water. — [Brick.ttl](https://github.com/BrickSchema/Brick/releases/download/nightly/Brick.ttl)
- Brick docs say `s223:connectedTo` "is equivalent to `brick:feeds`", and that Brick uses 223P's `s223:cnx`/Connection pattern, with Duct (air), Pipe (water) and Wire (electricity) as the three media examples. Wire is "mentioned but not detailed". — [Brick connections docs](https://docs.brickschema.org/modeling/connections.html) (FETCHED)
- Electrical classes under `Electrical_Equipment`: Breaker_Panel, Bus_Riser, Switchgear (with Automatic_Switch, Circuit_Breaker -> Main_Circuit_Breaker, Disconnect_Switch -> Building_Disconnect_Switch / Main_Disconnect_Switch, Isolation_Switch, Transfer_Switch), Transformer, Motor_Control_Center, Inverter, Battery, PlugStrip, EV charging station/port. Also Electrical_Meter, Building_Electrical_Meter and Electrical_Room. — [Brick.ttl](https://github.com/BrickSchema/Brick/releases/download/nightly/Brick.ttl)
- Electrical classes I searched for and did NOT find in Brick: Pipe, Wire/Cable, Electrical Circuit (only Circuit_Breaker exists), Contactor, Fuse, UPS, and any "panel schedule"/branch-circuit object. — same file
- Water/plumbing classes that exist: Water_Heater (subclass of Equipment; Boiler sits under it), Domestic_Hot_Water_System, Domestic_Hot_Water_Plant, Water_Distribution, Booster_Pump, Domestic_Hot_Water_Circulator_Pump, Hot_Water_Circulator_Pump, Domestic_Hot_Water_Valve, Thermostatic_Mixing_Valve, Water_Pressure_Reducing_Valve, Water_Pressure_Relief_Valve, Backflow_Preventer_Valve, Check_Valve, Isolation_Valve, Building_Water_Meter, Building_Hot_Water_Meter, Water_Leak_Sensor, Leak_Detector_Equipment, Plumbing_Room, Water_Tank/Hot_Water_Storage_Tank. DHW points exist (e.g., Domestic_Hot_Water_Supply_Temperature_Sensor, Domestic_Hot_Water_Differential_Pressure_Sensor, Domestic_Hot_Water_System_Enable_Command). — same file
- Water/plumbing classes NOT found: Pipe, Fixture, Faucet, Toilet, Sink (only Heat_Sink...), and a pipe-riser concept. `Riser` exists but is a subclass of `Vertical_Space`, not a pipe riser. `Bus_Riser` is the electrical one. — same file
- Brick relationships that matter for this tool exist: `feeds`/`isFedBy`, `hasPart`/`isPartOf`, `hasPoint`, `isMeteredBy`/`meters` ("meter measuring the consumption/production of some substance by the subject entity"). — same file

**ASHRAE 223P (DIRECT read of defs.open223.info `223p.ttl`, owl:versionInfo "v1.0.0-2026")**
- Source file: [223p.ttl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open223/defs.open223.info/main/ontologies/223p.ttl).
- Directional connection: `s223:connectedTo` — "A is connectedTo B, means a direction of flow from A to B", inverse `connectedFrom`. — same file
- `s223:Pipe` is a Connection "used primarily to transport liquids and gases such as water, sewage, natural gas, and compressed air". `s223:Conductor` is a Connection "that represents one or more wires used to convey Constituent-Electricity. Each instance of Conductor applies to no more than one electrical circuit", with `hasElectricalPhase`. There is no class literally named `s223:Wire` in this version, although older docs mention Wire. — same file; cf. [Brick connections docs](https://docs.brickschema.org/modeling/connections.html)
- Electrical equipment present: ElectricityBreaker ("automatically opens an electric circuit... at a predetermined overcurrent"), ElectricEnergyTransformer, ElectricEnergyInverter, ElectricEnergyDCDCConverter, ElectricityMeter, ElectricityOutlet, Generator, Battery, PhotovoltaicModule, Motor, ElectricCurrentSensor, VoltageSensor, Domain-Electrical, mediums Electricity-AC/DC/Neutral/Earth, phase identifiers (A/B/C/AB/BC/CA/ABC), single/three-phase enumerations. — same file
- Plumbing present: Domain-Plumbing, Pipe, Valve/TwoWayValve/ThreeWayValve, Faucet ("A Valve that allows, regulates, or stops the flow of hot or cold water... into a Sink, Shower, Bathtub"), Sink, Shower, FlushToilet, WaterOutlet, Pump, Boiler, GaugePressureSensor, FlowSensor, TemperatureSensor, mediums Fluid-Water, Water-HotWater, Water-ChilledWater, Water-GlycolSolution, Water-Steam. — same file
- Not found in 223P: a panel/switchboard class, a water-heater class (Boiler only), a tank class, a water-meter class (ElectricityMeter only), Relay/Contactor/Fuse, Riser. — same file (regex/grep over class names; a class could exist under an unexpected name)
- 223P docs: mediums are "gas, electricity, water", and the focus is topology (how things connect), not geometry. — [open223 overview](https://docs.open223.info/overview/) (FETCHED); [AutomatedBuildings 2026 piece](https://www.automatedbuildings.com/2026/09/a-building-that-can-say-what-it-is-what-ashrae-223p-suggests-about-the-next-decade/) (SNIPPET only)

**Project Haystack**
- Tags exist for water/plumbing: domestic, water, pipe, hotWaterRef ("Hot water flows from the referent to this entity"), boiler, valve, pump, meter, tank, makeupWaterRef, condensateRef. — [Haystack tags appendix](https://project-haystack.org/doc/appendix/tags) (FETCHED)
- Tags exist for electrical: elec, circuit ("Electrical circuit and its associated components such as breakers"), panel, wire, ac, dc, elecRef ("Electricity flows from the referent to this entity"). — same page
- `elec-panel` is "Physical housing for electrical circuits, fuses, and breakers"; per the search summary, a panel should define `elecRef` to its most direct upstream source, so Haystack encodes the electrical tree as an elecRef chain. — [elec-panel](https://project-haystack.org/doc/lib-phIoT/elec-panel) (FETCHED; the elecRef-chain guidance is SNIPPET from [Haystack search result](https://project-haystack.org/doc/docHaystack/ElecPanels))
- Haystack has an equivalent of "feeds" as per-medium Ref tags (elecRef, hotWaterRef) rather than one general relation. Water-heater, riser and pipe-segment equipment types were not confirmed from the page I read.

**RealEstateCore (REC)**
- REC v4.1 (current repo): the Equipment hierarchy is "sourced from our collaboration with Brick Schema". The Transformer doc page shows Equipment inheriting `feeds` and `isFedBy`. Electrical equipment folder: Breaker_Panel, Bus_Riser, Disconnect_Switch, Inverter, Motor_Control_Center, PlugStrip, Switchgear, Transformer, Battery. Water meters and valves mirror Brick. — [REC GitHub](https://github.com/RealEstateCore/rec), [Transformer.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RealEstateCore/rec/main/Doc/Asset/Equipment/Electrical-/Transformer.md), [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RealEstateCore/rec/main/README.md) (DIRECT)
- REC v3.3 docs (older, own hierarchy) list ElectricalPanelboard, ElectricalCircuit (1/2/3-pole), Switchboard, Transformer, ElectricityMeter, WaterHeater (tank/tankless), PlumbingPump, PlumbingValve, WaterMeter, Faucet and PlumbingFixture. The summarizer said "feeds" was not defined there. — [REC 3.3 full doc](https://doc.realestatecore.io/3.3/full.html) (FETCHED, lossy). This conflicts with Brick.ttl declaring `owl:equivalentProperty rec:feeds` and with a search-result description of REC's feeds property ([REC search result](https://doc.realestatecore.io/3.3/core.html), SNIPPET). Treat REC v3.3's feeds status as unresolved. Note that v3.x had richer plumbing/circuit classes (Faucet, PlumbingFixture, ElectricalCircuit) that were apparently dropped when REC 4 re-based on Brick.

**IFC / BIM**
- IfcDistributionSystem groups distribution elements and ports. IfcFlowSegment is the supertype of IfcCableCarrierSegment, IfcCableSegment, IfcConveyorSegment, IfcDuctSegment and IfcPipeSegment. IfcElectricDistributionBoard is an IfcFlowController subtype. IfcDistributionPort carries connectivity via IfcRelNests and system membership via IfcRelAssignsToGroup. — [IfcDistributionPort (IFC 4.3)](https://standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4_3/HTML/lexical/IfcDistributionPort.htm), [IfcFlowSegment](https://ifc43-docs.standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4x3/HTML/lexical/IfcFlowSegment.htm), [IfcElectricDistributionBoard](http://www.bim-times.com/ifc/IFC4_3/buildingsmart/IfcElectricDistributionBoard.htm) (all SNIPPET)
- IFC is geometric and as-designed, while Brick/223P/Haystack are operational semantics. Extracting MEP topology from BIM geometry is an active research topic ("Geometric relationships among components can be used to identify connectivity patterns and instantiate topology knowledge graphs"). — [BIM semantic enrichment paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0926580525001219) (SNIPPET)

**SAREF4BLDG**
- Defines about 62 device classes, including Pump, Valve, Tank, Boiler, Transformer, ProtectiveDevice, SwitchingDevice, SanitaryTerminal and Outlet. The flow properties inherited from IFC are mostly attribute-like (flowCoefficient, connectionSize). I found no explicit upstream/downstream relation in what I read. — [SAREF4BLDG v1.1.2](https://saref.etsi.org/saref4bldg/v1.1.2/) (FETCHED, lossy)

### Inferences
- The tool's core abstraction (a directed feeds graph with points attached) maps directly onto Brick (`feeds`, `hasPoint`, `isMeteredBy`) for the equipment-level chain: utility -> transformer -> switchgear/main breaker -> breaker panel -> breaker -> load (electrical), and water heater/DHW plant -> circulator pump -> valves -> loop -> fixtures. Brick covers the upstream half of both chains but stops at the panel and the DHW plant.
- The last-mile objects that matter for the two target symptoms are missing from Brick: individual circuits/branches, pipe risers and branches, fixtures. They exist in 223P (Pipe, Conductor with one-circuit scope, Faucet/Sink/Shower/FlushToilet) and older REC 3.x (ElectricalCircuit, PlumbingFixture), but not in the Brick graph the HVAC tool already consumes. Extending would mean either 223P-style Connection modelling or custom Brick extensions.
- Electrical point coverage in all standards is thin compared with HVAC. Brick has Electric_Power_Sensor etc., 223P has VoltageSensor/ElectricCurrentSensor, but nothing resembling breaker trip-event or waveform points was found (not specifically searched).
- Standards are necessary but not sufficient: existence of a class says nothing about whether any real building has the relationships populated.

### Gaps
- Did not directly read Brick's `Electrical_System`/`Water_System` subtree shapes (SHACL) or its `hasPoint` constraints for electrical equipment.
- Haystack defs repo (equipment types for water heater, riser, sub-panel) not read directly, only the tags appendix. Haystack GitHub paths I tried returned 404.
- IFC predefined types for IfcDistributionSystemEnum (DOMESTICHOTWATER, ELECTRICAL, etc.) could not be confirmed (Cloudflare block).
- REC 3.3 `feeds` conflict unresolved.
- No evidence found of any real building dataset with Brick or 223P models populated for electrical branch circuits or plumbing risers. Not searched exhaustively.

## 2. Open datasets suitable for validation

### Takeaway
There is no open dataset analogous to the LBNL FDD set (ground-truth, labeled faults in an operating building) for building plumbing or building electrical distribution. What exists falls into four bins: (a) municipal water-network leak benchmarks (BattLeDIM, LeakDB), mostly simulated and network-scale, not building plumbing; (b) electrical waveform datasets for arc fault and NILM, labeled at the device or event level, not the building-distribution level; (c) unlabeled building meter/circuit data (BDG2, CLEMD, Dataport); (d) large symptom-labeled complaint data (NYC HPD) with no ground-truth cause.

### Cited Findings

**Water network leak benchmarks**
- BattLeDIM: Zenodo record 4017659, 550.5 MB in 16 files, SCADA pressure/flow/level for the L-TOWN network over 2018-2019, with `2018_Leakages.csv`, `2019_Leakages.csv`, a fixed-leakages report, and L-TOWN.inp / L-TOWN_Real.inp hydraulic models. License CC BY 4.0 per the record page. — [Zenodo 4017659](https://zenodo.org/records/4017659) (FETCHED). A search summary instead said the data is under EUPL v1.2, likely mixing up the code repo ([search result](https://zenodo.org/records/4017659)); record-page CC BY 4.0 is the more direct source. Companion code and dataset generator: [Zenodo 6962143](https://zenodo.org/records/6962143), [KIOS-Research/BattLeDIM](https://github.com/KIOS-Research/BattLeDIM). Journal paper: [J. Water Resour. Plan. Manage. (ASCE) 2022](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/abs/10.1061/(ASCE)WR.1943-5452.0001601) (abstract not readable, 403).
- LeakDB: Zenodo 1313116, 1.2 GB, CC BY 4.0, version 1 published 2018-07-16, "artificially created but realistic leakage scenarios" on different networks, with a MATLAB scoring algorithm. — [Zenodo 1313116](https://zenodo.org/records/1313116) (FETCHED). Per a secondary summary, 1,000 scenarios for each of two networks, Net1 (9 nodes, 1 reservoir, 1 tank) and Hanoi (1 reservoir, 32 nodes, 34 pipes). — [search summary of LeakDB sources](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353961860_Experiments_based_comparative_evaluations_of_machine_learning_techniques_for_leak_detection_in_water_distribution_systems) (SNIPPET). The data is synthetic by construction, so it cannot calibrate real detection rates.
- A 2026 Scientific Data multisource dataset for urban water distribution anomaly detection and fault prediction (hourly CSV with a binary fault label) exists, but the article was behind a login redirect and I could not verify size, license or real-vs-simulated status. — [Nature Sci Data 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07203-5) (SNIPPET)
- A 2026 MDPI systematic review addresses "Standardization and Benchmarking of Datasets and Evaluation Protocols for Machine Learning-Based Water Leak Detection". Title and venue only; content blocked (403). — [MDPI Water 18(18):2267](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/18/18/2267)
- A 2024 survey states that labeled leaking-condition data in in-service networks is scarce and that sim-to-real generalization is a limitation (SNIPPET). — [Vaquet et al., arXiv 2410.12461 (ICANN 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12461) (the abstract itself says domain particularities challenge ML; the data-scarcity wording is from [search results for the Water 17(13):1988 paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/w17131988))

**Building water end-use and hot water**
- WEUSEDTO: one apartment in Naples, 7 fixtures (washbasin, bidet, kitchen faucet, shower, washing machine, dishwasher, toilet) at 1 s resolution, 10 s at household level, about one year (2019-2020, with gaps). Data CC BY 4.0, software GPL v3. Labels are end-use identity, not faults. — [Zenodo 4651443](https://zenodo.org/records/4651443) (FETCHED); [paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352711022001327)
- Other Zenodo items surfaced but not examined: [Residential Household Dataset: Occupancy, Water, and Electricity](https://zenodo.org/records/15180141) and [Smart Meter Water Consumption Measurements](https://zenodo.org/records/7585347) (titles only).
- A labeled district-heating substation fault dataset built from service data (arXiv 2511.14791) exists. It is hydronic rather than potable DHW, so it is adjacent at best. — [arXiv 2511.14791](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14791) (SNIPPET)
- I found no open dataset of building domestic hot water recirculation temperatures with labeled faults. See Gaps.

**Building electrical metering (mostly unlabeled)**
- BDG2: 3,053 meters, 1,636 non-residential buildings, 2016-2017 hourly, 53.6M measurements, 19 sites, meter types include electricity, chilled/hot water, steam, gas, water, irrigation, solar. CC BY 4.0, 595.3 MB archive. No fault labels described. — [Zenodo 3887306](https://zenodo.org/records/3887306) (FETCHED); [Nature Sci Data paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-020-00712-x) (SNIPPET)
- CLEMD (Malaysia): circuit-level measurements at the main switchboard and distribution boards, 8 circuits/boards, 1 s samples, 59 columns, about 519k rows per CSV (101-164 MB each), 40 days (2023-09-16 to 2023-10-25). CC BY 4.0, Figshare DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.c.6946581, code at [github.com/CLEMDatabase/CLEMD](https://github.com/CLEMDatabase/CLEMD). No systematic fault labels, with noted analyzer hang-ups. — [CLEMD, Sci Data 2024 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11156870/) (FETCHED). The same source says public datasets from commercial buildings are rare (names I-BLEND, BLOND, CREAM, CU-BEMS) ([Nature version](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-024-03433-7), SNIPPET).
- Pecan Street Dataport: described as the largest residential energy dataset, now including water, with circuit-level electricity and water data. Licensing and pricing terms are on separate pages not read. — [Dataport](https://www.pecanstreet.org/dataport/) (FETCHED). Terms for academic use are not verified here.
- NILM sets: REDD (10 houses, 119 days), BLUED (1 house, about 8 days, 12 kHz, with an event list), PLAID (1,094 one-second waveforms at 30 kHz), UK-DALE (655 days, CC BY 4.0). — [comparison summary](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Comparison-between-high-frequency-NILM-Datasets_tbl2_344672778), [UK-DALE paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261289463_The_UK-DALE_dataset_domestic_appliance-level_electricity_demand_and_whole-house_demand_from_five_UK_homes) (SNIPPET). Licenses for REDD, BLUED and PLAID were not confirmed (a summarizer said BLUED uses a "proprietary agreement": [HiFAKES paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.00062), low confidence). These are residential appliance-event datasets, not distribution-fault data.

**Electrical fault waveform data**
- Series arc fault: a study of 7 appliances (electric heater, vacuum cleaner, desktop PC, induction cooker, fluorescent lamp, dimmer, drill), 1 MHz sampling, 14,000 grayscale images (1,000 half-wave samples per class), reported 98.36% accuracy across 14 classes and 99.20% for arc vs. normal only. The paper says signatures are downloadable on Zenodo, but the URL was not in the text I read. — [PMC7506653](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7506653/) (FETCHED)
- Other arc datasets named in search results: AFDACA (21 arc fault experiments, "public validation dataset") and a Scientific Data waveform dataset for arcs under low pressure for aircraft power systems (not building-relevant). — [Sci Data 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-024-04253-5), [AFDACA mention](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-17235-7) (SNIPPET)
- I did not locate an open labeled dataset of building ground faults, breaker-trip root causes, or building-level power-quality events with causes.

**Symptom/complaint (work-order-like) data**
- NYC HPD "Housing Maintenance Code Complaints and Problems" (Socrata id ygpa-z7cr): I queried the API directly. 16,352,881 problem rows from 2001-12-16 to 2026-10-05, with fields including building_id, apartment, space_type, major_category, minor_category, problem_code, complaint/problem status and dates. Major-category counts: HEAT/HOT WATER 2,912,778; HEATING 2,509,671; PLUMBING 2,078,065; ELECTRIC 846,938; WATER LEAK 499,772 (plus others). Minor categories in plumbing/electric/leak/hot-water groups include WATER SUPPLY 392,731, HEAVY FLOW 224,780, SLOW LEAK 205,811, LIGHTING 192,358, WIRING 176,358, ELECTRIC-SUPPLY 139,625, OUTLET/SWITCH 99,302, POWER OUTAGE 81,143, STEAM PIPE/RISER 20,003, BOILER 3,032. The license field was empty in the API metadata (public NYC Open Data, terms not confirmed). — [NYC Open Data via API](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/ygpa-z7cr.json) (DIRECT, queried 2026-10-06); landing page [data.gov catalog](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/housing-maintenance-code-complaints-and-problems) (last updated 2026-09-29)
- A third-party site quoted different HPD totals for a 2025-mid-2026 window (heat/hot water 524,038, water leak 67,712, electric 55,909). I did not use these. They are not comparable to the all-time counts above. — [landlordtenantresources.com](https://www.landlordtenantresources.com/) (aggregator, unverified)

**Hot-pipe thermal diagnosis**
- An automatic IR-image diagnosis algorithm for insulated hot water pipe faults (leakage, insulation damage) reported 97.59% overall accuracy experimentally and 92.74% in commercial-building field studies. — [Building and Environment 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132322003481) (SNIPPET)

### Inferences
- The closest thing to ground truth for a building-scale plumbing/electrical tool would have to be assembled: simulated truth (EPANET/WNTR on a building riser model, or circuit simulation) plus real nuisance/background data (BDG2, CLEMD, WEUSEDTO). That is a weaker validation story than the LBNL FDD dataset gave for HVAC.
- NYC HPD is a real source of symptom frequency and spatial co-occurrence (same building, same line/apartment stack, same category). It could test shared-cause grouping (do co-reported complaints cluster by building/stack?) and symptom priors, but it has no cause labels (no cause field in the schema), so it cannot validate diagnostic accuracy. Complaints are tenant-reported; apartment-level plumbing/electrical issues dominate over base-building system faults.
- Arc-fault and PQ waveform papers can supply sourced detection rates for specific checks (e.g., an "AFCI-style detection" check, about 98-99% accuracy in the lab), but lab accuracy on seven appliances does not transfer to field detection rate, as the HVAC LBNL experience (0.69 combined vs. an assumed 0.92) already showed.
- Real-data ground truth for NILM-style event detection exists (BLUED event lists), which could help a "load isolation by signature" check, but only for residential loads.

### Gaps
- Open building plumbing datasets: no labeled hot-water recirculation, pressure-booster, or PRV fault data found. Search budget ended before checking OEDI/NREL, Kaggle, OSF, or utility smart-meter leak sets specifically.
- Real vs. simulated status of BattLeDIM's L-TOWN data and its network size (pipes, sensors) not confirmed (GitHub page excerpt lacked it).
- Mortar, ORNL and LBNL electrical content not rechecked (the requester already holds them).
- Licenses for REDD, BLUED, PLAID, Pecan Street academic access: unverified.
- No work-order dataset with root-cause labels found (HPD has symptoms and status only). The requester's own 600 real work orders (memory notes) remain the only cause-bearing source known.
- Brick/223P-annotated real electrical or plumbing models: none found.

## 3. Academic state of the art: FDD beyond HVAC and decision-theoretic troubleshooting

### Takeaway
The literature is rich but mismatched to the tool's job. Electrical research is dominated by waveform-level classification (arc fault, power quality, distribution-network faults) at 90-99% reported accuracy on lab or utility data. Water research is dominated by network-scale leak detection/localization benchmarked mostly on simulated data, plus a separate Legionella/temperature literature that finds temperature at the point of use is the better risk indicator and that surrogate measurements are unreliable. I found no building-scale equivalent of the HVAC FDD literature (no reviews of building plumbing FDD, DHW recirculation FDD). Decision-theoretic troubleshooting (Heckerman et al.) is domain-agnostic and is the right theoretical home for the tool.

### Cited Findings

**Decision-theoretic troubleshooting**
- Heckerman, Breese and Rommelse, "Decision-Theoretic Troubleshooting," Communications of the ACM 38, May 1995, and Breese and Heckerman, UAI 1996 ("A Framework for Repair and Experiment"). Goal: a sequence of actions minimizing expected repair cost; Bayesian networks with fault, action and question variables, each with costs. Under their simplifying assumptions, optimal ordering is by decreasing p_i/C_i. — [Breese & Heckerman via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235633745_Decision-Theoretic_Troubleshooting_A_Framework_for_Repair_andExperiment), [search summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235633745_Decision-Theoretic_Troubleshooting_A_Framework_for_Repair_andExperiment) (SNIPPET)
- Computing approximately optimal troubleshooting strategies is NP-hard (Václav Lín; the arXiv version was withdrawn after acceptance at IJAR). — [arXiv 1304.6551](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.6551) (FETCHED; note the withdrawal)
- Search results also show Bayesian-network troubleshooter authoring/knowledge-acquisition patents (e.g., US 6,456,622, US 7,385,716). Not analyzed. — [US 6456622](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6456622), [US 7385716](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7385716) (SNIPPET; freedom-to-operate unknown)

**Electrical: building and distribution FDD**
- A building electrical-system fault diagnosis method (variational mode decomposition plus a quantum-GA-optimized SVM) reported 91.67% accuracy. Data source (real vs. simulated) and fault classes could not be verified because the page returned a CAPTCHA. — [PMC10027836](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10027836/) (SNIPPET)
- Reviews: AI/signal-processing fault diagnosis for electrical distribution networks ([Processes 2025, 13(1):48](https://doi.org/10.3390/pr13010048)); fault diagnosis for electrical systems and power networks ([IEEE 9064533](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9064533/)); arc fault detection from an AI perspective, Thakur et al. ([arXiv 2311.16804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16804), FETCHED: argues AI outperforms traditional methods; no accuracy figures in what I read).
- Power-quality: voltage-sag classification with end-to-end LSTM reported above 90% accuracy on real-world datasets, and an ICA/TLBO sag-cause method a 0.86% detection error rate. These are SNIPPET-level claims: [LSTM sag paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/360003797_An_End-to-End_Deep_Learning_Method_for_Voltage_Sag_Classification), [real-time sag diagnostics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0263224120306357).
- Circuit-level submetering is described by GSA as enabling FDD and ECMs, but with cost/deployment limits. — [GSA single-circuit meter findings](https://www.gsa.gov/system/files/046-Submeters%20and%20Analytics-%20Single%20circuit%20meter-Findings-508.pdf), [GSA full-panel findings](https://www.gsa.gov/system/files/041-Submeters%20and%20Analytics-Full%20Panel-Findings-508.pdf) (SNIPPET)

**Water: leaks, DHW, Legionella**
- Leak detection/localization survey (Vaquet et al., ICANN 2024): [arXiv 2410.12461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12461). Data scarcity, sim-to-real generalization, sensor sparsity and heterogeneous networks are named limits in [search results](https://doi.org/10.3390/w17131988) (SNIPPET).
- Legionella and hot-water temperature, big-data study: routine monitoring data from public and apartment buildings (292,937 data sets), with calculated tipping points of 56 C (supply flow) and 53 C (return flow), below which relative Legionella probability rises. — [Water Supply 24(6):2116](https://iwaponline.com/ws/article/24/6/2116/102792/Utilizing-big-data-to-determine-the-temperature) (SNIPPET; the page itself 403'd, the figures appeared in two search summaries)
- Point-of-use temperature is a better risk indicator than distance from the heater, and sampling points in an unbalanced system should be chosen by temperature. — [Building and Environment 2016, hot water hydraulics via temperature monitoring](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360132316303407) (SNIPPET)
- Logistic regression on single outlets: temperature after flushing, stagnation and pipe-length proportion were the best predictors of Legionella contamination. — [ResearchGate: Modelling characteristics to predict Legionella contamination risk](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282410088_Modelling_characteristics_to_predict_Legionella_contamination_risk_-_Surveillance_of_drinking_water_plumbing_systems_and_identification_of_risk_areas) (SNIPPET)
- Highest Legionella pneumophila colonization at 30-35 C, falling markedly above 50 C. — same snippet set ([Building and Environment search results](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1438463924001378))
- 28 buildings (NYC, SF, NJ), 269 samples: Legionella in 24.2% of samples and 53.6% of buildings. Water-quality surrogates (hot-return temperature at 124 F, free chlorine 0.5 mg/L, pH, metals) did not reliably predict distal-site positivity (sensitivity 55%, specificity 96%, PPV 91.7%, NPV 72.7%). Authors conclude surrogates cannot replace culturing distal sites. — [PMC6963558](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6963558/) (FETCHED)
- Regensburg, Germany (2004-2017): no connection found between exceeding the technical measurement limit in routine apartment tests and disease cases; staff spent about 39% of daily working hours on Legionella administration. — [GMS Hyg Infect Control 2020, PMC7336046](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336046/) (FETCHED)
- A digital-twin style tool combining hydraulics (WNTR), demand (pySIMDEUM) and heat transfer for Legionella growth risk in premise plumbing exists; validation status not verified. — [MDPI Eng. Proc. 69(1):162](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4591/69/1/162) (SNIPPET; page 403)
- Regulatory frame: ANSI/ASHRAE 188 and Guideline 12 require water management plans and monitoring. — [ASHRAE 188 addendum PDF](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/standards%20and%20guidelines/standards%20addenda/188_2015_h_20180628.pdf) (SNIPPET)
- A practitioner forum thread mentions stuck-open check valves in DHW recirculation lines as a common fault, which is anecdotal and not peer-reviewed. — [Heating Help forum](https://forum.heatinghelp.com/discussion/199159/best-type-check-valve-for-dhw-recirc-system) (SNIPPET, low quality)

### Inferences
- The decision-theory backbone transfers unchanged. What does not transfer is the evidence base for the inputs (cause priors, detection rates, check costs) that the HVAC tool got from LBNL-style data.
- The literature's accuracy numbers (91.67%, 98.36%, over 90%) are classification accuracies on waveforms or event windows, not "root cause among N candidates after a human takes sequential manual steps". They are not usable as the tool's per-check detection rates without re-derivation.
- The Legionella literature is a warning for the plumbing side: automated surrogate signals (temperature, chlorine) predict poorly at the outlet level (sensitivity 55% in the NYC/SF/NJ study), and monitoring effort has unclear protective value in Regensburg. A "detection rate" for any DHW sensor check would therefore need to be estimated conservatively and per-target.
- Hardness of troubleshooting (NP-hard in general) is not a practical constraint at the tool's scale (small candidate sets), as the HVAC work already shows.

### Gaps
- No review of building-scale plumbing FDD found. No source for DHW recirculation fault frequencies or detection rates. Search budget ended before checking AHRI/PNNL/ASPE/PHCC literature, ASHRAE Journal, or NBI-style fault-prevalence studies for plumbing and electrical.
- No electrical "fault prevalence in buildings" stat analogous to the economizer 64% figure found.
- Accuracy and data provenance for the building electrical fault diagnosis paper (PMC10027836) not verified.
- Heckerman p/C optimality conditions (independence, single-fault, each action inspects and repairs one component) are from my background knowledge, not verified in the sources read.
- No decision-theoretic troubleshooting application to buildings, plumbing or electrical found (not exhaustively searched).

## 4. Structural differences from HVAC: safety, data continuity, hidden topology, liability

### Takeaway
Four differences change the design: (1) tests are hazardous and some are code-restricted, so the tool needs hard constraints, not just costs; (2) almost no continuous data exists at the level where these symptoms live (branch circuits, fixtures); (3) topology is physically hidden and typically undocumented at the branch level, so the map is the bottleneck; (4) liability and licensing weigh more heavily. Only (1) and (2) are well-sourced below; (3) and (4) are mostly inference.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Safety**
- NFPA 70E (2021 edition per the source): the intent is to establish an electrically safe work condition unless there is justified energized work. Energized work is permitted when de-energizing creates a greater hazard or is infeasible, among other conditions. Only qualified persons may perform testing, troubleshooting and voltage measuring on energized conductors at 50 V or more, and only qualified persons may cross the limited approach boundary. — [IAEI magazine, NFPA 70E basics](https://iaeimagazine.org/electrical-safety/electrically-safe-work-practices-nfpa-70e-basics/) (FETCHED)
- Testing, troubleshooting and voltage measuring are among the cases where an energized electrical work permit (EEWP) is not required, provided a qualified person uses appropriate safe work practices and PPE. — [NFPA 70E 130.2(B)(3) summary](https://www.electricallicenserenewal.com/Electrical-Continuing-Education-Courses/NEC-Content.php?sectionID=492), [Shaw Engineering](https://shawengr.com/energized-electrical-work-permit-nfpa-70e/) (SNIPPET; secondary sources)
- Plumbing side: Legionella growth is highest at about 30-35 C and falls above 50 C (see section 3), so plumbing "tests" such as raising heater setpoint or flushing hot water interact with both scald and Legionella risk. Brick itself includes Thermostatic_Mixing_Valve and Backflow_Preventer_Valve as modeled equipment. — [Brick.ttl](https://github.com/BrickSchema/Brick/releases/download/nightly/Brick.ttl) (DIRECT). Specific scald thresholds and plumbing code limits were not sourced.

**(b) Continuous data**
- Public commercial-building circuit-level datasets are described as rare, and circuit-level submetering is positioned by GSA as an emerging FDD enabler, i.e., not the default. — [CLEMD paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-024-03433-7), [GSA](https://www.gsa.gov/system/files/046-Submeters%20and%20Analytics-%20Single%20circuit%20meter-Findings-508.pdf) (SNIPPET)
- Brick has electrical meter and DHW plant points (supply/discharge temperature, differential pressure) but no branch-level or fixture-level points. — [Brick.ttl](https://github.com/BrickSchema/Brick/releases/download/nightly/Brick.ttl) (DIRECT)

**(c) Hidden topology**
- Haystack models the electrical tree by each panel pointing to its upstream source via `elecRef`; 223P assigns one Conductor per circuit; Brick lacks both circuit and pipe objects. See section 1 sources.
- BIM-based topology extraction from geometry is an active research area rather than a solved import step. — [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0926580525001219) (SNIPPET)

**(d) Regulation**
- ASHRAE 188 requires a water management program for Legionella risk, with monitoring, corrective action and documentation. — [ASHRAE 188](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/standards%20and%20guidelines/standards%20addenda/188_2015_h_20180628.pdf) (SNIPPET)
- NFPA 70E qualified-person and permit regimes (above).

### Inferences
- In HVAC the tool optimizes cost/time/detection. For electrical it additionally needs an exclusion set (checks that require exposed energized parts, panel cover removal, or de-energization with lockout/tagout), a "who may perform this" attribute (qualified person vs. generalist), and an energized/de-energized state variable. In the plumbing case the analogous attributes are scald risk, Legionella risk (stagnation, temperature), and water-damage risk. This is a different decision problem, a constrained one, rather than the same one with different numbers.
- Most plumbing and electrical checks are single manual readings (clamp meter, IR gun, pressure gauge, flush-and-time), so each check gives one noisy snapshot, and intermittent faults (flicker, intermittent trips, time-of-day low pressure) may not be present when the technician arrives. HVAC has trend history in the BAS; here that exists only if metering was installed.
- The map is the bottleneck because the relevant edges (branch circuit to panel position to downstream loads; hot-water branch to riser to loop) are typically in paper/PDF drawings, panel schedules and tribal knowledge, not in the BAS. Panel schedules and circuit directories are a classic documentation-mismatch setting (commonly stated in practice but not sourced here), so the requester's existing doc-mismatch work (182-case benchmark, see memory notes) is directly relevant to electrical, and is arguably a more natural entry point than diagnosis.
- Liability and licensing (electricians and plumbers are licensed trades; a recommendation that leads to an injury or a Legionella outbreak carries different exposure than an HVAC recommendation) are plausible concerns but not sourced. Mark as unverified.

### Gaps
- No sources found for plumbing-code limits (scald, mixing valve ASSE standards), licensing/liability treatment of diagnostic software, or panel-schedule accuracy statistics.
- No source on how often DHW/electrical systems have BAS visibility (ratio of buildings with DHW BAS points or circuit submetering).

## 5. How the HVAC pipeline's components would translate, and where they break (concrete assessments for one plumbing and one electrical symptom)

### Takeaway
Shared-cause reasoning and sequential p/C test selection translate best, and electrical distribution is closer to the HVAC tree structure than plumbing (radial feed tree vs. a recirculating loop). The cause catalogue and check catalogue (priors and detection rates) are the parts without an evidence base, and the safety constraint layer is new. The honest overall status: the algorithm ports; the data that made the HVAC result credible does not exist yet. All of this section is INFERENCE built on the cited facts in sections 1-4, except where a source is noted.

### Component-by-component assessment (INFERENCE unless cited)
| HVAC component | Plumbing/electrical translation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship map (feeds graph from Brick/point names) | Electrical: Brick covers transformer -> switchgear -> breaker panel -> breaker -> load, but not individual circuits; Haystack elecRef chain and 223P Conductor can express branches ([Brick.ttl](https://github.com/BrickSchema/Brick/releases/download/nightly/Brick.ttl), [223p.ttl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open223/defs.open223.info/main/ontologies/223p.ttl)). Plumbing: Brick covers DHW plant, pumps, valves, meters; pipes, risers and fixtures need 223P. The data source is no longer BAS point names; it is panel schedules, riser diagrams, as-builts. | Partly translates; map construction is the bottleneck |
| Cause catalogue with priors | No public frequency data for electrical or plumbing causes (HPD gives symptom frequencies only). Priors would be expert-elicited or mined from the requester's work-order data. | Breaks without new evidence |
| Check catalogue (cost, time, detection rate) | Checks are manual readings; cost and time are estimable by experts. Detection rates have no source; arc-fault/NILM papers give lab accuracies that do not transfer. | Breaks on detection rates |
| Shared-cause reasoning (shared parent) | Strong fit: complaints under one panel, one riser, one recirculation branch point to the common ancestor. Electrical radial trees are clean; DHW recirculation loops create cycles, so "common parent" becomes "common upstream cut-set". | Translates, with loop handling |
| Sequential Bayesian test selection | Domain-agnostic (Heckerman 1995). Needs a hard safety/qualification constraint layer added. | Translates plus a new constraint layer |
| Validation on public data | LBNL-style ground truth does not exist for these systems; leak benchmarks are synthetic and network-scale. | Largest gap |

### Plumbing symptom: "hot water not reaching far fixtures" (INFERENCE)
- Candidate causes: failed or undersized recirculation pump; stuck-open or stuck-closed recirc check valve (anecdotal: [forum](https://forum.heatinghelp.com/discussion/199159/best-type-check-valve-for-dhw-recirc-system)); unbalanced or misadjusted balancing valves on loop branches; low heater/mixing-valve setpoint; failed thermostatic mixing valve ([Brick has Thermostatic_Mixing_Valve](https://github.com/BrickSchema/Brick/releases/download/nightly/Brick.ttl)); closed or partially closed isolation valve; scaled or fouled heater; cross-connection between hot and cold lines; failed pipe insulation; air lock; undersized or long dead-leg branches.
- Checks and data: DHW supply/return temperature and circulator status exist as Brick points but only at the plant; riser/branch temperatures and fixtures are unmetered. Cheap manual checks: temperature at the heater outlet and loop return (touch/IR), pump amp draw/sound, valve positions, flush-and-time at a far fixture. IR imaging of hot pipes has a reported 92.74% field accuracy ([Building and Environment 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132322003481), SNIPPET), but for insulated heating pipe fault detection, not DHW branch diagnosis.
- What translates: shared-cause reasoning by branch (complaints on the same riser/branch implicate the shared upstream element); the p/C ordering (cheap setpoint and pump checks first). Hydraulic and thermal behavior is physically modelable (WNTR/EPANET style, [Legionella tool example](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4591/69/1/162)), which could give physics-based likelihoods that HVAC lacked, but only if pipe lengths, diameters and loop layout are known, i.e., the hidden-topology problem.
- Where it breaks: (1) recirculation loop is a cycle, not a tree; (2) causes interact (a low setpoint changes the temperature profile of every branch); (3) a "safe" test (raise setpoint, flush hot water) carries scald and Legionella risk; (4) outlet-level automatic detection performs poorly (PMC6963558: sensitivity 55% for surrogate vs. culture), so detection rates must be conservative; (5) the HVAC-style cause catalogue has no empirical frequencies. A realistic first scope would be restricted to the plant-side subset (setpoint, pump, mixing valve, return-temp checks) where BAS data exists, which then reduces to something close to the HVAC hydronic case (hot-water loop) that Brick already models.

### Electrical symptom: "breaker keeps tripping" and "lights flicker on a floor" (INFERENCE)
- "Breaker keeps tripping" candidate causes: sustained overload from added load; short/ground fault in a branch or appliance; arc fault or AFCI nuisance trip; loose termination with heating; failing breaker; shared-neutral fault; motor inrush or failing motor; GFCI trip from moisture. A highly discriminating, low-risk, low-cost step is load isolation (unplug or switch off everything on the circuit, reset, then reconnect in halves). Clamp-meter current reading and IR panel scans are cheap but usually need panel access, which brings NFPA 70E qualified-person requirements ([IAEI](https://iaeimagazine.org/electrical-safety/electrically-safe-work-practices-nfpa-70e-basics/)). Insulation-resistance testing needs de-energization (lockout/tagout overhead). Waveform-based arc detection has reported 98-99% lab accuracy ([PMC7506653](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7506653/)), but that is a lab figure on seven appliances.
- "Lights flicker on a floor": shared upstream causes dominate (loose neutral or feeder connection, large-load inrush such as motors or VFDs causing voltage dips, failing transformer tap, dimmer/LED driver incompatibility). With circuit-level or panel-level metering ([GSA](https://www.gsa.gov/system/files/041-Submeters%20and%20Analytics-Full%20Panel-Findings-508.pdf), [CLEMD](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11156870/) shows 1 s voltage/current/PF/harmonics are collectable), a voltage-dip look would be informative; without it, the check is a manual voltage reading at several panels.
- What translates: the feeds tree (transformer -> panel -> breaker -> load) is a clean tree in most buildings and matches the shared-parent logic exactly; checks have clear cost and hazard attributes; and the map itself (panel schedule vs. reality) is the same documentation-mismatch problem the requester has already built a benchmark for.
- Where it breaks: (1) per-check detection rates have no empirical basis and intermittent faults may not be present at check time; (2) safety/qualification constraints are binding, not just costly; (3) breaker trip events and waveforms are rarely logged, so there is little equivalent of the BAS trend look; (4) panel schedules are often unreliable (commonly asserted, not sourced), so the map may be wrong in exactly the way that matters, creating a circularity where a diagnostic tool depends on the mapping accuracy it would need to check; (5) the requester's HVAC findings that safety can override speed (Addendum 37) would likely be a bigger issue here.

### Inferences (summary)
- Highest-value, lowest-risk entry points suggested by the evidence: (i) electrical documentation-mismatch detection (panel schedules/circuit directories), since it reuses finished work and avoids recommending hazardous tests; (ii) plant-side DHW diagnosis using Brick's existing classes and points; (iii) shared-cause grouping on complaint data (HPD) as a symptom-level study.
- A credible validation plan would need either a simulation-based ground truth (EPANET/WNTR for plumbing, circuit simulation for electrical) plus public background data, or a partner building with instrumented branch-level data. I found no public alternative.

### Gaps
- No evidence on how technicians actually sequence plumbing or electrical troubleshooting (no analogue of the video review the requester did for HVAC). No sourced check cost/time numbers for any plumbing or electrical check.
- No attempt was made to verify the above candidate cause lists against trade literature (NECA, PHCC, ASPE). They are expert-knowledge guesses and must be checked with working tradespeople before use.
- Liability, licensing and insurance implications not researched.
