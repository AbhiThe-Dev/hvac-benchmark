# Commercial/Institutional Building Plumbing: Diagnostic Pain Points (US, as of Oct 2026)

Evidence-grading legend used throughout: **[HARD]** = government, peer-reviewed, or insurer/standards-body primary data; **[HARD-2ND]** = a hard number that I could only reach through a secondhand page (original not located/verified); **[VENDOR]** = vendor, contractor-blog, or trade-association marketing (treat as directional only); **[ANECDOTE]** = practitioner account or forum thread.

Method caveats (read first):
- Reddit (r/Plumbing, r/Plumbing_Pros) is blocked to my fetch/search tools, so I could NOT sample the forums the requester named. Practitioner voice below comes from a heatinghelp.com forum thread (residential), Plumbing & Mechanical (PME) magazine, and contractor blogs. Plumbing Zone, Contractor Talk, and Facebook groups yielded nothing usable.
- Several statistics that circulate widely (e.g., "70.2% of multifamily operators," "60% of maintenance delays from bad as-builts," "$89,000 average commercial water claim") could not be traced to a primary source; they are flagged individually.
- I found no plumbing-specific first-time-fix, callback, or as-built-accuracy dataset from an independent source. That absence is itself a finding.

## 1. What are the most common and costly plumbing symptoms/failures in commercial buildings, and what do they cost?

### Takeaway
Insurance data consistently put plumbing/pipe failure among the largest non-weather property-loss categories, and water damage is low-frequency/high-severity (single events routinely $1M-$7M); Legionella in building (premise) plumbing is the costliest public-health plumbing failure, concentrated in healthcare and hotels. Hard, independent per-symptom cost data for water hammer, PRVs, boosters, flush valves, and recirc imbalance does not exist in what I could find; those symptoms are documented almost only by trade press and vendor material.

### Cited Findings

**Insurance / property loss (leaks, pipe failure, water heaters)**
- [HARD-2ND] AIG reportedly analyzed ~3,800 commercial claims over four years: ~34% of all reported claims were non-natural-peril plumbing damage (largest single category); burst/leaking pipes were 42% of water-damage claims and 45% of water-damage dollars. Reached only via Safe Piping Matters, an industry-oriented site with no disclosed funding (likely piping-materials advocacy); the AIG original was not located. — [Safe Piping Matters, Aug 2026](https://safepipingmatters.org/2026/08/19/insurance-claims-show-pipe-failures-pose-significant-risk/)
- [HARD-2ND / VENDOR-adjacent] Same page cites Chubb: ~$1.2B/yr global pipe-failure losses (non-weather), average commercial pipe-failure loss $89,000, and sharply rising $1M+ claims. I could not locate Chubb's primary source for the $89,000 figure. — [Safe Piping Matters](https://safepipingmatters.org/2026/08/19/insurance-claims-show-pipe-failures-pose-significant-risk/)
- [HARD] Chubb's own commercial-claims breakdown of sources of interior water damage (data period not stated on page): roofs 15%, toilets 15%, sprinklers 14%, water heaters 11%, HVAC 8%, vacancy 8%, boiler & machinery 7%, sewer/sewage backup 5%, water tanks 4%, water mains 3% (mains small in count but severe). Note this is a mix of "plumbing" and non-plumbing sources; toilets + water heaters + sewer backup + tanks + mains ~38%. — [Chubb: 10 most common sources of commercial water damage](https://www.chubb.com/us-en/businesses/resources/10-most-common-sources-of-commercial-water-damage.html)
- [HARD, case-level] Chubb-cited single-event losses (article dated Dec 4, 2020): $6.5M from a pressure-relief-valve failure, $4M hospital pipe burst (>200,000 gallons released), $4M faulty pipe fitting, $3M frozen pipe burst; "250 gallons/day can leak from a 1/8-inch pipe crack"; "75% of heaters fail before 12 years." Case studies, not a rate. — [Chubb: commercial water damage claims](https://www.chubb.com/us-en/businesses/resources/6-commercial-water-damage-claims-their-impact-on-business.html)
- [HARD-2ND] The Hartford: 75% of water-damage losses come from "plumbing, HVAC systems and other appliances that discharge liquid accidentally"; water damage is "a leading cause of commercial property losses across all real estate asset classes, as well as hospitality," and separate water-damage deductibles ($50k-$100k cited) have become common. Article is carrier-sourced, with no quantified sensor-effectiveness data. — [Risk & Insurance](https://riskandinsurance.com/water-damage-is-a-leading-cause-of-commercial-real-estate-claims-how-sensor-technologies-help-mitigate-these-claims/)
- [HARD] ISO: the average water-damage loss is "five figures"; three recent plumbing-leak claims ranged $200k-$540k. Water damage is lower frequency but higher severity than wind/hail. — [Insurance Journal, Sept 2024](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2024/09/16/792469.htm)
- [HARD, contrary framing] Allianz AGCS (470,000+ business claims, 200 countries, 5 years, published Dec 2018): water damage was only 6% of claims by count (3rd) and 3% of value (8th). Global all-peril commercial, so not directly comparable to US building-property claims, but it cautions against assuming water dominates every book of business. — [Allianz Commercial: Water damage](https://commercial.allianz.com/news-and-insights/expert-risk-articles/water-damage.html)
- [HARD-2ND, unverified] "70.2% of multifamily operators report water damage as their top insurance issue (NMHC)" appears on multiple broker/vendor blogs. I checked NMHC's 2024 risk-survey key-findings PDF and found no water-damage statistic in the readable text; treat as unverified. NMHC's survey itself exists and is about insurance cost/catastrophe exposure. — [NMHC 2024 key findings PDF](https://www.nmhc.org/contentassets/30d7f0e5763944d79c741ad1616b61cc/2024_nmhc_mf_risk_key_findings.pdf); the claim as repeated at [DrizzleX (vendor)](https://www.drizzlex.com/post/multifamily-water-damage-insurance-claims-what-owners-need-to-know)
- [VENDOR/broker] Multifamily "water damage is the single largest loss driver" claim from a broker blog; no data shown. — [Skyscraper Insurance](https://skyscraperinsurance.com/water-damage-continues-to-be-the-single-largest-loss-driver-for-multi-family-properties/)

**Water waste / leaks**
- [HARD] EPA WaterSense: on average, leaks can account for more than 6% of a facility's total water use; hospitals/healthcare = ~7% of commercial-institutional water use in the US. EPA recommends meters/submeters to find leaks and malfunctioning equipment. — [EPA WaterSense Fight Leaks checklist](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2019-02/documents/ws-falw-commercial-checklist.pdf); [EPA hospital factsheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2017-01/documents/ws-commercial-factsheet-hospitals.pdf)

**Legionella / water quality (the largest health-and-liability pain)**
- [HARD] CDC MMWR (2015-2020 US drinking-water outbreaks): 214 outbreaks, 2,140 cases, 563 hospitalizations, 88 deaths; 184 (86%) were biofilm-associated, predominantly Legionella (786 cases, 544 hospitalizations = 97%, 86 deaths = 98%). 53% of outbreaks occurred in hospitals/long-term care/assisted living; 16% in hotels/motels. "Premise or point-of-use" location was the most-cited contributing factor (287 instances, 63% of biofilm-outbreak factors); leading factors included Legionella in the system, inadequate chlorine, temperatures at/above 86F, and aging plumbing. Outbreak count rose from 14 (2015) to a 34 peak (2018). — [CDC MMWR SS 73(1)](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/ss/ss7301a1.htm)
- [HARD] CDC (data year 2014): waterborne disease drives ~7.15M illnesses/yr, 118,000 hospitalizations, 6,630 deaths, $3.33B direct healthcare cost; NTM, Pseudomonas and Legionella account for $2.39B of that; Legionnaires' ~995 deaths and ~$37,300 per hospital stay (wide credible intervals). — [CDC EID 27(1)](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/27/1/19-0676_article)
- [HARD, scope note] The 2024 Minnesota Legionnaires' outbreak (34 confirmed cases, 2 deaths, 30 hospitalizations) was traced to a community water supply, not premise plumbing; included to show not every outbreak is a building-diagnosis problem. — [CDC EID 32(1), 2026](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/32/1/25-1232_article)
- [HARD-2ND, weak] Claim that ASHRAE 188 has had "minimal" impact because very few facilities that should have water management programs actually do; I found no percentage and the source was a search summary of vendor/advocacy pages. — see [ELI overview](https://www.eli.org/environmental-health-indoor-environments-buildings/legionella-management-building-water-systems) (not verified in text)

**Hot-water distribution / recirculation**
- [HARD, field-monitoring literature cited in a 2024 ACEEE paper] Recirculation losses ~one-third of DHW system energy use; Zhang 2013 monitored 28 multifamily buildings: average DHW system efficiency 35%, recirculation heat-loss fraction 33%; Bøhm 2013 (13 apartment buildings + 2 offices, Denmark) found 23-70% of supplied heat lost in recirculation/distribution. Field-measured recirc loss "could be much higher than design and modeling predictions" due to poor insulation, unbalanced flow, or cross flows. — [ACEEE 2024 Summer Study, PG&E/ZYD Energy](https://www.aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Best%20Practices%20for%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20Systems%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings-A%20Comparative%20Evaluation%20of%20Balancing%20Methods.pdf)
- [HARD, lab not field] Same paper: unbalanced systems starve the highest-resistance riser (long wait for hot water) and over-flow others (energy loss, erosion, pump failure); manual balancing valves gave good balance but "required a time-consuming initial setup"; no tested valve could balance below 0.1 GPM/riser; best valve could cut ~12% of thermal losses. Authors note lab data are "unavailable from field studies." — same ACEEE link above
- [ANECDOTE/trade press, no data] "Many DHW recirculation systems run into trouble due to improper balancing"; manual balancing "often leads to frustrating call-backs after occupancy." The article gives no prevalence or callback numbers and is manufacturer-aligned (promotes thermostatic valves). — [PHCP Pros: Balancing Hot Water Recirculation Systems](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/21560-balancing-hot-water-recirculation-systems)
- [ANECDOTE/trade press] ASPE Pipeline: recirc systems are "typically left to operate with no visibility on system or fixture performance," often are not on service-provider maintenance schedules, and balancing valves get moved "within a couple years" with "no indication on the flow or water velocity"; erosion-corrosion pipe failures follow excessive velocity (<2 ft/s recommended). No failure-rate data given. — [ASPE Pipeline](https://aspe.org/pipeline/pipeline-exclusive-corrosion-in-hot-water-recirculating-systems-and-velocity-effects/)
- [ANECDOTE] Hotel DHW case (PME): three 1995 storage tanks (two leaking), all three recirc pump bearings leaking for 6+ months, mixing valves scaled such that outlet temps swung 125-140F; diagnosis was visual + thermometer only, and the owner asked for a minimal "simple" tank-replacement proposal. — [PME: In hot water](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/106230-in-hot-water-a-dhw-logistical-predicament)
- [ANECDOTE, residential] Heatinghelp thread: lukewarm/fluctuating hot water with a recirc pump took "months" to trace to a speck of scale holding an integral check valve open; it recurred (valve replaced four times) until a separate spring check + strainer was added. Residential, but a clean example of a multi-candidate, trial-and-error diagnosis with a shared-cause structure. — [Heating Help forum](https://forum.heatinghelp.com/discussion/197801/lukewarm-fluctuating-hot-water-with-recirculation-pump)

**Other named symptoms (thin evidence)**
- Backflow-preventer test failure: figures of 10-25% annual and 42.5% initial failure circulate, from contractor/utility blogs (the 42.5% is one regional utility's residential assemblies). [VENDOR/unverified]. — [Pacific Backflow](https://www.pacificbackflow.com/post/annual-backflow-testing-and-what-happens-next-essential-steps-to-ensure-compliance); [Atlas Backflow](https://www.atlasbackflow.com/post/skipping-annual-backflow-testing)
- Recurring commercial drain/sewer backups: contractor blogs say grease and roots are the usual root causes and that a camera inspection is the only reliable way to find the catch point; the "28% roots / 22% grease" splits are unsourced marketing numbers. [VENDOR] — [Acme Plumbing](https://www.acmeplumbing.com/blog/recurring-backups-caused-by-debris-catch-points-only-visible-on-sewer-camera/); [Harold Bros](https://www.haroldbros.com/blog/commercial-drain-keeps-backing-up)
- Sensor faucet / flush valve faults: manufacturer and distributor troubleshooting guides list the same few causes (dead battery pack, dirty sensor lens, failed solenoid, clogged aerator/strainer). Short, well-bounded checklist rather than open-ended diagnosis. [VENDOR] — [Plumbing Supply and More](https://www.plumbingsupplyandmore.com/how-to-troubleshoot-commercial-sensor-faucet-wont-activate)
- Low pressure upper floors: backflow assembly can add a ~12 psi drop and starve upper-floor flush valves; stack effect/head loss of several psi per story (old trade-press piece, no data). [ANECDOTE/trade press] — [PHCP Pros: High-rise plumbing design](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/1899-high-rise-plumbing-design-and-plumbing-codes)
- PRV failure / water hammer: found only explanatory/contractor content (diaphragm cracking, 12-15 yr life belowground, hammer damaging PRVs); no failure-rate or cost data. [VENDOR] — [InspectApedia](https://inspectapedia.com/water/Water_Pressure_Regulator_Failures.php). Note the single largest case in the Chubb data above ($6.5M) was a pressure-relief-valve failure.

### Inferences
- Plumbing's economic pain is dominated by rare, severe events (flood, Legionella outbreak) rather than high-frequency service calls; the proof points available are insurance severity and CDC outbreak data, not callback or truck-roll data. This differs from HVAC, where the pain is mostly comfort complaints and repeat visits.
- The plumbing symptom with the closest structural analogue to the requester's HVAC tool is "hot water not hot / uneven hot water," because it has a shared-parent topology (heater/mixing valve/recirc pump/check valve upstream of many risers/branches) and many plausible, confusable causes (check valve stuck, balancing valve moved, pump failing, tank sediment, mixing valve scaled). That is my inference from the trade-press and forum accounts, not a finding any source states.
- Water-damage claims "from plumbing" are a mixed bag (toilets, heaters, sewer, tanks, mains, sprinklers). Most of those are detect/shut-off/replace problems rather than "which of 6 causes?" diagnostic problems, which limits the share of the dollar pain that a diagnostic aid could address.

### Gaps
- No independent per-symptom cost or frequency data for water hammer, PRV failure, booster-pump failure, flush-valve/sensor-faucet faults, or recirc imbalance.
- AIG and Chubb primary reports behind the 34%, 42%/45%, and $89,000 figures were not located.
- Multifamily "70.2% top insurance issue" unverified.
- Could not retrieve FM Global, Verisk, Travelers, or Zurich plumbing-specific commercial loss data; searches returned catastrophe/natural-peril material or generic blogs.
- No US data splitting Legionella outbreak root causes into diagnosable categories (e.g., failed mixing valve vs. low heater setpoint vs. dead leg) beyond the MMWR contributing-factor tallies.

## 2. Which of these involve trial-and-error or repeated truck rolls? Is there first-time-fix / repeat-visit data for plumbing?

### Takeaway
There is no credible plumbing-specific (let alone commercial plumbing) first-time-fix or repeat-visit dataset in the public sources I could reach; only generic field-service and HVAC benchmarks from vendors. Qualitative evidence says recirc balancing, recurring clogs, and intermittent hot-water complaints are the trial-and-error categories.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR, generic field service] "Industry median" first-time-fix rate ~75% (top 20% = 86%, bottom 20% = 53%); a failed first visit averages 2.7 total visits and ~13 extra days to resolve. These are repeated across vendor blogs with no stated original methodology or trade split. — [vsight.io Field Service KPIs](https://vsight.io/field-service-kpis/); [OptimoRoute](https://optimoroute.com/first-time-fix-rate/)
- [VENDOR, HVAC] HVAC average first-time-fix ~73%, top quartile 85%+; fewer than 75% of residential/light-commercial HVAC calls fixed on the first visit. — [FieldProxy HVAC benchmarks](https://www.fieldproxy.ai/benchmarks/hvac); [Fieldpie](https://www.fieldpie.com/blog/hvac-first-time-fix-rate/)
- [VENDOR] ServiceTitan's callback/recall chapter defines recalls as errors within 30 days of service (1 year for installs), names rushing/minor errors and individual technician knowledge gaps as typical causes, and gives only one customer anecdote (a Albuquerque contractor cut customer concerns 35% after tracking recalls). It provides no industry callback rate or cost per callback. — [ServiceTitan Contractor Playbook](https://www.servicetitan.com/guides/contractor-playbook/recalls-callbacks-warranty)
- [ANECDOTE/trade press] Recirc balancing "often leads to frustrating call-backs after occupancy" (no numbers). — [PHCP Pros](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/21560-balancing-hot-water-recirculation-systems)
- [VENDOR/contractor blogs] Recurring commercial drain backups commonly require repeated jetting until a camera inspection finds the actual obstruction or defect. — [Acme Plumbing](https://www.acmeplumbing.com/blog/recurring-backups-caused-by-debris-catch-points-only-visible-on-sewer-camera/)
- [ANECDOTE, residential] The heatinghelp case above: months of troubleshooting and four replacement parts before the actual cause was addressed. — [Heating Help](https://forum.heatinghelp.com/discussion/197801/lukewarm-fluctuating-hot-water-with-recirculation-pump)
- [ANECDOTE] Newer water-heating systems "require a stronger understanding of controls, electrical components, venting, and condensate management" than plumbers' traditional experience, which PME frames as a skills-gap driver. (From a search summary of PME coverage; article text not fetched.) — [PME: Continuing Education Moves from Optional to Essential](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/107589-continuing-education-moves-from-optional-to-essential-in-plumbing)

### Inferences
- Trial-and-error is plausible wherever the symptom is downstream of several hidden shared components (recirc loops, pressure zones, drain laterals) and the technician has no flow/temperature/pressure visibility. Evidence for that is qualitative only.
- The generic 75% FTFR figure is a weak proxy; plumbing break/fix calls (a clog, a leaking fixture) probably have higher FTFR than complex building-system complaints, but I found no data either way.

### Gaps
- No plumbing-specific or commercial-plumbing FTFR/repeat-visit numbers from Service Council, ServiceTitan, ACCA, PHCC, or MCAA. The Service Council search surfaced nothing.
- No data on truck rolls per commercial hot-water or pressure complaint.
- Reddit/forum sampling of "repeat visit" complaints was not possible (blocked).

## 3. Technician labor shortage and skill-gap data; how much is tribal knowledge?

### Takeaway
Shortage data is well documented at the trade level (BLS, ABC, AGC) but not for commercial service plumbers specifically; the tribal-knowledge claim ("70% of knowledge is unwritten") rests entirely on vendor blogs and is not usable as hard evidence.

### Cited Findings
- [HARD] BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook (current edition): 510,600 plumbers/pipefitters/steamfitters (2025); projected +7% 2025-35 (+34,500 jobs); ~42,000 openings/yr (mostly replacement); median pay $63,800 (2025); most learn via a 4-5 year apprenticeship; 65% work for plumbing/HVAC contractors, 3% for government. — [BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/plumbers-pipefitters-and-steamfitters.htm)
- [HARD, prior vintage] Earlier BLS projection (2024-34) reported as 504,500 jobs in 2024, +4%, ~44,000 openings/yr; the OOH page above now shows the newer 2025-35 vintage, so the two figures differ by edition rather than conflicting. — secondhand summary at [SimPRO](https://www.simprogroup.com/blog/plumbing-industry-statistics-2026)
- [HARD] ABC (Jan 15, 2026): construction needs 349,000 new workers in 2026 (down from 439,000 for 2025, 501,000 for 2024, 546,000 for 2023); 456,000 projected for 2027. This is all construction, not plumbing alone. — [ABC news release](https://www.abc.org/News-Media/News-Releases/abc-construction-industry-must-attract-349000-workers-in-2026-despite-macroeconomic-headwinds); [NCCER summary](https://www.nccer.org/newsroom/report-construction-needs-349k-new-workers-in-2026/)
- [HARD] AGC 2025 survey: 92% of construction firms report difficulty filling open positions; 45% say labor shortages are delaying projects; nearly one-third affected by immigration enforcement. Not broken out by plumbing. — [AGC](https://news.agc.org/workforce-development/workforce-shortages-delay-projects/)
- [HARD-2ND] ADP data via PME (2026): construction job changers got an average 12.9% raise in June 2026 (vs 6.6% all industries); median age of electricians/plumbers/HVAC techs fell by up to five years since Jan 2020; experienced technicians "have more employment options than ever." — [PME: Why Skilled Technicians Are Harder Than Ever to Keep](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/107656-why-skilled-technicians-are-harder-than-ever-to-keep)
- [HARD-2ND/VENDOR] "For every five plumbers retiring only one enters apprenticeship" (trade-press quote, unsourced). — [PME 2025 Plumbing Industry Outlook](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/106223-2025-plumbing-industry-outlook)
- [VENDOR/aggregator, unverified] "60-65% of PHCC member contractors turned down work in 2024 for lack of staff" and "200,000+ unfilled skilled-trade positions per year (PHCC 2025)"; also a widely repeated "550,000 plumber shortage by 2027" and ">20% of plumbers over 55." I could not trace these to PHCC or a primary survey. — [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/plumbing-industry-staffing-costs-2026); [Milwaukee Tool](https://onekeyresources.milwaukeetool.com/en/plumber-shortage)
- [VENDOR] "70% of critical operational knowledge is tribal"; "70% of maintenance technicians are over 50"; "AI knowledge capture cuts repeat failures 40%." These come from CMMS/AI-software vendor blogs and are mostly manufacturing-focused; do not treat as evidence. — [OxMaint](https://oxmaint.com/article/capturing-tribal-knowledge-before-technicians-retire); [Tractian](https://tractian.com/en/blog/preserve-maintenance-tribal-knowledge-ai-powered-cmms)
- [ANECDOTE/trade press] Skills-gap drivers: compressed timelines and a broader range of systems technicians are asked to work on (controls, electrical, venting for modern water heating). — [PME](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/107589-continuing-education-moves-from-optional-to-essential-in-plumbing)

### Inferences
- The quantitative shortage is real and similar to HVAC; BLS projects steady replacement-driven openings, so a tool that shortens diagnosis or lets less-experienced staff do more fits the problem. Unlike HVAC, there is no evidence that plumbing technicians commonly work from BAS data, which changes how such a tool would reach them.
- Because only 3% of plumbers work in government and most work for contractors, institutional in-house teams (hospital/campus facilities) are a minority of the labor pool; contractors serving many buildings are the dominant plumbing workforce. (Inference from BLS industry shares.)

### Gaps
- No plumbing-specific skill-gap survey (PHCC/ASPE) accessible; no measured share of commercial plumbing diagnosis that depends on undocumented/tribal knowledge.
- ABC/AGC/PHCC raw survey instruments not reviewed beyond press releases.

## 4. Documentation problem: how often are as-builts, riser diagrams, valve schedules, and shutoff locations missing or wrong?

### Takeaway
I found no independent, plumbing-specific measurement of as-built accuracy. What exists is general construction-documentation data, mostly from reality-capture/scanning vendors with a commercial interest, plus practitioner assertions that balancing valves and set positions drift unrecorded.

### Cited Findings
- [HARD-2ND, old but frequently cited] NIST-derived figure: inadequate interoperability (including inaccurate as-built information) costs the US capital-facilities industry ~$15.8B/yr (original study is NIST GCR 04-867, 2004; not re-fetched; covers all capital-facility data exchange, not plumbing). — repeated at [Existing Conditions](https://www.existingconditions.com/insights/the-what-why-and-how-of-as-built-drawings)
- [VENDOR/unverified] "Over 60% of maintenance delays are caused by missing or inaccurate as-built data (a FacilitiesNet report)"; "average commercial building shows 20-40% MEP routing deviation from design drawings"; "48% of construction rework stems from poor project data (FMI/Autodesk)." The first two are from scanning/drafting-service sites; the FMI/Autodesk study is real but concerns construction rework, not operations. — [Harmony AT](https://www.harmony-at.com/en/blog/as-built-drawings); [Primaverse](https://www.primaverse.com/post/as-built-drawing-services-why-accuracy-costs-less-now); [The Future 3D](https://www.thefuture3d.com/blog/common-as-built-documentation-mistakes/)
- [ANECDOTE/trade press] ASPE Pipeline: balancing valves get moved within a couple of years with no record of set position; recirc systems "do not even show up on the maintenance schedules given to a service provider." — [ASPE Pipeline](https://aspe.org/pipeline/pipeline-exclusive-corrosion-in-hot-water-recirculating-systems-and-velocity-effects/)
- [ANECDOTE] In the hotel DHW case the practitioner still had to infer equipment condition by visual inspection and spot temperature readings, indicating no live data or reliable records. — [PME](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/106230-in-hot-water-a-dhw-logistical-predicament)
- [HARD-adjacent, procedural] ASHRAE 188 / CDC water-management-program toolkits require a building water-system schematic/flow diagram and hazard analysis; the CDC toolkit exists because many facilities lack one (stated in guidance, not measured). — [CDC WMP Toolkit](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/toolkit/wmp-toolkit.html)
- [ANECDOTE, healthcare outbreak literature] A published healthcare case found no water management program, unmonitored water quality and prolonged disuse of a space causing stagnation. — via [search summary of state health guidance](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/health/documents/cedep/LD_HC_Guide.pdf)

### Inferences
- The strongest proxy for plumbing documentation gaps is that Legionella water-management programs require a system schematic; each such program effectively forces a mapping exercise, so facilities in healthcare and hospitality (already pressured by CMS/Joint Commission requirements, not verified here) will have the best plumbing maps. Everywhere else the maps are likely sparse. This is speculation.
- Plumbing documentation is likely worse than HVAC for concealed vertical risers, loops, isolation valves, and PRV zoning, but I found no data to confirm the comparison.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or industry survey quantifying missing/incorrect plumbing riser diagrams, valve schedules, or shutoff maps in existing commercial buildings.
- FacilitiesNet "60%" claim could not be traced to the original report.
- No data on how often valve tagging/shutoff-location records exist in campus or hospital portfolios.

## 5. What instrumentation/data exists in a typical commercial building for plumbing, and how much is on the BAS vs. unmonitored?

### Takeaway
Evidence points to plumbing being largely unmonitored: recirc loops run with no flow/temperature visibility, valve positions are not recorded, and the monitoring that does exist (hospital Legionella programs, submeters) is usually a separate retrofit layer, not native BAS points. I found no census of plumbing points on BAS; this determines feasibility and is the biggest data gap.

### Cited Findings
- [ANECDOTE/trade press, authoritative society] ASPE Pipeline says complex hot-water recirculation arrangements are "typically left to operate with no visibility on system or fixture performance." — [ASPE Pipeline](https://aspe.org/pipeline/pipeline-exclusive-corrosion-in-hot-water-recirculating-systems-and-velocity-effects/)
- [VENDOR-authored standards summary] ASHRAE 514 Appendix F lists what a monitored system would measure: entry-point temperature/pH/conductivity/disinfectant residual/turbidity; temperature in cold-water storage tanks; temperature at master thermostatic mixing valve outlets; temperature and flow at water-heater outlets, recirc lines before return, and each hot-water return balancing valve. Authors are employees of HC Info (water-management software vendor); text also says facilities "lacking building automation systems" use handheld instruments, implying many do. — [HC Info](https://hcinfo.com/blog/monitoring-building-water-quality-to-reduce-risk-and-stay-in-legionella-compliance/)
- [VENDOR] Halogen Systems (sensor vendor): disinfectant/quality sensors are costly (>$700/yr consumables) and traditional units waste ~70,000 gal/yr of heated water, so water quality is "difficult to monitor in building hot water systems." — [Water Online](https://www.wateronline.com/doc/sensors-are-the-missing-link-for-legionella-control-in-buildings-and-hospitals-0001)
- [HARD, peer-reviewed] Continuous retrofit monitoring of 220 thermostatic mixing valves in a hospital over three years: 73% of 865 samples positive for heterotrophic bacteria, only 0.5% positive for L. pneumophila; heterotroph counts were significantly associated with the number of flushing events in the prior seven days; temperatures spanned 25-89C (median 25.7C). Shows continuous temperature/flow data can flag stagnation risk, but sensors had to be installed for the study. — [IJERPH 2019 / PMC6518245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6518245/)
- [HARD, via search snippet only; full text not retrieved (HTTP 403/cookie wall)] A 2024 Journal of Hospital Infection review concluded multi-point continuous remote sensor monitoring is effective at identifying redundant/low-use outlets, hydraulic imbalance, and inconsistent temperature delivery. A new UK hospital study (Sept 2024-May 2025) monitored 258 assets: 118 not used daily, >40% chronically underused (<2 events/week) so water stagnated at room temperature. — [JHI 2024](https://www.journalofhospitalinfection.com/article/S0195-6701(24)00223-8/abstract); [JHI 2026 study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0195670126002288)
- [VENDOR] Modern submeters/flow meters connect to BAS over BACnet/Modbus, and cloud submetering offers leak alarming; marketing, no penetration data. EPA WaterSense (government) recommends meters and submeters for leak detection but does not say how many buildings have them. — [Keyence](https://www.keyence.com/blog/the-smart-guide-to-flow-meters-for-building-automation-systems.jsp); [EPA WaterSense metering BMP](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-11/ws-commercial-bmp-watersenseatwork_section2.1_meteringandsubmetering.pdf)
- [VENDOR] Aquastat/time-clock controlled recirc pumps are the common control scheme (return-line temperature sensor starts the pump); hospitality owners often bypass time-clock controls so hot water is always available. — [Grundfos](https://product-selection.grundfos.com/us/applications/commercial-buildings/commercial-hot-water-recirculation); [MnCEE hospitality recirc controls](https://www.mncee.org/hot-water-recirculation-controls-hospitality-buildings-closed)
- [HARD, procedural] The Chubb/Hartford sources recommend leak sensors and automatic shut-off valves, but both note adoption is low (Chubb: 1.5% of insured US residences have shut-off devices, 2019, residential). — [Chubb media room](https://chubb.mediaroom.com/waterdamage)

### Inferences
- HVAC diagnostics can lean on points that nearly every BAS already trends (zone temp, damper, discharge air). For plumbing, the equivalent points (recirc return temp per branch, balancing valve flow, booster pump discharge pressure, PRV outlet pressure) are mostly absent; the likely common exceptions are boiler/water-heater enable/alarm status, tank/booster alarms, and in newer or healthcare buildings, some mixing-valve or return temperatures. This is inference from the absence of any "typical BAS plumbing points" source, not a measured finding.
- A data-driven approach is most feasible in hospitals and large hospitality/campus plants that already run Legionella monitoring programs; elsewhere the tool would probably be technician-guided check ordering with manual readings, which is a different product from BAS-point-driven troubleshooting.

### Gaps
- No census or survey of which plumbing points exist on BAS in US commercial buildings (the Mortar/BMS-dataset route the requester already uses might be the only way to answer this; I did not search those datasets).
- No data on share of buildings with water submeters or leak detection tied to BAS.
- JHI/ScienceDirect papers behind paywalls/403, so only snippet-level findings.

## 6. What do technicians themselves say are their biggest headaches?

### Takeaway
I could not sample Reddit, Plumbing Zone, Contractor Talk, or Facebook groups, so there is no direct trade-forum evidence here; the practitioner voice that was retrievable points to recirc/hot-water faults, aging equipment with unrecorded modifications, intermittent faults, and skill gaps on newer water-heating controls.

### Cited Findings
- [ANECDOTE, residential] Months-long hot-water fluctuation traced to scale lodged in an integral check valve; repeated recurrence. Pros on the thread proposed multiple candidate causes (pump mode, flow direction, check valve, insulation, mixing valve) before the real cause was found. — [Heating Help forum](https://forum.heatinghelp.com/discussion/197801/lukewarm-fluctuating-hot-water-with-recirculation-pump)
- [ANECDOTE, trade press, engineer voice] Hotel DHW system with 29-year-old tanks, leaking pumps, scaled mixing valves, and an owner who wanted a minimal fix; the author stresses logistics and constrained access over technical complexity. — [PME](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/106230-in-hot-water-a-dhw-logistical-predicament)
- [ANECDOTE] Hot-water complaints push operators to raise setpoints or recirc flow, increasing losses, rather than diagnosing imbalance. — as summarized from [ACEEE 2024](https://www.aceee.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/ssb24/pdfs/Best%20Practices%20for%20Hot%20Water%20Distribution%20Systems%20in%20Multifamily%20Buildings-A%20Comparative%20Evaluation%20of%20Balancing%20Methods.pdf)
- [VENDOR/contractor blogs] Frequent check-valve failures in recirc lines (backflow creates cold spots) and recirc pump failure causing property-wide hot-water loss within hours. — [Hotel Facility Guide](https://hotelfacilityguide.com/building-systems/hotel-plumbing-systems-guide/)
- [ANECDOTE/trade press] Technicians increasingly need controls/electrical/venting/condensate knowledge for modern water heating; PME frames this as a skills-gap challenge. — [PME](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/107589-continuing-education-moves-from-optional-to-essential-in-plumbing)
- [ANECDOTE/vendor] ServiceTitan-sourced callback guidance: rushing/minor errors and individual knowledge gaps (e.g., a tech strong on toilets but weak on kitchen faucets) drive callbacks. — [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/guides/contractor-playbook/recalls-callbacks-warranty)

### Inferences
- The one complaint pattern recurring across independent practitioner voices (ASPE, PME, the forum thread, hotel guidance) is hot-water temperature/circulation faults with several confusable upstream causes and no instrumentation. That is the best candidate for a symptom-scoped diagnostic tool, but this rests on a handful of anecdotes, not a systematic sample.
- A 30-year-veteran-type reaction (as in the requester's existing HVAC pilot) is still needed for plumbing; I recommend a short expert-interview round with a commercial service plumber and a hospital/campus plumbing foreman before any build decision.

## Cross-cutting note on the "analogous opportunity" question (inferences only; every claim here is derived from the cited findings above)

- Where the HVAC analogue holds: hot-water recirculation/temperature complaints share a topology of shared upstream causes and many confusable downstream candidates, are documented by independent engineers as chronically unbalanced and unmonitored, and carry a safety multiplier through Legionella (CDC: 86% of 2015-2020 outbreaks biofilm-associated, 53% in healthcare settings).
- Where it breaks: (1) plumbing lacks the ubiquitous BAS trend data the HVAC tool relies on; (2) the largest dollar losses (flood, burst pipe, water heater, toilet) are mostly detect-and-isolate problems, not multi-cause diagnostic problems; (3) there is no public first-time-fix or callback dataset to size the truck-roll waste; (4) the best-instrumented segment (hospital Legionella programs) is already served by compliance-monitoring vendors whose marketing I could not independently validate.
- Highest-value follow-ups: obtain a plumbing point census from BAS datasets already on hand; interview 3-5 commercial service plumbers and hospital facility engineers about the recirc/hot-water complaint workflow; request actual claim-cause breakdowns from a carrier (Chubb/AIG/FM Global) rather than relying on secondhand figures.
