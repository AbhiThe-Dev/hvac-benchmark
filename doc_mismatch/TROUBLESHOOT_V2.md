# Technician-guided troubleshooting: working model v2 (one visit, several complaints)

v0 asked "which box feeds this room?". v1 worked out what is wrong with ONE complaint. **v2 takes a visit with several complaints and works them as one investigation**, because a fault at a shared parent (an air handler) can explain several complaints at once, and one reading of that parent then speaks for all of its rooms. It sits beside v0 and v1 and changes neither. It uses no trend data.

**Nothing here has been validated.** It has been exercised only in simulation and by unit tests. Every probability and every minute is a placeholder (v1's catalogue, plus a handful of new rates below). No claim is made that it saves a technician any time; the study that compared it with working each complaint separately is Addendum 30 in `PREREGISTRATION_DR.md`.

## Run it

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m docmismatch.troubleshoot.v2 --complaints Room-0,Room-1,Room-2,Room-3          # interactive, on the synthetic example building (3 air handlers, 24 rooms)
.venv/bin/python -m docmismatch.troubleshoot.v2 --demo --tier T3 --complaints Room-0,Room-1,Room-2,Room-3,Room-4,Room-5,Room-6 --truth ahu_fan_off,ahu_fan_off,ahu_fan_off,ahu_fan_off,ahu_fan_off,ahu_fan_off,ahu_fan_off
.venv/bin/python -m docmismatch.troubleshoot.v2 --building my_building.json --tier T2 --complaints Room-12,Room-13     # your own building, v1's JSON format
.venv/bin/python doc_mismatch/scripts/74_troubleshoot_joint.py                                     # the pre-registered study (Addendum 30)
.venv/bin/python -m pytest doc_mismatch/tests/test_troubleshoot_v2.py doc_mismatch/tests/test_troubleshoot_joint_study.py
```

The demo answers for the technician with a simulated one: it is an illustration, not a result.

## What it does

1. The complaints (rooms) are grouped by their **documented** air handler. Per group there is one shared state: the air handler is fine, its fan is off, or its duct static pressure is low.
2. It keeps a belief over the shared state and over each complaint's cause (v1's causes: hardware, configuration, "the documented box is wrong", and the two upstream ones). A second complaint on the same air handler makes an upstream cause likelier; rooms on that air handler that do **not** complain make it less likely.
3. It recommends **one check for the whole visit**: the one that tells the most per minute, summed over the complaints it informs. A reading of an air handler is one option that informs every complaint of its group; checks at a room or box are per complaint. Location-aware, never repeated, v1's availability and minutes.
4. After each answer it updates and recommends again. A complaint is concluded when its leading cause reaches 95% **and a real reading has informed it**: the pattern of complaints alone never concludes.

## Adopted change (2026-09-27)

The live tool's default `min_rate` (the bits-per-minute a check must be worth to be offered) is **0.015**, up from the historical placeholder 0.003, in two steps:
- **0.01** is confirmed across all 18 of Addendum 30's cells at both tiers (Addendum 35): faster everywhere, never a confidence cost, and at T2 only a real 2-3 point accuracy cost on about a third of them (6 of 18).
- **0.015** is a further step the user chose directly (2026-09-27) and asked to have confirmed the same way (Addendum 36). **The confirmation came back NOT CONFIRMED, and not narrowly: at T3 it is exactly as clean a win as 0.01 (18 of 18 cells, no confidence cost), but at T2 it trades real accuracy (2-4 points) in 13 of the 18 cells, not the minority 0.01 affected.** Never a net-worse outcome and never a confidence cost, at either value, in any of the 72 (cell, tier, value) results measured. Told this, the user judged a 1.2-3.7 point accuracy cost an acceptable price for the speed and kept the live default at 0.015 -- a considered decision made with the NOT CONFIRMED result in hand, not an oversight. `--min-rate 0.01` gives the value with full 18-cell backing instead.

Override with `--min-rate` on the command line; v1's own single-complaint tool and all frozen study scripts are unchanged.

## What it assumes (placeholders)

| Assumption | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `f` | 0.03 | probability that an air handler has an upstream fault in the visit's window (fan-off : low-static = 5 : 12, v1's shares) |
| `rho` | 0.03 | probability that a room has its own local fault |
| `r_fan`, `r_low` | 0.9, 0.5 | probability that a room on an air handler with its fan off (low static) complains |
| the drawings | mostly right | v1's `mostly_right` prior (5% of documented boxes wrong); the documented air handler of every box is assumed right |
| one first-order cause per complaint | | a room is affected by the upstream fault or has its own, not both |

## What the study found

Simulation only (Addendum 30, `results/docmismatch/troubleshoot_joint/joint_report.md`); 18 cells, 1000 visits each; the visits average 2.1 complaints and 17% have a shared upstream cause in the primary cell.

- **Using what the other complaints say is where the value is, and a cheap routine gets it.** Asking how many other rooms on the same air handler complain (and how many do not), and never reading the same air handler twice, takes 67% (no control system) to 81% (with one) of the minutes off visits with a shared cause, compared with working each complaint on its own.
- **The full joint machinery did not beat that routine:** on shared-cause visits it was 29-32% slower for 1-4 points more accuracy, and slower where the complaints have nothing in common. Frozen decision: *the routine captures it*.
- Against my invented fast stand-ins it is slower without a control system (+19%) and equal with one, and far more accurate; those stand-ins are not technicians.
- **A flaw I found after the run:** the rule "a diagnosis needs a real reading" marks only the complaint (or air handler) actually read as informed, so a reading at one box that bears on the air handler does not count for the other complaints. That probably handicaps the joint tool and the routine alike; not yet tested.
- Two-step look-ahead: mixed (about 7% faster without a control system in the primary cell, a wash with one); three cells only.

**Follow-up (Addendum 31): the flaw was fixed for both, and the verdict stands.** Letting a reading that bears on the air handler (the inlet static pressure) count for the whole group took 25% off the joint tool's minutes on shared-cause visits without a control system, and the same idea (carrying a low inlet static to the other tickets of that air handler) took 15% off the routine. The joint machinery is still 17% (no control system) to 29% (control system) slower than the routine on those visits for 0.4-2.2 points more accuracy. **Frozen decision: the routine still wins.** The switch is `JointSession(group_informing=True)`; the study's policies are `tool_ticket_memo2`, `tool_joint2`, `tool_joint2_la2` and (secondary) `tool_joint3`.

## Against a language model playing the experienced technician (Addendum 33)

A pilot (10 visits of each kind, both tiers, 60 sessions): a fresh Claude session (`sonnet`) per visit worked through a terminal with the tool's own checks, minutes and answers, shown no probabilities. Simulation only; a language model is not a technician. **Without a control system (T2) it was about a sixth faster than the routine (76 against 92 minutes per visit) at about the same exact accuracy (82% against 84%), but 'confident' far more often when wrong. With a control system (T3) the routine was faster (58 against 76), entirely on shared-cause visits (18 against 175 minutes): the LLM read the air handler first, every time, and then went on to confirm each box, lost the shared cause on some, and was less accurate (82% against 97%).** So the finding that the tool is not faster than a fast technician without a control system is not an artefact of my rule-based stand-ins, and the routine's real edge is stopping early on shared-cause evidence. Wide intervals; one model, one prompt; `results/docmismatch/troubleshoot_llm/llm_report.md`. Run: `.venv/bin/python doc_mismatch/scripts/77_llm_technician.py --dir RUNDIR prepare`, then one model session per `RUNDIR/tech S<nn>`, then `analyze`.

**Why the joint tool still costs minutes (exploratory note E1, `PREREGISTRATION_DR.md`).** With every policy given the same answers, the joint tool's extra minutes come mostly from two design choices, not from joint reasoning: (1) its check budget is pooled (12 x the number of complaints) while the routine caps each ticket at 12, so it keeps digging on hard complaints (that is where its extra accuracy on several-complaint visits came from); (2) its exact confidence after one strong air-handler reading can sit just under the 0.95 stopping threshold (0.948 against the routine's 0.952), so it pays for a 19-minute visit to clear it. Putting the routine's cap on the joint session removes about 60-80% of the several-complaint excess and half of the shared-cause excess; at a slightly lower threshold it is level with the routine on shared-cause visits. Over all visits it is then still 1-4% slower for the same accuracy, so the routine remains the thing to build first. Working the complaints in arrival order did not help. Simulation only.

## Is the routine just too conservative about when to stop? (Addendum 34)

A parameter sweep of the routine's own engine at T2 (no LLM): lowering the belief needed to conclude (`tau`, default 0.95) recovers only PART of the LLM's speed edge without a matching cost. **The largest change that costs neither accuracy nor confidence, `tau` = 0.90, recovers 7 of the LLM's 17 points; going further trades accuracy or safety away.** A different, unregistered knob told a better story: raising the bar for a check to be worth its time (`min_rate`, default 0.003 bits/minute) to 0.01 cut 30% off T2 minutes with accuracy unchanged and, in this sample, fewer confident-wrong diagnoses.

**Confirmed across cells (Addendum 35): at T3 it holds up everywhere.** Checked on all 18 of Addendum 30's cells at both tiers: at T3 the change is faster in every single cell (8-15% fewer minutes) with accuracy and confidence unchanged or better, no exception. **At T2 it is faster in every cell too, but a real (2-3 point) accuracy cost shows up in a third of them** (25-39% fewer minutes there); which third is not tied to map faults specifically. In none of the 36 (cell, tier) results does the change make the tool more likely to be confidently wrong. Frozen decision: NOT CONFIRMED (the registered bar needed a 15-of-18-cell majority with no accuracy cost at T2; it reached 12). `results/docmismatch/troubleshoot_minrate/minrate_report.md`.

**Head to head with the language model, on Addendum 33's own visits.** Applying `min_rate` = 0.01 to those same 30 visits: **at T2 the tuned routine and the language model are statistically the same speed, while the routine keeps its own accuracy and a near-zero wrong-and-confident rate (0.0% against the LLM's 16.1%). At T3 the tuned routine beats the language model outright on speed, accuracy and safety.** The one place the language model still loses badly, shared-cause visits, is untouched by this tuning; that gap is the routine's shortcut, not something `min_rate` explains. So the T2 speed question this chain (Addenda 33-35) set out to answer resolves without a language model: one existing setting closes it. `results/docmismatch/troubleshoot_minrate/vs_llm_report.md`. `results/docmismatch/troubleshoot_stop/stop_report.md`.

## Is the economizer-fault gap a real, common thing, or a one-off? (2026-09-28, literature check)

Checked whether the stuck-low economizer found in "VAV Not Cooling EP173" (above) is a recognized, common fault or a one-off from a single video. It is one of the most studied and regulated faults in commercial HVAC:

- **Prevalence.** A California Energy Commission PIER case study, citing New Buildings Institute research, found **64% of economizers surveyed suffer from faults and failures** -- one of five major RTU fault categories studied (with refrigerant circuit, airflow, thermostat, sensors) ([WCEC/CEC case study](https://wcec.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/07/Case-Study_FaultDetectionDiagnostics_PRINT.pdf)).
- **Regulation.** California Title 24 has required automated fault detection and diagnostics (AFDD) on new commercial RTUs since 2014 specifically because of this; the code names five faults AFDD must detect, two of which are almost exactly what the video showed: **"not economizing when it should"** and **"damper not modulating"** ([Title 24 Stakeholders](http://title24stakeholders.com/economizer-fault-detection-diagnostics-fdd-requirements/); [WCEC/CEC case study](https://wcec.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/07/Case-Study_FaultDetectionDiagnostics_PRINT.pdf)).
- **A published, named diagnostic exists.** PNNL's "Rooftop Unit Embedded Diagnostics" defines seven algorithms; AFDD1 checks whether the outdoor-air damper is modulating, AFDD3 checks whether the RTU economizes when it should, using mixed-air, outdoor-air and return-air temperatures plus the damper command -- a comparison of a commanded position against a handful of BAS temperature points, the same kind of evidence this tool's `bas_*` checks already use for other causes ([PNNL-23790](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/PNNL-23790.pdf)).
- **Cost and detectability.** The same case study's Table 1 gives "economizer damper failure" a 75% probability of occurring within a 15-year equipment life, a 24% efficiency penalty, and only a **25% probability of being caught without AFDD tools** (75% with); "non-optimal economizer setpoint" is similar (30% penalty). Katipamula et al. (2003), cited in PNNL-23790, found **$100-$12,000/year in wasted energy cost per unit** from improper economizer operation (not fully economizing, stuck damper, bad scheduling).

**Reading.** This is not a one-off. It is a well-documented, regulator-recognized fault category, and the 25%-without-AFDD detection rate is a real-world number supporting exactly the premise this tool is built on: a fault a technician can easily miss without a prompt. **Still NOT modeled:** v2's only two upstream/AHU-level causes are `ahu_fan_off` and `low_static`; nothing represents an economizer that fails to open enough, or a discharge/supply-air setpoint that is wrong. Adding this would be a real change to the causes shared by v0/v1/v2 (a new upstream state, its own placeholder frequency and detection rates, a new `bas_*`-style check built on PNNL's AFDD1/AFDD3 logic) and would need the same pre-registration as every other addition here -- not done, and not started without the user's go-ahead.

Sources: [WCEC/California Energy Commission case study](https://wcec.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/07/Case-Study_FaultDetectionDiagnostics_PRINT.pdf); [PNNL-23790, Rooftop Unit Embedded Diagnostics](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/PNNL-23790.pdf); [Title 24 Stakeholders, economizer FDD requirements](http://title24stakeholders.com/economizer-fault-detection-diagnostics-fdd-requirements/).

## `econ_fault` built and studied (Addendum 37, 2026-09-28)

Added as a third upstream state, purely additively: `causes.ECON_FAULT` and its two checks (`bas_economizer`, `ahu_economizer_test`, built on PNNL's AFDD1/AFDD3 logic) are defined but not enrolled in the shared `causes.CAUSES` / `catalogue.CATALOGUE`, so every existing script, test and stored result is untouched (verified directly: 3,720 replayed episodes against Addenda 30's and 31's original stored numbers, 0 differ). A case opts in via the existing `extra_causes`/`shares`/`checks` parameters; `JointSettings`/`WorldParams` generalise from two upstream states to N via new `extra_states`/`extra_affect`/`extra_share` fields, algebraically identical to today when unset.

**One cell (Addendum 30's primary shared/map/reliability levels), both tiers, 1000 events** (`results/docmismatch/troubleshoot_econ/econ_report.md`). All gates passed. The routine correctly names `econ_fault` 78-88% of the time. **On the 16 `econ_fault`-shared visits (rare, as this cause's low 0.15 affect rate predicts), the routine matched the joint machinery's minutes at T2 and took only 49% of them at T3 (81 vs 166 minutes) -- the shared-cause verdict from Addenda 30/31 extends to this structurally different cause, more decisively than expected.** But the routine's wrong-and-confident rate on `econ_fault` complaints (5.9% at both tiers) is about 4.3-4.5 points above a fresh-world `low_static` reference (1.4-1.6%) -- **frozen decision: UNSAFE**, since the pre-registered safety check overrides the speed result. A plausible but untested mechanism: `econ_fault`'s lower prior share and affect rate mean the routine's belief travels further before crossing `tau`, on evidence from a placeholder detection rate (`bas_economizer` "strong" = 0.92, unmeasured) that was never tuned for this lower base rate. The 18-cell sweep this would need next, and a check of that one placeholder rate, are both not done.

**Did it make the overall tool faster? No.** P3 above is scoped to the rare (1.6% of visits) subset where `econ_fault` causes a shared visit; averaged over all 1000 visits, minutes went 111.7->119.6 (T2 routine, +7%), 118.8->129.5 (T2 joint, +9%), 64.6->64.0 (T3 routine, flat), 71.2->77.9 (T3 joint, +9%) versus Addendum 31's original numbers -- flat at best, slower in three of four rows. Adding any candidate cause taxes every visit a little (it must be weighed and usually ruled out even when absent), and that constant cost outweighs a saving earned on only 1.6% of visits. The addendum's predictions never claimed otherwise; this just heads off reading P3 as a net win for the tool as a whole.

**Did accuracy at least hold steady? No -- it fell in all four rows too**: 79.7%->77.4% (T2 routine), 80.2%->79.2% (T2 joint), 82.5%->80.1% (T3 routine), 84.3%->79.6% (T3 joint), a drop of 1.0 to 4.7 points. Same mechanism as the speed cost, and the whole-tool version of what P4 already found: `econ_fault` is now a candidate on every complaint, including the ones that have nothing to do with it, and belief mass can drift onto it (`bas_economizer` weakly cross-detects `low_static` too) where before there was no such option. Honest scorecard: no speed win, a real accuracy cost, and a safety failure on the complaints it targets -- three problems, not one.
