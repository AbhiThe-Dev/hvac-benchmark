# Fewer dead-end checks on HVAC call-outs

**A request for 45 minutes of a technician's time and a small sample of anonymized service tickets**

I'm [YOUR NAME] at [YOUR AFFILIATION]. I'm building a decision aid for technicians called out for an HVAC complaint (too hot, too cold, no airflow), especially where the drawings can't be fully trusted. You give it the complaint and what the drawings say; it suggests the next check, ranked by how likely it is to find the cause and how much time it costs. The goal is fewer dead-end checks.

It is an early research prototype and has not been tried with technicians. I want to build it on how call-outs actually go, not on my assumptions.

## What I'm asking for

| | Ask | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A conversation** with a service technician or lead, ideally on buildings built before 2000. We walk through 3-5 recent call-outs: the complaint, the checks in order, what it turned out to be, how long it took. | 45 min |
| 2 | **A sample of closed HVAC service tickets**, 50-200, anonymized. | Your export |
| 3 | *Optional:* **ride along** on a call-out or two. I observe only, with the building contact's OK. | Half a day |
| 4 | *Optional:* for buildings with a building automation system, whether trends are stored. The tool can use this but does not depend on it. | 15 min |

**Ticket fields** (all optional; skip anything you can't share): month and year, building type and approximate year built, system type, the complaint as written, the root cause as recorded, actions in order if recorded, labor hours, and whether the drawings were wrong or missing.

**What I don't need:** trend data, control-system access, tenant or client names, addresses, floor plans, or pricing.
